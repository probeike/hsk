What you'll learn

  1. Simple directional complements (V + 来 / 去) — direction encoded from the speaker's point of view.
  2. V₁了 … 就 V₂ … — one action immediately triggers the next.
  3. Rhetorical 能 … 吗? / 不 … 吗? — questions that aren't questions.

1. V + 来 / 去 Simple directional complements — motion, relative to the speaker

When a speaker reaches for this: to say something moves, and to make clear which way relative to them — towards themselves (来) or away (去). Common stems: 上, 下, 进, 出, 回, 过, 起 — plus learned verbs 买, 带, 搬.

The English trap

English says "come up" or "go up" with different verbs, but uses up the same way either way. Chinese folds speaker-orientation into the complement: 上来 vs 上去. Ask yourself where is the speaker standing? — 来 if motion is towards them, 去 if away. You cannot pick 来/去 by guessing; the physical setup decides.

Why this order? (PTS)

The main verb names the motion, then 来/去 tells you which way the motion ended up. Action, then destination — literally in the order it unfolds.

V + 来 / 去

我们在楼上等你呢，你上来吧。

Wǒmen zài lóushàng děng nǐ ne, nǐ shànglai ba.

we / upstairs / waiting for you, / you / come-up

We're waiting for you upstairs — come on up. (Speaker is upstairs, so motion is towards them → 来.)

谁在外边？你出去看看吧。

Shéi zài wàibian? Nǐ chūqu kànkan ba.

who / outside? / you / go-out / take-a-look

Who's outside? Go out and have a look. (Speaker is inside, so motion is away → 去.)

Place objects go before 来/去

If the object is a place (school, upstairs, the classroom), it slots between the verb and 来/去:

老师进教室来了。

Lǎoshī jìn jiàoshì lai le.

teacher / enter / classroom / come / PERF

The teacher came into the classroom. (Speaker is in the classroom.)

If the object is a thing, it can go on either side:

明天要带作业来。 / 明天要带来作业。

Míngtiān yào dài zuòyè lai.

(You) need to bring your homework tomorrow.

Guided walkthrough: choosing 来 or 去

Think in space

Before you answer, place the speaker in the scene. Then name the motion. If the motion ends nearer to the speaker, use 来. If it moves away from the speaker's current place, use 去. If a place is named, put that place before 来/去.

speaker upstairs
你上来吧
You are coming up to where I am.
speaker downstairs
你上去吧
You are going up away from where I am.
speaker in classroom
老师进教室来了
The teacher entered toward the speaker's location.
speaker outside classroom
老师进教室去了
The teacher entered away from the speaker's location.

Practice

You're upstairs, on the phone with a friend downstairs. You want them to come up. Pick:

  • 你上来吧。✓ Motion towards speaker (up to where I am).
  • 你上去吧。✗ That's "go up" — used if the speaker is on the ground floor sending someone else up.
  • 你来上吧。✗ Word order is reversed — the main verb (上) comes first.

Build: "(My) friend went back home."

朋友 回 家 去 了

Build: "The teacher came into the classroom." (Speaker is in the classroom.)

老师 进 教室 来 了

Build: "Tomorrow I'll bring (my) homework." (Speaker is the teacher in class.)

明天 我 带 作业 来

A place-object ("upstairs", "classroom") must slot where?

  • Between the verb and 来/去 (e.g. 上楼来).✓ Place objects always sit before 来/去.
  • After 来/去 (e.g. 上来楼).✗ That's ungrammatical — place objects must precede the directional.
  • Either side, freely.✗ Only thing-objects have that flexibility; places are fixed.

Fill the blank: 门没关，你 ___ 吧。
(The door's open — come in. Speaker is inside.)

进来 — speaker is inside, motion is toward them, so 来.

Fill the blank: 你怎么还没 ___ ？我都在楼下等你半小时了。
(The speaker is downstairs waiting.)

Wait — downstairs, waiting for them to come to me? Actually, if I'm downstairs and they need to come down… answer is 下来 (come down). Motion is towards speaker → 来.

2. V₁了 … 就 V₂ … Action A finishes, then immediately action B

When a speaker reaches for this: two actions happen back-to-back, with the second triggered by the first. English often uses "as soon as…" or "once I've finished…, I'll…". Chinese does it compactly: V₁了 … 就 V₂.

Why this order? (PTS)

Action one, then action two — same order they actually happen. The 了 closes off the first action; 就 then launches the second immediately.

Subj + V₁ + 了 …+ 就 + V₂

我下了课就吃饭。

Wǒ xià le kè jiù chī fàn.

I / finish-PERF / class / then-immediately / eat / meal

As soon as I finish class, I'll eat.

妈妈起了床就做早饭。

Māma qǐ le chuáng jiù zuò zǎofàn.

mom / get-PERF / bed / then-immediately / make / breakfast

Mom makes breakfast as soon as she gets up.

Two different subjects? Second subject hugs 就

If the two actions have different subjects, the second subject sits right before 就:

你下了课，我们就去书店。

Nǐ xià le kè, wǒmen jiù qù shūdiàn.

you / finish-PERF / class, / we / then / go / bookstore

As soon as you finish class, we'll go to the bookstore.

Practice

Finish the thought: 我太累了，___ 。
(I'm exhausted — as soon as I get home I'll sleep.)

e.g. 回家了就睡觉 (get home → sleep) or 吃了饭就睡觉.

Build: "As soon as he arrives at the office, I'll tell him."

他 到 了 办公室 我 就 告诉 他

Build: "As soon as I finish class, I'll go to the bookstore."

我 下 了 课 就 去 书店

Two different subjects. Which is correct for "As soon as you finish class, we'll go eat."?

  • 你下了课我们就去吃饭。✓ Second subject 我们 hugs 就 directly.
  • 你下了课就我们去吃饭。✗ 就 must come right before V₂, after the second subject.
  • 下了课你我们就去吃饭。✗ Subject of V₁ (你) belongs at the start of its clause.

Fill: 妈妈 ___ 做早饭。
(Mom makes breakfast as soon as she gets up.)

起了床就 — action 1 (get up) finishes, 就 kicks off action 2 (make breakfast).

What does this imply?
他到了家就给我打电话。

  • He got home a while ago and then called me.✗ The pattern stresses immediacy, not a delay.
  • The moment he gets home, he (will) call me.✓ V₁了…就V₂ = action 2 follows action 1 immediately.
  • He called before getting home.✗ 了 closes action 1 first; action 2 follows.

3. Rhetorical questions: 能 … 吗? / 不 … 吗? Questions that mean the opposite

When a speaker reaches for this: they're making a point, not asking. English does the same thing ("Didn't I tell you?", "Can you really expect…?"). The form is a question, but it flips to deliver the answer. Positive question → negative meaning. Negative question → positive meaning.

The English trap

You're tempted to read these as real yes/no questions and answer them. They're not — they're assertions dressed as questions. Context and tone tell you it's rhetorical; your job is to hear the implied claim.

你不做作业，也不练习，能学好吗？

Nǐ bù zuò zuòyè, yě bù liànxí, néng xué hǎo ma?

you / not do homework / nor / practise / — can-you-learn-well?

You don't do homework or practise — how could you possibly learn it well? (= you can't.)

你每天晚上吃了饭就睡觉，能不胖吗？

Nǐ měi tiān wǎnshang chī le fàn jiù shuìjiào, néng bù pàng ma?

you / every night / eat-PERF / then sleep, / can-not-be-fat?

You eat dinner and go straight to bed every night — how could you not put on weight? (= of course you will.)

Decoding rule (keep this handy)

positive question
能…吗?
= negative assertion ("no, you can't")
negative question
能不…吗?
= positive assertion ("yes, of course")

Mini conversion table

Literal question Intended meaning
不练习，能学好吗？
Can you learn it well?		 You can't learn it well.
每天吃了饭就睡觉，能不胖吗？
Can you not get fat?		 Of course you'll put on weight.
每天都练习，能打不好吗？
Can you play badly?		 Of course you can play well.

Practice

What is the speaker actually claiming?
天气这么冷，你穿得这么少，能不生病吗？

  • You probably won't get sick.✗ Negative question 能不…吗? flips to positive.
  • You're definitely going to get sick.✓ 能不生病吗? = "how could you not get sick?" = you will.
  • The speaker is asking for information.✗ It's rhetorical — a claim, not a real question.

What is the speaker claiming?
你今天爬山爬得那么快，能不累吗？

  • You're obviously tired (no surprise).✓ 能不累吗 → "how could you not be tired" → you are.
  • Maybe you won't be tired.✗ Negative form + 吗 gives positive assertion.

A friend says 我不练习，也不复习，能学好吗？ What are they really claiming?

  • They definitely won't learn it well.✓ Positive 能…吗? → negative meaning.
  • They definitely will learn it well.✗ That would require 能不…吗?
  • They're genuinely asking.✗ It's rhetorical — an assertion disguised as a question.

Build: "You exercise every day — how could you not get thin?"

你 每天 都 运动 能 不 瘦 吗

Fill: 天气这么冷，你穿得这么少， ___ ？
(You dress so lightly in this cold — how could you not get sick?)

能不生病吗 or 能不感冒吗 — negative rhetorical → positive claim (you will get sick).

Decode: 你每天都练习，能打不好吗？

  • You probably play badly.✗ Negative rhetorical flips to positive.
  • Of course you play well.✓ 能…不好吗? = "how could you play badly?" = you play well.
  • The speaker is unsure.✗ Rhetorical form → confident assertion.

Mixed practice

You're downstairs; your friend is upstairs. Pick the right version.

  • 我上楼去拿伞。✓ Speaker is downstairs, motion is away from them (up) → 去.
  • 我上楼来拿伞。✗ 来 implies motion toward the speaker — but the speaker is the one moving.

Build: "Once I grab the umbrella I'll come down."

我 拿 了 伞 就 下来

Build: "When Manager Zhou gets back, have him call me."

周经理 回来 了 就 让 他 给我 打电话

Fill: 我在五楼等你，你 ___ 吧。
(I'm on the 5th floor — come up.)

上来 — motion toward speaker who is upstairs.

Manager Zhou's secretary says: 他出去办事了，下午回来。 What does 出去 tell you?

  • He's on his way back to the office right now.✗ 出去 = went out, motion away from speaker's location.
  • He left the office to handle something.✓ 出去 = went out (away from where the secretary is).
  • He came into the office from outside.✗ That would be 进来, not 出去.
