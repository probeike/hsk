What you'll learn

This is a custom lesson, not a textbook chapter. It gathers everything about when — naming days and months, counting forwards and backwards from now, and, above all, where a time word is allowed to sit in a Chinese sentence. Lesson 6 already gave you clock times and duration; this lesson builds the calendar around them.

  1. 上 / 这 / 下 — calendar navigation — 上个月, 下个星期, 前天 … 后天, 前年 … 后年.
  2. Counting from now — 两个月以前, 两个星期之后, 过几天, 下课以后, 吃饭以前.
  3. 前两个月 vs 两个月前 — the trap: a span or a point? Plus duration word order.
  4. Where the time word sits — never at the end; Time → Place → Verb; big → small.
  5. Sequencing a story — …以后 + 就, and …的时候.
  6. Mixed practice

1. 上 / 这 / 下 Calendar navigation — naming a week, a month, a year without a date

When a speaker reaches for this: to point at a day, week, month or year relative to right now, without saying an actual date. "Next month I'm going to look for a dance class." "Last week I slept badly." "The year before last I was working in Beijing." These are the most common time words in the language — you will use them in almost every conversation about plans or the past.

The English trap

English says last and next — words about a line you walk along. Chinese says 上 (up) and 下 (down), which feels arbitrary until you picture the object: a wall calendar. You flip the finished pages upward over the top of the pad, so everything 上 is behind you, and the pages still hanging below are what's coming. Up = earlier. Down = later.

The wall-calendar picture. Pages you have already flipped sit above — 上个月, 上个星期. Pages still to come hang below — 下个月, 下个星期. Whenever 上/下 feels random, put your hand on the pad and flip.
Unitearlier ←now→ later
星期 / 周 上(个)星期 这(个)星期 下(个)星期
月 上个月 这个月 下个月
年 (no 上/下!) 前年 · 去年 今年 明年 · 后年
天 (no 上/下!) 前天 · 昨天 今天 明天 · 后天
Days and years use a different pair: 前 (in front / two back) and 后 (behind / two forward) — never *上天, *下年. Learn the five-dot line as one picture; the year line 前年–去年–今年–明年–后年 has exactly the same shape.

Two things these words never do

  • No 的. 下个月我要辞职 ✓ — *下个月的我要辞职 ✗. They are already time words; they don't need to be turned into modifiers.
  • No 在. 明天我去银行 ✓ — *在明天我去银行 ✗. English on Monday / in March tempts you into 在, but 在 is for places (在纽约), not for these calendar words.

下个月我想去找一个舞蹈课。

Xià ge yuè wǒ xiǎng qù zhǎo yí ge wǔdǎo kè.

next month / I / want / go / find / one / CL / dance class

Next month I want to go and find a dance class.

上个星期我睡得不好。

Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ shuì de bù hǎo.

last week / I / sleep / DE / not good

Last week I slept badly.

前年我在北京工作，去年我搬到了纽约。

Qiánnián wǒ zài Běijīng gōngzuò, qùnián wǒ bāndào le Niǔyuē.

The year before last I was working in Beijing; last year I moved to New York.

Practice

Fill: 今天是三号，___ 是五号。
(Today is the 3rd, so the 5th is …)

后天 — two days forward. 后 = "behind you on the road ahead". Note what you can't say: *下天 and *下个天 don't exist — 天 has its own five-word set.

Write your own. Two true sentences off your own calendar: one about something you actually did 上个星期 or 上个月, and one about something coming up 下个星期 or 下个月. Use a different verb in each.

上个星期我买了一个新手机。下个月我要跟朋友一起去上海旅游。

Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ mǎi le yí ge xīn shǒujī. Xià ge yuè wǒ yào gēn péngyou yìqǐ qù Shànghǎi lǚyóu.

Last week I bought a new phone. Next month I'm going travelling to Shanghai with a friend.

Check your two sentences:

  • Flip the calendar pad: 上 = a page already turned up (past), 下 = a page still hanging below (future). Did each one point the way you meant?
  • Nothing stuck onto the time word — no 的 and no 在 (在下个月 ✗, 下个月的 ✗)?
  • Is each time word in front of its verb — at the head of the sentence or right after the subject — and never at the end?

What does this mean?
我叔叔下个星期从中国回来。

  • My uncle came back from China last week.✗ That's 上个星期 — a page already flipped up over the top of the pad.
  • My uncle is coming back from China next week.✓ 下 = the page still hanging below, so 下个星期 is ahead of us. Nothing else in the sentence marks the direction — the single character 下 carries it.
  • My uncle comes back from China every week.✗ A habit needs 每个星期.

Which sentence is correct for "Next month I'm going to look for a new flat"?

  • 下个月我要找一个新房间。✓ The bare time word sits in front of the subject (or right after it) and needs nothing added to it.
  • 下个月的我要找一个新房间。✗ No 的 — 下个月 is already a time word, not a modifier hunting for a noun.
  • 在下个月我要找一个新房间。✗ No 在. English in / on tempts you into it, but 在 introduces places (在超市, 在家), never calendar words.

Which sentence is correct for "The year after next we're moving to Beijing"?

  • 后年我们搬到北京。✓ 年 uses the 前/后 pair: 前年 · 去年 · 今年 · 明年 · 后年.
  • 下年我们搬到北京。✗ 下年 is not a word at all. 上/下 work for 星期 and 月 only.
  • 下个年我们搬到北京。✗ The same mistake with a 个 wedged in. Adding a measure word can't rescue a pair that 年 never takes.

Transform. 上个星期我妹妹去医院了。 → she didn't go; the appointment is next week. Rewrite the whole sentence with 下个星期.

下个星期我妹妹要去医院。 — two things change together. 上 flips to 下, and the 了 has to go: 了 reports something that already happened, and nothing has happened yet. 下个星期我妹妹去医院了 ✗ is the slip to watch for.

Translate. "The day before yesterday I bought a new bicycle."
用: 前天 · 买了 · 一辆 · 新自行车

前天我买了一辆新自行车。 — 前天 may stand at the very front of the sentence or right after 我; both are correct and equally common. The one thing it can never do is follow the verb: 我买了一辆新自行车前天 ✗.

2. Counting from now X 以前 · X 以后 / 之后 · 过几天 · 下课以后

When a speaker reaches for this: when the calendar words of point 1 aren't enough — because the distance is "two months", not "last month", or because the landmark is an event ("after class", "before dinner") rather than a date. This is how you say two months ago, in two weeks, in a few days, after I quit.
length of time / clause + 以前 / 以后 · 之后

The English trap

English puts the landmark word first: after class, before dinner, in two weeks. Chinese puts it last, like a postposition: 下课以后, 吃饭以前, 两个星期以后. Say the event, then say which side of it you mean. Nothing goes in front.

Measure the distance from 现在. The length of time comes first, then 以前 (backwards) or 以后 (forwards). 以 is optional in speech: 两个月前, 两个星期后.

两个星期之后我要辞职。

Liǎng ge xīngqī zhīhòu wǒ yào cízhí.

two / CL / week / after / I / will / resign

In two weeks I'm going to quit my job.

下课以后我就去跳舞。

Xià kè yǐhòu wǒ jiù qù tiào wǔ.

finish class / after / I / then / go / dance

After class I go straight off to dance.

吃饭以前我不喝饮料。

Chī fàn yǐqián wǒ bù hē yǐnliào.

I don't drink anything sweet before a meal. (Whole clause + 以前 — the clause needs no 了.)

过几天我给你打电话。

Guò jǐ tiān wǒ gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.

pass / a-few / days / I / to you / make phone call

I'll call you in a few days. (Also 过两天 — "two" here just means "a couple".)

Standing alone: 以前 = "formerly", 以后 = "in the future"

With nothing in front of them, these two words stop being "before X / after X" and become whole eras: everything up to now, and everything from now on.

以前 — back then
以前我不喜欢跳舞
"I didn't use to like dancing" — the era before now.
以后 — from now on
以后你想当舞蹈老师吗？
"In the future, do you want to be a dance teacher?" — the era after now.

以后 or 之后?

After a length of time or a clause they are interchangeable — 两个星期以后 = 两个星期之后, 辞职以后 = 辞职之后. 之后 sounds slightly more written or deliberate, which is why it suits the sentence in the title. But standing alone for "in the future", only 以后 works: 以后我想当老板 ✓, *之后我想当老板 ✗.

Practice

What does this mean?
以前这儿没有地铁。

  • There didn't use to be a subway here.✓ Bare 以前, with nothing in front of it, means "the era before now".
  • There won't be a subway here later on.✗ That's the other era word: 以后这儿没有地铁.
  • There's no subway in front of this place.✗ "In front of" as a place is 前面. 以前 is only ever about time.

Which says "Before an exam I always get anxious"?

  • 考试以前我总是很着急。✓ Event first, marker last — 以前 is a postposition, the mirror image of English before.
  • 以前考试我总是很着急。✗ Fronted, 以前 reads as "in the past" and leaves 考试 stranded: "in the past, exams…".
  • 我总是很着急考试以前。✗ The time frame can't be tacked onto the end of the sentence — see point 4.

Which sentence is correct for "In the future I'd like to live in a big city"?

  • 以后我想住在大城市。✓ Standing alone, 以后 means "from now on". This is the one job 之后 cannot do.
  • 之后我想住在大城市。✗ 之后 always needs something in front of it (辞职之后, 两年之后). On its own it is not "in the future".
  • 我想住在大城市以后。✗ Time never trails at the end — and here 以后 has nothing to hang off anyway.

Write your own. Two true sentences measuring from where you stand now: (1) one where the landmark is an event — what you always do …以前 or …以后 (before bed, after you get home, after work); (2) one where the landmark is a length of time — what you'll be doing X 以后 (三个星期以后, 两个月以后…).

回家以后我一般先洗澡，然后做饭。三个星期以后我打算开始每天早上跑步。

Huí jiā yǐhòu wǒ yìbān xiān xǐzǎo, ránhòu zuò fàn. Sān ge xīngqī yǐhòu wǒ dǎsuan kāishǐ měi tiān zǎoshang pǎobù.

After I get home I usually shower first, then cook. In three weeks I plan to start running every morning.

Check your two sentences:

  • Does 以前 / 以后 hang off the back of the event or the length (回家以后 ✓, 三个星期以后 ✓) — never in front of it (以后回家 ✗)?
  • Is the whole time phrase in front of the verb, never trailing at the end of the sentence?
  • Standing alone, 以后 stops meaning "after X" and becomes "in the future" — if that isn't what you meant, make sure something is sitting in front of it. (And 之后 can never stand alone at all.)

Fill: 我现在很忙，___ 我们再见面吧。
(I'm busy right now — let's meet up again in a few days.)

过几天 (or 过两天) — 过 means "let some time pass", so the phrase always points forward. It is not "a few days ago"; that would be 几天以前.

Transform. 三年前我住在北京。 → turn the memory into a plan: in three years you'll be living in Beijing. Use 三年以后 and 要. Type the whole sentence.

三年以后我要住在北京。 — the length of time never moves; only the marker on the far side of it flips, 前 → 以后, and the sentence re-points from memory to plan. 以 is optional in speech (三年后), but the 前/后 must stay behind the number: 以后三年我要住在北京 ✗ would mean "for the three years from now on".

Translate. "In five days I'm going to the airport to meet a friend."
用: 五天以后 · 去机场 · 接朋友 (no 要 — the time word already does the work)

五天以后我去机场接朋友。 — English puts in first and the length second; Chinese measures the distance first (五天) and only then says which side of now you mean (以后). 五天之后 and bare 五天后 are equally correct.

Answer the question. 你打算什么时候开始锻炼？ — in three months. Start with 我, and don't repeat 打算.

我三个月以后开始锻炼。 — the offset phrase answers 什么时候 and therefore sits where every time word sits: in front of the verb, straight after 我. 我开始锻炼三个月以后 ✗ — that end slot simply does not exist in Chinese.

3. 前两个月 vs 两个月前 A span, or a point? The word order decides

When a speaker reaches for this: you have "two months" and "before" in your head, and you must choose which one comes first — because the choice changes the meaning completely. One says at a moment two months back. The other says throughout the two months just gone.

The English trap

English hides the difference behind the same three words: "two months ago I moved house" and "for the past two months I haven't practised". Both start with "two months", so an English speaker reaches for 前两个月 for both — and gets one of them wrong. In Chinese the position of 前 is the meaning: 前 after the number = a point; 前 before the number = a span.

Same two words, two shapes. 两个月前 is a dot you can put an event on. 前两个月 / 最近两个月 is a band you can spread a habit — or the lack of one — across.
两个月前 — a point
两个月前我搬家了
"Two months ago I moved house." One event, at one moment.
前两个月 — a span
前两个月我很忙
"Over the two months just gone I was busy." A whole stretch of time.

The sentence you actually said

You wrote 前两个月我搬家了 meaning "Two months ago I moved house." A Chinese ear reads that as "throughout the last two months, I was moving house" — a two-month removal job. For the single event, flip the two halves: 两个月前我搬家了 ✓. When you want the span, 最近两个月 is the safest choice of all — it can never be misread as a point.

Duration: after the verb — except when it's a gap

Lesson 6 gave you the rule: how long an action lasted goes after the verb (学习了两年汉语). But when you say how long something didn't happen, the same length of time jumps to the front, before 没. A gap is not a duration of doing — it's a stretch of time you're describing, so it behaves like a time word.

Doing — duration after V
我在纽约住了三年了
"I've lived in New York for three years."
Not doing — duration before V
我两个月没跳舞了
"I haven't danced for two months."

最近两个月我没练习汉语。

Zuìjìn liǎng ge yuè wǒ méi liànxí Hànyǔ.

recent / two / CL / month / I / not / practise / Chinese

I haven't practised Chinese for the past two months.

我学习了两年汉语了。

Wǒ xuéxí le liǎng nián Hànyǔ le.

I / study-PERF / two years / Chinese / LE

I've been studying Chinese for two years (and still am).

Practice

What does this mean?
三个星期前我换了工作。

  • Three weeks ago I changed jobs.✓ 前 after the number = one point in time. The change of job happened at that moment.
  • I spent the last three weeks changing jobs.✗ That span reading needs the words the other way round: 前三个星期 / 最近三个星期.
  • In three weeks I'm changing jobs.✗ Forwards would be 三个星期以后. 前 points backwards, always.

Which says "Yesterday I watched TV for two hours"?

  • 昨天我看了两个小时电视。✓ How long an action lasted sits between the verb and its object.
  • 昨天我看电视两个小时。✗ English order. The duration must squeeze in ahead of the object, not trail behind it.
  • 昨天我两个小时看了电视。✗ Fronting the length turns it into a time frame ("during a two-hour period"), not a measure of how long the watching went on.

Fill: 我昨天晚上 ___ 三个小时作业。
(Last night I did three hours of homework.)

写了 (or 做了) — the gap is the verb slot, and everything hangs on where it goes: V + 了 + duration + object. Put the object first and you get 写了作业三个小时 ✗.

Type the whole sentence. Work has swallowed your evenings: "I haven't been to the cinema for three months." Put the length of time in the one slot it can occupy.
用: 我 · 三个月 · 没 · 去看电影 · 了

我三个月没去看电影了。 — writing the whole thing forces the two decisions the sentence hides. The length of the gap goes in front of 没, not after the verb (我没去看电影三个月了 ✗), because a stretch of not doing something behaves like a time word. And the 了 sits at the very end, where it says the gap is still running — never on the verb.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 前三天我感冒了。
(Intended: "Three days ago I caught a cold.")

三天前我感冒了。 — 前三天 is a band of time, so the sentence as written says you spent three solid days catching a cold. Catching one is a single moment, so 前 has to move behind the number. Everything else in the sentence stays exactly where it was.

Transform. 我打了半个小时篮球。 → now say the opposite: you haven't played basketball for half a year. Use 半年 and 没…了. Type the whole sentence.

我半年没打篮球了。 — the length of time jumps across the verb. In the positive sentence it sat after 打; in the negative it must sit in front of 没. And the 了 moves too: off the verb (打了) and onto the end of the sentence, where it says the gap is still running.

Translate. "For the past two weeks the weather has been bad the whole time."
用: 最近两个星期 · 天气 · 一直 · 不好

最近两个星期天气一直不好。 — this is the span shape: a band you spread a state across, so the number comes second (最近 两个星期) and the whole phrase fronts the sentence. Say 两个星期前天气不好 instead and you have pinned one single day a fortnight back.

Build: "My older sister hasn't been home for a year." (Only one order works — the length of the gap has a fixed seat.)

我姐姐 一年 没 回家 了

Personalise it. Write two true sentences: (1) one thing that happened at a single moment some months or years back — use X 前; (2) one thing you genuinely haven't done for a long stretch — use …没…了. Use two different time units (天 · 个星期 · 个月 · 年).

四年前我开始学汉语。最近工作太忙了，我已经三个星期没练习了。

Sì nián qián wǒ kāishǐ xué Hànyǔ. Zuìjìn gōngzuò tài máng le, wǒ yǐjīng sān ge xīngqī méi liànxí le.

Four years ago I started learning Chinese. Work has been too busy lately — I haven't practised for three weeks.

Check your own two sentences:

  • Sentence 1: is 前 after the number (四年前), marking one moment — not 前四年, a whole band?
  • Sentence 1: is the time phrase in front of the verb, never at the end?
  • Sentence 2: does the length of the gap sit before 没, and does the sentence end in 了?
  • Sentence 2: is the verb bare — no 了 stuck on it (没练习了汉语 ✗)?
  • Two different time units, and both statements actually true?

4. Where the time word sits Before the verb — never at the end

When a speaker reaches for this: every single time you mention when. This is the rule that makes the difference between "sounds foreign" and "sounds Chinese", and it is broken more often than any other by English speakers.

The English trap

English parks time at the end: "I'll go to the bank tomorrow." "I didn't eat this morning." Chinese has no such slot. The time word must be in front of the verb — either at the very start (topic position) or right after the subject. *我去银行明天 ✗ is not a small stylistic wobble; it is ungrammatical.

Time + Subject + Time + Place + Verb + Object
A Chinese sentence sets the stage before anything happens on it: when, then where, then the verb. English does the opposite and sweeps the setting to the back.

今天早上我还没吃饭。

Jīntiān zǎoshang wǒ hái méi chī fàn.

today morning / I / still / not / eat / meal

I still haven't eaten this morning. (Time fronted as the topic — very common.)

我明天去银行。

Wǒ míngtiān qù yínháng.

I / tomorrow / go / bank

I'm going to the bank tomorrow. (Time after the subject — equally correct.)

我想每天在纽约跳舞、工作。

Wǒ xiǎng měitiān zài Niǔyuē tiào wǔ, gōngzuò.

I / want / every day / in New York / dance, work

I want to dance and work in New York every day.

Stacking time words: big → small

When you stack two or more time words, Chinese runs from the largest container down to the smallest — the opposite of English's "at 3 o'clock on Saturday next week".

English (small → big)Chinese (big → small)
in March next year明年三月
this morning今天早上
at 3 p.m. the day after tomorrow后天下午三点
on Saturday evening next week下个星期六晚上

Practice

Which says "My mum takes the bus to work every day"?

  • 我妈妈每天坐公共汽车上班。✓ Time sits between the subject and the verb.
  • 我妈妈坐公共汽车上班每天。✗ The English habit — time can't be parked at the end. This is the single commonest error English speakers make in Chinese.
  • 我妈妈坐每天公共汽车上班。✗ Now 每天 looks like a modifier of 公共汽车 — "an everyday bus".

Which is the standard Time → Place order?

  • 我每天在公园跑步。✓ When (每天) before where (在公园) before the verb — the stage is set, then the action happens on it.
  • 我在公园每天跑步。✗ Place before time — the frame built backwards. Understandable, but not what a native ear expects.
  • 我跑步每天在公园。✗ Both frame words have slid off the end, English-style.

Fill: ___ 我要去火车站接我同学。
(At eight tomorrow morning I'm going to the station to meet my classmate.)

明天早上八点 — day, then part of day, then clock time: the container before its contents. English stacks them the other way up ("at eight tomorrow morning"), so a good check is to read your English backwards before you write the Chinese.

Answer the question. 你明天晚上在家吗？ — no: tomorrow evening you're watching a film at a friend's place. Give just the statement itself (no 不 in front), as a full sentence using 明天晚上, 在朋友家 and 看电影.

明天晚上我在朋友家看电影。 — the answer runs 时间 → 地点 → 动作: when, then where, then what. English says it in exactly the reverse order ("I'm watching a film at a friend's place tomorrow evening"), so you have to turn the whole sentence inside out before you type it.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 我去图书馆昨天下午。
(Intended: "I went to the library yesterday afternoon.")

我昨天下午去图书馆了。 — the time phrase has fallen off the end of the sentence, exactly where English keeps it. Move it in front of the verb: either at the very start (昨天下午我…) or straight after 我. Both are fine; the end slot does not exist.

Build: "I'll wait for you at the airport at ten tomorrow morning." (Two orders work — but the time must stay in one piece, and it must come before 在机场.)

明天 上午十点 我 在机场 等你

5. Sequencing a story …以后 + 就 · …的时候

When a speaker reaches for this: to lay two events out in order — one after the other, or one during the other. Chinese has no tense endings to do this work, so the order of the clauses and a small marker at the join carry the whole story.

Principle of Temporal Sequence

Chinese clauses come in the order the events happened. So the "after" clause is always written first, and the result follows: 下课以后 …then… 我就去跳舞. The adverb 就 sits right before the second verb and means "and then, right away" — you already met it in Lesson 2 as V₁了…就 V₂.

Event 1 + 以后 , S + 就 + Event 2

下课以后我就去跳舞。

Xià kè yǐhòu wǒ jiù qù tiào wǔ.

As soon as class finishes, off I go to dance.

辞职以后我想当老板。

Cízhí yǐhòu wǒ xiǎng dāng lǎobǎn.

resign / after / I / want / be / boss

After I quit, I want to be my own boss.

…的时候 — "when / while"

Where 以后 puts event 2 after event 1, 的时候 puts it inside event 1. Same postposition habit: the clause comes first, the marker last.

以后 — one then the other
吃饭以后我们聊天儿
We chat after the meal — first eat, then talk.
的时候 — at the same time
吃饭的时候我们聊天儿
We chat while we eat — both at once.

跳舞的时候，我的压力就没有了。

Tiào wǔ de shíhou, wǒ de yālì jiù méiyǒu le.

dance / when / my / pressure / then / not-have / LE

When I'm dancing, my stress just disappears.

我小的时候，妈妈是老师。

Wǒ xiǎo de shíhou, māma shì lǎoshī.

When I was little, my mother was a teacher.

Practice

What does this mean?
我搬到这儿以前，一直住在爷爷家。

  • After I moved here, I went on living at my grandfather's.✗ That would be 我搬到这儿以后 — and it would be a strange thing to say.
  • Before I moved here, I lived at my grandfather's the whole time.✓ 以前 hangs off the back of the clause 我搬到这儿, so everything in the second half is dated to before the move.
  • I used to live at my grandfather's, so I moved here.✗ 以前 is purely about order in time. A reason would need 因为…所以….

Which says "While I'm doing my homework I always listen to music"?

  • 写作业的时候我总是听音乐。✓ 的时候 = during; the two things fill the same stretch of time.
  • 写作业以后我总是听音乐。✗ That's "once the homework is finished" — one thing, then the other, not both at once.
  • 我总是听音乐写作业的时候。✗ The time clause must come first. Chinese never lets it trail at the end.

Fill: 考试结束以后，我 ___ 去旅游。
(As soon as the exams are over, off I go travelling.)

就 — it sits immediately in front of the second verb, after the subject, never at the head of the clause: 考试结束以后，就我去旅游 ✗. 就 is what upgrades a plain "after" into "as soon as".

Transform. 我先复习，然后去跑步。 → say the same thing as one sentence, using …以后 and 就.

复习以后我就去跑步。 — the "after" clause always comes first, because Chinese clauses run in the order the events actually happened. 以后 hangs off the end of event 1; 就 stands in front of event 2. Neither marker ever swaps sides, and 先…然后… simply drops out.

Translate. "When it rains I take a taxi to work."
用: 下雨的时候 · 坐出租车 · 上班

下雨的时候我坐出租车上班。 — 的时候 hangs off the back of 下雨, and the whole time clause leads the sentence. English puts when in front of the event, which is exactly why this order feels back-to-front at first.

Answer the question. 你什么时候去洗澡？ — as soon as you've finished your run. Start with 我 and use …以后 + 就.

我跑完步以后就去洗澡。 — 什么时候 can be answered with a whole clause, not only a bare time word: the event 跑完步 plus 以后 does the dating. 就 then adds "and then straight away". Note there is no 了 inside the 以后 clause — it hasn't happened yet.

Personalise it. Write two true sentences about an ordinary day of yours: one with …以后…就… (one thing straight after another) and one with …的时候… (two things at once).

每天工作结束以后我就去超市买菜。做饭的时候我一边听音乐一边跳舞。

Měitiān gōngzuò jiéshù yǐhòu wǒ jiù qù chāoshì mǎi cài. Zuò fàn de shíhou wǒ yìbiān tīng yīnyuè yìbiān tiào wǔ.

Every day, as soon as work finishes I go to the supermarket for food. While I'm cooking I listen to music and dance at the same time.

Check your own two sentences:

  • Does the "after" clause come first, with 以后 hanging off its end — not 以后工作结束?
  • Is 就 immediately in front of the second verb, after the subject?
  • Does 的时候 sit at the end of its clause too (做饭的时候), never in front of it?
  • Is there no 了 on the verb inside the 以后 / 的时候 clause?
  • Are both sentences actually true of your own day?

Mixed practice Shuffled — plus a few things from other lessons

Nothing below comes in section order, so you have to work out which decision a sentence is asking about — point or span, before or after, time or place — before you can build it. Three items aren't from this lesson at all: one 得 decision, one 得-complement and one imminent 就要…了 frame, dropped in to keep the older machinery running.

What does this mean?
后天下午三点我在公司。

  • The day before yesterday at 3 p.m. I was at the office.✗ 后天 is forward, not back — 前天 is back.
  • At 3 p.m. the day after tomorrow I'll be at the office.✓ 后天 → 下午 → 三点: big to small.
  • Every afternoon at 3 I'm at the office.✗ That would need 每天下午.

From an earlier lesson. Fill with 的, 得 or 地: 奶奶走 ___ 很慢，我们等一下吧。

得 — the gap comes after a verb and introduces a judgement of how the walking goes, so it is the complement 得. 的 would need a noun behind it; 地 goes in front of a verb (她慢慢地走 — a different, more literary sentence).

From another lesson. Transform. 我爸爸妈妈到纽约了。 → it hasn't happened yet: they land in three days. Rewrite it with 三天以后 and the imminent frame 就要…了.

三天以后我爸爸妈妈就要到纽约了。 — two rules meet in one sentence. The offset 三天以后 must stand in front of the verb, at the head of the sentence or right after the subject; and because a time is stated, the frame has to be 就要…了 rather than 快要…了 (快要 refuses a stated time). The closing 了 is compulsory — leave it off and the frame is half-built.

From an earlier lesson. Translate. "My little brother swims really fast."
用: 弟弟 · 游泳 · 得 · 快

弟弟游泳游得很快。 — when the verb already has an object welded to it (游泳), you must copy the verb before 得: V + O + V + 得 + adj. 弟弟游泳得很快 ✗. And the 很 isn't "very" — it's the neutral filler an adjective needs in a plain statement.

Build: "My older sister cleans her room at home every Sunday morning." (Two orders work — but the two time words must stay together, and both come before 在家.)

我姐姐 每个星期天 上午 在家 打扫房间

Three errors. This little paragraph has exactly three mistakes, one in each sentence. Find them, write the corrected paragraph, then check.
我以后上班就没有时间练习汉语了。所以我每天在地铁上学习汉语二十分钟。周末我在图书馆下午复习。

我上班以后就没有时间练习汉语了。所以我每天在地铁上学习二十分钟汉语。周末下午我在图书馆复习。

  1. 我以后上班 → 我上班以后. 以后 is a postposition: it hangs off the back of the event. Put it in front and it turns into the bare "in the future" word, leaving 上班 stranded with nothing to attach to.
  2. 学习汉语二十分钟 → 学习二十分钟汉语. How long an action lasted squeezes in between the verb and its object, never after it. The English order (study Chinese for twenty minutes) is the trap.
  3. 我在图书馆下午复习 → 周末下午我在图书馆复习. Chinese frames the scene time-first, then place: 时间 → 地点 → 动词. And stacked time words run big → small, so 周末 and 下午 must sit side by side, not with a place wedged between them.

Write it all. Put your own timeline into 5–6 sentences: what your past two months have been like as a whole stretch, one thing that happened at a single moment in the past, one thing you haven't done for ages, and what you're doing in the next two weeks. Use at least four of: 上个/下个… · X前 / X以后 · 最近… · …没…了 · time + place + verb · …以后…就…

最近两个月我特别累，几乎每天都在公司工作到很晚。一个月前我搬到了公司附近，可是新房间我一直没时间打扫。我已经三个星期没运动了，身体不太舒服。下个星期开始，工作结束以后我就去公园跑步。后天我还要去医院检查一下身体。

Zuìjìn liǎng ge yuè wǒ tèbié lèi, jīhū měitiān dōu zài gōngsī gōngzuò dào hěn wǎn. Yí ge yuè qián wǒ bāndào le gōngsī fùjìn, kěshì xīn fángjiān wǒ yìzhí méi shíjiān dǎsǎo. Wǒ yǐjīng sān ge xīngqī méi yùndòng le, shēntǐ bú tài shūfu. Xià ge xīngqī kāishǐ, gōngzuò jiéshù yǐhòu wǒ jiù qù gōngyuán pǎobù. Hòutiān wǒ hái yào qù yīyuàn jiǎnchá yíxià shēntǐ.

These past two months I've been shattered — almost every day I'm at the office working till late. A month ago I moved to a place near the office, but I still haven't had time to clean the new room. I haven't exercised for three weeks now and I don't feel great. Starting next week, as soon as work finishes I'm going to the park to run. And the day after tomorrow I'm going to the hospital for a check-up.

Self-check — go through your paragraph once per line:

  • Is every time word in front of its verb? Nothing left dangling at the end of a sentence?
  • Span or point? 最近两个月 / 前两个月 for a whole stretch, 两个月前 for one moment. Check each one you wrote.
  • In your "haven't done it for X" sentence, does the length sit before 没, with 了 at the end and no 了 on the verb?
  • In any "I did it for X" sentence, does the length sit after the verb and before its object?
  • Where two time words stack, do they run big → small, and does time come before place (下个星期在公园, not 在公园下个星期)?
  • No 在 and no 的 stuck onto a calendar word (在下个月 ✗, 下个月的 ✗)?
