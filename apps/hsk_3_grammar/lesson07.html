What you'll learn

  1. 又 vs 再 — two "agains" split by past vs. future.
  2. Question words used as generics — 谁都 / 什么都 / 哪儿都 meaning "everyone / anything / anywhere".

Title note: this recycles Lesson 6

我跟她都认识五年了 is not a new duration pattern. It points back to Lesson 6: a duration reaches up to now, so the sentence ends with 了. With a continuing relationship like 认识 ("know"), Chinese often says 认识五年了: "have known for five years and still know each other."

1. 又 vs 再 "Again" — but which again?

When a speaker reaches for 又: something already happened a second time (past-tense again). 再: something will (or should) happen again (future "again"). This matches English did it again vs will do it again, but English doesn't require two different words.

The English trap

One English word, two Chinese words. Rule of thumb: if the action already took place → 又. If it hasn't happened yet (request, plan, offer) → 再. Getting this backwards is one of the most frequent errors at this stage.

又 — already repeated (past)
他昨天又去了。
He went again yesterday (it's already happened).
再 — will repeat (future)
明天我再去。
I'll go again tomorrow (hasn't happened yet).

你再说一次。

Nǐ zài shuō yí cì.

Say it one more time. (Future request — 再.)

这个电影我又看了一次。

Zhège diànyǐng wǒ yòu kàn le yí cì.

I watched this movie one more time. (Already done — 又.)

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about things that keep coming round in your own life — a class you go back to, a person you keep calling, a place you keep returning to. Use 又 at least twice for repeats that have already happened (with 了), and 再 at least once for a repeat you are still planning. Use three different verbs, and make one sentence negative (不想再… or 还没再…). Every sentence has to be true for you.

上个星期六我又去跳舞了，跳得比以前好一点儿。星期三晚上我又给妈妈打了一次电话，我们聊了很久。这个月我还没再去看医生，因为我的病已经好了。下个月我想再上一次做菜课。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ yòu qù tiàowǔ le, tiào de bǐ yǐqián hǎo yìdiǎnr. Xīngqīsān wǎnshang wǒ yòu gěi māma dǎ le yí cì diànhuà, wǒmen liáo le hěn jiǔ. Zhège yuè wǒ hái méi zài qù kàn yīshēng, yīnwèi wǒ de bìng yǐjīng hǎo le. Xià ge yuè wǒ xiǎng zài shàng yí cì zuò cài kè.

Last Saturday I went dancing again, and danced a bit better than before. On Wednesday evening I called my mum one more time, and we talked for ages. This month I haven't been back to the doctor again, because I'm already better. Next month I want to take another cookery class.

Why these are right: the first two sentences report repeats that are already in the past, so they take 又 — and 又 pulls 了 along with it (又去跳舞了 · 又打了一次电话). The last two are still ahead: a repeat that hasn't happened yet is 再, whether it is negated (还没再去) or planned (想再上一次). Note where 再 sits after the modal — 想再上一次, never 再想上一次.

Check your own sentences:

  • Every 又 sentence: has it already happened, and is 了 there? 又来了 ✓ · 又来 ✗ (bare 又 with no 了 sounds unfinished).
  • Every 再 sentence: is it still unrealised — a plan, a request, an offer? If it already happened, it must be 又.
  • Both words stand in front of the verb, after the subject and the time word: 明天我再去 ✓ · 再明天我去 ✗
  • With a modal, 再 goes after it: 想再去 · 要再看 ✓ · 再想去 ✗ (that means "think about going again").
  • Three different verbs, and every sentence actually true about your week — not a textbook week.

2. Question words as "every/any" 谁都 · 什么都 · 哪儿都

When a speaker reaches for this: to sweep over all options or deny any. "Everyone likes her." "She doesn't eat anything." Chinese reuses the question words (谁, 什么, 哪儿) as generic placeholders, with 都 / 也 doing the "every/any" work.

The English trap

English has distinct words: everyone, anything, anywhere. Chinese overloads the question words for both asking and for "any/every". The giveaway: if 都 or 也 follows, it's the universal reading. The question words are no longer questions.

First ask: is it really a question?

A real question word asks for missing information. A question word followed by 都 or 也 sweeps across all possible answers.

Real question Generic sweep Meaning
谁喜欢音乐？ 谁都喜欢音乐。 Who likes music? → Everyone likes music.
你想吃什么？ 我什么都不想吃。 What do you want to eat? → I don't want to eat anything.
你去哪儿？ 我哪儿也不去。 Where are you going? → I'm not going anywhere.
谁 / 什么 / 哪儿 + 都 / 也 + V

谁都喜欢她。

Shéi dōu xǐhuan tā.

who / all / like / her

Everyone likes her.

她什么都不吃。

Tā shénme dōu bù chī.

she / what / all / not / eat

She doesn't eat anything.

我哪儿也不想去。

Wǒ nǎr yě bù xiǎng qù.

I don't want to go anywhere.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about your own life using a question word swept by 都 or 也. Use at least three different question words (谁 · 什么 · 哪儿), make at least one sweep positive ("everyone / everything") and at least one negative (…也/都 + 不/没), and use three different verbs. Every sentence has to be true for you.

在我们公司，谁都很忙，中午谁也不休息。我什么运动都喜欢，可是最喜欢跳舞，在纽约哪儿都有跳舞的地方。上个星期六我什么事都没做，在家睡了一天。这个周末很冷，我谁也不想见。

Zài wǒmen gōngsī, shéi dōu hěn máng, zhōngwǔ shéi yě bù xiūxi. Wǒ shénme yùndòng dōu xǐhuan, kěshì zuì xǐhuan tiàowǔ, zài Niǔyuē nǎr dōu yǒu tiàowǔ de dìfang. Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ shénme shì dōu méi zuò, zài jiā shuì le yì tiān. Zhège zhōumò hěn lěng, wǒ shéi yě bù xiǎng jiàn.

At our company everyone is busy, and nobody rests at lunchtime. I like every kind of sport, but dancing best — and in New York there are places to dance everywhere. Last Saturday I did absolutely nothing and slept at home all day. This weekend it's cold and I don't want to see anybody.

Why these are right: each question word sits in front of the verb with 都/也 right behind it — 谁都很忙, 什么运动都喜欢, 哪儿都有, 谁也不想见 — which is exactly where English puts the word after the verb ("like everything", "see anybody"). 什么 can take a noun with it (什么运动 = "any sport at all"), and in a negative sweep 也 and 都 are interchangeable, while 没 (not 不) is what you need for a past one: 什么事都没做.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the question word before the verb? 我什么都不买 ✓ · 我不买什么 ✗ (that's English order).
  • Is 都/也 the last thing before the verb or its negation? 谁都知道 ✓ · 谁知道都 ✗
  • Negation order is 都/也 then 不/没: 什么都不想吃 ✓ · 什么不都想吃 ✗
  • Past and negative ⇒ 没, not 不: 哪儿都没去 ✓ · 哪儿都不去了 ✗ (that means "not going anywhere any more").
  • Three different question words, three different verbs — and each sentence really about you.

Write the paragraph. A shopping trip for 小丽's birthday present that went wrong. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using both structures of this lesson at least once each: 又 / 再 and question word + 都 / 也. Cover: which shop you went back to and why, what you thought of the things there, what your friends all told you to do, what you ended up buying, and what you plan to do next weekend.

上个星期六我又去了那家商店，因为小丽的生日快到了。我想给她买一件新衣服，可是那儿的衣服我什么都不喜欢。我问了很多朋友，谁都说送蛋糕最好。那天下午我什么也没买，就回家了。下个星期天我想再去一次，这次跟小刚一起去。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ yòu qù le nà jiā shāngdiàn, yīnwèi Xiǎolì de shēngrì kuài dào le. Wǒ xiǎng gěi tā mǎi yí jiàn xīn yīfu, kěshì nàr de yīfu wǒ shénme dōu bù xǐhuan. Wǒ wèn le hěn duō péngyou, shéi dōu shuō sòng dàngāo zuì hǎo. Nà tiān xiàwǔ wǒ shénme yě méi mǎi, jiù huí jiā le. Xià ge xīngqītiān wǒ xiǎng zài qù yí cì, zhè cì gēn Xiǎogāng yìqǐ qù.

Last Saturday I went back to that shop again, because Xiaoli's birthday is nearly here. I wanted to buy her a new piece of clothing, but I didn't like a single thing they had. I asked a lot of friends, and every one of them said a cake would be the best present. That afternoon I bought nothing at all and just went home. Next Sunday I want to go one more time, this time together with Xiaogang.

Why this works as a paragraph: the two structures are doing different jobs. 又去了 sets the scene — this was not the first visit, and the repeat is finished, so 了 comes with it; 想再去一次 closes the story with a repeat that hasn't happened yet, with 再 sitting after the modal 想. In between, the sweeps carry the plot: 什么都不喜欢 is the problem, 谁都说 is the advice, 什么也没买 is the outcome — note 没 (not 不) because it reports what didn't happen that afternoon. Time words (上个星期六 · 那天下午 · 下个星期天) are what hold the sentences together.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Did the finished repeat get 又 (with 了) and the planned one 再 (no 了)? 下个星期我又去 ✗
  • Is 再 after the modal? 想再去 ✓ · 再想去 ✗
  • Every sweep: question word before the verb, 都/也 immediately after it, negation last — 什么都不喜欢 ✓ · 不喜欢什么 ✗
  • A sweep about the past that didn't happen takes 没, and 没 kills the 了: 什么也没买了 ✗
  • Does it read as one story — time words and 可是 joining it — rather than two grammar demos side by side?

2. 下次你又来我家玩吧。

1. 上个星期六我又去游泳了。
2. 下次你再来我家玩吧。
3. 妈妈什么都不想买。
4. 小刚上个周末哪儿都没去。

1. Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ yòu qù yóuyǒng le. 2. Xià cì nǐ zài lái wǒ jiā wán ba. 3. Māma shénme dōu bù xiǎng mǎi. 4. Xiǎogāng shàng ge zhōumò nǎr dōu méi qù.

1. Last Saturday I went swimming again. 2. Come to my place again next time. 3. Mum doesn't want to buy anything. 4. Xiaogang didn't go anywhere last weekend.

  1. 再去…了 → 又去…了. 上个星期六 puts the whole thing in the past and 了 confirms it, so the "again" has to be 又. 再 can never sit in a sentence that already happened — the 了 at the end is the giveaway.
  2. 又来 → 再来. 下次 and the suggestion particle 吧 both point forward: this repeat is an invitation, not a report. Unrealised ⇒ 再.
  3. 什么不都想买 → 什么都不想买. The order inside the sweep is fixed: question word + 都/也 + 不/没 + verb. 都 leans on the negation, so nothing may come between them.
  4. 没去哪儿 → 哪儿都没去. English puts "anywhere" after the verb; Chinese puts the swept question word in front of it and needs 都 or 也. 没去哪儿 can only be read as a real question ("didn't go where?").

Check your own rewrites:

  • Did you decide 又 vs 再 by asking "has it happened yet?", not by the English word?
  • Is 了 present with 又 and absent with 再?
  • In every sweep: question word → 都/也 → 不/没 → verb, in that order?
  • Is the swept question word ahead of the verb, never behind it?

Why these are right: (1) 上个月 puts the repeat in the past, so 又 + 了 — and 又 goes after the time word, in front of the verb. (2) A polite request is unrealised, so 再; it stands in front of the verb, and the counted 一次 comes after it: 再检查一次. (3) 谁都 is the subject of the whole sentence — it comes first, and 感兴趣 keeps its 对 phrase in front of it. (4) The sweep sits before the verb, and because this is a past event that never happened, the negation is 没, with no 了 anywhere.

  • Sentence 1: 又 after the time word and before the verb, with 了 at the end of the clause.

  • Sentence 3: 谁都 heads the sentence — 那个游戏谁都感兴趣它 ✗; there is no resumptive object in Chinese.
  • Sentence 4: 什么都 before the verb, and past + didn't ⇒ 没做, never 不做 or 没做了.
