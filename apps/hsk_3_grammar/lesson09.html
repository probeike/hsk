1. A 跟 B + 一样 (+ Adj) Review + 得-complement extension

When a speaker reaches for this: to say two things are equivalent on some dimension — "my phone is the same as yours", "she speaks Chinese like a native", "today is just as cold as yesterday". It's the equality counterpart to 比 (which marks difference).

The English trap

English uses "same as", "like", or "as … as …". Chinese has one tidy frame: A 跟 B 一样 (plain equivalence) or A 跟 B 一样 + Adj (equally Adj). Do not try to use 比 here — 比 compares, 跟…一样 equates.

A + 跟 + B + 一样 + (Adj)

Review first, then extend it after 得

Lesson 8 used A 跟 B 一样 + Adj for equality: two things are equally tall, cold, interesting, and so on. The title of this lesson puts that same comparison inside a 得-complement to describe how well an action is done.

Review: compare two things
他的汉语跟我的一样好。
His Chinese is as good as mine.
Extension: describe an action
她的汉语说得跟中国人一样好。
She speaks Chinese as well as a native.
Topic / object + V + 得 + 跟 + B + 一样 + Adj

我的手机跟你的一样。

Wǒ de shǒujī gēn nǐ de yíyàng.

my / phone / same-as / yours / equal

My phone is the same as yours.

她的汉语说得跟中国人一样好。

Tā de Hànyǔ shuō de gēn Zhōngguórén yíyàng hǎo.

her / Chinese / speak-DEG / same-as / Chinese-person / equally / good

She speaks Chinese as well as a native.

今天跟昨天一样冷。

Jīntiān gēn zuótiān yíyàng lěng.

today / same-as / yesterday / equally / cold

Today is just as cold as yesterday.

Negation: 不一样 or 跟…不一样

To say two things are different, put 不 before 一样. You can also negate the whole equivalence with 不 placed before 跟 (less common).

我的想法跟他不一样。

Wǒ de xiǎngfǎ gēn tā bù yíyàng.

My idea is different from his.

跟…一样 (equivalence)
A 跟 B 一样 (+ Adj)
A and B are the same (on that dimension).
比 (difference)
A 比 B + Adj
A is more Adj than B.

Common pitfall

The adjective after 一样 is not preceded by 很/非常 — just like in 比 sentences, you don't stack intensifiers. *跟他一样很好 (✗) → 跟他一样好 (✓).

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences comparing people or things in your own life that come out equal. At least two sentences must use the action version V + 得 + 跟 B + 一样 + Adj ("does it as well as …") with two different verbs; at least one must be the plain A 跟 B 一样 + Adj; and one must be negative (跟 … 不一样). Every sentence has to be true for you.

我妹妹跳舞跳得跟她的老师一样好，可是她才学了两年。我写中文写得跟小丽一样慢，我们都得多练习。我的自行车跟同事的一样老，我们都不想买新的。我做的咖啡跟商店里的不一样，我觉得我做的更好喝。

Wǒ mèimei tiàowǔ tiào de gēn tā de lǎoshī yíyàng hǎo, kěshì tā cái xué le liǎng nián. Wǒ xiě Zhōngwén xiě de gēn Xiǎolì yíyàng màn, wǒmen dōu děi duō liànxí. Wǒ de zìxíngchē gēn tóngshì de yíyàng lǎo, wǒmen dōu bù xiǎng mǎi xīn de. Wǒ zuò de kāfēi gēn shāngdiàn li de bù yíyàng, wǒ juéde wǒ zuò de gèng hǎohē.

My younger sister dances as well as her teacher, though she has only been learning for two years. I write Chinese as slowly as Xiaoli does — we both need more practice. My bike is as old as my colleague's, and neither of us wants to buy a new one. The coffee I make is not the same as the shop's; I think mine tastes better.

Why these are right: when the comparison is about how an action is done, the verb is repeated before 得 (跳舞跳得 · 写中文写得) and the whole 跟…一样 frame hangs off 得, with the adjective last. When it is about two things, no 得 is involved at all. B keeps just 的 when the noun is obvious (同事的 = "my colleague's"), and 不 lands on 一样, never on 跟.

Check your own sentences:

  • Action sentences: is 得 there, and is the verb repeated after the object? 做菜做得跟…一样好 ✓ · 做菜得跟…一样好 ✗
  • Is the adjective the last word? 跟他一样快 ✓ · 跟他快一样 ✗
  • No intensifier inside the frame: 跟他一样很好 ✗ — 一样 already fixes the degree.
  • Negative: 跟…不一样 ✓ · 不跟…一样 ✗
  • Did you resist 比? 比 says one side wins; this exercise is about the ones that come out level.

2. A 比 B + Adj + degree "A is X-er than B by this much"

When a speaker reaches for this: they're comparing A to B and want to say how much bigger/better/hotter A is. Not just "A is bigger" — but "a bit bigger" (一点儿 / 一些) or "a lot bigger" (得多 / 多了).

The English trap

English puts the intensifier before the adjective: "A is a bit bigger than B" / "A is much bigger". Chinese puts the degree word after: A 比 B 大 + 一点儿 / 得多. Do not try to use 很, 非常, or 太 in a 比-sentence — that's ungrammatical. Only comparison-specific degree words work.

A + 比 + B + Adj + degree
a little
A 比 B 贵一点儿 / 一些
A is a bit more expensive than B.
much
A 比 B 贵得多 / 多了
A is much more expensive than B.

他的汉语比我好多了。

Tā de Hànyǔ bǐ wǒ hǎo duō le.

His Chinese is much better than mine.

今天比昨天冷一点儿。

Jīntiān bǐ zuótiān lěng yìdiǎnr.

Today is a bit colder than yesterday.

Don't stack 很 with 比

*他比我很高 (✗) is wrong. With 比 you can't use 很/非常/太 — use 一点儿, 一些, 得多, or 多了 to express degree instead. If you want to say "A is just generally tall", drop 比 entirely.

Practice

Which is correct for "This dish is a lot tastier than that one"?

  • 这个菜比那个菜很好吃。✗ 很 doesn't work in 比 sentences.
  • 这个菜得多比那个菜好吃。✗ 得多 must follow the adjective, not precede 比.

  • Beijing is a little colder than Shanghai.✗ That would be 冷一点儿/一些.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences comparing things in your own life where one side really does win — this year vs last year, this job vs the last one, two places you know. Every sentence uses A 比 B + Adj + degree. Use 一点儿 or 一些 at least once, 得多 or 多了 at least once, and three different adjectives. No 很, 非常 or 太 anywhere. Every sentence has to be true for you.

我现在的工作比以前的忙多了，可是也有意思得多。周末的地铁比上班的时候快一点儿，人也少一些。我做的菜比去年做的好一点儿，家里人都愿意来吃。今年的天气比去年的冷得多，我几乎每天都穿两件衣服。

Wǒ xiànzài de gōngzuò bǐ yǐqián de máng duō le, kěshì yě yǒu yìsi de duō. Zhōumò de dìtiě bǐ shàngbān de shíhou kuài yìdiǎnr, rén yě shǎo yìxiē. Wǒ zuò de cài bǐ qùnián zuò de hǎo yìdiǎnr, jiā li rén dōu yuànyì lái chī. Jīnnián de tiānqì bǐ qùnián de lěng de duō, wǒ jīhū měi tiān dōu chuān liǎng jiàn yīfu.

My current job is much busier than my old one, but also far more interesting. The subway at the weekend is a bit faster than at commuting time, and less crowded too. My cooking is a little better than what I made last year — everyone at home is willing to come and eat. This year's weather is much colder than last year's; I wear two layers almost every day.

Why these are right: the degree word always comes after the adjective — 忙多了, 快一点儿, 冷得多 — which is the mirror image of English "much busier". 一点儿/一些 mean a small margin; 得多/多了 mean a wide one. And nothing else may modify the adjective: 很/非常/太 are all barred inside a 比 sentence.

Check your own sentences:

  • Degree after the adjective: 贵一点儿 ✓ · 一点儿贵 ✗
  • Zero 很/非常/太: 比以前很忙 ✗ — if you mean plain "very busy", drop 比 and the comparison with it.
  • Is B a fair match for A? 我现在的工作比以前的 — the 的 stands in for the repeated noun; comparing a job with a person is not a comparison.
  • Did you mix margins — at least one small (一点儿/一些) and one large (得多/多了)?
  • Three different adjectives, and every comparison actually true of your life?

3. Approximate numbers: 几 and 多 "A few", "more than"

When a speaker reaches for this: to give a rough number — not exact, but in the ballpark. "A few days", "twenty-some people", "more than three hours".

The English trap

English has messy ways to do this ("a few", "-ish", "or so"). Chinese is tidier:

  • 几 before a measure word = "a few" (2–9, vague). 几个人 = a few people.
  • 多 after a number = "more than / -something". 三十多岁 = thirtysomething. 一个多小时 = more than an hour.

Where 多 goes matters. With round tens, put 多 right after the ten-number. With number + measure phrases, put 多 after the measure/unit.

他几岁了？

Tā jǐ suì le?

How old is he? (For children: 几 doubles as "what small number" and "a few".)

我有几个中国朋友。

Wǒ yǒu jǐ ge Zhōngguó péngyou.

I have a few Chinese friends.

这个东西三十多块钱。

Zhège dōngxi sānshí duō kuài qián.

This thing costs thirty-something kuài.

我等了一个多小时。

Wǒ děng le yí ge duō xiǎoshí.

I waited for more than an hour.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences giving rough numbers from your own life — how long you have done something, how many people are around you, how long a journey takes, what something cost. Use 几 + measure once, and 多 in two different positions: after a round ten (二十多个…) and after a measure or time unit (一个多小时 · 两年多). Every number has to be true for you.

我学中文学了两年多了，现在班里有十几个同学。每天早上我坐地铁去公司，路上要坐一个多小时。我们公司有五十多个人，可是中间那层只有几张桌子。上个星期我买了几件新衣服，一共二百多块钱。

Wǒ xué Zhōngwén xué le liǎng nián duō le, xiànzài bān li yǒu shí jǐ ge tóngxué. Měi tiān zǎoshang wǒ zuò dìtiě qù gōngsī, lù shang yào zuò yí ge duō xiǎoshí. Wǒmen gōngsī yǒu wǔshí duō ge rén, kěshì zhōngjiān nà céng zhǐ yǒu jǐ zhāng zhuōzi. Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ mǎi le jǐ jiàn xīn yīfu, yígòng èrbǎi duō kuài qián.

I've been learning Chinese for over two years, and there are ten-odd people in my class now. Every morning I take the subway to work; the trip takes over an hour. There are fifty-something people in our company, but the middle floor has only a few desks. Last week I bought a few new clothes, two hundred-odd kuai in total.

Why these are right: 多 always follows the unit it stretches. After a round ten it comes before the measure (五十多个人); after a number + measure it comes after the measure (一个多小时); after a bare time unit it comes last (两年多). 几 sits before a measure word for "a few" (几张桌子 · 几件衣服) and inside 十几 for "ten-odd".

Check your own sentences:

  • Round ten: 五十多个人 ✓ · 五十个多人 ✗
  • Number + measure: 一个多小时 ✓ · 一多个小时 ✗
  • Is 几 followed by a measure word, never by a bare noun? 几个朋友 ✓ · 几朋友 ✗
  • Did you avoid using 几 for a number you actually know? 几 means you genuinely don't count it.
  • Two different 多 positions in your set, and every quantity true for you?

Wǒ jiā fùjìn yǒu liǎng ge chāoshì, yí ge lí wǒ jiā hěn jìn, yí ge zài gōngyuán pángbiān. Jìn de nàge gēn yuǎn de nàge yíyàng dà, kěshì lǐmiàn de dōngxi bú tài yíyàng. Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ gēn Xiǎolì zǒulù qù le yuǎn de nàge, lù shang yòng le èrshí duō fēnzhōng, tā zǒu de gēn wǒ yíyàng kuài. Nàr de shuǐguǒ bǐ wǒmen chángcháng mǎi de piányi yìdiǎnr, miànbāo yě piányi de duō. Kěshì mǎi dōngxi de rén tài duō, wǒmen zài hòumiàn yòu děng le shí jǐ fēnzhōng. Zuìhòu Xiǎolì shuō: “Yǐhòu háishi qù jìn de nàge ba, suīrán guì yìxiē, kěshì bú yòng děng nàme jiǔ.”

There are two supermarkets near my place: one close to my flat, one next to the park. The near one is the same size as the far one, but what's inside them isn't quite the same. Last Saturday Xiaoli and I walked to the far one — twenty-odd minutes on the way, and she walks just as fast as I do. The fruit there is a bit cheaper than what we usually buy, and the bread is much cheaper. But there were far too many shoppers, and we spent another ten-odd minutes at the back. In the end Xiaoli said: "From now on let's go to the near one — it's a bit pricier, but you don't have to wait that long."

Why this works as a paragraph: the three structures are doing three different jobs. 跟…一样大 sets the two shops level so that the differences afterwards are worth mentioning; 走得跟我一样快 is the same frame hung off 得, describing how an action goes; 比…便宜一点儿 / 便宜得多 then says which side wins and by how much; and the rough numbers (二十多分钟 · 十几分钟) keep the story honest without pretending you timed it. The last line uses 一些 to concede a small difference before choosing — exactly what degree words are for.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Equality: adjective last, no intensifier — 跟…一样大 ✓ · 跟…一样很大 ✗
  • Action equality: 得 present and the verb repeated after any object — 走路走得跟…一样快 ✓
  • 比 sentences: degree word after the adjective, and zero 很/非常/太 anywhere inside them.
  • Rough numbers: 二十多分钟 (round ten, 多 before the measure) vs 十几分钟 vs 两个多小时 — did you pick by the shape of the number?
  • Does it read as one story — 上个星期六 · 可是 · 最后 joining it — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

1. 小刚游泳得跟他哥哥一样快。
2. 我姐姐的头发比我的很长。
3. 这个蛋糕比那个一点儿甜。

1. 小刚游泳游得跟他哥哥一样快。
2. 我姐姐的头发比我的长多了。
3. 这个蛋糕比那个甜一点儿。
4. 我在这个城市住了五年多了。

1. Xiǎogāng yóuyǒng yóu de gēn tā gēge yíyàng kuài. 2. Wǒ jiějie de tóufa bǐ wǒ de cháng duō le. 3. Zhège dàngāo bǐ nàge tián yìdiǎnr. 4. Wǒ zài zhège chéngshì zhù le wǔ nián duō le.

1. Xiaogang swims as fast as his older brother. 2. My sister's hair is much longer than mine. 3. This cake is a bit sweeter than that one. 4. I've lived in this city for over five years.

  1. 游泳得 → 游泳游得. 游泳 is verb + object glued together, so the 得-complement has nothing to attach to until you say the verb again: V + object + V + 得 + 跟 … 一样 + Adj. Same for 唱歌唱得, 做菜做得, 说话说得.
  2. 比我的很长 → 比我的长多了. 很/非常/太 are all barred inside a 比 sentence — the comparison already supplies the degree. Say how big the gap is instead: 长多了 or 长得多.
  3. 一点儿甜 → 甜一点儿. English puts the amount first ("a bit sweeter"); Chinese hangs it on the end of the adjective. 一点儿/一些 and 得多/多了 all sit in that same tail slot.
  4. 五多年 → 五年多. 多 only slips in before the measure after a round ten (五十多年). After an ordinary number it waits until the unit has been said: 五年多 · 五个多月.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Every 得-complement: is there a verb immediately in front of 得, even if you just said it?
  • Scan each 比 sentence for 很/非常/太 — if one survived, replace it with a degree tail.
  • Is every degree word (一点儿 · 一些 · 得多 · 多了) after its adjective, at the very end?
  • Round ten ⇒ 多 before the measure; any other number ⇒ after the unit. Did you check the number's shape before placing 多?
  • Did you fix each sentence with the smallest change that heals it, and leave the meaning intact?

1. My neighbour sings as well as the people on TV. [V + 得 + 跟 … 一样 + Adj] · 用: 邻居 · 唱歌 · 电视
2. Last night's film was far more interesting than last month's. [比 … + Adj + 多了 / 得多] · 用: 电影 · 有意思
3. There were twenty-odd people in the park this morning, and only a few children. [round ten + 多 · 几 + measure] · 用: 公园 · 孩子
4. This week's homework is not the same as last week's — I spent over two hours on it. [跟 … 不一样 · number + measure + 多] · 用: 作业 · 小时

1. 我邻居唱歌唱得跟电视上的人一样好。
2. 昨天晚上的那个电影比上个月的有意思多了。
3. 今天上午公园里有二十多个人，只有几个孩子。
4. 这个星期的作业跟上个星期的不一样，我做了两个多小时。

1. Wǒ línjū chànggē chàng de gēn diànshì shang de rén yíyàng hǎo. 2. Zuótiān wǎnshang de nàge diànyǐng bǐ shàng ge yuè de yǒu yìsi duō le. 3. Jīntiān shàngwǔ gōngyuán li yǒu èrshí duō ge rén, zhǐ yǒu jǐ ge háizi. 4. Zhège xīngqī de zuòyè gēn shàng ge xīngqī de bù yíyàng, wǒ zuò le liǎng ge duō xiǎoshí.

1. My neighbour sings as well as the people on TV. 2. Last night's film was far more interesting than last month's. 3. There were twenty-odd people in the park this morning, and only a few children. 4. This week's homework is not the same as last week's — I spent over two hours on it.

Why these are right: (1) 唱歌 carries its own object, so the verb comes back before 得 (唱歌唱得), and the adjective closes the sentence. (2) The two sides of a 比 sentence have to be comparable, so B shrinks to 上个月的 — a film against a film, not a film against a month — and 多了 lands after 有意思. (3) 二十多个人 puts 多 straight after the round ten, while 几个孩子 puts 几 in front of a measure word: both are "rough", but they are not interchangeable. (4) Two things are being compared, so there is no 得 anywhere; the negative is 跟…不一样, and 多 waits for the measure 个 before it appears (两个多小时).

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: 得 with a verb in front of it — the object 歌 does not count as one.
  • Sentence 2: is B a film too? Comparing a film with 上个月 is the standard slip.
  • Sentence 3: 多 before the measure (round ten), 几 before the measure (a few) — and never 几 + bare noun.
  • Sentence 4: no 得 in an A-vs-B comparison, and 不 sits on 一样, not on 跟.
