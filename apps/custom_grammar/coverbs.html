HSK 2 Review · Sheet 02 · 在·离·从·往·对

我家离地铁站很近，从我家到公司只要二十分钟。

Wǒ jiā lí dìtiězhàn hěn jìn, cóng wǒ jiā dào gōngsī zhǐ yào èrshí fēnzhōng.

My home is close to the subway station; it only takes twenty minutes from my place to the office.

What this sheet drills

You named prepositions and location as your weakest spot. The good news: Chinese has almost no prepositions — it has coverbs, little verbs that grab a noun and then hand the sentence over to the real verb. There are only five you need at this level (在 · 离 · 从 · 往 · 对), and they all obey one word-order rule. Learn that rule once and five separate problems collapse into one. Companion theory: HSK 1/2 location sheet.

What you'll learn

  1. The placement rule — every coverb phrase sits before the verb.
  2. 在 — the one that moves — before the verb vs after a placement verb.
  3. 离 — static distance: A 离 B 很远／很近.
  4. 从 … 到 … — starting point and journey.
  5. 往 — the direction you move in.
  6. 对 — the person or thing you aim something at.
  7. Mixed practice

1. The placement rule Subject + [coverb + noun] + Verb — one rule, five coverbs

When a speaker reaches for this: any time you want to say where, from where, toward where, how far, or at whom. All of that information is loaded in before the verb fires. Chinese sets the scene first and acts second.

The English trap

English trails its prepositional phrases after the verb: "I eat at home", "I work in New York", "I walk to the station". So your instinct is wrong in exactly the same direction every single time — you produce 我吃饭在家 instead of 我在家吃饭. Because the error is always the same shape, you only have to fix it once: the place phrase can never come after the verb.

The purple block is the coverb phrase. In Chinese it always sits to the left of the green verb; English drags it to the right. Every 在／离／从／往／对 sentence in this sheet is the same picture.
Subject + 在/离/从/往/对 + noun + Verb + (Object)
CoverbIts jobExample
在where the action happens我在纽约工作。
离distance between two fixed points我家离公司很远。
从starting point of a journey我从家走到地铁站。
往direction you move in一直往前走。
对the person or thing you aim it at老板对我很好。

我在纽约工作。

Wǒ zài Niǔyuē gōngzuò.

I / at-New-York / work

I work in New York. (Not 我工作在纽约.)

我在家吃饭。

Wǒ zài jiā chīfàn.

I / at-home / eat-rice

I eat at home.

小丽每天在地铁上看书。

Xiǎo Lì měitiān zài dìtiě shang kàn shū.

Xiao Li / every-day / on-the-subway / read / book

Xiao Li reads on the subway every day. — a time word may go before or after the subject, but the coverb phrase still hugs the verb.

Common pitfall

A bare place noun cannot stand in that slot on its own — it needs the coverb. 我家吃饭 means "my family eats"; only 我在家吃饭 means "I eat at home".

Practice

Fix the sentence: 我吃饭在家。

我在家吃饭。 — 在家 is a coverb phrase, so it must sit between the subject and the verb. English says "eat at home"; Chinese says "at-home eat".

"I practise dancing at home every day." Which is right?

  • 我每天在家练习跳舞。✓ Subject 我 → time 每天 → coverb phrase 在家 → verb 练习跳舞.
  • 我每天练习跳舞在家。✗ Straight out of English ("practise dancing at home"). The place phrase can never trail the verb.
  • 我每天家练习跳舞。✗ The slot is right but the coverb is missing — a place noun needs 在 in front of it.

Build: "Xiao Li reads on the subway every day." (The time word may go before or after 小丽 — both are accepted.)

小丽 每天 在地铁上 看书

2. 在 — the one that moves Before the verb, or after it — and the meaning changes

When a speaker reaches for this: 在 is the exception that proves the rule. Ask yourself what the place is describing. Is it where the action takes place? Then 在 goes first, like every other coverb. Is it where a thing or person ends up after the action? Then 在 rides on the back of the verb.
Only a small family of verbs takes 在 on its back — the ones that put something somewhere: 放 (place), 住 (live), 坐 (sit), 站 (stand), 写 (write). Everything else keeps 在 in front.

Position A — 在 before the verb: where the action happens

我在纽约工作，可是我想辞职。

Wǒ zài Niǔyuē gōngzuò, kěshì wǒ xiǎng cízhí.

I / in-New-York / work, / but / I / want / quit-job

I work in New York, but I want to quit.

下班以后我在家练习跳舞。

Xiàbān yǐhòu wǒ zài jiā liànxí tiàowǔ.

finish-work / after / I / at-home / practise / dance

After work I practise dancing at home.

Position B — V + 在: where the thing ends up

你的书放在桌子上。

Nǐ de shū fàng zài zhuōzi shang.

your / book / put / at / table-on

Your book is on the table. — the table is where the book came to rest.

小丽住在地铁站旁边。

Xiǎo Lì zhù zài dìtiězhàn pángbiān.

Xiao Li / live / at / subway-station / beside

Xiao Li lives right next to the subway station.

请坐在这儿。／ 请写在本子上。

Qǐng zuò zài zhèr. / Qǐng xiě zài běnzi shang.

please / sit / at / here. — please / write / at / notebook-on

Please sit here. / Please write it in the notebook.

在 first — action location
小丽在地铁站旁边看书。
The reading happens beside the station. She could be anywhere afterwards.
在 after — end position
小丽住在地铁站旁边。
The living deposits her beside the station — that's where she stays.

Quick test

Say the sentence in English and ask: "at the table" — does it tell me where the event was, or where the object is now? Event → 在 goes first. Object's resting place → 在 goes after the verb.

Practice

You're telling 小刚 where his coffee is — you put it down there. Which is right?

  • 你的咖啡放在桌子上。✓ 放 is a placement verb: the resting place rides on the back of the verb.
  • 你的咖啡在桌子上放。✗ 在 in front would say the act of putting was carried out on the table. The end position must follow 放.
  • 你的咖啡放桌子上在。✗ 在 can never come last — it always sits in front of its place word.

Fill with 在 + the place: 下班以后我 ___ 练习跳舞。
(After work I practise dancing at home.)

在家 (or 在家里) — 练习 is not a placement verb, so the location stays in front of it.

3. 离 — static distance A 离 B + 远／近 — nobody is going anywhere

When a speaker reaches for this: you're measuring the gap between two things that are just sitting there — your flat and the office, your flat and the subway, New York and Beijing. Nothing moves. The whole sentence is A 离 B + how far.
A + 离 B + 很远 / 很近 / 多远

我家离公司很远，坐地铁要一个小时。

Wǒ jiā lí gōngsī hěn yuǎn, zuò dìtiě yào yí gè xiǎoshí.

my / home / apart-from / company / very / far, / sit / subway / need / one / CL / hour

My place is a long way from the office — an hour by subway.

我家离地铁站很近，走路只要五分钟。

Wǒ jiā lí dìtiězhàn hěn jìn, zǒulù zhǐ yào wǔ fēnzhōng.

my / home / apart-from / subway-station / very / near, / walk / only / need / five / minutes

My place is close to the subway — only a five-minute walk.

你家离地铁站远吗？
纽约离北京多远？

Nǐ jiā lí dìtiězhàn yuǎn ma? Niǔyuē lí Běijīng duō yuǎn?

your / home / apart-from / subway-station / far / MA? — New-York / apart-from / Beijing / how / far?

Is your place far from the subway? How far is New York from Beijing?

Common pitfall — 离 is not 从

我家从公司很远 is wrong. 从 needs a journey — somebody has to travel, so the sentence needs a verb of going: 我从家坐地铁到公司。 For a plain, motionless measurement, only 离 works. English blurs the two with a single word "from" ("far from the office", "walk from home"), which is why you keep reaching for 从.

Bonus — 离 also measures time

The gap doesn't have to be in space: 离下班还有一个小时。 (Lí xiàbān hái yǒu yí gè xiǎoshí.) — "There's still an hour to go until I finish work."

Practice

离, 从 or 往? 我家 ___ 公司很远，坐地铁要一个小时。
(My place is a long way from the office — an hour by subway.)

离 — nothing is travelling in this clause; it's a plain measurement between two fixed points, so 离 is the only option.

"My home is very far from the office." Which is right?

  • 我家离公司很远。✓ A 离 B + 很远 — the standard distance frame.
  • 我家从公司很远。✗ 从 marks the start of a journey and needs a motion verb. Plain distance takes 离.
  • 我家很远离公司。✗ The coverb phrase 离公司 has to come before 很远, exactly like every other coverb phrase comes before the verb.

4. 从 … 到 … Where the journey starts, and where it ends

When a speaker reaches for this: something is travelling — you, a train, a message. 从 marks the starting point, and it very often brings 到 along for the end point. The pair also works on the clock: 从九点到五点.
Subject + 从 A + (到 B) + Verb

我是从纽约来的。

Wǒ shì cóng Niǔyuē lái de.

I / SHI / from / New-York / come / DE

I'm from New York. (Literally: it's from New York that I came.)

小刚每天六点从公司回家。

Xiǎo Gāng měitiān liù diǎn cóng gōngsī huí jiā.

Xiao Gang / every-day / six / o'clock / from / company / return / home

Xiao Gang heads home from the office at six every day.

我每天从家走到地铁站。

Wǒ měitiān cóng jiā zǒu dào dìtiězhàn.

I / every-day / from / home / walk / to / subway-station

Every day I walk from home to the subway station.

从我家到公司只要二十分钟。

Cóng wǒ jiā dào gōngsī zhǐ yào èrshí fēnzhōng.

from / my / home / to / company / only / need / twenty / minutes

It only takes twenty minutes from my place to the office. — the 从…到… pair can open the whole sentence when it is the topic.

从九点到五点，我都在公司工作。

Cóng jiǔ diǎn dào wǔ diǎn, wǒ dōu zài gōngsī gōngzuò.

from / nine / o'clock / to / five / o'clock, / I / all / at / company / work

From nine to five I'm at the office working. — same pair, measuring time instead of space.

Practice

Build: "Every day I walk from home to the subway station." (The time word may go before or after 我 — both are accepted.)

我 每天 从家 走到 地铁站

Type the two missing characters in order:
___ 九点 ___ 五点，我都在公司工作。
(From nine to five I'm at the office working. Two characters, e.g. 从到 — no spaces needed.)

从 · 到 — the same pair that spans two places also spans two clock times: 从九点到五点.

5. 往 — the direction you move in 往前走, 往左走, 往右走

When a speaker reaches for this: giving or following directions. 往 takes a direction word — 前 / 左 / 右 / 回 — and hands it to a motion verb, almost always 走. It is a pure movement coverb: no movement, no 往.
往 + 前/左/右 + 走

往前走。／ 往左走。／ 往右走。

Wǎng qián zǒu. / Wǎng zuǒ zǒu. / Wǎng yòu zǒu.

toward-front / walk. — toward-left / walk. — toward-right / walk.

Go straight ahead. / Go left. / Go right.

一直往前走，到了路口往右走。

Yìzhí wǎng qián zǒu, dào le lùkǒu wǎng yòu zǒu.

straight-on / toward-front / walk, / arrive / LE / intersection / toward-right / walk

Keep going straight, and at the intersection go right.

地铁站在前面，往前走三分钟就到了。

Dìtiězhàn zài qiánmiàn, wǎng qián zǒu sān fēnzhōng jiù dào le.

subway-station / at / front, / toward-front / walk / three / minutes / then / arrive / LE

The subway station is up ahead — three minutes straight on and you're there.

Common pitfall

往 is not a general-purpose "to". You go to a place with a plain verb (我去公司) or with 到 (我走到地铁站) — 我往公司 is not a sentence. Keep 往 for which way you turn: 往前, 往左, 往右.

Practice

Fill: 一直往前走，到了路口 ___ 右走就到了。
(Keep going straight; turn right at the intersection and you're there.)

往 — 往右走 is the everyday way to say "turn right". (向 is also correct here but belongs to a later level; stick with 往 for directions.)

Build: "The subway station is up ahead — three minutes straight on and you're there."

地铁站 在前面 往前走 三分钟 就到了

6. 对 — the one you aim at 他对我很好 · 跳舞对身体很好

When a speaker reaches for this: the "place" is not a place at all — it's a person, or something that benefits. 对 points your attitude, your behaviour, or a good/bad effect at a target. Its literal sense is "facing", which is exactly the picture you want: you turn and face someone, then act.
A + 对 B + 很好 / 很有兴趣

我老板对我很好，所以辞职不容易。

Wǒ lǎobǎn duì wǒ hěn hǎo, suǒyǐ cízhí bù róngyì.

my / boss / toward / me / very / good, / so / quit-job / not / easy

My boss is very good to me, so quitting isn't easy.

老师对学生很好。

Lǎoshī duì xuésheng hěn hǎo.

teacher / toward / student / very / good

The teacher is very kind to her students.

跳舞对身体很好。

Tiàowǔ duì shēntǐ hěn hǎo.

dance / toward / body / very / good

Dancing is very good for you. — the target can be a thing that benefits, not just a person.

我对韩国的舞蹈很有兴趣。

Wǒ duì Hánguó de wǔdǎo hěn yǒu xìngqù.

I / toward / Korea / DE / dance / very / have / interest

I'm really interested in Korean dance.

对 — an attitude aimed at someone
老板对我很好。
How he treats me. Nothing changes hands.
给 — something handed over
老板给我打电话。
He does something for me, or delivers something to me — a call, a coffee, help.

Common pitfall

English uses one word, "to", for both jobs — "he's good to me", "he gave it to me" — so 老板给我很好 feels fine and is wrong. And 在 can never take a person: 老板在我很好. Attitude → 对. Handover → 给. Place → 在.

Practice

"My boss is very good to me." Which is right?

  • 我老板对我很好。✓ An attitude aimed at a person takes 对.
  • 我老板给我很好。✗ 给 hands something over — 给我打电话, 给我买咖啡. It can't carry an attitude.
  • 我老板在我很好。✗ 在 needs a place. It can never take a person as its noun.

Fill: 跳舞 ___ 身体很好，所以我每天都练习。
(Dancing is good for your health, so I practise every day.)

对 — 身体 is the target that benefits: 对身体很好 is a set phrase you'll hear constantly.

7. Mixed practice All five coverbs in one commute

选词填空。 Pick the right coverb for each blank (one word is extra).

我家地铁站很近，走路只要五分钟。我每天早上家走到地铁站，坐地铁去公司，公司工作八个小时。下班以后我去练习跳舞，出了地铁站右走，三分钟就到了。舞蹈老师我很好，他说我跳舞跳得越来越好。

Translate: "Keep going straight, at the intersection go right, and you'll arrive at the coffee shop."

一直往前走，到了路口往右走，就到咖啡馆了。 — two 往 phrases, each one sitting in front of its 走.

Translate: "I work in New York; my home isn't far from the office, and my boss is very good to me."

我在纽约工作，我家离公司不远，我老板对我很好。 — three coverbs, three different jobs: 在 = where the work happens, 离 = the distance, 对 = who the kindness is aimed at. All three sit in front of what they modify.
