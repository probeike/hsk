What you'll learn

Chinese has no tense. What it has instead is a way of saying how far along an action is. Picture one action — say 吃早饭 — travelling left to right along a line. This lesson gives you the four station names on that line, plus the words for saying what you intend to do before the train even leaves.

  1. 还没(有)…呢 — station 1: hasn't happened yet.
  2. 正在 / 在…呢 — station 2: right now, in the middle of it.
  3. 快要…了 / 就要…了 / 快…了 — station 3: about to.
  4. 已经…了 + 刚 — station 4: already done. Plus the whole line assembled.
  5. 要 · 想 · 会 · 打算 — talking about the future without a future tense.
  6. Mixed practice

What this lesson assumes

You already met 刚 vs 刚才 in Lesson 5 — section 4 recycles it rather than re-teaching it. And 了 does three separate jobs; this lesson uses two of them without explaining them from scratch. If any 了 here feels unmotivated, read the three 了s appendix alongside.

1. 还没(有)…(呢) Station 1 — "not yet", and still coming

When a speaker reaches for this: to report that something everyone expected to have happened hasn't happened so far — and is still on the list. English: I haven't eaten yet, he isn't home yet, I haven't told my boss yet. The word 还 is what carries "yet"; the sentence-final 呢 adds "…and that's still the situation."
The action line. Station 1: nothing has happened yet, but the speaker still expects it to.

The English trap

English marks the difference between "I didn't eat breakfast" and "I haven't eaten breakfast yet" with tense — did vs have. Your brain will look for the Chinese equivalent of have and reach for 了. It isn't there. The difference is carried by 还 alone: 没吃 = didn't eat, 还没吃呢 = haven't eaten yet. And a negated verb never takes 了 — 我没吃了早饭 is not a sentence.

Subj + 还没(有) + V + (Obj) + (呢)

今天早上我还没吃早饭呢。

Jīntiān zǎoshang wǒ hái méi chī zǎofàn ne.

today / morning / I / still-not / eat / breakfast / NE

I haven't had breakfast yet this morning. (And I intend to.)

小刚还没回家呢，妈妈已经做好饭了。

Xiǎogāng hái méi huí jiā ne, māma yǐjīng zuò hǎo fàn le.

Xiaogang / still-not / return / home / NE, / mum / already / cook / well / meal / LE

Xiaogang isn't home yet — Mum has already finished cooking.

我还没告诉老板呢。

Wǒ hái méi gàosu lǎobǎn ne.

I / still-not / tell / boss / NE

I haven't told my boss yet.

最近两个月我没练习汉语。

Zuìjìn liǎng ge yuè wǒ méi liànxí Hànyǔ.

recently / two / CL / month / I / not / practise / Chinese

I didn't practise Chinese these past two months. (A closed report — no promise about next week.)

没 — a closed fact
我没吃早饭
"I didn't have breakfast." That's simply what happened. Maybe I skipped it on purpose.
还没…呢 — still pending
我还没吃早饭呢
"I haven't had breakfast yet." The meal is still coming — it just hasn't arrived on the timeline.

Common pitfall

Three shapes to keep apart: 没(有) negates a completed action, 不 negates a habit, wish or future, and neither of them tolerates 了 on the verb. 我还没吃了 ✗ · 我还没吃呢 ✓ · 我不吃了 ✓ (that last one means "I'm not eating anymore" — the 了 is the sentence-final change-of-state one, not a verb 了).

Practice

Fill: 今天早上我 ___ 吃早饭呢。
(I haven't had breakfast yet this morning.)

还没 — 还 carries "yet"; 呢 at the end says "that's still the state of things".

Which sentence means "I haven't eaten yet"?

  • 我还没吃呢。✓ 还 + 没 + V + 呢 — the "not yet, still coming" frame.
  • 我没吃了。✗ A negated verb never takes 了. This shape doesn't exist.
  • 我不吃了。✗ That means "I'm not eating anymore" — a decision, not a report on the timeline.

What does this mean?
最近两个月我没练习汉语。

  • I haven't practised Chinese yet, but I'm about to.✗ That would need 还…呢: 我还没练习呢.
  • I didn't practise Chinese for the last two months.✓ Plain 没 = a closed report about a finished stretch of time.
  • I don't practise Chinese.✗ A habit would use 不: 我不练习汉语.

Fill: 妈妈已经做好饭了，可是小刚 ___ 回家呢。
(Mum has already finished cooking, but Xiaogang isn't home yet.)

还没 — the two halves sit at opposite ends of the line: 已经…了 at station 4, 还没…呢 at station 1.

2. 正在 / 在…(呢) Station 2 — freeze-frame: it's happening now

When a speaker reaches for this: to freeze the action mid-motion. Somebody rings you and you say "I'm working right now"; you look through a window and report "she's dancing". The point is not when it happened but that at the reference moment it was in progress.
Station 2: the action has started and has not finished. 正在 puts a spotlight on that middle stretch.

The English trap

English has two present tenses and uses them for two different jobs: "I work in New York" (that's my life) vs "I'm working" (right this second). English speakers assume Chinese must also mark the difference every time — so they stick 正在 onto habits: 我正在纽约工作 for "I work in New York". Wrong direction. A bare verb in Chinese already covers the habit. 正在 is the extra you add only when you want the freeze-frame.

Subj + 正在 / 在 + V + (Obj) + (呢)

我正在工作呢，一会儿给你打电话。

Wǒ zhèngzài gōngzuò ne, yíhuìr gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.

I / right-now / work / NE, / in-a-moment / to-you / make / phone-call

I'm working right now — I'll call you in a bit.

他在跳舞。

Tā zài tiào wǔ.

he / ZAI / dance

He's dancing. (Bare 在 is the light, everyday version — 正在 is a touch more emphatic.)

小丽正在练习舞蹈呢。

Xiǎolì zhèngzài liànxí wǔdǎo ne.

Xiaoli / right-now / practise / dance / NE

Xiaoli is practising her dancing right now.

A: 你在做什么呢？
B: 我在准备早饭。

A: Nǐ zài zuò shénme ne? / B: Wǒ zài zhǔnbèi zǎofàn.

A: What are you doing? / B: I'm getting breakfast ready.

Bare verb — habit / life
我每天在纽约工作
"I work in New York every day." Here 在 is the place word ("at"), not a marker. This is my routine.
正在…呢 — freeze-frame
我正在工作呢
"I'm working right now." Say this when someone interrupts you.

Common pitfall — the two jobs of 在

在 before a place means "at / in": 我在纽约. 在 before a verb is the progressive marker: 我在工作. Both at once is perfectly normal and reads left to right: 我在纽约工作 = I work in New York. If you want "I'm working, in New York, right now", say 我正在工作 and let context supply the city.

The 呢 here is not the 呢 from Lesson 5

Lesson 5 taught N + 呢? = "where's X?" (我的手机呢？). This one is different: a statement-final 呢 that means "…and it's still going on". Same character, different job — same as English "still" vs "still?".

Practice

Fill: 别叫我，我 ___ 工作呢。
(Don't call me — I'm working right now.)

正在 (or plain 在) — the freeze-frame marker sits directly before the verb.

Which sentence means "I work in New York" (that's my job, not what I'm doing this second)?

  • 我在纽约工作。✓ Here 在 is the place word: 在 + place + verb. A bare verb covers the habit.
  • 我正在工作呢。✗ That's the freeze-frame: "I'm working right now."
  • 我还没工作呢。✗ That's station 1: "I haven't started working yet."

What does this mean?
我给她打电话的时候，她正在练习舞蹈呢。

  • When I phoned her, she had already finished dancing.✗ That would be 已经练习完了.
  • When I phoned her, she was in the middle of practising her dancing.✓ 正在…呢 freezes the action at the reference moment — which here is "when I phoned".
  • When I phoned her, she was about to practise.✗ That's station 3: 快要练习了.

Complete: A: 你 ___ ？ B: 我在准备早饭。
(What are you doing? — I'm getting breakfast ready.)

在做什么呢 — the everyday "what are you up to?".

3. 快要…了 / 就要…了 / 快…了 Station 3 — on the edge of happening

When a speaker reaches for this: to say something is about to happen — the train is pulling out, spring is nearly here, you're two weeks from handing in your notice. It is a warning, an announcement, a countdown.
Station 3: the action is one step from its next boundary — from starting, or from finishing. The final 了 is what marks that boundary as imminent.

The English trap

In English, soon is a loose adverb you can drop almost anywhere: "I'm quitting soon", "Soon I'm quitting", "I'll quit soon". In Chinese this is not an adverb at all — it is a frame that wraps the verb, and the closing 了 is not optional: 快要 … 了. Leave the 了 off and the sentence is broken: 我快要辞职 ✗ / 我快要辞职了 ✓.

(Time) + Subj + 快要 / 就要 / 快 + V / Adj + 了

我快要辞职了。

Wǒ kuàiyào cízhí le.

I / about-to / quit-job / LE

I'm about to quit my job. (No date given — just "soon".)

两个星期以后我就要辞职了。

Liǎng ge xīngqī yǐhòu wǒ jiù yào cízhí le.

two / CL / week / after / I / then-about-to / quit-job / LE

In two weeks I'm quitting. (A date is on the table, so 就要 — not 快要.)

春天快要到了。

Chūntiān kuàiyào dào le.

spring / about-to / arrive / LE

Spring is nearly here.

火车就要开了，快跑！

Huǒchē jiù yào kāi le, kuài pǎo!

train / about-to / depart / LE, / fast / run

The train is about to leave — run!

快八点了，我要走了。

Kuài bā diǎn le, wǒ yào zǒu le.

nearly / eight / o'clock / LE, / I / going-to / leave / LE

It's nearly eight — I need to go. (Before a clock time or a number, use bare 快, not 快要.)

快要…了 — vague "soon"
我快要辞职了
Soon, sometime. Do not put a specific time in front of this one.
就要…了 — soon, and here's when
两个星期以后我就要辞职了
"In two weeks." 就要 is the one that accepts a stated time; it also sounds more certain.

Common pitfall

The two errors that show up together: dropping the 了, and using 快要 with a stated time. 两个星期以后我快要辞职了 ✗ — a time phrase forces 就要. Memory hook: 就 already means "right then", so it is the one that can hold hands with a clock.

Practice

Pick the correct word: 两个星期以后我 ___ 辞职了。

  • 就要✓ A specific time is stated (两个星期以后), so 就要 is the only option.
  • 快要✗ 快要 refuses a stated time. Use it only for a bare "soon".
  • 刚✗ 刚 points backwards ("just now"), not forwards.

Fill: 春天 ___ 到了，天气越来越好。
(Spring is nearly here — the weather's getting better and better.)

快要 — no date is given, so 快要…了. Bare 快 also works.

What does this mean?
火车就要开了。

  • The train has already left.✗ That's station 4: 火车已经开了.
  • The train is about to leave (any second now).✓ 就要…了 — imminent, and more urgent-sounding than 快要.
  • The train hasn't left yet.✗ True in a sense, but that flat report is 火车还没开呢 — no urgency.

Fill: ___ 八点了，我们走吧。
(It's nearly eight — let's go.)

快 — before a clock time or a number there is no verb, so bare 快 …了 does the work: 快八点了, 快三十岁了.

Which sentence is correct?

  • 我快要辞职。✗ The closing 了 is not optional in this frame.
  • 我快要辞职了。✓ 快要 opens the frame, 了 closes it.
  • 我辞职快要了。✗ 快要 is a frame around the verb, not a word you can move to the end.

Build: "In two weeks I'm going to quit my job."

两个星期以后 我 就要 辞职 了

4. 已经…了 + 刚 Station 4 — done, and freshly done

When a speaker reaches for this: to report that the action is behind us. 已经…了 = "already", often with a hint of "sooner than you'd think". 刚 = "just", the action finished a moment ago and its result is still warm.
Station 4: the boundary has been crossed. 已经 announces it; the sentence-final 了 is what actually marks the crossing.

The English trap

English already is a standalone adverb — "I already ate" is a complete sentence. Chinese 已经 is only half the structure; it needs its 了: 我已经吃早饭 ✗ / 我已经吃早饭了 ✓. Meanwhile 刚 works the other way round — it usually takes no 了 at all: 我刚起床, not 我刚起床了.

Subj + 已经 + V + (Obj) + 了

我已经吃过早饭了。

Wǒ yǐjīng chī guo zǎofàn le.

I / already / eat / GUO / breakfast / LE

I've already had breakfast. (过 adds "I've had the experience of it" — very common with meals.)

我刚起床。

Wǒ gāng qǐ chuáng.

I / just / get-up

I just got up. (No 了 — 刚 already carries the freshness.)

妈妈已经做好饭了，我们吃吧。

Māma yǐjīng zuò hǎo fàn le, wǒmen chī ba.

mum / already / cook / well / meal / LE, / we / eat / BA

Mum's already finished cooking — let's eat.

我刚搬家，还没准备好呢。

Wǒ gāng bānjiā, hái méi zhǔnbèi hǎo ne.

I / just / move-house, / still-not / prepare / well / NE

I've just moved house — I'm not settled in yet.

Recycled from Lesson 5: 刚 vs 刚才

Both mean "just now", and you sorted them out in Lesson 5 §3: 刚 is an adverb that sits only right before a verb and stretches as far as the speaker's sense of "recent" allows (我刚搬到纽约两个月); 刚才 is a time noun meaning strictly "a few minutes ago", and it can start the sentence (刚才你在做什么？). Nothing new here — just don't let station 4 tempt you into 刚才我起床 for "I just got up".

The whole line, one action

Here is a single morning, told five times. Same verb, same breakfast — only the station changes. This table is the lesson; if you can produce these five sentences on demand you have the system.

Station Clock Chinese English
1 · 还没…呢 7:00 我还没吃早饭呢。 I haven't had breakfast yet.
2 · 正在…呢 7:30 我正在吃早饭呢。 I'm having breakfast right now.
3 · 快要…了 7:50 我快要吃完了。 I'm about to finish.
4 · 已经…了 8:00 我已经吃完早饭了。 I've already finished breakfast.
4 · 刚 8:15 我刚吃完早饭。 I've just finished breakfast.
The full line. Notice what changes and what doesn't: the verb never moves, the subject never moves. Only the wrapper around the verb changes.

Practice

Fill: 我 ___ 吃过早饭了，你呢？
(I've already had breakfast — how about you?)

已经 — and note the 了 at the end is doing half the work. Drop it and the sentence breaks.

Fill with 刚 or 刚才: ___ 我给你打电话，你没听见吗？
(I phoned you a moment ago — didn't you hear?)

刚才 — it's a time noun, so it can open the sentence. 刚 cannot (Lesson 5 §3).

What does this mean?
我刚搬家，还没准备好呢。

  • I'm about to move house and haven't got ready.✗ "About to" would be 快要搬家了. 刚 points backwards.
  • I've just moved house and I'm not settled in yet.✓ 刚 = station 4 (freshly done); 还没…呢 = station 1 for the second action.
  • I moved house a long time ago but never got settled.✗ 刚 specifically means the move was recent.

Which sentence is correct?

  • 我已经告诉老板。✗ 已经 needs its partner 了 at the end.
  • 我已经告诉老板了。✓ 已经 … 了 — the pair works together.
  • 我已经没告诉老板了。✗ 没 and 了 can't share a clause, and 已经没 isn't how you say "haven't yet" — that's 还没…呢.

Build: "I've already told my colleagues."

我 已经 告诉 我的 同事 了

5. 要 · 想 · 会 · 打算 Talking about the future without a future tense

When a speaker reaches for this: everything so far described an action that exists. This section is for actions that don't exist yet. Chinese has no "will"; instead it asks you to say what kind of future this is — a decision, a wish, a forecast, or a worked-out plan.
Back at the left edge. The action still hasn't happened — but instead of just reporting that (还没…呢), you now commit to it, wish for it, or predict it.

The English trap

English collapses all four into two words, will and want to, and then leans on tone of voice for the rest. "I'm going to quit", "I want to quit", "I'll probably quit" and "I'm planning to quit" are four different Chinese verbs, and picking the wrong one changes how committed you sound. Choose deliberately.

Word Job Example How committed?
要 decided plan — "am going to" 两个星期以后我要辞职。 Decided. Diary entry made.
想 wish — "would like to" 我想当舞蹈老师。 A desire. Might never happen.
会 prediction — "will (turn out)" 辞职以后我会很高兴。 A forecast about the world, not a promise.
打算 plan / intend 我打算明年当老板。 Thought through, but still a plan.

现在我是一个软件工程师，可是两个星期以后我要辞职。

Xiànzài wǒ shì yí ge ruǎnjiàn gōngchéngshī, kěshì liǎng ge xīngqī yǐhòu wǒ yào cízhí.

now / I / am / one / CL / software engineer, / but / two / CL / week / after / I / going-to / quit-job

Right now I'm a software engineer, but in two weeks I'm quitting.

以后你想当舞蹈老师吗？

Yǐhòu nǐ xiǎng dāng wǔdǎo lǎoshī ma?

later / you / would-like / be / dance teacher / MA

Do you want to be a dance teacher one day?

辞职以后我会很高兴。

Cízhí yǐhòu wǒ huì hěn gāoxìng.

quit-job / after / I / will / very / happy

Once I've quit I'll be really happy. (A prediction — you can't decide to be happy.)

这个夏天我打算当老板。

Zhège xiàtiān wǒ dǎsuan dāng lǎobǎn.

this / CL / summer / I / plan-to / be / boss

This summer I plan to become my own boss.

要 — decided
我要辞职
"I'm quitting." The decision is made; I'm telling you, not consulting you.
想 — wished
我想辞职
"I'd like to quit." Still a feeling. Nobody at work needs to worry yet.

Sidebar: 当 vs 成为

Both come out as English become, and you have produced both 我想成为老板 and 我想当老板. Prefer 当:

  • 当 + a job is the everyday spoken verb: 当老师, 当老板, 当医生, 当舞蹈老师. Short, plain, what people actually say.
  • 成为 is formal and written, and it wants a fuller noun phrase after it: 成为一个很好的舞蹈老师. In conversation it sounds like a speech.

Rule of thumb for now: if you are talking about your own life out loud, say 当.

Common pitfall — 会 is not "will"

会 is a forecast, so it fits states and outcomes: 我会很高兴, 明天会下雨. For a plan you have already made, 会 sounds oddly detached — 我会辞职 reads more like "I imagine I'll end up quitting" than "I'm quitting". Say 我要辞职.

Practice

You'd like to be a dance teacher one day, but nothing is decided. Which do you say?

  • 我想当舞蹈老师。✓ 想 = wish / would like to. Exactly the right level of commitment.
  • 我要当舞蹈老师。✗ 要 announces a decision already taken.
  • 我会当舞蹈老师。✗ 会 is a forecast — "I predict I'll end up being one".

Fill: 两个星期以后我 ___ 辞职，我已经告诉老板了。
(In two weeks I'm quitting — I've already told my boss.)

要 — the boss has been told, so this is a decision, not a wish.

What does this mean?
辞职以后我会很高兴。

  • After I quit I want to be happy.✗ 会 isn't a wish; a wish would be 想.
  • Once I've quit I'll be happy — that's how I expect it to go.✓ 会 = prediction about how things will turn out.
  • After I quit I know how to be happy.✗ 会 does also mean "know how to", but only with a learnable skill — 我会跳舞. 高兴 isn't a skill.

Talking to a friend about your plans, which sounds most natural?

  • 我想当老板。✓ 当 + job is the everyday spoken verb.
  • 我想成为老板。✗ Not wrong, but 成为 is formal/written and wants a fuller phrase after it — it sounds like a speech.
  • 我想是老板。✗ 是 links two nouns ("I am the boss"); it can't express "become".

Fill: 我 ___ 明年当老板，可是现在还没准备好呢。
(I'm planning to be my own boss next year, but I'm not ready yet.)

打算 — a thought-through plan, which is exactly why the second half can admit it isn't ready.

Build: "Later on I'd like to be a dance teacher."

Mixed practice All five points, one line

Fill: 我 ___ 起床，今天早上我还没吃早饭呢。
(I've just got up — I haven't had breakfast yet this morning.)

刚 — right before the verb, no 了. The sentence puts station 4 and station 1 side by side: getting up is done, breakfast isn't.

Someone rings while you're eating. Which reply is right?

  • 我还没吃早饭呢。✗ Station 1 — that says you haven't started.
  • 我正在吃早饭呢。✓ Station 2 — the freeze-frame, exactly what "I'm eating right now" needs.
  • 我已经吃早饭了。✗ Station 4 — that says you've finished.

What does this mean?
妈妈已经做好饭了，可是小刚还没回家呢。

  • Mum is cooking and Xiaogang is on his way home.✗ Both halves would need 正在: 妈妈正在做饭呢.
  • Mum has already finished cooking, but Xiaogang still isn't home.✓ 已经…了 at station 4, 还没…呢 at station 1 — the contrast is the whole point of the sentence.
  • Mum is about to cook and Xiaogang has come home.✗ That would be 妈妈快要做饭了，小刚已经回家了.

Sequencing: put the four stations in time order, from "not started" to "done".

我已经练习完了。 我快要练习完了。 我还没练习舞蹈呢。 我正在练习舞蹈呢。

Fill: 下课以后我 ___ 回家工作了。
(As soon as class ends I'm going home to work.)

就要 — "下课以后" pins the moment, so 就要…了, not 快要…了.

Which sentence has a mistake?

  • 我已经吃过早饭了。This sentence is fine — 已经 … 了 wrapped round 吃过. Keep looking.
  • 最近两个月我没练习了。✓ Found it. A negated verb can't take 了 — say 最近两个月我没练习.
