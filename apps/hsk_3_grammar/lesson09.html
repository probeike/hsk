Lesson 09

她的汉语说得跟中国人一样好

Tā de Hànyǔ shuō de gēn Zhōngguórén yíyàng hǎo

She speaks Chinese like a native

What you'll learn

  1. A 跟 B + 一样 — equivalence comparison.
  2. A 比 B + Adj + 一点儿 / 一些 / 得多 / 多了 — specifying how much more.
  3. Approximate numbers (几, 多) — rough quantities.

See also: discourse particles appendix · the three 了s appendix

1. A 跟 B + 一样 (+ Adj) "A is the same as B" / "A is just as Adj as B"

When a speaker reaches for this: to say two things are equivalent on some dimension — "my phone is the same as yours", "she speaks Chinese like a native", "today is just as cold as yesterday". It's the equality counterpart to 比 (which marks difference).

The English trap

English uses "same as", "like", or "as … as …". Chinese has one tidy frame: A 跟 B 一样 (plain equivalence) or A 跟 B 一样 + Adj (equally Adj). Do not try to use 比 here — 比 compares, 跟…一样 equates.

A + 跟 + B + 一样 + (Adj)

我的手机跟你的一样。

Wǒ de shǒujī gēn nǐ de yíyàng.

my / phone / same-as / yours / equal

My phone is the same as yours.

她的汉语说得跟中国人一样好。

Tā de Hànyǔ shuō de gēn Zhōngguórén yíyàng hǎo.

her / Chinese / speak-DEG / same-as / Chinese-person / equally / good

She speaks Chinese as well as a native.

今天跟昨天一样冷。

Jīntiān gēn zuótiān yíyàng lěng.

today / same-as / yesterday / equally / cold

Today is just as cold as yesterday.

Negation: 不一样 or 跟…不一样

To say two things are different, put 不 before 一样. You can also negate the whole equivalence with 不 placed before 跟 (less common).

我的想法跟他不一样。

Wǒ de xiǎngfǎ gēn tā bù yíyàng.

My idea is different from his.

跟…一样 (equivalence)
A 跟 B 一样 (+ Adj)
A and B are the same (on that dimension).
比 (difference)
A 比 B + Adj
A is more Adj than B.

Common pitfall

The adjective after 一样 is not preceded by 很/非常 — just like in 比 sentences, you don't stack intensifiers. *跟他一样很好 (✗) → 跟他一样好 (✓).

Build: "My computer is the same as yours."

我的电脑 跟 你的 一样

Build: "Today is just as cold as yesterday."

今天 跟 昨天 一样 冷

Which sentence means "His Chinese is as good as mine"?

  • 他的汉语跟我的一样好。✓ A 跟 B 一样 + Adj = A is just as Adj as B.
  • 他的汉语比我的一样好。✗ 比 and 一样 can't combine — 比 compares; 一样 equates.
  • 他的汉语跟我的一样很好。✗ No 很 after 一样 — drop it.

Fill the blank: 我的想法___他不一样。
(My idea is different from his.)

跟 — marks what you're comparing to. 和 is also acceptable in this slot.

What does this mean?
她做饭做得跟妈妈一样好吃。

  • Her cooking is better than her mother's.✗ That would need 比, not 跟…一样.
  • Her cooking tastes just as good as her mother's.✓ 跟…一样好吃 = equally tasty.
  • Her mother taught her how to cook.✗ No teaching verb here — this is an equivalence comparison.

2. A 比 B + Adj + degree "A is X-er than B by this much"

When a speaker reaches for this: they're comparing A to B and want to say how much bigger/better/hotter A is. Not just "A is bigger" — but "a bit bigger" (一点儿 / 一些) or "a lot bigger" (得多 / 多了).

The English trap

English puts the intensifier before the adjective: "A is a bit bigger than B" / "A is much bigger". Chinese puts the degree word after: A 比 B 大 + 一点儿 / 得多. Do not try to use 很, 非常, or 太 in a 比-sentence — that's ungrammatical. Only comparison-specific degree words work.

A + 比 + B + Adj + degree
a little
A 比 B 贵一点儿 / 一些
A is a bit more expensive than B.
much
A 比 B 贵得多 / 多了
A is much more expensive than B.

他的汉语比我好多了。

Tā de Hànyǔ bǐ wǒ hǎo duō le.

His Chinese is much better than mine.

今天比昨天冷一点儿。

Jīntiān bǐ zuótiān lěng yìdiǎnr.

Today is a bit colder than yesterday.

Don't stack 很 with 比

*他比我很高 (✗) is wrong. With 比 you can't use 很/非常/太 — use 一点儿, 一些, 得多, or 多了 to express degree instead. If you want to say "A is just generally tall", drop 比 entirely.

Practice

Which is correct for "This dish is a lot tastier than that one"?

  • 这个菜比那个菜好吃得多。✓ 得多 = "by much" comes after the adjective.
  • 这个菜比那个菜很好吃。✗ 很 doesn't work in 比 sentences.
  • 这个菜得多比那个菜好吃。✗ 得多 must follow the adjective, not precede 比.

Build: "He is a little older than me."

他 比 我 大 一点儿 一些

Fill the blank: 他的成绩比我好___。
(His grades are much better than mine.)

得多 / 多了 — both mean "by a lot" after the adjective in a 比 sentence.

Build: "This item of clothing is a little more expensive than that one."

这件衣服 比 那件 贵 一点儿 一些

What does this sentence mean?
北京比上海冷多了。

  • Beijing is a little colder than Shanghai.✗ That would be 冷一点儿/一些.
  • Beijing is much colder than Shanghai.✓ 多了 after the adjective = "by much".
  • Beijing is as cold as Shanghai.✗ That would use 跟…一样冷.

Which sentence is ungrammatical?

  • 他比我很高。✓ 比 cannot combine with 很/非常/太. Use 一点儿/得多 instead.
  • 他比我高一点儿。✗ This is correct — "a little taller".
  • 他比我高得多。✗ This is correct — "much taller".

3. Approximate numbers: 几 and 多 "A few", "more than"

When a speaker reaches for this: to give a rough number — not exact, but in the ballpark. "A few days", "twenty-some people", "more than three hours".

The English trap

English has messy ways to do this ("a few", "-ish", "or so"). Chinese is tidier:

  • 几 before a measure word = "a few" (2–9, vague). 几个人 = a few people.
  • 多 after a number = "more than / -something". 三十多岁 = thirtysomething. 一个多小时 = more than an hour.

Where 多 goes matters: put it after the number, before the measure word (for numbers that don't end in zero), or before the measure word (for round tens).

他几岁了？

Tā jǐ suì le?

How old is he? (For children: 几 doubles as "what small number" and "a few".)

我有几个中国朋友。

Wǒ yǒu jǐ ge Zhōngguó péngyou.

I have a few Chinese friends.

这个东西三十多块钱。

Zhège dōngxi sānshí duō kuài qián.

This thing costs thirty-something kuài.

我等了一个多小时。

Wǒ děng le yí ge duō xiǎoshí.

I waited for more than an hour.

多 after round ten
三十多个人
More than 30 people (31–39).
多 after number+measure
三个多小时
More than 3 hours (3 + part of another).

Practice

Fill the blank: 我们班有___学生。
(We have 20-something students.)

二十多(个) — 多 after the round ten. Also accepts 二十来 ("roughly twenty").

"I've been here for more than 2 years." Which is natural?

  • 我在这儿两年多了。✓ 多 slides in after 年 (more than 2, into the 3rd).
  • 我在这儿多两年了。✗ 多 must follow the measure, not precede it.
  • 我在这儿两多年了。✗ 多 goes after the measure 年, not before it (for non-round numbers).

Fill the blank: 我有___个中国朋友。
(I have a few Chinese friends.)

几 — before a measure word means "a few" (a vague small number, 2–9).

What does this sentence mean?
他三十多岁了。

  • He is exactly 30 years old.✗ 多 makes it approximate, not exact.
  • He is thirty-something years old.✓ 多 after the round ten = "more than 30" (31–39).
  • He is more than 300 years old.✗ 三十 is thirty, and 多 means "and a bit more", not multiplying.

Which correctly says "more than 5 people"?

  • 五个多人✓ For non-round numbers, 多 goes after the measure word (个).
  • 五多个人✗ 多 cannot sit between 五 and the measure word when it's not a round ten.
  • 多五个人✗ 多 follows the number/measure, not precedes it.
