What you'll learn

  1. V 得/不 + complement — the "can/can't + achieve result" pattern.
  2. N + 呢? — a super-short way to ask where is X?.
  3. 刚 vs 刚才 — two words for "just now", used differently.
  4. 越来越 + Adj / mental verb — "more and more…" from the lesson title.

1. V 得/不 + complement Can you succeed at that verb?

When a speaker reaches for this: to say whether an action can successfully produce its result. Not "do I know how to do it" (会) or "do I have the right to do it" (能) — but "if I try, will it work out?" English: can I make it out?, can you see clearly?, can you hear?

The English trap

English uses can for many senses. Chinese carves them out: 会 (know how), 能 (allowed / able in circumstance), and this structure (will the attempt succeed?). The marker 得 (positive) or 不 (negative) sits inside the verb+complement: 看得懂 "can read/understand it" / 看不懂 "can't read/understand it".

V + 得 / 不 + Complement (result / direction / adj)

我看得懂那个汉字。

Wǒ kàn de dǒng nàge Hànzì.

I / look / CAN / understand / that character

I can read (understand) that character.

我找不到好饭馆。

Wǒ zhǎo bu dào hǎo fànguǎn.

I / look-for / CAN'T / reach / good restaurant

I can't find a good restaurant (despite trying).

老师说的话你听得见听不见？

Lǎoshī shuō de huà nǐ tīng de jiàn tīng bu jiàn?

Can you hear what the teacher is saying? (The "A-not-A" question form: V得C V不C?)

会 — skill
我会游泳
I know how to swim (I was taught).
V得/不+C — success
我游得过去
I can make it across (I'll succeed if I try).

Decision tree: which "can"?

Ask this in order

  1. Is it a learned skill? Use 会: 我会游泳 = I know how to swim.
  2. Is it permission, time, physical condition, or circumstance? Use 能: 今天我不能去 = I can't go today.
  3. Is it whether this attempt reaches its result? Use V得/不+C: 听得懂, 找不到, 写得完.

Practice

Fill: 你说话说得太快了，我 ___ 。
(You're speaking too fast — I can't follow.)

听不懂 — "listen-can't-understand".

Fill: 这本书的汉字不难，我 ___ 。
(The characters in this book aren't hard — I can read them.)

看得懂 — "look-can-understand".

Fill: 我的手机 ___ 了，你看见了吗？
(I can't find my phone — have you seen it?)

找不到 — "look-can't-reach" = can't find.

Which expresses "I can finish the homework before 7"?

  • 7点以前写得完。✓ V得完 = can finish (success complement).
  • 7点以前会写完。✗ 会 is "know how" — not what we want here.
  • 7点以前写完不。✗ 不 goes between the verb and complement: 写不完.

What does this mean?
饭太多了，我吃不完。

  • I don't know how to eat.✗ That would be 我不会吃.
  • There's too much food — I can't finish it.✓ 吃不完 = can't finish (even if I try).
  • I'm not allowed to eat.✗ That would use 不能.

Build: "I can understand what the teacher says."

我 听得懂 老师 说的话

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about what you can and can't actually pull off — following someone on the phone, making out a sign, finishing a portion, locating an address, hearing someone across a room. Use V 得 + complement at least twice and V 不 + complement at least twice, across four different verbs, and finish with one A-not-A question (V得C V不C?) you could really put to somebody today.

我学中文两年了，现在中文的电视节目我听得懂一半。可是打电话的时候我还是听不懂，因为看不见说话的人。上个星期我一个人去买东西，那家店的名字太小，我看不清楚，走了半个小时才找到。我妈妈做的菜太多了，我一个人吃不完，所以都带回家。你今天的工作七点以前做得完做不完？

Wǒ xué Zhōngwén liǎng nián le, xiànzài Zhōngwén de diànshì jiémù wǒ tīng de dǒng yíbàn. Kěshì dǎ diànhuà de shíhou wǒ háishi tīng bu dǒng, yīnwèi kàn bu jiàn shuōhuà de rén. Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ yí ge rén qù mǎi dōngxi, nà jiā diàn de míngzi tài xiǎo, wǒ kàn bu qīngchu, zǒu le bàn ge xiǎoshí cái zhǎodào. Wǒ māma zuò de cài tài duō le, wǒ yí ge rén chī bu wán, suǒyǐ dōu dài huí jiā. Nǐ jīntiān de gōngzuò qī diǎn yǐqián zuò de wán zuò bu wán?

I've been studying Chinese for two years, and I can now follow half of a Chinese TV programme. On the phone I still can't follow it, though, because I can't see the person speaking. Last week I went shopping on my own; the shop's name was too small for me to make out, and I walked for half an hour before I found it. My mum cooks far too much — I can't finish it on my own, so I take it home. Can you get today's work done before seven, or not?

Why these are right: the 得/不 lives inside the verb+complement pair (听得懂 · 看不见 · 吃不完), never in front of the verb and never doubled up with 能 or 会. Note 看不见 in a past-tense story and 看不清楚 too — this negative is always 不, even about yesterday, because it is about capability, not about something that failed to happen. The A-not-A question repeats the whole pair twice, so no 吗 is needed.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the marker sandwiched between verb and complement? 听得懂 ✓ · 得听懂 / 不听懂 ✗
  • Negative is 不, never 没 — 昨天我没找到那家店 is a different sentence ("didn't find it"), not "couldn't find it".
  • Don't stack the other "cans": 我能听得懂 ✗ — pick 能 or the complement, not both.
  • Four different verbs, at least two positive and two negative — the pattern only sticks if the verb keeps moving.
  • In the A-not-A question, is the complement repeated on both halves (做得完做不完), with no 吗 at the end?

2. N + 呢? A two-character shortcut: "where's X?"

When a speaker reaches for this: to ask where something or someone is, without the full sentence. English equivalents: where's Mom?, and the dog?. It's casual and efficient.

The English trap

You might want to say the full 妈妈在哪儿？ — which is fine — but natives often drop it to 妈妈呢？ in context. Same meaning, far more natural conversationally.

Noun + 呢 ?

A: 你的书呢? 放在哪儿了？
B: 就在桌子上啊。

A: Nǐ de shū ne? Fàng zài nǎr le? / B: Jiù zài zhuōzi shang a.

A: Where's your book? Where did you put it? / B: Right on the table.

A: 我的手机呢? 你看见了吗？
B: 我没看见啊。

A: Wǒ de shǒujī ne? Nǐ kànjiàn le ma? / B: Wǒ méi kànjiàn a.

A: Where's my phone? Have you seen it? / B: I haven't.

Practice

Complete: A: ___？ B: 在我包里。
(Where's your phone? — In my bag.)

Complete: A: 妈妈在厨房做饭， ___？ B: 他还没回来。
(Mom's cooking — and Dad? — He's not back yet.)

爸爸呢 — shortcut for "where's Dad?".

Which is the most natural casual way to ask "Where's the dog?"

  • 小狗呢？✓ Noun + 呢 is the casual shortcut.
  • 小狗吗？✗ 吗 turns it into yes/no, not "where".
  • 哪儿小狗？✗ Wrong word order.

What does this mean?
你的裙子呢？

  • Is this your skirt?✗ That would use 吗.
  • Where's your skirt?✓ Noun + 呢 = "Where's X?" in context.
  • Do you like the skirt?✗ Completely different meaning.

Build: "Where's my book?"

我 的 书 呢

Build: "Where's your cold medicine?"

你 的 感冒药 呢

Personalise it. Write a short exchange of 5–6 lines that could really happen where you live or work this week — somebody is hunting for things that actually go missing in your life. Ask three times with Noun + 呢?, using three different nouns (at least one of them a person), and write a real reply after each question, not just the questions.

A: 我的词典呢？我记得放在书包里了。
B: 不在书包里，是不是在教室的桌子上？
A: 找到了，谢谢。小刚呢？他不是说八点来吗？
B: 他刚给我打电话，说路上人太多，要晚一点儿到。
A: 那我们的票呢？
B: 票在我这儿，你放心吧。

A: Wǒ de cídiǎn ne? Wǒ jìde fàng zài shūbāo li le. B: Bú zài shūbāo li, shì bu shì zài jiàoshì de zhuōzi shang? A: Zhǎodào le, xièxie. Xiǎogāng ne? Tā bú shì shuō bā diǎn lái ma? B: Tā gāng gěi wǒ dǎ diànhuà, shuō lùshang rén tài duō, yào wǎn yìdiǎnr dào. A: Nà wǒmen de piào ne? B: Piào zài wǒ zhèr, nǐ fàngxīn ba.

A: Where's my dictionary? I remember putting it in my bag. B: It's not in the bag — could it be on the desk in the classroom? A: Found it, thanks. And Xiaogang? Didn't he say he'd come at eight? B: He just rang me — the roads are packed, he'll be a bit late. A: And our tickets? B: I've got them, don't worry.

Why these are right: 呢 goes straight onto the bare noun phrase and nothing else — no verb, no 在, no 哪儿. It works because the conversation has already supplied the situation: with 小刚呢？the answer is about when he'll turn up, with 票呢？it's about who has them. That is the whole point of the shortcut — 呢 asks "what's the story with X?", and context decides what counts as an answer.

Check your own exchange:

  • Is 呢 immediately after the noun phrase, ending the question? 我的词典呢？ ✓ · 我的词典在哪儿呢？ is a full question, not this shortcut.
  • Not 吗: 我的票吗？ asks "is it my ticket?", never "where is it?"
  • Does the line before it make the question answerable? Out of nowhere, 词典呢？leaves the other person guessing.
  • Three different nouns, and one of them a person — 呢 handles people just as happily as objects.
  • Did you actually write the replies? The shortcut only lives in dialogue.

3. 刚 vs 刚才 Two "just nows" — one's an adverb, one's a noun

When a speaker reaches for this: to say something happened recently. But "recently" is flexible — 刚才 means literally a few minutes ago, while 刚 can mean anything from minutes to months, depending on the speaker's sense of "short".

The English trap

English smashes both senses into just. Chinese separates them:

  • 刚 (adverb) — sits only right before a verb. "I've only been in China for 2 months" = 我刚来中国两个月.
  • 刚才 (noun) — literally "just-now moment", can sit before the verb or at the start of the sentence. "A moment ago you were doing homework" = 刚才你在做作业.
刚 — subjective "a short time"
我刚来中国两个月
"I've only been in China two months" — the speaker feels two months is short.
刚才 — literally a few minutes ago
刚才你在做作业
"A moment ago you were doing homework" — strictly recent.

我刚做完作业，真累啊！

Wǒ gāng zuò wán zuòyè, zhēn lèi a!

I've just finished the homework — I'm exhausted!

刚才你去哪儿了？

Gāngcái nǐ qù nǎr le?

Where did you go a moment ago?

Common pitfall

Don't put 刚 at the start of a sentence — *刚你去哪儿了 (✗) is wrong. It must attach right before a verb. If you want to front-load the time, use 刚才.

Practice

Pick the correct word: ___ 我做完作业，真累啊！

  • 刚✗ 刚 must come directly before the verb, not at the start of the sentence.
  • 刚才✓ 刚才 can begin a sentence; 刚 cannot.

Fill with 刚 or 刚才: 他 ___ 到北京，还没休息呢。

刚 — sits right before the verb 到 (to arrive); could be minutes or hours, felt as recent.

Fill with 刚 or 刚才: ___ 你在做什么？我叫你，你没听见。
(What were you doing a moment ago? I called you and you didn't hear.)

刚才 — fronted time word; strictly "a few minutes ago".

Fill with 刚 or 刚才: 我 ___ 来中国两个月，汉语还不太好。
(I've only been in China two months — my Chinese isn't great yet.)

刚 — the speaker feels two months is short; 刚 attaches directly before the verb 来.

What does this mean?
刚才我看见小丽了。

  • I always see Xiaoli.✗ 刚才 is specifically "a moment ago".
  • I saw Xiaoli a moment ago.✓ 刚才 = a few minutes ago (fronted).
  • I just started to see Xiaoli.✗ Not the meaning.

Build: "He just arrived in Beijing."

他 刚 到 北京 了

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences covering both your last hour and your last few months. Use 刚才 twice — once at the very front of a sentence, the slot 刚 can never take — and use 刚 twice, each time glued directly in front of a verb. One 刚 sentence must be about a stretch of time you consider short: a job, a city, a class, a hobby you have started.

刚才我在楼下喝咖啡，一边喝一边看手机。我刚回到办公室，同事就问我下午开不开会。刚才有人给我打电话，可是我没听见。我刚开始学跳舞，已经三个月了，还跳得不太好，可是我越来越喜欢了。

Gāngcái wǒ zài lóuxià hē kāfēi, yìbiān hē yìbiān kàn shǒujī. Wǒ gāng huí dào bàngōngshì, tóngshì jiù wèn wǒ xiàwǔ kāi bu kāihuì. Gāngcái yǒu rén gěi wǒ dǎ diànhuà, kěshì wǒ méi tīngjiàn. Wǒ gāng kāishǐ xué tiàowǔ, yǐjīng sān ge yuè le, hái tiào de bú tài hǎo, kěshì wǒ yuè lái yuè xǐhuan le.

A few minutes ago I was downstairs having a coffee, drinking and looking at my phone at the same time. I'd only just got back to the office when a colleague asked me whether there's a meeting this afternoon. Somebody rang me a moment ago but I didn't hear it. I've only just started learning to dance — it's been three months now — and I still don't dance very well, but I like it more and more.

Why these are right: 刚才 is a time noun, so it can stand at the head of the sentence (刚才我在楼下…) or after the subject, and it means literally a few minutes back. 刚 is an adverb: it has exactly one slot, immediately in front of its verb (刚回到 · 刚开始), and "short" is the speaker's judgement — three months of dance classes still counts as 刚开始 for someone who expects to keep at it for years.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is every 刚 directly in front of a verb, with nothing wedged in? 我刚回到办公室 ✓ · 刚我回到办公室 ✗
  • Is at least one 刚才 at the very start of its sentence? That position is the clearest proof you have the two words apart.
  • 刚才 = minutes. If you mean "over these past months", you want 刚 (or 最近), not 刚才.
  • 刚才-sentences usually carry 了 or 没 — they report a moment that is already over.
  • Is the 刚 stretch honestly short for you? That judgement is what the word is really encoding.

4. 越来越 + Adj / mental verb "More and more…" — the title grammar

When a speaker reaches for this: to describe a trend that keeps increasing over time. The lesson title 我最近越来越胖了 does not mean "I am very fat"; it means the degree is moving upward lately.

The English trap

English often says "getting more and more..." with a separate verb like getting. Chinese puts the trend marker directly before the adjective or mental verb: 越来越胖, 越来越喜欢. Do not add 很 after it: *越来越很胖 is wrong.

Subject / topic + 越来越 + Adj / mental verb + 了

我最近越来越胖了。

Wǒ zuìjìn yuè lái yuè pàng le.

I / lately / more-and-more / fat / LE

I've been getting fatter and fatter lately.

我越来越喜欢夏天了。

Wǒ yuè lái yuè xǐhuan xiàtiān le.

I / more-and-more / like / summer / LE

I like summer more and more now.

Why final 了 often appears

越来越 already says the degree is changing. Sentence-final 了 fits naturally because the speaker is noticing a new trend: before it was less true, now it is more true.

Practice

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about how somebody or somewhere else in your life is changing — a brother or sister, a colleague, a neighbour, the street or park nearest you. Use 越来越 + Adj at least twice with two different adjectives, use it once with a mental verb (喜欢 · 想 · 怕), and end at least one sentence with the change 了.

我弟弟今年上大学，越来越高，说话也越来越像我爸爸。我的同事最近越来越忙，中午常常在办公室吃东西。我家旁边的公园越来越干净了，周末很多人带着孩子在那儿玩。我妈妈越来越喜欢跟我聊天，一个星期给我打三次电话。

Wǒ dìdi jīnnián shàng dàxué, yuè lái yuè gāo, shuōhuà yě yuè lái yuè xiàng wǒ bàba. Wǒ de tóngshì zuìjìn yuè lái yuè máng, zhōngwǔ chángcháng zài bàngōngshì chī dōngxi. Wǒ jiā pángbiān de gōngyuán yuè lái yuè gānjìng le, zhōumò hěn duō rén dàizhe háizi zài nàr wán. Wǒ māma yuè lái yuè xǐhuan gēn wǒ liáotiān, yí ge xīngqī gěi wǒ dǎ sān cì diànhuà.

My younger brother started university this year — he's getting taller and taller, and he sounds more and more like my dad. My colleague has been busier and busier lately; at midday she often eats at her desk. The park next to my place is getting cleaner and cleaner, and at the weekend a lot of people bring their children there to play. My mum likes talking to me more and more — she rings me three times a week.

Why these are right: 越来越 needs something with a scale to climb, so 高／忙／干净 all work bare — and bare is the point, because 越来越 is itself the degree word and leaves no room for 很. 越来越像 and 越来越喜欢 show the same slot taking a verb, with the object trailing behind as usual. The 了 in 越来越干净了 is optional: without it you state the trend, with it you report having noticed it.

Check your own sentences:

  • Bare adjective after 越来越: 越来越忙 ✓ · 越来越很忙 ✗
  • 越来越 stands before what it intensifies: 忙越来越 ✗
  • Is what follows gradable? Something can be 越来越忙, but nothing is 越来越是老师 — there's no scale there.
  • Mental verb keeps its object in the normal place: 越来越喜欢跟我聊天 ✓
  • Any 了 sits at the very end of its clause — and only if this really is news compared with a while ago.

Mixed practice All four structures together

Fill: 我最近 ___ 胖了，应该少吃点儿。
(I've been getting fatter lately — I should eat less.)

越来越 — "more and more" before an adjective.

Which sentence is grammatical?

  • 刚你去哪儿了？✗ 刚 cannot start a sentence; use 刚才.
  • 刚才你去哪儿了？✓ 刚才 can sit at the start of a sentence.
  • 你刚才去哪儿？✗ Missing 了 for past event — awkward without it in this context.

What does this mean?
春天来了，天气越来越暖和了。

  • Spring's coming — the weather is cold.✗ 暖和 means warm.
  • Spring has come — the weather is getting warmer and warmer.✓ 越来越 + Adj = increasing change.
  • Spring will come — the weather might get warmer.✗ 了 here marks a change already happening.

Write the paragraph. The first evening of a class you have just started — a language class, a cooking class, a dance class, whatever fits your life. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all four structures of this lesson at least once each: V 得/不 + complement, Noun + 呢?, 刚 or 刚才, and 越来越 + Adj. Cover: when you started, what you could not manage on the first evening, a question you asked the person next to you, and how it is going now.

我刚开始上晚上的中文课，一个星期两次。第一天我到得太晚，老师说的话我一句也听不懂，只听得懂“你好”。“刚才用的那本书呢？”我问旁边的同学，她笑着给了我一本。下课以后我们一起走到地铁站，她说她刚来纽约三个月，也常常听不懂别人说话。现在我上了六次课，老师说的话越来越听得懂了，我也越来越喜欢星期三的晚上了。

Wǒ gāng kāishǐ shàng wǎnshang de Zhōngwén kè, yí ge xīngqī liǎng cì. Dì-yī tiān wǒ dào de tài wǎn, lǎoshī shuō de huà wǒ yí jù yě tīng bu dǒng, zhǐ tīng de dǒng "nǐ hǎo". "Gāngcái yòng de nà běn shū ne?" wǒ wèn pángbiān de tóngxué, tā xiàozhe gěi le wǒ yì běn. Xiàkè yǐhòu wǒmen yìqǐ zǒu dào dìtiězhàn, tā shuō tā gāng lái Niǔyuē sān ge yuè, yě chángcháng tīng bu dǒng biérén shuōhuà. Xiànzài wǒ shàng le liù cì kè, lǎoshī shuō de huà wǒ yuè lái yuè tīng de dǒng le, wǒ yě yuè lái yuè xǐhuan xīngqīsān de wǎnshang le.

I've only just started an evening Chinese class, twice a week. On the first day I arrived too late, couldn't understand a single thing the teacher said, and only caught "hello". "The book we were using a minute ago?" I asked the classmate next to me, and she smiled and handed me one. After class we walked to the subway station together; she said she'd only been in New York three months and often can't follow people either. Now I've had six classes, I can understand more and more of what the teacher says, and I like Wednesday evenings more and more.

Why this works as a paragraph: the four structures are doing four different jobs. 听不懂／听得懂 mark the edge of what you could actually achieve on the night; 那本书呢？is the fastest possible way to ask a stranger for something, and it works precisely because you are both staring at the same book; 刚开始 and 刚来 date two beginnings the speakers both feel are recent; and 越来越听得懂了 measures the distance travelled since. The time markers (第一天 · 下课以后 · 现在) hold it together.

Check your own paragraph:

  • V得/不+C: is the marker inside the pair (听不懂), and did you use 不 rather than 没 even for that first evening?
  • 呢: bare noun phrase + 呢, and is there enough context for it to be answerable?
  • 刚 right in front of its verb; 刚才 only for something minutes old.
  • 越来越 + bare adjective or verb, no 很 — and it needs a stretch of time behind it, which the class gives you.
  • Does it read as one evening's story, joined by time words, rather than four grammar demos in a row?

Four errors. Each sentence below contains exactly one mistake, and all four are mistakes with this lesson's structures. Rewrite all four correctly in the box, then check.
1. 这本书太难了，我不看懂。
2. 妈妈在家，爸爸吗？
3. 刚我到公司，就有人找我。
4. 我最近忙越来越了。

1. 这本书太难了，我看不懂。
2. 妈妈在家，爸爸呢？
3. 我刚到公司，就有人找我。
4. 我最近越来越忙了。

1. Zhè běn shū tài nán le, wǒ kàn bu dǒng. 2. Māma zài jiā, bàba ne? 3. Wǒ gāng dào gōngsī, jiù yǒu rén zhǎo wǒ. 4. Wǒ zuìjìn yuè lái yuè máng le.

1. This book is too hard — I can't read it. 2. Mum's at home; and Dad? 3. I'd only just got to the office when somebody came looking for me. 4. I've been getting busier and busier lately.

  1. 不看懂 → 看不懂. English puts "can't" in front of the verb, so learners put 不 there too. In this pattern the marker is an infix: it sits between the verb and its complement. 不看懂 would be trying to negate the act of reading; 看不懂 negates the result — you looked, and understanding did not arrive.
  2. 爸爸吗 → 爸爸呢. 吗 makes a yes/no question out of the noun ("is it Dad?"). The "and what about X?" / "where's X?" shortcut is 呢, and it needs exactly this kind of set-up: one person has just been placed, so the listener knows what is being asked about the next one.
  3. 刚我到 → 我刚到. 刚 is an adverb with one legal position — immediately before its verb — so it cannot open a sentence. If you want the time word out in front, the word for that job is 刚才.
  4. 忙越来越了 → 越来越忙了. English builds "busier and busier" out of the adjective, so the trend word drifts to the end. In Chinese 越来越 is a pre-modifier: it goes in front of the adjective, and nothing else may join it there (越来越很忙 ✗).

Check your own rewrites:

  • Is 不 inside the verb+complement pair, not in front of the verb?
  • Did you swap 吗 for 呢 rather than adding 在哪儿 and rebuilding the whole question?
  • Is 刚 sitting immediately before its verb, after the subject?
  • Is 越来越 in front of the adjective, with the change 了 at the end and no 很 anywhere near?
  • Did you retype the whole sentence each time? Naming the fix is not the same as producing the sentence.
