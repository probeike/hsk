Appendix A

语气词

Yǔqìcí — "mood words"

Discourse particles: 吧, 啊, tag-呢, 啦, 好吧, 是吗

What this is

The 20 lessons teach grammar. This appendix covers the tiny sentence-final syllables that carry attitude — softening, checking, exclaiming, redirecting, conceding. English speakers routinely leave these off (because English has no equivalent), and their Chinese comes out sounding flat, blunt, or textbook-robotic. Each particle has a specific emotional job; learn the job, not a translation.

  1. 吧 — soften a suggestion, a command, or a guess.
  2. 啊 — agree, exclaim, or warm up the sentence.
  3. Tag-呢 — "and you?" / "where's…?" — NOT the location 呢.
  4. 啦 — casual 了+啊 contour, breezy completion.
  5. 好吧 — reluctant "fine, okay."
  6. 是吗? — recognition marker ("oh really?"), not a real question.
  7. Mixed practice

1. 吧 Suggestion, softener, or "…right?" check

When a speaker reaches for this: to take the sharp edge off a sentence. Three flavours: (a) proposing something together — "let's…"; (b) softening a command — "go on, do it" instead of barked orders; (c) checking an assumption — "you're a teacher, right?" The sentence would still be grammatical without 吧 — it would just sound bossy, blunt, or cold.

The English trap

English speakers' brain reaches for the bare imperative ("Close the window.") or a question ("Are you a teacher?"). Chinese has both of those, but everyday speech softens most of them with 吧. Without 吧, 关窗户 sounds like a military order; 你是老师 sounds like a flat accusation. Add 吧 and it becomes conversational.

Flavour 1 — Suggestion: "let's…" / "why don't you…"

Subj + V + … + 吧

那喝杯水吧。

Nà hē bēi shuǐ ba.

in-that-case / drink / CL / water / BA

Here, have a glass of water then.

我们走吧。

Wǒmen zǒu ba.

we / go / BA

Let's go.

Flavour 2 — Softened command

把空调关了吧。

Bǎ kōngtiáo guān le ba.

BA / AC / turn-off / PERF / BA

Go on, turn off the AC.

Flavour 3 — "…right?" assumption check

When you already believe something is true but want confirmation. The voice falls gently, not rising like a real question.

你是老师吧?

Nǐ shì lǎoshī ba?

you / are / teacher / BA

You're a teacher, right?

Suggestion 吧
我们走吧。
"Let's go." — the speaker is proposing the action.
Assumption-check 吧
你要走吧?
"You're heading off, right?" — the speaker already thinks so and wants a nod.

Common pitfall

Don't use 吧 with 吗. They're competing particles: 吗 makes a real yes/no question; 吧 means you've already half-guessed. 你是老师吗? = "Are you a teacher?" (no assumption). 你是老师吧? = "You're a teacher, right?" (assumption). Never 你是老师吧吗?

Practice

Which sounds most natural when proposing a plan to a friend?

  • 我们去看电影。✗ Grammatical but flat — sounds like you're announcing a fact, not proposing.
  • 我们去看电影吧。✓ 吧 turns it into "let's go see a movie" — a warm suggestion.
  • 我们去看电影吗?✗ 吗 makes it a yes/no question, which sounds odd for a proposal.

Fill the blank: 你是王老师___? 我见过你的照片。
(You're Teacher Wang, right? I've seen your photo.)

吧 — the speaker already suspects it's Teacher Wang. 吗 would mean no guess at all.

What does the speaker mean? 把空调关了吧。

  • Did you turn off the AC?✗ This is not a question; 吧 softens an instruction, it doesn't ask.
  • Go on, turn off the AC.✓ 吧 softens a command — a gentle nudge rather than a bark.
  • The AC is off, right?✗ That would be 空调关了吧? with rising intonation.

Build: "Let's go tomorrow."

我们 明天 去 吧

You meet someone at a party and are pretty sure he's from Beijing. Which is the natural way to check?

  • 你是北京人吗?✗ This is a neutral yes/no — it sounds like you have no idea, which contradicts the setup.
  • 你是北京人吧?✓ 吧 signals "I think so — confirm?"
  • 你是北京人。✗ Flat declarative — sounds like you're labelling him, not asking.

2. 啊 Emotive — agreement, exclamation, warmth

When a speaker reaches for this: they want to emote. 啊 is a warmth tag: it turns a cold "yes" into a hearty "yeah!", a bare exclamation into a genuine one, and a plain vocative into a friendly one. It has no meaning of its own — it's pure emotional colour.

The English trap

English speakers' brain reaches for bare replies: "Right." "Yes." "So pretty." In Chinese, those come out as 对, 是, 真漂亮 — and they sound curt, even dismissive. Add 啊 and the same words become warm, engaged, human: 对啊, 是啊, 真漂亮啊.

Flavour 1 — Agreement / affirmation

A: 今天真冷。 B: 对啊!

A: Jīntiān zhēn lěng. / B: Duì a!

A: today / really / cold / B: right / A

A: It's really cold today. / B: Yeah, totally!

Flavour 2 — Exclamation

Often with 多 ("how…") or 真 ("really"):

多漂亮啊!

Duō piàoliang a!

how / pretty / A

How pretty!

这个菜真好吃啊!

Zhège cài zhēn hǎochī a!

this / dish / really / tasty / A

This dish is delicious!

Flavour 3 — Vocative hook

Attached to a name or address term to get attention warmly — like "hey" prefixed to someone's name.

小刚啊, 你过来一下。

Xiǎo Gāng a, nǐ guòlái yíxià.

Xiao Gang / A, / you / come-over / a-bit

Hey Xiao Gang, come over for a sec.

Without 啊
对。
"Right." Curt. Sounds like you're closing the conversation.
With 啊
对啊!
"Yeah, totally!" Engaged, warm — the conversation keeps breathing.

Common pitfall

啊 can't carry propositional content on its own — it needs a sentence to attach to. And don't stack it with 吗 (the question particle) or 吧 (the softener). Each of those slots takes exactly one particle.

Practice

Fill the blank: 这个孩子多可爱___!
(How cute this child is!)

啊 — the emotive tag at the end of an exclamation. Required for the sentence to feel genuine.

Your friend says 今天真累。 Which reply sounds warm and engaged?

  • 是。✗ Grammatical but cold — it sounds like you're barely listening.
  • 是啊!✓ 啊 makes the agreement warm and human: "Yeah, right?!"
  • 是吗?✗ 是吗? questions what they said — implies you don't agree or are surprised.

What role is 啊 playing here? 小李啊, 你来一下。

  • It turns the sentence into a question.✗ 啊 is not a question particle.
  • It's a warm vocative hook — "Hey Xiao Li…"✓ 啊 after a name softens the call and makes it friendly.
  • It means "ah, I remember now".✗ That's an interjection in initial position (啊, 我想起来了); here 啊 is sentence-medial warmth.

Build: "This place is really pretty!" (with emotional warmth)

这个 地方 真 漂亮 啊

3. Tag-呢 "And you?" / "Where's…?" — NOT the location 呢

When a speaker reaches for this: (a) to redirect a question back — "and you?"; (b) to ask "where is X?" when X is missing and presumed-known — "my keys?"; (c) as a gentle "so what about…?" pivot. It's the conversational handoff — a way to pass the mic or register a missing thing.

The English trap

English speakers see 呢 and think "that's the location 呢 from Lesson 5 — 书在桌子上呢." Wrong one. This 呢 is a tag particle that attaches to a noun phrase alone (no verb) and does one of two jobs: redirect or check on a missing thing. Your English brain reaches for a full question — "and how about you?" — but Chinese does it with just 你呢?

Flavour 1 — "And you?" redirect

NP + 呢 ?

A: 我很好。你呢?

A: Wǒ hěn hǎo. Nǐ ne?

I / very / well. / you / NE

A: I'm doing well. And you?

A: 我喜欢喝茶。你呢?

A: Wǒ xǐhuan hē chá. Nǐ ne?

I / like / drink / tea. / you / NE

A: I like tea. What about you?

Flavour 2 — "Where's my X?" check

A noun + 呢 with nothing else means "where is this thing I expected to be here?" — you're flagging its absence.

我的钥匙呢?

Wǒ de yàoshi ne?

my / keys / NE

Where are my keys? (They were here a second ago.)

小王呢? 他怎么还没来?

Xiǎo Wáng ne? Tā zěnme hái méi lái?

Xiao Wang / NE? / he / why / still / not / come

Where's Xiao Wang? How come he's not here yet?

Tag-呢 (this appendix)
书呢?
"Where's the book?" — NP + 呢, no verb. Flagging a missing thing.
Location-呢 (Lesson 5)
书在桌子上呢。
"The book's on the table (as you can see)." — full sentence + 呢, softens the informative tone.

Common pitfall

Don't mix tag-呢 with 吗. Tag-呢 already is the question — it stands in for "and how about…?" or "where is…?" Adding 吗 doubles up and sounds wrong: 你呢吗?

Practice

You've answered "How are you?" and want to redirect. Which is right?

  • 你吗?✗ 吗 doesn't attach to a bare pronoun like this.
  • 你呢?✓ Tag-呢 redirects: "and you?"
  • 你吧?✗ 吧 is for assumption-checking, not redirecting a question.

Fill the blank: 我的手机___? 刚才还在桌子上。
(Where's my phone? It was on the table just now.)

呢 — NP + 呢 flags a missing thing you expected to find. 吗 wouldn't work: it needs a full yes/no question.

What does the speaker mean? 妈妈呢?

  • Are you Mom?✗ That would be 你是妈妈吗?
  • Where's Mom? (She's expected to be here.)✓ NP + 呢 = "where is the person/thing I was expecting?"
  • Mom is here.✗ That would be 妈妈在这儿 or 妈妈在这儿呢 (location-呢).

Build: "I like coffee. And you?"

我 喜欢 咖啡 你 呢

Which 呢 is the tag-呢 (not the location-呢)?

  • 他在家里呢。✗ Full sentence + 呢 — that's the softening location-呢 from Lesson 5.
  • 他呢?✓ NP + 呢 with no verb — checking on a missing person. Tag-呢.
  • 她在看书呢。✗ Full progressive sentence + 呢 — again, sentence-final softener, not the tag use.

4. 啦 The breezy 了+啊 contour

When a speaker reaches for this: to close a sentence with both completion (了) and emotional flavour (啊) at once — casually, in one syllable. 啦 = 了 + 啊 fused. Think of it as the upbeat, conversational "…and that's that!"

The English trap

English speakers often write 了啊 as two syllables. Native speakers usually collapse it to 啦 in speech and informal writing. It's not a new particle — it's a phonetic shortcut. Use 啦 when the speech register is relaxed and the tone is upbeat.

我来啦!

Wǒ lái la!

I / come / LA (= 了+啊)

I'm here! / I'm coming!

别担心, 没事啦。

Bié dānxīn, méi shì la.

don't / worry, / no / matter / LA

Don't worry about it, it's all fine.

Practice

What is 啦 roughly equivalent to?

  • 吗 + 啊✗ 吗 turns a sentence into a question; 啦 doesn't do that.
  • 了 + 啊 fused into one syllable✓ Completion + emotional warmth, casually merged.
  • 吧 + 啊✗ 吧 is for softening/checking; that's a different function.

Fill the blank (casual tone): 好___, 别生气了。
(Alright, alright — don't be mad.)

啦 — a breezy 了+啊 contour, softening and de-escalating.

5. 好吧 Reluctant assent — "fine, okay"

When a speaker reaches for this: they're conceding — agreeing to something they'd rather not, or accepting a plan with mild resignation. The 好 says "okay", the 吧 says "I suppose". Together: "…fine."

The English trap

English speakers reach for 好 alone to mean "okay" — but bare 好 is enthusiastic agreement ("Great!"). If you're grudgingly giving in, you want 好吧. Leaving off 吧 makes it sound like you're thrilled — which reads as sarcasm when the context is "I'd rather not."

A: 今天我不想做饭, 我们出去吃吧。 B: 好吧。

A: Jīntiān wǒ bù xiǎng zuò fàn, wǒmen chūqù chī ba. / B: Hǎo ba.

A: today / I / not / want / cook, / we / go-out / eat / BA. / B: fine / BA

A: I don't feel like cooking, let's eat out. / B: Fine, okay.

好吧, 我跟你一起去。

Hǎo ba, wǒ gēn nǐ yìqǐ qù.

fine / BA, / I / with / you / together / go

Okay, fine — I'll go with you.

Practice

Your friend asks you to help move furniture on your day off. You don't really want to, but you agree. What do you say?

  • 好!✗ Bare 好 sounds enthusiastic — which doesn't match your actual feeling.
  • 好吧。✓ 吧 adds the "I suppose…" reluctance. Honest and natural.
  • 好啊!✗ 好啊 is warm agreement — even more enthusiastic than 好 alone.

What's the feeling behind 好吧?

  • Excited agreement.✗ That's 好啊! or 太好了!
  • Reluctant or resigned agreement.✓ 好吧 = "fine, I suppose." The 吧 softens the 好 with concession.
  • A real question.✗ 好吧 is a response, not a question.

6. 是吗? Recognition marker — "oh really?"

When a speaker reaches for this: the other person has just told them something mildly surprising or newly-recognised, and they're registering it. It looks like a yes/no question ("Is it?") but it's not — it's the Chinese equivalent of "oh really?" or "huh, is that so?"

The English trap

English speakers parse 是吗? literally as "Is it?" and expect an answer. Native speakers say 是吗? to acknowledge information and keep the conversation flowing — no answer required. If you do answer, you've misread the move.

A: 他就是小刚啊。 B: 是吗? 我没认出来。

A: Tā jiù shì Xiǎo Gāng a. / B: Shì ma? Wǒ méi rèn chūlái.

A: he / exactly / is / Xiao Gang / A. / B: SHI-MA? / I / not / recognize-out

A: That's Xiao Gang, actually. / B: Really? I didn't recognize him!

A: 我明天要去北京。 B: 是吗? 出差吗?

A: Wǒ míngtiān yào qù Běijīng. / B: Shì ma? Chūchāi ma?

A: I / tomorrow / will / go / Beijing. / B: SHI-MA? / business-trip / MA

A: I'm going to Beijing tomorrow. / B: Oh really? For business?

Practice

Your friend says 我刚搬家了。 What does 是吗? mean as a reply?

  • A literal yes/no question expecting 是/不是.✗ It looks like one, but it's a discourse move — no answer required.
  • "Oh really?" — registering the news and inviting more.✓ 是吗? is recognition/acknowledgment, not a true question.
  • "Is this right?" — checking the other person for confirmation.✗ That would be 对吗? or 是吧?

A says 她去年结婚了。 B says 是吗? 我不知道! What is B doing?

  • Doubting what A said.✗ Doubting would sound contrarian — 是吗? is neutral-to-interested.
  • Registering surprise — "Oh really? I didn't know!"✓ Classic recognition-marker move.
  • Asking whether marriage is a good idea.✗ Nothing in the sentence is about opinion.

All six particles, in the wild.

Which particle fits? 我们一起去吃饭___。 (proposing a plan)

  • 吗✗ 吗 would make it a yes/no question, not a proposal.
  • 呢✗ Tag-呢 needs a bare NP, not a full verb phrase.

Fill the blank: A: 我喜欢看电影。B: 我也是! 你最喜欢哪种电影___?
(Which kind do you like best?)

呢 — here softening the follow-up question, pivoting to the listener's preference.

What's going on in B's reply? A: 这件衣服二百块。B: 是吗? 有点儿贵啊。

  • B is asking whether the price is correct and waiting for confirmation.✗ 是吗? here is recognition, not a real question. And 啊 at the end is emotive, not interrogative.
  • B is registering the info ("oh really?") and then exclaiming it's expensive.✓ 是吗? = acknowledgment; 啊 = emotional punch on the exclamation.
  • B is making a suggestion.✗ No proposal here — it's reaction + reaction.

你 是 新来的 吧
