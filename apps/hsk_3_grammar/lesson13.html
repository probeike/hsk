What you'll learn

  1. 把字句 3 — the full 把 frame with result or direction complements. This fuses Lessons 10, 11, 12 into one system.
  2. 先 … 再/又 … 然后 … — sequencing multiple steps in order.

1. 把字句 3: A 把 B + V + result/direction complement Disposal + what became of B

When a speaker reaches for this: when they want to say how the object ended up after the action — not just "where" (L11) or "done" (L10), but in what shape. Did you finish writing it (写完)? Did you cook it and have it ready (做好)? Did you bring it back (拿回来)? The 把 frame is where Chinese collects "what happened + what state it's in now".

The cognitive picture — 把 as the full disposal story

Lessons 10-12 were not separate tricks. They were the same 把 engine with different endings:

  • L10: 把 + B + V + 了 → "grab B, do V, done." 我把门关了.
  • L11: 把 + B + V + 在/到 + place → "grab B, move it, and it ended up here." 我把书放在桌子上.
  • L12: 把 + B + V + direction → "grab B, move it, and it ended up toward/away from me." 把水果拿过来.
  • This lesson: 把 + B + V + result or direction → "grab B, do V, and this is the state now." 我把作业写完了 (writing → finished). 他把礼物拿回来了 (taking → brought back here).

Temporal sequence again: grab → act → result/arrival. The sentence walks through the event in order.

The English trap

English fuses result into the verb ("I finished writing", "I brought it back"). Chinese keeps them separate — a plain action verb (写, 拿, 买) plus a complement that declares the outcome (完, 好, 回来, 出去). Your English brain will try to pack it all into one word; resist. Pick the action, then pick the result.

The 把 frame shown as a full timeline. Each slot is one beat of the event.
A + 把 + B + V + result / direction (+ 了 )

Slow parse: result vs direction

我把作业写完了。

  • 我 = actor; 把作业 = the known object we care about.
  • 写 = action; 完 = result, finished; 了 = the result is complete.

请把礼物拿出来。

  • 请 = request; 把礼物 = the known object.
  • 拿 = action; 出来 = direction/result, out toward the speaker.

Same 把 frame, different ending: one says what state the object reached; the other says where it moved relative to the speaker.

With a result complement

我把作业写完了。

Wǒ bǎ zuòyè xiě wán le.

I finished the homework.

他把饭做好了。

Tā bǎ fàn zuò hǎo le.

he / [disposal] / meal / make / to-good / PERF

He got the meal ready.

我真怕他累坏了。

Wǒ zhēn pà tā lèi huài le.

I / really / fear / he / tired / to-broken / PERF

I'm really afraid he'll wear himself out. (累坏 = "tired-to-broken", a resultative pair even without 把 — the object is implicit "himself".)

With a direction complement

Now fold in Lesson 12's compound directionals. Notice: the speaker's orientation (来/去) is still part of the result — it says where B ended up relative to me.

哥哥买回一个西瓜来了。

Gēge mǎi huí yí ge xīguā lai le.

elder-brother / buy / back / one / M / watermelon / come / PERF

Older brother bought a watermelon and brought it home. (No 把 here — B is indefinite "a watermelon", so plain V + direction works.)

请把礼物拿出来。

Qǐng bǎ lǐwù ná chūlai.

please / [disposal] / gift / take / out-come

Please take the gift out (so I can see it).

明天我们一起把礼物送过去。

Míngtiān wǒmen yìqǐ bǎ lǐwù sòng guòqu.

tomorrow / we / together / [disposal] / gift / deliver / across-go

Tomorrow we'll take the gift over (to them) together.

Definite vs indefinite B

Use 把 when B is specific and known (the gift, the computer, the homework). When B is a brand-new indefinite ("a watermelon", "some gifts"), you can skip 把 and put B between the stem and 来/去: 哥哥买回一个西瓜来. Rule of thumb: if English would use "the" — reach for 把. If it would use "a" — plain V + direction is fine.

定 (the) → use 把
他把西瓜买回来了
He brought the watermelon back (the one we discussed).
不定 (a) → skip 把
他买回一个西瓜来
He bought a watermelon and brought it home (news).

Practice

What's the end state in 他把礼物送过去了?

  • He's about to deliver the gift.✗ 了 + 过去 = completed action; the gift has already arrived.
  • The gift is now with the recipient (away from the speaker).✓ 送 + 过去 + 了 traces: grabbed gift → delivered → away from me → done.
  • He is bringing the gift toward the speaker.✗ That would be 送过来. 过去 = away from speaker.

Use 把 + B + V + result/direction complement at least three times, with three different verbs and three different complements (完 · 好 · 干净 · 回来 · 过去 · 上楼来 …), and make at least one of them a direction complement rather than a result. Zhège xīngqīliù wǒ zài jiā zuò le hěn duō shìqing. Wǒ bǎ shàng ge yuè jiè de liǎng běn shū huán huí túshūguǎn qu le, yě bǎ huài le de zìxíngchē sòng dào lóuxià de shāngdiàn qu le. This Saturday I got a lot done at home. I took the two books I borrowed last month back to the library, and I took my broken bike down to the shop downstairs. At midday I brought home the fruit my mum had asked for, and in the evening I laid out all the clothes I'm wearing tomorrow, so I won't have to rush in the morning.

Why these are right: every B is definite — 上个月借的两本书, 坏了的自行车, 妈妈要的水果, 明天要穿的衣服 — which is exactly what licenses 把. The verb stays a bare action word (还 · 送 · 买 · 放) and the whole outcome is carried by what follows it: 回…去 and 到…去 say where B ended up, 回来 says it ended up here with me, 好 says it is ready. The 了 lands after the complete verb + complement unit, never inside it.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is every B specific and already known? If your English says "a…", drop 把 and use plain V + complement instead.
  • Complement welded straight onto the verb: 放好了 ✓ · 放了好 ✗ · 好放了 ✗
  • Place word inside a direction complement: 还回图书馆去 ✓ · 还回去图书馆 ✗
  • Three different complements — 完 three times practises one word, not the frame.
  • Does each sentence really report a state B reached, and is it true of your own week?

2. 先 … 再/又 … 然后 … Sequencing steps in order

When a speaker reaches for this: to lay out a sequence — "first this, then that, then the other". Directions, routines, plans, recipes.

The English trap

English lists with "first… then… after that…". Chinese uses a trio: 先 (first) — 再 or 又 (next) — 然后 (after that). They don't replace verbs; they sit before each verb like signposts on a timeline.

再 vs 又 here: 再 = next step still to come (future), 又 = next step already happened (past). Use 再 for plans, 又 for narration.

先 + V₁ , 再 / 又 + V₂ , 然后 + V₃

我先写作业，再吃饭，然后看电视。

Wǒ xiān xiě zuòyè, zài chī fàn, ránhòu kàn diànshì.

I'll do my homework first, then eat, and after that watch TV.

他先去了商店，又去了书店，然后回家了。

Tā xiān qù le shāngdiàn, yòu qù le shūdiàn, ránhòu huí jiā le.

He went to the shop first, then to the bookstore, and after that went home. (All past — 又 not 再.)

plan (future) — 再
我先吃饭，再休息
I'll eat first, then rest. (Still to come.)
narration (past) — 又
我先吃了饭，又休息了
I ate first, then rested. (Already done.)

Mini timeline: 再 vs 又

Both can mean "then", but they live on different sides of the timeline:

  • Plan still ahead: now → 先买菜 → 再做饭 → 然后吃饭. Use 再 because the next step has not happened yet.
  • Story already happened: 先去了商店 → 又去了书店 → 然后回家了. Use 又 because the speaker is adding another completed action.

If the sentence has tomorrow / later / plan language, expect 再. If it narrates completed actions with 了, expect 又.

Practice

You're describing tomorrow's plan: "I'll buy groceries first, then cook, then eat." Fill the middle slot:

  • 我先买菜，再做饭，然后吃饭。✓ Future plan → 再.
  • 我先买菜，又做饭，然后吃饭。✗ 又 marks a past sequence; this is a plan.
  • 我先买菜，然后做饭，再吃饭。✗ The order is 先 → 再/又 → 然后, not 先 → 然后 → 再.

What does this sentence tell us?
他先去了商店，又去了书店。

  • He will go to the shop, then to the bookstore.✗ 又 + 了 = past. For future plans you'd say 先…再… .

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences laying out sequences from your own life. Use 先 … 再 … 然后 … at least once for something you plan to do, and 先 … 又 … 然后 … at least once for something you already did; give each chain three steps with three different verbs. Add a time word to each chain (明天早上 · 上个周末 · 每天晚上 …) so it is clear which side of the timeline you are on. Everything must be true.

明天早上我先去银行，再买点儿东西，然后回家做午饭。上个星期天我先去了公园锻炼身体，又在奶奶家吃了午饭，然后跟她一起看了一个电影。每天晚上我一般先洗澡，再复习汉语，然后十一点以前睡觉。

Míngtiān zǎoshang wǒ xiān qù yínháng, zài mǎi diǎnr dōngxi, ránhòu huí jiā zuò wǔfàn. Shàng ge xīngqītiān wǒ xiān qù le gōngyuán duànliàn shēntǐ, yòu zài nǎinai jiā chī le wǔfàn, ránhòu gēn tā yìqǐ kàn le yí ge diànyǐng. Měi tiān wǎnshang wǒ yìbān xiān xǐzǎo, zài fùxí Hànyǔ, ránhòu shíyī diǎn yǐqián shuìjiào.

Tomorrow morning I'll go to the bank first, then buy a few things, and after that go home and make lunch. Last Sunday I first went to the park to exercise, then had lunch at my grandma's, and after that watched a film with her. In the evening I usually shower first, then revise Chinese, and after that go to bed before eleven.

Why these are right: 再 and 又 are not stylistic variants — they mark opposite sides of the timeline. The first and third sentences look forward (明天早上, a habit), so the middle step is 再 and no 了 appears; the second one narrates finished events, so the middle step is 又 and every verb carries 了. In all three the order of the signposts is the same: 先 → 再/又 → 然后, each one standing directly in front of its own verb, with the subject named once at the start.

Check your own sentences:

  • Plan or habit ⇒ 再; finished narration ⇒ 又. Check each chain against its own 了s.
  • Is the order 先 → 再/又 → 然后? 先…然后…再… ✗ — 然后 always closes.
  • Does each marker sit before its verb, with the subject stated once at the front rather than repeated?
  • Three genuinely different steps per chain, three different verbs — not 去 three times.
  • Does each chain carry a time word, and is every step something you really do or really did?

Tell the day in 4–6 connected sentences, using both structures of this lesson: 把 + B + V + 完/好/干净/回来/过去 … at least twice, with two different complements, and one 先 … 再/又 … 然后 … chain of three steps. Cover: what you tidied and where you put it, the order you did things in, how you got to the station, and how the evening ended.

奶奶今天下午从南方来我这儿住几天。上午我先把房间打扫干净了，又把她要用的东西都放好了，然后去超市买了她最爱吃的西瓜。三点半我把车开过去，在火车站门口等她。回来以后我把她的行李箱拿上楼来，她一边喝茶，一边给我讲南方的天气。

Nǎinai jīntiān xiàwǔ cóng nánfāng lái wǒ zhèr zhù jǐ tiān. Shàngwǔ wǒ xiān bǎ fángjiān dǎsǎo gānjìng le, yòu bǎ tā yào yòng de dōngxi dōu fàng hǎo le, ránhòu qù chāoshì mǎi le tā zuì ài chī de xīguā. Sān diǎn bàn wǒ bǎ chē kāi guòqu, zài huǒchēzhàn ménkǒu děng tā. Huílai yǐhòu wǒ bǎ tā de xínglixiāng ná shàng lóu lai, tā yìbiān hē chá, yìbiān gěi wǒ jiǎng nánfāng de tiānqì.

Grandma is coming up from the south this afternoon to stay with me for a few days. In the morning I cleaned the room first, then put away everything she would need, and after that went to the supermarket for the watermelon she loves most. At half past three I drove over and waited for her at the station entrance. Once we were back I carried her suitcase upstairs, and she drank tea while telling me about the weather down south.

Why this works as a paragraph: the 先…又…然后 chain organises the morning — 又 rather than 再 because the morning is already over by the time the story is told, which is also why each step keeps its 了 — and the 把 sentences hanging off it each report a different end state — 打扫干净 (a state the room reached), 放好 (things ready to use), 开过去 (the car ended up over there), 拿上楼来 (the suitcase ended up up here with me). Note how the direction complements quietly carry Lesson 12 as well: 过去 because the station is away from home, 上楼来 because the narrator is telling it from home. The last clause borrows 一边…一边 to close the day rather than adding a fifth 把.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Every 把 has a definite B and a complement stuck to the verb — 把房间干净打扫 ✗
  • Two different complements, at least one saying a state and one saying a direction.
  • 先 → 再/又 → 然后 in that order; 再 if the morning is still ahead, 又 if you are narrating it afterwards.
  • Any 来/去 checked against where you are telling the story from.
  • Does it read as one day, joined by time words (上午 · 三点半 · 回来以后), rather than two grammar demos?

2. 请把行李箱拿上楼去。
3. 他带回一个蛋糕来了。
4. 我明天先去银行，再去公司，然后回家。

1. Jiějie bǎ xīn mǎi de yīfu fàng hǎo le. 2. Qǐng bǎ xínglixiāng ná shàng lóu qu. 3. Tā dài huí yí ge dàngāo lai le. 4. Wǒ míngtiān xiān qù yínháng, zài qù gōngsī, ránhòu huí jiā.

1. My older sister put the newly bought clothes away. 2. Please carry the suitcase upstairs. 3. He brought a cake back. 4. Tomorrow I'll go to the bank first, then to the office, and after that home.

  1. 好放了 → 放好了. The complement declares the outcome, so it can only come after the action: V then result, then 了. Putting 好 in front turns it into an adverb-shaped thing the frame has no slot for.
  2. 拿上去楼 → 拿上楼去. A place word never follows 来/去; it goes between the stem and the orientation: 上 + 楼 + 去. Also note 去 rather than 来 — the speaker is not upstairs.
  3. 把一个蛋糕 → 带回一个蛋糕来. 把 needs a B that both speakers already know about. 一个蛋糕 is brand-new information ("a cake"), so the 把 frame is unavailable and the indefinite object slots inside the complement instead.
  4. 又去公司 → 再去公司. 明天 puts the whole chain in the future, and 又 can only add a step that has already happened. Plans take 再; narration with 了 takes 又.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Is the complement always after the verb, with 了 after the pair?
  • Place words tucked inside the complement, never after 来/去?
  • Definite B ⇒ 把; indefinite "a …" ⇒ no 把, object inside the direction complement.
  • Did you pick 再 or 又 by looking at the time word and the 了s, not by feel?

Answer the four questions. Write a full sentence in Chinese for each, using the structure named in brackets. Answers 1 and 2 must be true about your own week.
1. 你今天下午打算做什么？ [先 … 再 … 然后 …, three steps]
2. 上个周末你都做了什么？ [先 … 又 … 然后 …, three steps]
3. 我的自行车你送到商店去了吗？ [answer yes, with 把 + V + direction complement]
4. 房间打扫好了吗？ [answer "not yet", with a negative 把 sentence]

1. 我下午先把电子邮件写完，再去医院看奶奶，然后回家复习汉语。
2. 上个周末我先去运动了，又跟同事一起吃了饭，然后回家把房间打扫干净了。
3. 送到了，我上午就把你的自行车送到商店去了。
4. 还没有，我还没把房间打扫好呢。

1. Wǒ xiàwǔ xiān bǎ diànzǐ yóujiàn xiě wán, zài qù yīyuàn kàn nǎinai, ránhòu huí jiā fùxí Hànyǔ. 2. Shàng ge zhōumò wǒ xiān qù yùndòng le, yòu gēn tóngshì yìqǐ chī le fàn, ránhòu huí jiā bǎ fángjiān dǎsǎo gānjìng le. 3. Sòng dào le, wǒ shàngwǔ jiù bǎ nǐ de zìxíngchē sòng dào shāngdiàn qu le. 4. Hái méiyǒu, wǒ hái méi bǎ fángjiān dǎsǎo hǎo ne.

1. This afternoon I'll finish the email first, then go and see my grandmother at the hospital, and after that go home and revise Chinese. 2. Last weekend I went to exercise first, then had a meal with a colleague, and after that went home and got the room cleaned. 3. Yes — I took your bike to the shop this morning. 4. Not yet — I still haven't got the room cleaned.

Why these are right: answers 1 and 2 are the same three-step frame pointed in opposite directions — 再 with no 了 for the plan, 又 with 了 on each finished step for the weekend. Answer 3 shows the natural Chinese way of saying yes: echo the verb and its complement (送到了) and then give the full sentence. Answer 4 is the one worth memorising — under 没 the 把 sentence keeps its complement but loses its 了, and 还…呢 supplies the "not yet".

Check your own four:

  • Did you answer in full sentences, not with bare 是 or 对?
  • Answer 1: no 了 anywhere — it hasn't happened yet.
  • Answer 3: 商店 inside the complement (送到商店去), and the yes-echo repeats the verb, not 是.
  • Answer 4: 还没把房间打扫好呢 ✓ · 还没把房间打扫好了 ✗ — 没 and 了 cannot share a sentence.
  • Is 没 in front of 把, rather than inside the frame? 把房间没打扫好 ✗
