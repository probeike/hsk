HSK 2 Review · Sheet 05 · 了 · 着 · 过

门开着呢。

Mén kāi zhe ne.

The door is open.

What this sheet drills

Three single characters that hang off the back of a verb and change what it is claiming: 了 (it got done), 着 (it is still true right now), 过 (it has happened at least once in your life). They are the smallest, cheapest, most frequently botched piece of Chinese grammar you own — and the only way to fix them is to meet all three in the same room and force a choice.

Scope — what is not here

This sheet is the three markers only, as a contrast set. Two neighbouring topics are deliberately left out:

  • The status frames — 还没…呢, 正在/在…呢, 快要/就要…了, 已经…了 + 刚, and future 要/想/会/打算 — are all in Custom 3 · 我还没吃早饭呢. Those are wrappers around the verb; this sheet is about the character stuck to it.
  • 了 does three different jobs. Only job 1 (completed action) is drilled below. Change-of-state 了 and modal 了 live in the three 了s appendix.

Companion theory: HSK 1/2 sheet 11 (了) · sheet 14 (着) · sheet 15 (过).

1. At a glance one picture each — snapshot, freeze-frame, passport stamp

Chinese has no tense. What it marks instead is what kind of claim you are making about an action. Three markers, three pictures. Learn the picture and the grammar follows.

MarkerThe pictureExampleWhat it claimsUnder 没
了 A snapshot of the moment it got done. 吃了 The action reached its end. Finished, closed, in the book. 我没吃 — 了 is deleted.
着 A freeze-frame: the picture the action left behind, still on screen. 开着 Not "it opened" — it stands open, right now, as I speak. 门没开着 — 着 stays.
过 A stamp in your passport. 吃过 You have done this at least once in your life. When doesn't matter. 我没吃过 — 过 survives.

The English trap

English hands you one shape — "I ate", "I have eaten" — and lets tense sort it out. Chinese asks a question English never asks you: are you reporting a completed event, a state you can see, or a life experience? Pick the wrong marker and the sentence is still grammatical — it just says something you didn't mean. That is why these three are so hard to shake: nothing crashes.

了 — the event
我吃了
"I've eaten" / "I ate." A finished event on the timeline.
过 — the experience
我吃过
"I've had it before." Says nothing about today — it's a stamp, not a snapshot.

2. 了 — the completed action a snapshot of the moment it got done

When a speaker reaches for this: to report that an action got all the way done. It is not a past tense — it is a completion mark, which is why it also shows up in the future (我吃了饭就去练习 — "once I've eaten I'll go practise").
Subj + V + 了 + quantified Obj

Why the object usually needs a number

我买了书 on its own sounds like the first half of something — the listener waits for the rest. Give the object a quantity and the sentence closes: 我买了三本书. A second clause closes it too: 我买了书就回家了. This is a habit worth building now — your 了 sentences will stop sounding truncated.

昨天我看了一个中国电影。

Zuótiān wǒ kàn le yí ge Zhōngguó diànyǐng.

yesterday / I / watch / LE / one / CL / Chinese / movie

Yesterday I watched a Chinese movie.

我买了三本书。

Wǒ mǎi le sān běn shū.

I / buy / LE / three / CL / book

I bought three books. (Quantified object — the sentence stands on its own.)

昨天我练习了两个小时舞蹈。

Zuótiān wǒ liànxí le liǎng ge xiǎoshí wǔdǎo.

yesterday / I / practise / LE / two / CL / hour / dance

Yesterday I practised dancing for two hours.

Common pitfall — 没 kills 了

A negated verb never carries 了. The negation already says the action didn't reach completion, so the completion mark has nothing left to do:
我没买了书 ✗ · 我没买书 ✓ · 我还没买呢 ✓ ("haven't bought it yet").
Compare this with 过 in section 4, which does the opposite and survives 没.

Practice

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 上个星期我没参加了那个比赛。
(Intended: "I didn't take part in that competition last week.")

上个星期我没参加那个比赛。 — delete the 了. 没 already says the action never reached its end, so there is no completion left for 了 to mark; the two can never share a verb. If you meant "I haven't taken part yet", that is 我还没参加呢 — the partner of 还没 is 呢, never 了.

Fill: 昨天下午我打扫 ___ 房间，现在很干净。
(Yesterday afternoon I cleaned the room — it's nice and tidy now.)

了 — one dated job that reached its end. 打扫着 would describe somebody mid-sweep, and 打扫过 would file it away as "something I've done at some point in my life", which 昨天下午 flatly contradicts.

Build: "Yesterday I drank three cups of coffee." (The time word can sit before or after 我 — both orders are accepted.)

我 昨天 喝了 三杯 咖啡

Transform. 小刚昨天洗了那辆自行车。 → make it negative: "Xiao Gang didn't wash that bike yesterday." Keep 昨天 and 那辆自行车. Type the whole sentence.

小刚昨天没洗那辆自行车。 — two things happen at once: 没 goes in front of the verb and the 了 vanishes. 小刚昨天没洗了那辆自行车 ✗ is the error to watch for. Hold this side by side with section 4: under the very same 没, 过 would have survived.

3. 着 — the state that stays 门开着呢 — not "it opened", but "it stands open"

When a speaker reaches for this: to describe how something currently is. The action happened at some point and nobody cares when — what you are pointing at is the picture it left behind: a door standing open, a light left on, a red dress being worn, drinks sitting on a table.
Subj / Place + V + 着 + (Obj) + (呢)

门开着呢。

Mén kāi zhe ne.

door / open / ZHE / NE

The door is open. (Right now, as you're looking at it.)

你看，她今天穿着一件红衣服呢。

Nǐ kàn, tā jīntiān chuān zhe yí jiàn hóng yīfu ne.

you / look, / she / today / wear / ZHE / one / CL / red / clothes / NE

Look — she's wearing a red dress today.

桌子上放着很多饮料。

Zhuōzi shang fàng zhe hěn duō yǐnliào.

table / on / put / ZHE / very / many / drink

There are lots of drinks on the table. (Place first, then V着 — the "here's what's sitting there" pattern.)

V₁着 + V₂ — the manner slot

Put 着 on the first verb and it stops being the main action and becomes the manner of the second one: 笑着说 = say it smiling; 站着学习 = study standing up; 坐着看 = watch sitting down. Word order rule: the 着-verb is the background, the bare verb is what you actually did.

她总是笑着跟客人说话。

Tā zǒngshì xiào zhe gēn kèrén shuōhuà.

she / always / smile / ZHE / with / guest / speak

She always talks to customers with a smile. (笑着 = how; 说话 = what.)

小刚坐着看书，小丽站着练习舞蹈。

Xiǎo Gāng zuò zhe kàn shū, Xiǎo Lì zhàn zhe liànxí wǔdǎo.

Xiao Gang / sit / ZHE / read / book, / Xiao Li / stand / ZHE / practise / dance

Xiao Gang is reading sitting down; Xiao Li is practising her dancing standing up.

在…呢 — the act
他在开门呢
He is in the middle of opening the door — you can watch his hands move.
V着 — the state
门开着呢
The door is open. Nothing is moving. This is a description, not an event.

Common pitfall — 着 only likes state-ish verbs

着 needs a verb that leaves a visible result behind: 开、关、穿、拿、坐、站、放、写. Verbs that are pure motion don't take it — 我去着上海 ✗. If you want "in the middle of doing", that is 正在/在…呢, which lives in Custom 3, not here.

Practice

Fill: 外面刮 ___ 风呢，我们在家玩游戏吧。
(It's windy out — let's stay in and play a game.)

着 — the wind is not an event that got done, it is the weather you can feel out of the window right now, and the final 呢 insists on exactly that. 刮了风呢 is not a sentence, and 刮过风 would mean "it has been windy at some point in history" — true, useless.

Build: "There's someone standing downstairs." (Place first — this is the "here's what's there" pattern.)

楼下 站着 一个 人

"He asked the way holding a map." (Holding the map is the manner; asking is what he did.) Which is right?

  • 他拿着地图问路。✓ 着 marks the background verb (拿 = how he stood there), and the bare verb is the real action (问路 = what he did).
  • 他问着路拿地图。✗ You've swapped them — this says he picked up a map while asking directions. The map is now the main event.
  • 他拿地图着问路。✗ 着 must sit directly behind its own verb: 拿着. It can never be parked after the object.

Transform. 他早上穿了一件新衬衫。 → you are looking at him now, and the shirt is still on him. Rewrite it as a description of his current state: keep 他 and 一件新衬衫, drop 早上. Type the whole sentence.

他穿着一件新衬衫。 (adding 呢 is fine too) — 穿了 photographs the moment his arms went into the sleeves; 穿着 is the picture that moment left behind, which is what English "he's wearing" actually means. Same subject, same verb, same object: one character decides whether you reported an event or described a person.

Answer the question. 房间里的空调还开着吗？ — No, it isn't. Answer in the negative and keep 着 on the verb. Three or four characters is enough.

没开着。 — this is the negation where the marker stays put. 没 cancels the state, but 着 is the character that names the state, so it has to survive: 空调没开着 = "the on-state isn't in place". Drop it and 空调没开 reports an event that never happened — nobody ever switched it on — which is a different claim. Compare 了, which is deleted outright under 没.

4. 过 — been there, done that the stamp in your passport

When a speaker reaches for this: to say you have done something at least once in your life — or, negated, that you never have. It answers "have you ever…?", and it deliberately leaves out when.
Subj + V + 过 + (次数) + (Obj)

你看过那个电影吗？

Nǐ kàn guo nàge diànyǐng ma?

you / watch / GUO / that / CL / movie / MA

Have you (ever) seen that movie?

我去过两次上海。

Wǒ qù guo liǎng cì Shànghǎi.

I / go / GUO / two / times / Shanghai

I've been to Shanghai twice.

我还没去过韩国，可是我很喜欢韩国的舞蹈。

Wǒ hái méi qù guo Hánguó, kěshì wǒ hěn xǐhuan Hánguó de wǔdǎo.

I / still / not / go / GUO / Korea, / but / I / very / like / Korea / DE / dance

I still haven't been to Korea, but I love Korean dance.

我没当过老板，可是我很想当。

Wǒ méi dāng guo lǎobǎn, kěshì wǒ hěn xiǎng dāng.

I / not / be / GUO / boss, / but / I / very / want / be

I've never been a boss, but I'd really like to be one.

The contrast that matters — 过 survives 没, 了 does not

This is the single cleanest way to keep the two apart. Put both under negation:

PositiveNegative
了我买了书我没买书 — 了 gone
过我买过书我没买过书 — 过 stays

Reason: 了 marks completion, and "didn't happen" has no completion to mark. 过 marks whether the experience exists in your record at all — and "never, not once" is still a statement about that record. The stamp is simply missing from the passport.

Common pitfall — where the 次 goes

The count sits between 过 and the place, not at the end: 我去过两次上海 ✓ · 我去过上海两次 (marked at best) · 我两次去过上海 ✗. And 过 glues to the verb, never after the object: 我去上海过 ✗.

Practice

Fill: 你骑 ___ 马吗？我一直很想试试。
(Have you ever ridden a horse? I've always wanted to try.)

过 — "ever, at any point in your life", which is why no time word appears anywhere. 骑了马 would ask about one particular ride you both already know about, and 骑着马 would describe somebody currently sitting on a horse.

Build: "Xiao Li has taken part in three competitions." (Watch where the count goes.)

小丽 参加过 三次 比赛

You're chatting to a new colleague and want to ask: "Have you ever been on a boat?" Which do you say?

  • 你坐过船吗？✓ 过 = the life-experience question. Exactly "have you ever been on one" — and it deliberately says nothing about when.
  • 你坐了船吗？✗ That asks about one specific crossing you both already know about — "did you take the boat (as planned)?"
  • 你坐船过吗？✗ 过 glues to the verb, before the object: 坐过船.

Transform. 我做过中国菜。 → make it negative: "I've never cooked Chinese food." Keep the verb 做 and the object 中国菜. Type the whole sentence.

我没做过中国菜。 — put 没 in front and leave 过 exactly where it is. This is the mirror image of section 2: 了 is deleted under 没, 过 survives it. "Never, not once" is still a statement about your record, so the stamp slot stays in the sentence — it is simply empty. 我没做中国菜 would mean "I didn't cook Chinese food" on some particular occasion.

Answer the question. 你听过几次这个故事？ — twice. Start with 我 (or front the story), and put the count where 过 wants it.

我听过两次。 — the count sits between 过 and the object: 听过 + 两次 (+ 这个故事). 我听过这个故事两次 is marked at best, and 我两次听过这个故事 is simply wrong. Chinese is also happy for you to drop the object altogether once the question has established it.

5. Choice rapid-fire 了 · 着 · 过 — eight blanks, one right answer each

Every sentence below is built so that exactly one of the three works. Before you type, ask the question in that order: Is something still true right now? → 着. Am I claiming it's ever happened? → 过. Am I reporting one finished event? → 了.

1. 我学 ___ 两年汉语了，现在还在学呢。
(I've been studying Chinese for two years now — and I'm still at it.)

了 — V + 了 + duration, with a second 了 at the end for "and counting". 过 would say the studying is over and filed away, which the second half contradicts.

2. 他总是听 ___ 音乐做作业，我觉得这样不太好。
(He always does his homework with music on — I don't think that's a great idea.)

着 — V₁着 V₂: 听着音乐 is the background, 做作业 is what he is actually doing. 总是 rules the other two out on its own — a habit has no single completion to mark and no passport stamp to show.

3. 你吃 ___ 中国的面条吗？——吃过，真的很好吃。
(Have you ever had Chinese noodles? — I have, they're really good.)

过 — the reply gives it away: 吃过 is a life-experience answer, so the question must be the life-experience question. 吃了 would ask whether you've eaten today.

4. 你们看，黑板上还写 ___ 昨天的作业呢。
(Look — yesterday's homework is still up on the board.)

着 — place + V着 + object: the "here's what is sitting there" pattern, and 还…呢 points at what you can see on the board as you speak. 写了 would report the act of writing it, which happened yesterday and is not the news.

5. 上个月我姐姐搬 ___ 家，新房间比以前的大。
(My older sister moved house last month — the new place is bigger than the old one.)

了 — 上个月 dates the move and closes it. 搬过家 would mean "has moved house at some point, when doesn't matter" — and a stated date is precisely what 过 refuses. 搬 leaves no standing picture behind, so 着 is out too.

6. 小丽以前当 ___ 老师，现在在银行工作。
(Xiao Li used to be a teacher; now she works at a bank.)

过 — a role she once held and holds no longer. That is the passport stamp: the experience is on her record, and 过 quietly implies it is over, which is exactly what 现在…银行 confirms. 当了老师 would report the moment she became one.

7. 孩子们都站 ___ 唱歌呢，因为教室里没有椅子。
(The children are all singing standing up — there are no chairs in the classroom.)

着 — V₁着 V₂ again: 站着 is the posture they are in, 唱歌 is what they are doing. The 着-verb is always the background; the bare verb is the news.

8. 下 ___ 课我们就去图书馆复习。
(Once class is over we'll head to the library to revise.)

了 — the V了…就… frame: "once the first thing is finished, the second one happens." Notice this sentence is about the future — the clearest proof on the whole sheet that 了 marks completion, not past tense.

6. Finale all three markers in one breath

Nothing here comes in section order, so you have to work out which marker a sentence needs before you can build it. Two items aren't from this sheet at all — one 比 comparison and one coverb stack — dropped in to keep the older machinery running.

Coverbs — from an earlier sheet. Translate. "Yesterday I phoned my mum from the office."
用: 昨天 · 我 · 在办公室 · 给妈妈 · 打 · 了 · 一个电话

昨天我在办公室给妈妈打了一个电话。 — two coverb phrases stack up in front of the verb, in the order place → person: 在办公室 · 给妈妈 · 打. English puts both after ("phoned my mum from the office"), which is why word order is the whole difficulty here. Then the 了 glues straight onto 打, and 一个 gives the call a quantity so the sentence closes instead of dangling.

选词填空。 Place one marker in each blank (one word in the bank is extra).

上个星期六我跟小刚一起去超市。我们到的时候，超市还没开门，外面站很多人呢。小刚说：“我以前来一次，那时候人也这么多。”进去以后，我们一共买三瓶啤酒和一些水果。回家的路上，小刚带两个大包走在前面，笑得很奇怪。我问他为什么，他说他忘带钱，东西都是我买的。

比 — from an earlier sheet. Combine into one sentence with 比: 这条裤子很便宜。那条裤子不太便宜。 Start with 这条裤子, and add no degree word (no 更, 还, 一点儿).

这条裤子比那条便宜。 — 比 sentences drop 很 completely. 这条裤子比那条很便宜 ✗: in a comparison the adjective is already being measured against something, so it refuses its usual 很 crutch. Repeating 裤子 in the second half is fine but unnecessary — 那条 is enough.

Own it — all three markers, about your actual life. Look up from this page and write four or five sentences:

  • 2–3 sentences describing your room right now — what is open, switched on, or sitting somewhere, and what you are wearing. Use 着 in each.
  • 2 things you have already done today. Use 了, and give each object a quantity so the sentence closes.
  • 1 thing you have never done in your life. Use 没…过.

我的房间里开着空调，椅子上放着我的包和一本词典，桌子上还坐着我的猫呢。我今天穿着一件蓝衬衫。今天早上我打扫了房间，也洗了三件衣服。可是我还没爬过山，今年秋天很想去试试。

Wǒ de fángjiān lǐ kāi zhe kōngtiáo, yǐzi shang fàng zhe wǒ de bāo hé yì běn cídiǎn, zhuōzi shang hái zuò zhe wǒ de māo ne. Wǒ jīntiān chuān zhe yí jiàn lán chènshān. Jīntiān zǎoshang wǒ dǎsǎo le fángjiān, yě xǐ le sān jiàn yīfu. Kěshì wǒ hái méi pá guo shān, jīnnián qiūtiān hěn xiǎng qù shìshi.

The air-con is on in my room, my bag and a dictionary are sitting on the chair, and my cat is on the desk. I'm wearing a blue shirt today. This morning I cleaned the room and washed three items of clothing. But I've never climbed a mountain — I'd really like to try this autumn.

Self-check — one pass per line:

  • Does every 着 sentence describe a state you can see right now, rather than report an event? If a sentence would still be true with "…and then it stopped", it wanted 了.
  • No 了 anywhere on a 着 verb: 门开着了 ✗.
  • Do both 了 sentences have a quantified object (三件衣服, 两杯咖啡) or a second clause? 我洗了衣服 on its own still sounds unfinished.
  • In the 没…过 sentence: did 过 survive the 没, and is there no 了 anywhere in it?
  • A different verb in every sentence — no verb used twice across the whole paragraph?
