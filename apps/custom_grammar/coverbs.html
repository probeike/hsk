HSK 2 Review · Sheet 02 · 在·离·从·往·对

我家离地铁站很近，从我家到公司只要二十分钟。

Wǒ jiā lí dìtiězhàn hěn jìn, cóng wǒ jiā dào gōngsī zhǐ yào èrshí fēnzhōng.

My home is close to the subway station; it only takes twenty minutes from my place to the office.

What this sheet drills

You named prepositions and location as your weakest spot. The good news: Chinese has almost no prepositions — it has coverbs, little verbs that grab a noun and then hand the sentence over to the real verb. There are only five you need at this level (在 · 离 · 从 · 往 · 对), and they all obey one word-order rule. Learn that rule once and five separate problems collapse into one. Companion theory: HSK 1/2 location sheet.

What you'll learn

  1. The placement rule — every coverb phrase sits before the verb.
  2. 在 — the one that moves — before the verb vs after a placement verb.
  3. 离 — static distance: A 离 B 很远／很近.
  4. 从 … 到 … — starting point and journey.
  5. 往 — the direction you move in.
  6. 对 — the person or thing you aim something at.
  7. Mixed practice

1. The placement rule Subject + [coverb + noun] + Verb — one rule, five coverbs

When a speaker reaches for this: any time you want to say where, from where, toward where, how far, or at whom. All of that information is loaded in before the verb fires. Chinese sets the scene first and acts second.

The English trap

English trails its prepositional phrases after the verb: "I eat at home", "I work in New York", "I walk to the station". So your instinct is wrong in exactly the same direction every single time — you produce 我吃饭在家 instead of 我在家吃饭. Because the error is always the same shape, you only have to fix it once: the place phrase can never come after the verb.

The purple block is the coverb phrase. In Chinese it always sits to the left of the green verb; English drags it to the right. Every 在／离／从／往／对 sentence in this sheet is the same picture.
Subject + 在/离/从/往/对 + noun + Verb + (Object)
CoverbIts jobExample
在where the action happens我在纽约工作。
离distance between two fixed points我家离公司很远。
从starting point of a journey我从家走到地铁站。
往direction you move in一直往前走。
对the person or thing you aim it at老板对我很好。

我在纽约工作。

Wǒ zài Niǔyuē gōngzuò.

I / at-New-York / work

I work in New York. (Not 我工作在纽约.)

我在家吃饭。

Wǒ zài jiā chīfàn.

I / at-home / eat-rice

I eat at home.

小丽每天在地铁上看书。

Xiǎo Lì měitiān zài dìtiě shang kàn shū.

Xiao Li / every-day / on-the-subway / read / book

Xiao Li reads on the subway every day. — a time word may go before or after the subject, but the coverb phrase still hugs the verb.

Common pitfall

A bare place noun cannot stand in that slot on its own — it needs the coverb. 我家吃饭 means "my family eats"; only 我在家吃饭 means "I eat at home".

Practice

"She reads the newspaper at the library every day." Which is right?

  • 她每天在图书馆看报纸。✓ Subject 她 → time 每天 → coverb phrase 在图书馆 → verb 看报纸.
  • 她每天看报纸在图书馆。✗ Straight out of English ("reads the newspaper at the library"). The place phrase can never trail the verb.
  • 她每天图书馆看报纸。✗ The slot is right but the coverb is missing — a bare place noun cannot stand there; it needs 在 in front of it.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 我买东西在超市。
(Intended: "I do my shopping at the supermarket." Keep every word — change only the order.)

我在超市买东西。 — 在超市 is a coverb phrase, so the whole scene loads before the verb fires: subject → where → action. The broken version is a word-for-word photocopy of English "I shop at the supermarket", which is the exact shape your ear will keep offering you for all five coverbs.

Build: "Xiao Gang goes running in the park every morning." (The time phrase may go before or after 小刚 — both are accepted.)

小刚 每天早上 在公园 跑步

2. 在 — the one that moves Before the verb, or after it — and the meaning changes

When a speaker reaches for this: 在 is the exception that proves the rule. Ask yourself what the place is describing. Is it where the action takes place? Then 在 goes first, like every other coverb. Is it where a thing or person ends up after the action? Then 在 rides on the back of the verb.
Only a small family of verbs takes 在 on its back — the ones that put something somewhere: 放 (place), 住 (live), 坐 (sit), 站 (stand), 写 (write). Everything else keeps 在 in front.

Position A — 在 before the verb: where the action happens

我在纽约工作，可是我想辞职。

Wǒ zài Niǔyuē gōngzuò, kěshì wǒ xiǎng cízhí.

I / in-New-York / work, / but / I / want / quit-job

I work in New York, but I want to quit.

下班以后我在家练习跳舞。

Xiàbān yǐhòu wǒ zài jiā liànxí tiàowǔ.

finish-work / after / I / at-home / practise / dance

After work I practise dancing at home.

Position B — V + 在: where the thing ends up

你的书放在桌子上。

Nǐ de shū fàng zài zhuōzi shang.

your / book / put / at / table-on

Your book is on the table. — the table is where the book came to rest.

小丽住在地铁站旁边。

Xiǎo Lì zhù zài dìtiězhàn pángbiān.

Xiao Li / live / at / subway-station / beside

Xiao Li lives right next to the subway station.

请坐在这儿。／ 请写在本子上。

Qǐng zuò zài zhèr. / Qǐng xiě zài běnzi shang.

please / sit / at / here. — please / write / at / notebook-on

Please sit here. / Please write it in the notebook.

在 first — action location
小丽在地铁站旁边看书。
The reading happens beside the station. She could be anywhere afterwards.
在 after — end position
小丽住在地铁站旁边。
The living deposits her beside the station — that's where she stays.

Quick test

Say the sentence in English and ask: "at the table" — does it tell me where the event was, or where the object is now? Event → 在 goes first. Object's resting place → 在 goes after the verb.

Practice

You have just put 小丽's passport away for her, and you're telling her where it ended up. Which is right?

  • 你的护照放在包里。✓ 放 is a placement verb: the resting place rides on the back of the verb.
  • 你的护照在包里放。✗ 在 in front would say the act of putting was carried out inside the bag. The end position must follow 放.
  • 你的护照放在包。✗ Right slot, unfinished place word. A container noun has to be closed off with 里 (or 上, 旁边…) before 在 will accept it: 在包里.

Fill with 在 + the place: 我阿姨 ___ 医院工作，她的同事都很热情。
(My aunt works at a hospital; her colleagues are all very warm.)

在医院 — nothing is being set down anywhere, so 工作 is not a placement verb and the location stays in front of it. 我阿姨工作在医院 ✗ is the English order sneaking back in.

Translate. "My older sister lives near the park."
用: 姐姐 · 住 · 在 · 公园附近

姐姐住在公园附近。 — 住 belongs to the small family of verbs that deposit someone somewhere, so its place rides behind it, exactly like 放在／坐在／写在. Front the 在 and you get a different sentence: 姐姐在公园附近等我 ✓ (waiting is an event, so 在 goes first), but 姐姐在公园附近住 foregrounds the act of living as an event, which is not what you mean here.

3. 离 — static distance A 离 B + 远／近 — nobody is going anywhere

When a speaker reaches for this: you're measuring the gap between two things that are just sitting there — your flat and the office, your flat and the subway, New York and Beijing. Nothing moves. The whole sentence is A 离 B + how far.
A + 离 B + 很远 / 很近 / 多远

我家离公司很远，坐地铁要一个小时。

Wǒ jiā lí gōngsī hěn yuǎn, zuò dìtiě yào yí gè xiǎoshí.

my / home / apart-from / company / very / far, / sit / subway / need / one / CL / hour

My place is a long way from the office — an hour by subway.

我家离地铁站很近，走路只要五分钟。

Wǒ jiā lí dìtiězhàn hěn jìn, zǒulù zhǐ yào wǔ fēnzhōng.

my / home / apart-from / subway-station / very / near, / walk / only / need / five / minutes

My place is close to the subway — only a five-minute walk.

你家离地铁站远吗？
纽约离北京多远？

Nǐ jiā lí dìtiězhàn yuǎn ma? Niǔyuē lí Běijīng duō yuǎn?

your / home / apart-from / subway-station / far / MA? — New-York / apart-from / Beijing / how / far?

Is your place far from the subway? How far is New York from Beijing?

Common pitfall — 离 is not 从

我家从公司很远 is wrong. 从 needs a journey — somebody has to travel, so the sentence needs a verb of going: 我从家坐地铁到公司。 For a plain, motionless measurement, only 离 works. English blurs the two with a single word "from" ("far from the office", "walk from home"), which is why you keep reaching for 从.

Bonus — 离 also measures time

The gap doesn't have to be in space: 离下班还有一个小时。 (Lí xiàbān hái yǒu yí gè xiǎoshí.) — "There's still an hour to go until I finish work."

Practice

离, 从 or 往? 这家饭店 ___ 学校很远，走路要半个小时。
(That restaurant is a long way from the school — half an hour on foot.)

离 — the first clause contains no verb of motion at all, just a measurement between two things that are sitting still, so only 离 fits. The half-hour walk in the second clause is a separate statement; it does not license 从 back in the first.

"How far is the bank from here?" Which is right?

  • 银行离这儿多远？✓ A 离 B + 多远 — the standard way to ask for the gap between two fixed points.
  • 银行从这儿多远？✗ 从 marks the start of a journey and needs a motion verb behind it. A plain measurement takes 离.
  • 银行多远离这儿？✗ The coverb phrase 离这儿 has to come before 多远, exactly like every other coverb phrase comes before what it modifies.

Transform. 从我朋友家走到公园只要五分钟。 → say the same thing as a plain measurement, with nobody travelling in it. Start with 我朋友家, use 离 and 很近, and type that one clause only.

我朋友家离公园很近。 — the original had somebody walking, and that walking is the only reason 从…到 was allowed. Take the journey away and 从 has nothing to start, so the sentence collapses into the static frame A 离 B + 很近. 我朋友家从公园很近 ✗ is precisely the error this transform exists to kill.

Answer the question. 小刚家离机场远吗？ — yes, a long way: an hour and a half by bus. Answer in one sentence, starting with 他家.
用: 他家 · 离 · 机场 · 很远 · 坐公共汽车 · 要一个半小时

他家离机场很远，坐公共汽车要一个半小时。 — a 远吗？question is answered by keeping the whole 离 frame and swapping only the last piece. Notice that 离 does not drop out of the answer: 他家机场很远 ✗ just piles two nouns on top of each other. The travel time is a second, separate clause — it never gets folded into the 离 clause.

4. 从 … 到 … Where the journey starts, and where it ends

When a speaker reaches for this: something is travelling — you, a train, a message. 从 marks the starting point, and it very often brings 到 along for the end point. The pair also works on the clock: 从九点到五点.
Subject + 从 A + (到 B) + Verb

我是从纽约来的。

Wǒ shì cóng Niǔyuē lái de.

I / SHI / from / New-York / come / DE

I'm from New York. (Literally: it's from New York that I came.)

小刚每天六点从公司回家。

Xiǎo Gāng měitiān liù diǎn cóng gōngsī huí jiā.

Xiao Gang / every-day / six / o'clock / from / company / return / home

Xiao Gang heads home from the office at six every day.

我每天从家走到地铁站。

Wǒ měitiān cóng jiā zǒu dào dìtiězhàn.

I / every-day / from / home / walk / to / subway-station

Every day I walk from home to the subway station.

从我家到公司只要二十分钟。

Cóng wǒ jiā dào gōngsī zhǐ yào èrshí fēnzhōng.

from / my / home / to / company / only / need / twenty / minutes

It only takes twenty minutes from my place to the office. — the 从…到… pair can open the whole sentence when it is the topic.

从九点到五点，我都在公司工作。

Cóng jiǔ diǎn dào wǔ diǎn, wǒ dōu zài gōngsī gōngzuò.

from / nine / o'clock / to / five / o'clock, / I / all / at / company / work

From nine to five I'm at the office working. — same pair, measuring time instead of space.

Practice

Build: "Every morning Xiao Li takes the bus from home to school." (The time phrase may go before or after 小丽 — both are accepted.)

小丽 每天早上 从家 坐公共汽车 到学校

Type the two missing characters in order:
___ 星期一 ___ 星期五，我叔叔都在银行上班。
(Monday to Friday my uncle works at the bank. Two characters, e.g. 从到 — no spaces needed.)

从 · 到 — the pair that spans two places spans two points on the calendar just as happily: 从星期一到星期五, 从早上到晚上, 从去年到今年. Half a pair is not a sentence — 从星期一星期五 ✗.

Answer the question. 小刚每天怎么去上班？ — he cycles, from home to the office. Start with 他.
用: 他 · 从家 · 骑自行车 · 到办公室

他从家骑自行车到办公室。 — 从 opens the journey, 骑自行车 does the travelling, and 到 lands it. You may also keep the two halves of the pair together in front: 他骑自行车从家到办公室 ✓. What you may never do is let a coverb drift behind the verb — 他骑自行车到办公室从家 ✗.

5. 往 — the direction you move in 往前走, 往左走, 往右走

When a speaker reaches for this: giving or following directions. 往 takes a direction word — 前 / 左 / 右 / 回 — and hands it to a motion verb, almost always 走. It is a pure movement coverb: no movement, no 往.
往 + 前/左/右 + 走

往前走。／ 往左走。／ 往右走。

Wǎng qián zǒu. / Wǎng zuǒ zǒu. / Wǎng yòu zǒu.

toward-front / walk. — toward-left / walk. — toward-right / walk.

Go straight ahead. / Go left. / Go right.

一直往前走，到了路口往右走。

Yìzhí wǎng qián zǒu, dào le lùkǒu wǎng yòu zǒu.

straight-on / toward-front / walk, / arrive / LE / intersection / toward-right / walk

Keep going straight, and at the intersection go right.

地铁站在前面，往前走三分钟就到了。

Dìtiězhàn zài qiánmiàn, wǎng qián zǒu sān fēnzhōng jiù dào le.

subway-station / at / front, / toward-front / walk / three / minutes / then / arrive / LE

The subway station is up ahead — three minutes straight on and you're there.

Common pitfall

往 is not a general-purpose "to". You go to a place with a plain verb (我去公司) or with 到 (我走到地铁站) — 我往公司 is not a sentence. Keep 往 for which way you turn: 往前, 往左, 往右.

Practice

Fill: 出了银行 ___ 右走，医院就在第二个路口旁边。
(Come out of the bank and turn right; the hospital is by the second intersection.)

往 — 往右走 is the everyday way to say "turn right", and like every coverb phrase 往右 sits in front of its verb 走, never after it. (向 is also correct here but belongs to a later level; stick with 往 for directions.)

Build: "The library isn't far from here — keep going straight for ten minutes and you're there." (Two coverbs in one sentence: the distance first, then the direction.)

图书馆 离这儿不远 一直 往前走 十分钟 就到了

Translate. "Come out of the train station and turn left — the café is right at the intersection."
用: 出了火车站 · 往左走 · 咖啡馆 · 就在路口

出了火车站往左走，咖啡馆就在路口。 — 往 takes a direction word (前／左／右) and hands it straight to 走. It cannot take the destination: 往咖啡馆 on its own is not a sentence. The café's position is reported separately with 在, because that clause is about where a thing is, not where anybody is heading.

6. 对 — the one you aim at 他对我很好 · 跳舞对身体很好

When a speaker reaches for this: the "place" is not a place at all — it's a person, or something that benefits. 对 points your attitude, your behaviour, or a good/bad effect at a target. Its literal sense is "facing", which is exactly the picture you want: you turn and face someone, then act.
A + 对 B + 很好 / 很有兴趣

我老板对我很好，所以辞职不容易。

Wǒ lǎobǎn duì wǒ hěn hǎo, suǒyǐ cízhí bù róngyì.

my / boss / toward / me / very / good, / so / quit-job / not / easy

My boss is very good to me, so quitting isn't easy.

老师对学生很好。

Lǎoshī duì xuésheng hěn hǎo.

teacher / toward / student / very / good

The teacher is very kind to her students.

跳舞对身体很好。

Tiàowǔ duì shēntǐ hěn hǎo.

dance / toward / body / very / good

Dancing is very good for you. — the target can be a thing that benefits, not just a person.

我对韩国的舞蹈很有兴趣。

Wǒ duì Hánguó de wǔdǎo hěn yǒu xìngqù.

I / toward / Korea / DE / dance / very / have / interest

I'm really interested in Korean dance.

对 — an attitude aimed at someone
老板对我很好。
How he treats me. Nothing changes hands.
给 — something handed over
老板给我打电话。
He does something for me, or delivers something to me — a call, a coffee, help.

Common pitfall

English uses one word, "to", for both jobs — "he's good to me", "he gave it to me" — so 老板给我很好 feels fine and is wrong. And 在 can never take a person: 老板在我很好. Attitude → 对. Handover → 给. Place → 在.

Practice

"My neighbour is very kind to my children." Which is right?

  • 我的邻居对我的孩子很好。✓ An attitude aimed at a person takes 对 — and 对我的孩子 sits in front of 很好, like every coverb phrase.
  • 我的邻居给我的孩子很好。✗ 给 hands something over — 给孩子买东西, 给我打电话. It cannot carry an attitude.
  • 我的邻居很好对我的孩子。✗ The English order again ("kind to my children"). 对 + person is a coverb phrase, so it can never trail 很好.

Fill: 我弟弟 ___ 中国历史很感兴趣，每个周末都去图书馆看书。
(My little brother is really interested in Chinese history — he's at the library every weekend.)

对 — 感兴趣 always names its target with 对, and the whole phrase 对中国历史 is a coverb phrase like any other, so it loads first. 我弟弟很感兴趣中国历史 ✗ — English "interested in X" puts X last; Chinese puts it first.

Personalise it. Write two true sentences with 对: one about a person and how they treat someone (对 + person + 很好／很热情／很客气), and one about something that is good or bad for something else (对 + 身体／工作／学习 + 很好／不好). Use two different targets.

我的同事对新来的人都很热情，所以我在办公室很快乐。每天走路上班对身体很好，可是冬天太冷了。

Wǒ de tóngshì duì xīn lái de rén dōu hěn rèqíng, suǒyǐ wǒ zài bàngōngshì hěn kuàilè. Měitiān zǒulù shàngbān duì shēntǐ hěn hǎo, kěshì dōngtiān tài lěng le.

My colleagues are all very warm to newcomers, so I'm happy in the office. Walking to work every day is good for you — but winter is too cold.

Check your own two sentences:

  • Does 对 + its target sit before 很好／很热情, never after? (他很好对我 ✗)
  • Is the thing after 对 a target of an attitude or a benefit — never a location? 对 can't mean "at".
  • Did you resist 给? 给 hands something over; it cannot carry an attitude.
  • Adjective after the target, with its 很: 对身体很好 ✓, not 对很好身体 ✗.
  • Two genuinely different targets — one a person, one a thing?

7. Mixed practice Shuffled — plus two things from earlier sheets

Nothing below comes in section order, so you have to work out which coverb a sentence needs before you can build it. Two items aren't from this sheet at all — one 得 complement and one 比 comparison — dropped in to keep the older machinery running.

选词填空。 Pick the right coverb for each blank (one word is extra).

星期六早上小刚的妈妈给我打电话，请我去他们的新家吃饭。她我很客气，说饭店的菜没有家里的好吃。他们家火车站不远，所以我火车站走过去，只用了十几分钟。出了火车站前走，到了第二个路口再往右走就到了。那天下午我们他家里聊天，晚上我坐公共汽车回家。

From an earlier sheet. Fill with 的, 得 or 地: 我同事打篮球打 ___ 很好，每个周末都去公园打球。

得 — the gap sits after a repeated verb and introduces a judgement of how the playing goes, so it is the complement 得. 的 would need a noun behind it; 地 goes in front of a verb (他很快地走 — a different, more literary sentence).

From an earlier sheet. Combine into one sentence with 比: 坐出租车很快。坐公共汽车不太快。 Start with 坐出租车, and add no degree word (no 更, 还, 得多).

坐出租车比坐公共汽车快。 — 比 is a coverb too, and it obeys the same rule as the other five: 比坐公共汽车 sits in front of the adjective it measures. The other half of the rule is that a 比 sentence drops 很 completely — 坐出租车比坐公共汽车很快 ✗.

Three errors. This little paragraph has exactly three mistakes, one per sentence, and all three are coverb mistakes. Find them, write out the corrected paragraph, then check.
我家从医院很远，坐公共汽车要四十分钟。我每天早上从家走地铁站，然后坐地铁上班。我们的新经理很热情对每个人，所以大家都喜欢她。

我家离医院很远，坐公共汽车要四十分钟。我每天早上从家走到地铁站，然后坐地铁上班。我们的新经理对每个人很热情，所以大家都喜欢她。

  1. 从医院 → 离医院. Nothing travels in that clause — it is a plain measurement between two fixed points, and only 离 does that job. 从 needs a journey and a motion verb behind it. English hides the difference because it says "far from" and "walk from" with the same word.
  2. 走地铁站 → 走到地铁站. 从 opened a journey and then abandoned it halfway: once 从 A is on the table the sentence owes you an end point, and the end point is carried by 到 riding on the verb — 走到, 坐到, 开到.
  3. 很热情对每个人 → 对每个人很热情. The one rule this whole sheet is built on: a coverb phrase can never trail what it modifies. English "warm to everyone" puts the target last; Chinese loads it first.

Write it all — your commute. Describe how you actually get to work (or to your dance class) in four sentences, one coverb per sentence: 离 (how far it is), 从…到… (the journey itself), 在 (something you do on the way or once you arrive), and 往 (one turn somebody would have to make to find the place).

我家离办公室不太远，可是早上路上人很多。从我家到办公室要三十五分钟。路上我经常在公共汽车上看新闻。下了公共汽车往右走两分钟，就能看见我们的楼。

Wǒ jiā lí bàngōngshì bú tài yuǎn, kěshì zǎoshang lù shang rén hěn duō. Cóng wǒ jiā dào bàngōngshì yào sānshíwǔ fēnzhōng. Lù shang wǒ jīngcháng zài gōnggòng qìchē shang kàn xīnwén. Xià le gōnggòng qìchē wǎng yòu zǒu liǎng fēnzhōng, jiù néng kànjiàn wǒmen de lóu.

My place isn't too far from the office, but the roads are crowded in the morning. It takes thirty-five minutes from home to the office. On the way I often read the news on the bus. Get off the bus, walk two minutes to the right, and you can see our building.

Self-check — one line per sentence:

  • Sentence 1: is it 离 and not 从? Nothing moves in a distance measurement.
  • Sentence 2: did 从 get its 到? A journey that starts must also land.
  • Sentence 3: is 在 + place in front of the verb? (Only 住／放／坐／站／写 take it behind.)
  • Sentence 4: does 往 take a direction word (前／左／右) and hand it to 走 — not a destination? 往办公室 on its own is not a sentence.
  • Read all four aloud: is every coverb phrase to the left of its verb? That one check catches most of it.
