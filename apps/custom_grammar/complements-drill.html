HSK 2 Review · Sheet 03 · complements drill

题太多，我没做完。

Tí tài duō, wǒ méi zuòwán.

Too many questions — I didn't finish.

What this sheet drills

This is a drill pack, not a lesson. You already have the theory: 得 — the verb's report card, 地 — the manner marker and the which-de decision tree are all taught in Custom 1. Nothing about them is re-explained here — you said you just need reps, so this sheet is reps.

The one new thing is section 1: result complements (做完, 听懂, 买到, 看见) — a second word fused onto the verb that says how the action turned out. After that it's drills: 得-state (section 2), telling result and state apart (section 3), five rapid-fire 的/得/地 blanks plus sentences you have to type out in full (section 4), and a written finale (section 5).

1. Result complements V + 完 / 懂 / 到 / 见 / 错 / 好 / 会 — how the action turned out

When a speaker reaches for this: the verb alone doesn't say whether the action landed. 我看 = I looked; did I actually see anything? 我听 = I listened; did I get it? Chinese answers that by fusing a second word — a verb or an adjective — onto the back of the first: 看见, 听懂, 做完. The pair then behaves as one verb.

The English trap

English handles this three different ways, and none of them looks like a complement. Sometimes it's a whole extra verb — "finish doing it", "manage to buy it". Sometimes it's an adverb — "you said it wrong". And very often it's nothing at all: "Did you hear me?" already means "did the hearing succeed", so you translate it as 你听了吗 and it comes out wrong — Chinese needs 你听见了吗？ (perceive) or 你听懂了吗？ (understand). If the English implies an outcome, Chinese wants a character for it.

我做完作业了，现在可以去练习跳舞。

Wǒ zuòwán zuòyè le, xiànzài kěyǐ qù liànxí tiàowǔ.

I / do-FINISH / homework / LE, / now / can / go / practise / dance

I've finished the homework, so now I can go practise dancing.

老师说的话我没听懂。

Lǎoshī shuō de huà wǒ méi tīngdǒng.

teacher / say / DE / words / I / not / listen-UNDERSTAND

I didn't catch what the teacher said.

我在纽约没买到那本书。

Wǒ zài Niǔyuē méi mǎidào nà běn shū.

I / at / New York / not / buy-GET / that / CL / book

I couldn't get hold of that book in New York.

对不起，我说错了。

Duìbuqǐ, wǒ shuōcuò le.

sorry, / I / say-WRONG / LE

Sorry — I said that wrong.

Negating and questioning the cluster

Because V + result is one unit, you never split it. 没 goes in front of the whole thing, and 了 drops out after 没 — exactly like any other 没 sentence.

JobSentenceNote
Statement我做完作业了。The homework got finished.
Negative我没做完作业。没 in front of 做完 — and 了 is gone.
Question你做完作业了吗？Or 你做完作业了没有？

Common pitfall

我不做完作业 — 不 says "I'm not willing to / I don't habitually", which is never what you mean about a finished job. A result that failed to happen is always 没. And 我没做完了作业 keeps a 了 that must disappear.

Practice

Fill the result word: 这些盘子我已经洗 ___ 了。
(I've already finished washing these plates.)

完 — 洗完 = you got to the end of the pile, every plate. (洗好 is accepted too; it stresses "ready to use" rather than "got through them all".)

Fill the result word: 护照我找了很久，还是没找 ___ 。
(I hunted for my passport for ages and still didn't find it.)

到 — 找 on its own is only the searching; 到 is what says the search landed on something. 没找到 = the looking definitely happened, the finding didn't.

Fill the result word: 你刚才听 ___ 外面的声音了吗？
(Did you hear that noise outside just now?)

见 — 听见 = the sound physically reached you. (听到 is just as good and just as common here — 到 says the listening reached its target.) What you must not write is 懂: save that for when the question is whether the meaning got through, and a noise has no meaning to miss.

Fill the result word: 小刚坐 ___ 地铁了，所以上班迟到了。
(Xiao Gang took the wrong subway, so he was late for work.)

错 — 坐错 = the riding happened, just on the wrong thing. English hangs "wrong" on the noun ("the wrong train"); Chinese hangs it on the verb.

"I didn't understand the film." Which is right?

  • 那个电影我没看懂。✓ 没 negates the whole cluster 看懂, and 了 drops after 没.
  • 那个电影我不看懂。✗ 不 means unwilling / not habitually — "I refuse to understand it". A result that failed to happen always takes 没.
  • 那个电影我没看懂了。✗ Right cluster, but 了 can't survive next to 没 — delete it.

Build: "Have you seen my phone?" (English says just "seen" — Chinese needs the perceive-result.)

你 看见 我的 手机 了吗

Transform. 我看懂了那个菜单。 → flip it to a negative report: "I didn't understand that menu." Keep 我 and 看懂 intact, and negate with 没. Type the whole sentence.

我没看懂那个菜单。 — two things change at once. 没 goes in front of the whole cluster (never 看没懂), and the 了 vanishes: 没 says the understanding never happened, so there is no completed event left to mark. 我没看懂了那个菜单 ✗ is the slip to watch.

Transform. 小丽打扫房间了。 → she got the whole room done. Add the result word for "finished" and keep the 了. Type the whole sentence.

小丽打扫完房间了。 — 打扫房间了 only reports that cleaning happened; it says nothing about reaching the end. The result word fuses to the verb and the object follows the pair, so the order is V + 完 + O. 小丽打扫好房间了 also works ("the room is ready"), and 小丽把房间打扫完了 is the 把 version of the same claim.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 这本词典我找了很久，可是没找。
(Intended: "I looked for this dictionary for ages, but I couldn't find it." Change one thing only.)

这本词典我找了很久，可是没找到。 — 没找 is a real sentence, it just means "I didn't look", which flatly contradicts 找了很久. The failure is in the outcome, so the outcome needs a character: 找到. English hides this inside the verb find, which is why the complement goes missing here more than anywhere else.

2. 得-state drills No new theory — just reps

Reminder in one line: V + 得 + (很) Adj grades how the action went, and if the verb already carries an object the verb repeats (跳舞跳得很好). Full explanation: Custom 1 · point 3. Now drill it.

"Xiao Li isn't a good cook." Which is right?

  • 小丽做菜做得不好吃。✓ Negate the verdict, not the verb: 得不好吃.
  • 小丽不做菜做得好吃。✗ 不 in front of 做菜 says she doesn't cook at all — a different claim entirely.
  • 小丽做菜没做得好吃。✗ 没 cancels a result cluster (没做完), never a 得-verdict. A verdict is negated from the inside.

Fill: 这次考试你考 ___ 怎么样？
(How did you do on this exam?)

得 — a verb (考) in front, a verdict (怎么样) after. Note that 考完了吗 would be a completely different question: whether the exam is over, not how it went.

Fill the missing verb: 叔叔讲故事 ___ 得非常有意思。
(My uncle tells stories in a really entertaining way.)

讲 — 得 has to grab a verb, and 故事 is standing between it and 讲, so 讲 comes back for it. The rule is mechanical: any object before 得 forces the verb to repeat.

Build: "Xiao Gang writes characters very clearly."

小刚 写字 写 得 很清楚

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 她打篮球得很好。
(Intended: "She plays basketball very well." Keep 篮球 in the sentence.)

她打篮球打得很好。 — 篮球 has shoved 得 away from its verb, and 得 cannot dangle off a noun. Repeat the verb right in front of it. The other legal repair moves the object out to the front instead: 她篮球打得很好。 What you may not do is delete 篮球 and pretend the problem is gone.

Translate. "My grandfather gets up very early."
用: 爷爷 · 起床 · 得 · 早

爷爷起床起得很早。 — 起床 looks like one word, but 床 is an object sitting behind 起, so the verb-copy rule fires exactly as it does for 做菜 and 写字. The adjective still wants its 很: 爷爷起床起得早 sounds unfinished, as if a comparison were coming.

3. Result or state? 你写完了吗 (did you FINISH) vs 你写得怎么样 (how WELL)

Both hang something off the back of the verb, and they answer completely different questions. 写完 is a yes/no fact: is it done? 写得很好 is a grade: how did it turn out? Pick by asking which question the English is really asking.

Result — did it land?
你写完了吗？
"Have you finished writing?" Answer: 写完了 / 还没写完.
State — how did it go?
你写得怎么样？
"How well did you write it?" Answer: 写得很好 / 写得不太好.

You want to ask: "Have you finished today's newspaper?"

  • 今天的报纸你看完了吗？✓ 完 asks about completion — the yes/no fact.
  • 今天的报纸你看得怎么样？✗ That asks how well (or how fast) you read it, not whether you reached the end.
  • 今天的报纸你看懂了吗？✗ Also a result complement, but the wrong outcome: 懂 asks whether you understood it.

Cross-check — Sheet 5 (了 · 着 · 过). What does this mean?
叔叔手里拿着一个蛋糕。

  • My uncle picked up a cake.✗ That reports an event: 叔叔拿了一个蛋糕. 了 marks the moment of taking; it says nothing about now.
  • My uncle is standing there holding a cake.✓ 着 freezes an ongoing state — the holding is still true as you speak.
  • My uncle has held a cake before.✗ That's the experience marker: 叔叔拿过蛋糕.

You want to ask: "How well does your little brother swim?"

  • 你弟弟游泳游得怎么样？✓ 得 + 怎么样 asks for a grade — and 泳 in the way forces the verb-copy.
  • 你弟弟游完泳了吗？✗ That asks whether he has finished his swim — completion, not quality.
  • 你弟弟会游泳吗？✗ That asks whether he can swim at all, not how well.

Cross-check — Sheet 1 (比). "My older sister runs faster than I do." Which is right?

  • 我姐姐跑得比我快。✓ The comparison goes inside the verdict slot: 跑得 + 比我快. (我姐姐比我跑得快 is equally good — 比 may sit before the verb instead.)
  • 我姐姐比我跑快。✗ 比 doesn't excuse you from 得. Any verdict on a verb needs 得 to hang off.
  • 我姐姐跑得比我很快。✗ 比 and 很 can't share a comparison — 比 already supplies the degree. Drop the 很.

You want to say: "I studied it for ages, but I still haven't got it."

  • 我学了很久，可是还没学会。✓ The outcome (mastery) never arrived → result complement 会, negated with 没.
  • 我学了很久，可是学得不好。✗ 得不好 grades how well you studied — it doesn't say the skill failed to land.
  • 我学了很久，可是还不学会。✗ 不 can't cancel a result. Only 没 reports an outcome that didn't happen.

Own it — both questions about one thing. Pick something you worked on this week and write two sentences about it: first whether it got finished (V + 完, or 没 + V + 完), then how well it went (V + 得 + verdict). Same activity, two completely different claims.

这个星期我练习完了那个新舞蹈，可是跳得还不太好。

Zhège xīngqī wǒ liànxí wán le nàge xīn wǔdǎo, kěshì tiào de hái bú tài hǎo.

This week I got through that new dance, but I still don't dance it very well.

Check your two sentences:

  • Do they answer different questions — one "did it reach the end?", one "how did it turn out?" — rather than saying the same thing twice?
  • In the 完 sentence, is the result word welded straight onto the verb (练习完 ✓ / 练习了完 ✗), with the object after the pair — and if you went negative, did 没 replace the 了 entirely?
  • In the 得 sentence, does 得 sit directly behind a verb (verb repeated if an object is in the way), and does the verdict carry its 很 / 不太 / 特别?

4. 的 / 得 / 地 Five quick blanks, then two sentences you type out whole, then two of your own

Items 1–5 are one character each — don't think, just place it. Then the drill gets harder in the only way that matters: items 6 and 7 give you the same kind of sentence but ask for the whole thing, typed out, character in place — knowing which one it is and being able to produce the sentence around it are two different skills. If you stall, the three-question tree is at Custom 1 · point 5 — but try to answer first and check after.

1. 他是一个很认真 ___ 学生。 (He's a very conscientious student.)

的 — the curveball. 认真 is the poster child for 地 (认真地复习), so your hand reaches for it — but 地 is chosen by what sits on the right, and here that's a noun, 学生. Never decide from the word in front of the blank.

2. 医生小心 ___ 检查了我的眼睛。 (The doctor carefully examined my eyes.)

地 — the verb 检查 comes next; 小心 is how he goes about it. Compare item 1: the same kind of adjective, a different neighbour on the right, a different character.

3. 我在超市买 ___ 那些水果都很新鲜。 (The fruit I bought at the supermarket is all fresh.)

的 — the whole clause 我在超市买 describes 那些水果: "the fruit that I bought at the supermarket". Long modifier, same rule — noun next → 的.

4. 那只熊猫长 ___ 特别可爱。 (That panda is especially cute.)

得 — 长得 (zhǎng de) = "turns out looking"; 特别可爱 is the verdict that follows the verb. Worth memorising as a chunk: Chinese describes appearance with a verb + 得, where English just uses "is".

5. 大家努力 ___ 复习，因为明天有考试。 (Everyone is revising hard — there's an exam tomorrow.)

地 — 复习 is a verb and it follows the blank, so 努力 is being recast as a manner. (复习得很努力 would be legal too, but that's the after-the-fact version — a different sentence.)

6. Now the whole thing. Same job, one character — but type out the entire sentence with it in place:
那个服务员说话说 ___ 太快了，我没听懂。
(That waiter talked too fast and I didn't catch it.)

那个服务员说话说得太快了，我没听懂。 — 得, because 太快了 is a verdict handed down on the talking after it happened. Two things are worth noticing now that you have had to write the whole sentence rather than one character: 说话 puts an object (话) between the verb and 得, so 说 has to come back for it (说话得太快 ✗), and the second clause needs 没 + 听懂 with no 了 — the understanding is the outcome that failed to land.

7. Whole sentence again — and this one wants two different characters:
他认真 ___ 回答了老师 ___ 问题。
(He answered the teacher's question carefully.)

他认真地回答了老师的问题。 — the same adjective as item 1, and this time it takes 地: what stands to the right is the verb 回答, so 认真 is the manner of answering. Then, four characters later, the same test gives the opposite result — 问题 is a noun, so the glue in front of it is 的. One sentence, both characters, and the only thing that decided each one was the word on its right.

Own it. Write two true sentences about this week: one about something you did well (or badly) — grade it with 得 — and one about something you did carefully / patiently / happily — put the manner in front of the verb with 地. Different verbs in the two sentences.

这个星期我睡觉睡得不太好。昨天晚上我还是认真地准备了明天的考试。

Zhège xīngqī wǒ shuìjiào shuì de bú tài hǎo. Zuótiān wǎnshang wǒ háishì rènzhēn de zhǔnbèi le míngtiān de kǎoshì.

I've slept badly this week. Even so, last night I got ready for tomorrow's exam properly.

Check your two sentences:

  • Look at what sits on the right of each character: a verb after it → 地; a verdict after it, verb in front → 得.
  • In the 得 sentence, if an object stands between the verb and 得, did you repeat the verb? (睡觉睡得不太好 ✓ / 睡觉得不太好 ✗) And does the verdict have its 很 / 太 / 不太?
  • Is the 地 word really a manner — how you did it — and not a time word or a noun? (认真地准备 ✓ / 昨天地准备 ✗)

5. Finale Result complements and all three de in one paragraph

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 他昨天买得那件衬衫很贵。
(Intended: "The shirt he bought yesterday was very expensive.")

他昨天买的那件衬衫很贵。 — the test is what follows the character. Here it's a noun phrase (那件衬衫), so 他昨天买 is a modifier and the glue is 的. 得 would demand a verdict on the buying itself — 他买得很快 "he bought it quickly" — but 很贵 grades the shirt, not the act. Typing 得 for 的 in front of a noun is the single most common slip of the three.

Write the paragraph. A birthday you actually went to — your sister's, a friend's, a colleague's. In four or five sentences: whose birthday it was, something somebody cooked or made and how it turned out, something nobody managed to finish, and the hunt for a present. Your paragraph must use all five of these: 的 · 得 · 地 · 完 · 到.

上个月是我姐姐的生日。她做菜做得特别好吃，可是她做的那个蛋糕太大了，我们没吃完。那天下午我一直在商店里找她的礼物，最后终于买到了一条很漂亮的裙子。回家的时候，她高兴地跟我说了很多话。

Shàng ge yuè shì wǒ jiějie de shēngrì. Tā zuò cài zuò de tèbié hǎochī, kěshì tā zuò de nàge dàngāo tài dà le, wǒmen méi chīwán. Nà tiān xiàwǔ wǒ yìzhí zài shāngdiàn lǐ zhǎo tā de lǐwù, zuìhòu zhōngyú mǎidào le yì tiáo hěn piàoliang de qúnzi. Huí jiā de shíhou, tā gāoxìng de gēn wǒ shuō le hěn duō huà.

Last month was my older sister's birthday. She cooks wonderfully, but the cake she made was too big and we didn't finish it. That afternoon I hunted round the shops for her present, and in the end I managed to find a really pretty skirt. On the way home she talked to me happily about all sorts of things.

Self-check — one pass per character:

  • 的: is a noun what comes next? Long modifiers change nothing — 她做的那个蛋糕 works exactly like 姐姐的生日.
  • 得: directly behind a verb, verdict after it — and if an object stands in the way, is the verb repeated? (做菜做得特别好吃 ✓ / 做菜得特别好吃 ✗)
  • 地: a manner in front, a real verb behind. If a noun follows it, you wanted 的.
  • 完 and 到: welded straight onto their verbs with the object after the pair (吃完蛋糕, 买到礼物) — and under 没 the 了 is gone: 没吃完 ✓ / 没吃完了 ✗.
  • Five different verbs, and is this your birthday party rather than the model's?

Own it. Write three true sentences about your own week: one thing you finished (V + 完), one thing you managed to get or find (V + 到), and one thing you read or listened to but didn't understand (没 + V + 懂). Three different verbs.

这个星期我复习完了第三课。我在超市买到了很新鲜的水果。可是昨天晚上的新闻我没看懂。

Zhège xīngqī wǒ fùxí wán le dì sān kè. Wǒ zài chāoshì mǎidào le hěn xīnxiān de shuǐguǒ. Kěshì zuótiān wǎnshang de xīnwén wǒ méi kàndǒng.

This week I finished revising lesson three. I found some really fresh fruit at the supermarket. But I didn't understand last night's news.

Check your own three sentences:

  • Is each result word welded straight onto its verb, with nothing in between? (复习完 ✓ / 复习了完 ✗)
  • In the 没 sentence, did the 了 disappear? (没看懂 ✓ / 没看懂了 ✗)
  • Did you use 没 — not 不 — for the outcome that failed to land?
  • Does the object come after the whole V+result pair (V + 完 + O), never inside it?
  • Three different verbs, and three things that are actually true about your week?
