What you'll learn

  1. Compound directional complements — 上来, 下去, 进来, 回去… a system, not isolated pairs.
  2. 一边 … 一边 … — doing two things simultaneously.
  3. 就要 … 了 — imminent future ("about to…").

1. Compound directional complements Movement verb + direction + speaker orientation

When a speaker reaches for this: to describe motion with two pieces of direction info — which direction (up/down/in/out/back/over/rising) plus whether it moves toward or away from the speaker.

Set the scene before choosing 来 or 去

Directional complements are easier if you name four things first:

  • Speaker location: where "here" is in the sentence.
  • Mover: the person or thing moving.
  • Target: the place the mover enters, leaves, returns to, crosses toward, or moves up/down from.
  • Direction stem: 上, 下, 进, 出, 回, 过, 起 — the path shape.

Only after that do you pick 来 or 去: motion toward the speaker takes 来; motion away from the speaker takes 去.

The English trap

English piles prepositions: "climbed up onto the roof", "ran back down the hill". Chinese has a tidy two-slot system:

Direction stem (one of 上, 下, 进, 出, 回, 过, 起) + 来/去 (toward me / away from me).

You pick one from column A and one from column B. Remember 来/去 is from the speaker's perspective.

Think of the two slots as a matrix: 7 stems × 2 orientations = 14 complements. Each answers "which direction, and relative to me which way?"

From simple direction to compound direction

The first slot can work alone when the speaker's position is not important: 上楼 go upstairs, 进教室 enter the classroom, 回家 return home.

Add 来/去 when the sentence needs orientation: 上楼来 come upstairs toward me, 上楼去 go upstairs away from me; 进教室来 enter toward where I am, 进教室去 enter away from where I am.

The verb + complement pattern

If there's a main verb (走, 跑, 拿, 带…), it sits before the whole complement:

Main V + stem (上/下/进/出/回/过/起) + 来 / 去

老师走进教室来了。

Lǎoshī zǒu jìn jiàoshì lai le.

teacher / walk / enter / classroom / come / PERF

The teacher walked into the classroom. (Speaker is in the classroom.)

他跑下楼去了。

Tā pǎo xià lóu qu le.

he / run / down / building / go / PERF

He ran downstairs. (Speaker stayed upstairs.)

请把水果拿过来。

Qǐng bǎ shuǐguǒ ná guò lai.

please / [disposal] / fruit / take / across / come

Please bring the fruit over here.

Place objects slide inside

A place object (教室, 楼, 家…) sits between the stem and 来/去, as we saw above: 进教室来, 下楼去.

speaker outside
他跑进教室去了
He ran into the classroom (away from me).
speaker inside
他跑进教室来了
He ran into the classroom (toward me).

Practice

You're upstairs. A friend runs up the stairs to find you. Which is correct?

  • 他跑上楼来了。✓ Up the stairs, toward speaker (upstairs) → 上…来.
  • 他跑上楼去了。✗ 去 = away from speaker — but the speaker is where they're arriving.
  • 他跑下楼来了。✗ 下 = down. The motion is upward.

You're outside the classroom. The teacher walks in. Which describes it from your vantage point?

  • 老师走进教室去了。✓ Into the classroom, away from you (outside) → 进…去.
  • 老师走进教室来了。✗ 来 = toward speaker; you're outside, so the motion is away.
  • 老师走出教室去了。✗ 出 = exit. The teacher is entering.

What does this sentence imply about where the speaker is?
他跑下楼去了。

  • The speaker is downstairs.✗ Then 来 would be used (toward speaker).
  • The speaker is upstairs.✓ 去 = motion away; he went down away from where I am (up).
  • The speaker is outside the building.✗ 下楼 specifically means down within a building.

Personalise it. Pick a building you are in every week — your flat, your office, a gym, a station — and say in the first sentence where you are standing. Then write 3–5 true sentences about people or things moving around you there. Use Verb + 上/下/进/出/回/过 + 来/去 at least three times with three different main verbs, and in at least one sentence put the place word between the stem and 来/去. Every sentence has to be true for you.

我住在五楼，晚上经常在自己的房间里看书。八点的时候，小丽拿着一个大包走进房间来，说这是给我的礼物。我下楼去买牛奶，回来的时候她已经走了。后来妹妹从学校回来了，她把新买的裙子送上楼来，然后拿出来给我看。

Wǒ zhù zài wǔ lóu, wǎnshang jīngcháng zài zìjǐ de fángjiān li kàn shū. Bā diǎn de shíhou, Xiǎolì názhe yí ge dà bāo zǒu jìn fángjiān lai, shuō zhè shì gěi wǒ de lǐwù. Wǒ xià lóu qù mǎi niúnǎi, huílai de shíhou tā yǐjīng zǒu le. Hòulái mèimei cóng xuéxiào huílai le, tā bǎ xīn mǎi de qúnzi sòng shàng lóu lai, ránhòu ná chūlai gěi wǒ kàn.

I live on the fifth floor, and in the evening I usually read in my own room. At eight Xiaoli came into the room carrying a big bag and said it was a present for me. I went down to buy milk, and by the time I got back she had already left. Later my little sister came back from school, brought the new skirt she'd bought up to my floor, and then took it out to show me.

Why these are right: the first sentence fixes the vantage point (I am up in my room), and every 来/去 after it is measured against that one spot — 走进房间来 because she arrives where I am, 下楼去 because that motion leaves it, 送上楼来 because the skirt travels back up to where I am. The place words sit inside the complement (进 + 房间 + 来 · 上 + 楼 + 来), and 了 comes after the whole complement, never inside it.

Check your own sentences:

  • Did you state where you are? 来/去 cannot be graded — or written — without a fixed vantage point.
  • Place word between stem and 来/去: 走进房间来 ✓ · 走进来房间 ✗
  • Is 了 after the whole complement? 走进房间来了 ✓ · 走进了房间来 ✗
  • Three different main verbs in front of the complement (走 · 拿 · 搬 · 送 …), not 走 three times.
  • Go through each 来/去 one at a time: motion toward your spot ⇒ 来, motion away from it ⇒ 去.

2. 一边 … 一边 … Two activities, same time

When a speaker reaches for this: to say two actions are happening at the same time, balanced (neither is just background). "I eat while watching TV." "He sings while cooking."

The English trap

In Lesson 4 you met V₁着 + V₂ — "doing V₂ with V₁ as backdrop". 一边 … 一边 … is similar but different: both actions are on equal footing. Use 一边 when both actions are active and deliberate.

一边 + V₁ , 一边 + V₂

我一边吃饭，一边看电视。

Wǒ yìbiān chī fàn, yìbiān kàn diànshì.

I eat and watch TV at the same time.

他一边走路，一边听音乐。

Tā yìbiān zǒu lù, yìbiān tīng yīnyuè.

He walks while listening to music.

Practice

Which sentence best expresses "She sings while drawing"?

  • 她一边画画儿，一边唱歌。✓ Both actions active and deliberate → 一边…一边….
  • 她一边一边画画儿唱歌。✗ Each 一边 must sit directly before its verb phrase.
  • 她画画儿一边唱歌一边。✗ 一边 precedes V; it doesn't trail after it.

What does this sentence mean?
老师一边走路，一边听音乐。

  • The teacher walks, then listens to music.✗ 一边…一边… = simultaneous, not sequential.
  • The teacher walks and listens to music at the same time.✓ Both actions happen together, each deliberate.
  • The teacher usually walks while listening to music.✗ No habitual/usually marker (平时/常常). Just describes a simultaneous pair.

Build: "He works and drinks coffee at the same time."

Build: "The students eat and watch TV at the same time."

学生 一边 吃饭 一边 看电视

Build: "The teacher draws and teaches the students at the same time."

老师 一边 画画儿 一边 教学生

Fill the blanks: 我___听音乐，___做作业。
(I listen to music while doing homework.)

一边 … 一边 … — marker repeats before each verb phrase, showing the two actions are simultaneous and balanced.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about things you genuinely do two at a time. Use 一边 … 一边 … at least three times with three different pairs of activities (six different verbs in all); make one of the sentences about somebody else you know, and give at least one of them a time word (每天 · 周末 · 晚上 …). Everything must be true.

我早上一边刷牙，一边听新闻。周末在家的时候，我一般一边洗盘子，一边跟奶奶聊天。我姐姐跟我很不一样，她一边看足球比赛，一边写电子邮件。晚上太累的时候，我什么也不想做。

Wǒ zǎoshang yìbiān shuā yá, yìbiān tīng xīnwén. Zhōumò zài jiā de shíhou, wǒ yìbān yìbiān xǐ pánzi, yìbiān gēn nǎinai liáotiān. Wǒ jiějie gēn wǒ hěn bù yíyàng, tā yìbiān kàn zúqiú bǐsài, yìbiān xiě diànzǐ yóujiàn. Wǎnshang tài lèi de shíhou, wǒ shénme yě bù xiǎng zuò.

In the morning I brush my teeth while listening to the news. At weekends when I'm at home I usually wash the dishes while chatting with my grandma. My older sister is very different from me — she watches football matches while writing emails. When I'm too tired in the evening, I don't feel like doing anything.

Why these are right: each 一边 stands immediately in front of its own verb phrase, and the subject (我 · 她) is named once at the very front, before the first 一边 — it is not repeated in the second clause. Both halves of every pair are deliberate activities of equal weight, which is exactly what 一边 … 一边 … claims; the time words (早上 · 周末 · 晚上) sit before the first 一边, where any adverbial belongs.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is each 一边 directly in front of a verb? 一边洗盘子，一边聊天 ✓ · 一边一边洗盘子聊天 ✗
  • Does 一边 ever trail after its verb phrase? 洗盘子一边 ✗ — it only ever leads.
  • Subject once, at the front, ahead of the first 一边 — and the time word ahead of it too.
  • Are both actions really active and deliberate? If one is just the posture or backdrop for the other, Lesson 4's V₁着 + V₂ is the honest choice, not 一边.
  • Three different pairs — recycling 看电视 in every sentence practises the noun, not the pattern.

3. 就要 … 了 "About to…" — imminent future

When a speaker reaches for this: to announce something is about to happen — very soon. "The plane is about to take off", "it's about to rain", "I'm about to leave". Creates urgency or anticipation.

The English trap

English uses "about to", "going to…soon", "on the verge of…". Chinese wraps the verb with a bracket: 就要 … 了. The 就要 sits before the verb; the 了 closes the sentence. Both pieces are needed — drop either and the imminence vanishes.

Subj + 就要 + V + … + 了

飞机就要起飞了，快点儿吧！

Fēijī jiù yào qǐfēi le, kuài diǎnr ba!

plane / about-to / take-off / SOON / hurry / SUGG

The plane is about to take off — hurry up!

外边就要下雨了。

Wàibian jiù yào xià yǔ le.

outside / about-to / rain / SOON

It's about to rain outside.

我就要下班了，你等我一下。

Wǒ jiù yào xiàbān le, nǐ děng wǒ yíxià.

I / about-to / finish-work / SOON / you / wait / me / a-moment

I'm about to get off work — wait a moment for me.

Variants: 快要…了 / 快…了 / 要…了

Several frames express similar imminence:

就要…了
就要起飞了
About to — often implies "right this moment / within minutes". Can pair with specific time.
快(要)…了
快起飞了
About to — a bit softer/vaguer. Cannot take a specific time phrase before it.

他明天就要到北京了。

Tā míngtiān jiù yào dào Běijīng le.

He's arriving in Beijing tomorrow. (specific time 明天 → 就要…了, not 快…了)

Common pitfall

Don't forget the sentence-final 了. Without it, the sentence feels incomplete — 就要 alone can read as "just about to… and then what?". The 了 closes the imminence frame. Also: 快 (without 要) is fine conversationally, but pair it with a concrete imminent event, not an abstract state.

Practice

Build: "The plane is about to take off."

飞机 就要 起飞 了

Build: "It's about to rain outside."

外边 就要 下雨 了

Fill the blank: 我___下班了，你等我一下。
(I'm about to get off work — wait a moment.)

就要 — pairs with the sentence-final 了. 快要 or 快 also work here.

Which sentence is complete?

  • 火车就要开。✗ Missing the sentence-final 了 — the imminence frame needs both pieces.
  • 火车就要开了。✓ 就要 … 了 — "the train is about to depart".
  • 火车了就要开。✗ 了 goes at the END of the sentence, not in the middle.

What does this mean?
他明天就要到北京了。

  • He arrived in Beijing yesterday.✗ 就要…了 is future imminence, not past.
  • He'll be arriving in Beijing tomorrow.✓ 明天 + 就要 + V + 了 = imminent future pinned to a specific time.
  • He's not coming to Beijing.✗ 就要…了 signals the event WILL happen, not that it won't.

Build: "He's arriving in Beijing tomorrow."

他 明天 就要 到 北京 了

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about things that really are about to happen in your life — the next few days, weeks, months. Use 就要 … 了 at least three times with three different verbs, and give each of those three a concrete time phrase (下个月 · 下个星期 · 六月十号 …). Then add one sentence with 快要 … 了 about something with no fixed date, so the two frames stand side by side. Everything must be true for you.

下个月我就要参加汉语考试了，最近每天晚上都在复习。我哥哥下个星期就要结婚了，我们一家人都很高兴。我们的汉语课六月十号就要结束了，老师说那天大家一起吃饭。我的自行车太旧了，快要坏了，可是我还不想换。

Xià ge yuè wǒ jiù yào cānjiā Hànyǔ kǎoshì le, zuìjìn měi tiān wǎnshang dōu zài fùxí. Wǒ gēge xià ge xīngqī jiù yào jiéhūn le, wǒmen yì jiā rén dōu hěn gāoxìng. Wǒmen de Hànyǔ kè liù yuè shí hào jiù yào jiéshù le, lǎoshī shuō nà tiān dàjiā yìqǐ chī fàn. Wǒ de zìxíngchē tài jiù le, kuài yào huài le, kěshì wǒ hái bù xiǎng huàn.

Next month I'm sitting a Chinese exam, so lately I've been revising every evening. My older brother is getting married next week and the whole family is delighted. Our Chinese class finishes on 10 June — the teacher says we'll all eat together that day. My bike is too old and is about to fall apart, but I still don't want to replace it.

Why these are right: the frame is a bracket, and both ends are load-bearing — 就要 in front of the verb, 了 at the very end of the sentence. Each of the first three has a specific date sitting before 就要, which is exactly the environment 快…了 cannot enter; the bike sentence has no date, so 快要坏了 is the natural frame there. Note 参加 · 结婚 · 结束 · 坏 — four different events, so the pattern is what is being practised, not one noun.

Check your own sentences:

  • Are both halves there? 就要结婚了 ✓ · 就要结婚 ✗ — 就要 on its own leaves the sentence hanging.
  • Is 了 at the very END of the sentence? 我了就要下班 ✗
  • Time phrase before 就要, never after it — and once a time phrase is there, don't swap in 快…了.
  • Is each event genuinely imminent (days or weeks, not "one day")? 就要…了 promises soon.
  • Three different verbs, and every single sentence true about your own next few weeks.

Half past eight in the evening, I realised the supermarket downstairs was about to close. While I was getting dressed I phoned Xiaogang and asked him to come down from upstairs and go with me. Time words — 八点半 · 的时候 · 后来 — are what hold the five sentences together.

  • Directional complements: any place word sits inside the complement (拿上楼来), and 了 follows the whole thing.
  • Can a reader tell where you are standing in each sentence? Every 来/去 has to be answerable from that.
  • Does it read as one evening — clock, phone call, shop, home — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

3. 我同学一边做作业，一边喝咖啡。
4. 快一点儿，电影就要开始了。

1. Fúwùyuán bǎ yǐnliào ná guòlai le. 2. Nǎinai zǒu jìn fángjiān lai le. 3. Wǒ tóngxué yìbiān zuò zuòyè, yìbiān hē kāfēi. 4. Kuài yìdiǎnr, diànyǐng jiù yào kāishǐ le.

1. The waiter brought the drinks over. 2. Grandma came into the room. 3. My classmate does his homework while drinking coffee. 4. Hurry up — the film is about to start.

Why these are right: (1) 过来 = across, toward me; with 把 the object moves in front of the verb and the whole complement stays welded to 拿. (2) The English "into the room" is one prepositional phrase, but Chinese splits it — 进 + 房间 + 来 — and 来 is only correct because the speaker is the one already inside. (3) Two deliberate activities of equal weight, so each verb phrase takes its own 一边, with the subject stated once at the front. (4) 就要 leans on the verb and 了 closes the sentence; drop the 了 and the urgency behind "hurry up" disappears.

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: is the complement 过来 (not just 来)? "Over here" needs the 过 stem as well as the orientation.
  • Sentence 2: 房间 inside the complement — 走进来房间 ✗ — and 来 chosen because you are in the room.
  • Sentence 3: two 一边, each leading its own verb phrase; 同学 stated once, before the first one.
  • Sentence 4: 了 at the end, 就要 before 开始. Neither piece is optional.
  • Did every word listed after 用 actually make it into your sentence?
