## Grammar introduced

### 1. 把字句 3: A 把 B + V + result/direction complement — Disposal + what became of B

**When a speaker reaches for this:** when they want to say *how the object ended up* after the action — not just "where" (L11) or "done" (L10), but **in what shape**. Did you finish writing it (写**完**)? Did you cook it and have it ready (做**好**)? Did you bring it back (拿**回来**)? The 把 frame is where Chinese collects "what happened + what state it's in now".
 

### The cognitive picture — 把 as the full disposal story

You've met 把 three times now. Each time adds a new slot at the *end*:

- **L10**: 把 + B + V + 了 → "grab B, do V, done." 我把门关了.

- **L11**: 把 + B + V + 在/到 + place → "grab B, move it, and it ended up here." 我把书放在桌子上.

- **L13**: 把 + B + V + *anything* that shows result or direction → "grab B, do V, and *this* is the state now." 我把作业写完了 (writing → finished). 他把礼物拿回来了 (taking → brought back here).

Temporal sequence again: grab → act → *result/arrival*. The sentence walks through the event in order.

### The English trap

English fuses result into the verb ("I *finished* writing", "I *brought* it back"). Chinese keeps them separate — a plain action verb (写, 拿, 买) plus a complement that declares the outcome (完, 好, 回来, 出去). Your English brain will try to pack it all into one word; resist. Pick the action, then pick the result.

The 把 frame shown as a full timeline. Each slot is one beat of the event.

### With a *result* complement

我把作业写完了。

Wǒ bǎ zuòyè xiě wán le.

I / [disposal] / homework / write / finish / PERF

I finished the homework.

他把饭做好了。

Tā bǎ fàn zuò hǎo le.

he / [disposal] / meal / make / to-good / PERF

He got the meal ready.

我真怕他累坏了。

Wǒ zhēn pà tā lèi huài le.

I / really / fear / he / tired / to-broken / PERF

I'm really afraid he'll wear himself out. *(累坏 = "tired-to-broken", a resultative pair even without 把 — the object is implicit "himself".)*

### With a *direction* complement

Now fold in Lesson 12's compound directionals. Notice: the speaker's orientation (来/去) is still part of the result — it says where B ended up *relative to me*.

哥哥买回一个西瓜来了。

Gēge mǎi huí yí ge xīguā lai le.

elder-brother / buy / back / one / M / watermelon / come / PERF

Older brother bought a watermelon and brought it home. *(No 把 here — B is indefinite "a watermelon", so plain V + direction works.)*

请把礼物拿出来。

Qǐng bǎ lǐwù ná chūlai.

please / [disposal] / gift / take / out-come

Please take the gift out (so I can see it).

明天我们一起把礼物送过去。

Míngtiān wǒmen yìqǐ bǎ lǐwù sòng guòqu.

tomorrow / we / together / [disposal] / gift / deliver / across-go

Tomorrow we'll take the gift over (to them) together.

### Definite vs indefinite B

Use 把 when B is *specific and known* (the gift, the computer, the homework). When B is a brand-new indefinite ("a watermelon", "some gifts"), you can skip 把 and put B *between* the stem and 来/去: 哥哥买回一个西瓜来. Rule of thumb: if English would use "the" — reach for 把. If it would use "a" — plain V + direction is fine.

定 (the) → use 把

他把西瓜买回来了

He brought the watermelon back (the one we discussed).

不定 (a) → skip 把

他买回一个西瓜来

He bought a watermelon and brought it home (news).

### Practice

What's the end state in 他把礼物送过去了?

- He is bringing the gift toward the speaker.✗ That would be 送过来. 过去 = away from speaker.

### 2. 先 … 再/又 … 然后 … — Sequencing steps in order

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to lay out a sequence — "first this, then that, then the other". Directions, routines, plans, recipes.
 

### The English trap

English lists with "first… then… after that…". Chinese uses a trio: 先 (first) — 再 or 又 (next) — 然后 (after that). They don't *replace* verbs; they sit *before* each verb like signposts on a timeline.

**再 vs 又 here:** 再 = next step still to come (future), 又 = next step already happened (past). Use 再 for plans, 又 for narration.

我先写作业，再吃饭，然后看电视。

Wǒ xiān xiě zuòyè, zài chī fàn, ránhòu kàn diànshì.

I'll do my homework first, then eat, and after that watch TV.

他先去了商店，又去了书店，然后回家了。

Tā xiān qù le shāngdiàn, yòu qù le shūdiàn, ránhòu huí jiā le.

He went to the shop first, then to the bookstore, and after that went home. *(All past — 又 not 再.)*

plan (future) — 再

我先吃饭，再休息

I'll eat first, then rest. (Still to come.)

narration (past) — 又

我先吃了饭，又休息了

I ate first, then rested. (Already done.)
