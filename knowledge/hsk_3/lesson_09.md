## Grammar introduced

### 1. A 跟 B + 一样 (+ Adj) — "A is the same as B" / "A is just as Adj as B"

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to say two things are *equivalent* on some dimension — "my phone is the same as yours", "she speaks Chinese like a native", "today is just as cold as yesterday". It's the *equality* counterpart to 比 (which marks *difference*).
 

### The English trap

English uses "same as", "like", or "as … as …". Chinese has one tidy frame: A 跟 B 一样 (plain equivalence) or A 跟 B 一样 + Adj (equally Adj). Do **not** try to use 比 here — 比 compares, 跟…一样 equates.

我的手机跟你的一样。

Wǒ de shǒujī gēn nǐ de yíyàng.

My phone is the same as yours.

她的汉语说得跟中国人一样好。

Tā de Hànyǔ shuō de gēn Zhōngguórén yíyàng hǎo.

her / Chinese / speak-DEG / same-as / Chinese-person / equally / good

She speaks Chinese as well as a native.

今天跟昨天一样冷。

Jīntiān gēn zuótiān yíyàng lěng.

today / same-as / yesterday / equally / cold

Today is just as cold as yesterday.

### Negation: 不一样 or 跟…不一样

To say two things are *different*, put 不 before 一样. You can also negate the whole equivalence with 不 placed before 跟 (less common).

我的想法跟他不一样。

Wǒ de xiǎngfǎ gēn tā bù yíyàng.

My idea is different from his.

跟…一样 (equivalence)

A 跟 B 一样 (+ Adj)

A and B are the same (on that dimension).

比 (difference)

A 比 B + Adj

A is more Adj than B.

### Common pitfall

The adjective after 一样 is **not** preceded by 很/非常 — just like in 比 sentences, you don't stack intensifiers. **跟他一样很好* (✗) → 跟他一样好 (✓).

### 2. A 比 B + Adj + degree — "A is X-er than B by this much"

**When a speaker reaches for this:** they're comparing A to B and want to say *how much* bigger/better/hotter A is. Not just "A is bigger" — but "a bit bigger" (一点儿 / 一些) or "a lot bigger" (得多 / 多了).
 

### The English trap

English puts the intensifier **before** the adjective: "A is *a bit* bigger than B" / "A is *much* bigger". Chinese puts the degree word **after**: A 比 B 大 + 一点儿 / 得多. Do *not* try to use 很, 非常, or 太 in a 比-sentence — that's ungrammatical. Only comparison-specific degree words work.

A 比 B 贵得多 / 多了

A is much more expensive than B.

他的汉语比我好多了。

Tā de Hànyǔ bǐ wǒ hǎo duō le.

His Chinese is much better than mine.

今天比昨天冷一点儿。

Jīntiān bǐ zuótiān lěng yìdiǎnr.

Today is a bit colder than yesterday.

### Don't stack 很 with 比

**他比我很高* (✗) is wrong. With 比 you can't use 很/非常/太 — use 一点儿, 一些, 得多, or 多了 to express degree instead. If you want to say "A is just generally tall", drop 比 entirely.

### 3. Approximate numbers: 几 and 多 — "A few", "more than"

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to give a rough number — not exact, but in the ballpark. "A few days", "twenty-some people", "more than three hours".
 

### The English trap

English has messy ways to do this ("a few", "-ish", "or so"). Chinese is tidier:

- 几 before a measure word = "a few" (2–9, vague). 几个人 = a few people.

- 多 after a number = "more than / -something". 三十多岁 = thirtysomething. 一个多小时 = more than an hour.

Where 多 goes matters: put it **after the number**, before the measure word (for numbers that don't end in zero), or before the measure word (for round tens).

他几岁了？

Tā jǐ suì le?

How old is he? (For children: 几 doubles as "what small number" and "a few".)

我有几个中国朋友。

Wǒ yǒu jǐ ge Zhōngguó péngyou.

I have a few Chinese friends.

这个东西三十多块钱。

Zhège dōngxi sānshí duō kuài qián.

This thing costs thirty-something kuài.

我等了一个多小时。

Wǒ děng le yí ge duō xiǎoshí.

I waited for more than an hour.

多 after round ten

三十多个人

More than 30 people (31–39).

多 after number+measure

三个多小时

More than 3 hours (3 + part of another).
