What you'll learn

  1. 又 A 又 B — "both A and B" for listing qualities.
  2. V₁着 (O) + V₂ (O) — doing V₂ while V₁.
  3. 了 for change — sentence-final 了 signalling "things are different now".
  4. 越来越 + Adj — "more and more…".

1. 又 … 又 … Listing two qualities that coexist

When a speaker reaches for this: to say something or someone has two qualities at the same time — "both X and Y". Think English both tall and pretty, cheap and tasty.

The English trap

English uses and once: "tall and pretty". Chinese brackets each adjective with 又: 又高又漂亮. Forgetting the second 又 is the single most common mistake.

又 + Adj₁ + 又 + Adj₂

这个西瓜又大又红。

Zhège xīguā yòu dà yòu hóng.

this / watermelon / both / big / and / red

This watermelon is both big and red.

外边又黑又冷。

Wàibian yòu hēi yòu lěng.

It's dark and cold outside.

Practice

Build: "The things at this supermarket are both cheap and good."

这家超市的 东西 又 便宜 又 好

Fill the blank: 她 ___ ，大家都喜欢她。
(She is both clever and warm / hard-working — everyone likes her.)

e.g. 又聪明又热情 — bracket each adjective with 又.

Which sentence is correct?

  • 这个蛋糕又便宜好吃。✗ Second 又 is missing — you need 又 before each adjective.
  • 这个蛋糕又便宜又好吃。✓ 又 Adj₁ 又 Adj₂ — cheap and tasty.
  • 这个蛋糕便宜又好吃。✗ First 又 is missing — the pattern needs 又 in front of both adjectives.

Build: "That waitress is both young and pretty."

那个 服务员 又 年轻 又 漂亮

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences describing real things around you right now — the place you live, a shop or a station you pass every day, what you usually eat, somebody in your family. Use 又 … 又 … in at least three of them, with six different adjectives in total, and make one sentence describe a person rather than a thing. Every sentence has to be true for you.

我住的地方又安静又方便，走十分钟就到地铁站。楼下的小超市东西又新鲜又便宜，我几乎每天早上都去那儿买鸡蛋和面包。我姐姐又高又瘦，说话又快又清楚，跟她打电话总是很有意思。只有一件事不太好：我的房间夏天又小又热。

Wǒ zhù de dìfang yòu ānjìng yòu fāngbiàn, zǒu shí fēnzhōng jiù dào dìtiězhàn. Lóuxià de xiǎo chāoshì dōngxi yòu xīnxiān yòu piányi, wǒ jīhū měi tiān zǎoshang dōu qù nàr mǎi jīdàn hé miànbāo. Wǒ jiějie yòu gāo yòu shòu, shuōhuà yòu kuài yòu qīngchu, gēn tā dǎ diànhuà zǒngshì hěn yǒu yìsi. Zhǐyǒu yí jiàn shì bú tài hǎo: wǒ de fángjiān xiàtiān yòu xiǎo yòu rè.

The place I live is both quiet and convenient — ten minutes on foot and you're at the subway station. The little supermarket downstairs has things that are both fresh and cheap; nearly every morning I go there for eggs and bread. My older sister is tall and thin, and she talks fast and clearly — ringing her is always fun. Only one thing isn't great: my room is small and hot in summer.

Why these are right: every adjective gets its own 又 (又安静又方便 · 又快又清楚), and each pair pulls in the same direction — 又…又… groups qualities into one impression, so praise and complaint can't share a pair. The adjectives stay bare: 又 is already doing the work a 很 would normally do.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is there a 又 in front of both adjectives? 又高又瘦 ✓ · 又高瘦 / 高又瘦 ✗
  • No 很 / 非常 inside the pattern: 又很快又很清楚 ✗
  • Do the two qualities point the same way? 又便宜又不好吃 reads as a contradiction — use 可是 for that.
  • Six different adjectives across your sentences — recycling 好 and 大 hides the pattern from you.
  • Is at least one sentence about a person, and is every sentence actually true of your own surroundings?

2. V₁着 (O) + V₂ (O) Doing V₂ while V₁ — the accompanying action

Bridge from Lesson 3

Lesson 3 used Place + V着 + NP to freeze a scene: 桌子上放着一本书. Lesson 4 keeps the same held-state idea, but now the V着 phrase becomes the background for another action: 坐着看书 = sitting is held while reading happens. Same 着, new sentence job.

When a speaker reaches for this: to describe two actions going on at the same time, where V₁ is the ongoing state/posture and V₂ is the main activity. English usually uses -ing or while: "standing while chatting", "smiling as she spoke".

The English trap

English learners instinctively try to string verbs with "and" ("she smiled and talked"), but that loses the simultaneity. Chinese uses 着 on V₁ to say "V₁ is the backdrop, V₂ is what she's actually doing." Order: backdrop first (with 着), foreground activity second.

Why 着? (image schema)

着 freezes V₁ into a held state/picture — a still frame the main action plays out against. 笑着说话 = "smiling-as-backdrop, speaking-as-action".

V₁ + 着 + (O₁) + V₂ + (O₂)

她总是笑着跟客人说话。

Tā zǒngshì xiàozhe gēn kèrén shuōhuà.

she / always / smile-着 / with customers / speak

She always smiles while talking to customers.

弟弟吃着苹果写作业。

Dìdi chīzhe píngguǒ xiě zuòyè.

younger-brother / eat-着 / apple / write / homework

My little brother is doing his homework while eating an apple.

Practice

Build: "Many people are standing at the door holding flowers."

很多人 拿 着 鲜花 站 在 门口

Complete: 我喜欢 ___ 。
(I like to listen to music while doing homework.)

e.g. 听着歌写作业 / 听着音乐写作业.

Which sentence means "They watch TV while eating cake"?

  • 他们吃着蛋糕看电视。✓ V₁着 (吃着蛋糕) = backdrop; V₂ (看电视) = main action.
  • 他们吃蛋糕看着电视。✗ 着 is on the wrong verb — the foreground action (看电视) shouldn't take 着 here.
  • 他们看电视吃着蛋糕。✗ The 着-verb (backdrop) must come first, not after the main verb.

What does this sentence mean?
老师站着上课。

  • The teacher stops to teach class.✗ 站 means "stand", not "stop".
  • The teacher teaches class while standing.✓ 站着 = standing (backdrop), 上课 = teaching (main action).
  • The teacher stands up and leaves class.✗ No "leave" here — 上课 is "to teach / have class".

Build: "He is sitting and reading a book."

他 坐 着 看 书 呢

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about things you really do at the same time as something else — at your desk, on the train, in the kitchen, at the weekend. Every sentence uses V₁着 (O) + V₂ (O), with three different V₁着 verbs, and at least one sentence must be about somebody else, not you.

在公司我常常站着开会，坐一上午以后腿很累。回到家我喜欢听着音乐做菜，做完了才看手机。我妈妈总是拿着筷子跟我打电话，我一听就知道她在吃东西。周末我和朋友喝着咖啡聊天，一聊就是两三个小时。

Zài gōngsī wǒ chángcháng zhànzhe kāihuì, zuò yí shàngwǔ yǐhòu tuǐ hěn lèi. Huí dào jiā wǒ xǐhuan tīngzhe yīnyuè zuò cài, zuò wán le cái kàn shǒujī. Wǒ māma zǒngshì názhe kuàizi gēn wǒ dǎ diànhuà, wǒ yì tīng jiù zhīdào tā zài chī dōngxi. Zhōumò wǒ hé péngyou hēzhe kāfēi liáotiān, yì liáo jiù shì liǎng-sān ge xiǎoshí.

At work I often stand while we have meetings — after sitting all morning my legs ache. Once I'm home I like to cook with music on, and only look at my phone when I've finished. My mum always rings me holding her chopsticks; the moment I hear her I know she's eating. At the weekend a friend and I chat over coffee, and once we start it's two or three hours.

Why these are right: in each one the 着-verb is the backdrop that is being held (站 · 听 · 拿 · 喝) and the second verb is what the person is actually doing (开会 · 做菜 · 打电话 · 聊天). The backdrop comes first, 着 is welded to it, and its object sits between 着 and the main verb: 听着音乐做菜. One subject runs both verbs.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is 着 on the held verb, and is that verb first? 拿着筷子打电话 ✓ · 打电话拿着筷子 ✗ (that flips which one is the backdrop).
  • V₁'s object goes between 着 and V₂ — never after V₂.
  • One subject for both halves: 着 links two things the same person is doing at once, not two people.
  • No 了 or 过 on the 着-verb: 听了着音乐 ✗
  • Three different V₁ verbs — the posture and holding verbs (站 · 坐 · 拿 · 听 · 喝) are the natural ones.

3. Sentence-final 了 — change "Things are different now"

When a speaker reaches for this: to report a change from how things were. "I'm hungry now (wasn't before)." "It's not cold anymore." The 了 sits at the end of the sentence (different from the aspect 了 attached directly to a verb).

The English trap

English uses a new adverb ("now", "anymore", "these days") to signal change. Chinese uses a tiny particle — 了 — at the very end. Miss it and you miss the "but before, it wasn't so" layer of meaning.

Sentence-final 了 = "boundary crossed into a new state". The reader should infer: "so it wasn't this way before."

上个月很冷，现在天气不那么冷了。

Shàng ge yuè hěn lěng, xiànzài tiānqì bú nàme lěng le.

Last month was cold — it's not that cold anymore.

我现在喜欢这里了。

Wǒ xiànzài xǐhuan zhèlǐ le.

I / now / like / here / LE

I like it here now (I didn't use to).

Keep the 了 map visible

Open the three 了s appendix before you practise this section. Here, the 了 is sentence-final change-of-state 了, not verb-了 for completed action and not the V了...就 sequence from Lesson 2.

Practice

What does 我现在没钱了 actually imply?

  • I've never had money.✗ 了 implies a change of state — so before was different.
  • I used to have money; now I don't.✓ The change meaning is in the sentence-final 了.
  • I sometimes have no money.✗ Not the meaning — 了 locates a specific shift.

Fill the blank: 我有点儿饿 ___ ，我们去吃饭吧。
(I'm a bit hungry now — let's go eat.)

了 — sentence-final 了 marks "wasn't hungry before, am now".

What change does this sentence signal?
天气暖和了。

  • The weather is always warm.✗ 了 specifically signals a change, not a constant state.
  • The weather has become warm (it wasn't before).✓ Sentence-final 了 = crossed a boundary into a new state.
  • The weather will be warm.✗ 了 marks a change that has already happened, not a prediction.

Build: "I like it here now (I didn't before)."

我 现在 喜欢 这里 了

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about ways your own life is different now from a year or two ago — food, sleep, money, a habit, where you live, a language. At least three sentences must end with the change 了, use three different verbs or adjectives, and make one of them a negative change with 不 … 了 or 没有 … 了.

去年我一句中文也听不懂，现在我能看懂饭馆的菜单了。以前我每天喝三杯咖啡，现在我不喝那么多了。搬家以后我的房间大了，可是上班的路也远了。只有一件事跟以前一样：我还是不太会做菜。

Qùnián wǒ yí jù Zhōngwén yě tīng bu dǒng, xiànzài wǒ néng kàndǒng fànguǎn de càidān le. Yǐqián wǒ měi tiān hē sān bēi kāfēi, xiànzài wǒ bù hē nàme duō le. Bānjiā yǐhòu wǒ de fángjiān dà le, kěshì shàngbān de lù yě yuǎn le. Zhǐyǒu yí jiàn shì gēn yǐqián yíyàng: wǒ háishi bú tài huì zuò cài.

A year ago I couldn't understand a word of Chinese; now I can read a restaurant menu. I used to drink three coffees a day — I don't drink that many anymore. Since moving, my room is bigger, but the trip to work is longer too. Only one thing is the same as before: I still can't really cook.

Why these are right: each 了 sits at the very end of its clause, after the object, and each sentence carries a "before" the 了 can push against (去年 · 以前 · 搬家以后). 房间大了 doesn't describe an action — it says the room is bigger than it was. And 不喝那么多了 is "not anymore": drop the 了 and you get a flat statement with no history in it.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is 了 the last thing in the clause, after the object? 我能看懂菜单了 ✓ · 我能看懂了菜单 ✗
  • Is there a real "before" behind each sentence? If nothing actually changed, the 了 is wrong.
  • Negative change = 不 … 了 ("not anymore"), not bare 不.
  • Don't confuse it with verb-了: 我喝了三杯咖啡 reports a finished act; 我不喝咖啡了 reports a new state of affairs.
  • With an adjective, drop 很 — 房间大了 ✓ · 房间很大了 reads as a plain description, not a change.

4. 越来越 + Adj "More and more…" — trending change over time

When a speaker reaches for this: to note a trend — something's increasing in degree as time passes. "Colder and colder." "More and more people."

Common pitfall

Don't pair 越来越 with another degree adverb — *越来越很冷 (✗) is wrong. 越来越 already carries the "growing" intensity; stacking 很 / 非常 / 特别 is redundant and ungrammatical.

越来越 + Adj / mental verb

我认识的汉字越来越多。

Wǒ rènshi de Hànzì yuè lái yuè duō.

I know more and more characters.

你越来越漂亮。

Nǐ yuè lái yuè piàoliang.

You get prettier and prettier.

Practice

Which is grammatical?

  • 最近天气越来越热。✓ 越来越 + bare adjective.
  • 最近天气越来越很热。✗ Don't add 很/非常 — 越来越 already intensifies.
  • 最近天气热越来越。✗ 越来越 must precede the adjective, not follow it.

Fill: 我 ___ 运动。
(I like exercising more and more.)

越来越喜欢 — mental verbs work the same as adjectives with 越来越.

Build: "You're getting prettier and prettier."

你 越来越 漂亮 了

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about trends you can actually watch happening in your own life — your Chinese, your sleep, prices where you shop, how full your calendar is, the season. Use 越来越 + Adj at least three times with three different adjectives, and make one sentence use 越来越 with a mental verb (喜欢 · 想 · 怕 · 关心).

最近公司的事越来越多，我睡觉的时间越来越少。奇怪的是，我越来越喜欢早上六点起床，因为那个时候楼里很安静。我家附近的水果也越来越贵了，一个苹果要三块钱。只有一件事让我高兴：中文的电视节目我越来越能看懂了。

Zuìjìn gōngsī de shì yuè lái yuè duō, wǒ shuìjiào de shíjiān yuè lái yuè shǎo. Qíguài de shì, wǒ yuè lái yuè xǐhuan zǎoshang liù diǎn qǐchuáng, yīnwèi nàge shíhou lóu lǐ hěn ānjìng. Wǒ jiā fùjìn de shuǐguǒ yě yuè lái yuè guì le, yí ge píngguǒ yào sān kuài qián. Zhǐyǒu yí jiàn shì ràng wǒ gāoxìng: Zhōngwén de diànshì jiémù wǒ yuè lái yuè néng kàndǒng le.

Lately there's more and more on at work and less and less time to sleep. Oddly, I like getting up at six more and more, because the building is quiet at that hour. The fruit near my place is getting more and more expensive too — one apple costs three kuai. Only one thing makes me happy: I understand more and more of the Chinese TV programmes.

Why these are right: 越来越 sits directly in front of the adjective and nothing else joins it — the "increasingly" is already inside it, so 很/非常/特别 have no room. 越来越喜欢 shows the same slot taking a mental verb, with its whole phrase (早上六点起床) trailing behind. The optional sentence-final 了 in 越来越贵了 just adds "…and that's different from before".

Check your own sentences:

  • No second degree word inside: 越来越忙 ✓ · 越来越很忙 ✗
  • 越来越 comes before what it intensifies: 事多越来越 ✗
  • Is there a real trend over time? For a one-off comparison you want 比 or plain 很, not 越来越.
  • With a mental verb, the object follows the verb as usual: 越来越喜欢早上六点起床.
  • Three different adjectives — using 多 and 好 twice each tells you nothing new.

Mixed practice All four patterns together

Build: "She always smiles when talking to customers."

她 总是 笑 着 跟 客人 说话

What does this sentence imply?
最近学汉语的人越来越多了。

  • A lot of people have always studied Chinese.✗ 越来越 + 了 signals an ongoing increase, not a constant.
  • More and more people are studying Chinese these days.✓ 越来越多 = growing trend; final 了 reinforces the change.
  • Fewer people are studying Chinese.✗ 多 means "many / more", not fewer.

Build: "He works both conscientiously and enthusiastically."

他 工作 又 认真 又 热情

Fill the blank: 冬天到了，天气 ___ 。
(Winter is here — the weather is getting colder and colder.)

越来越冷了 — sentence-final 了 pairs naturally with 越来越 to flag the change.

Write the paragraph. A small restaurant opened downstairs from you three months ago, and you have watched it fill up. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all four structures of this lesson at least once each: 又 … 又 …, V₁着 + V₂, sentence-final 了 for change, and 越来越 + Adj. Cover: what the place is like, what the owner does while he waits for people, what is different for you since it opened, and how busy it has become.

我家楼下三个月前开了一家小饭馆，菜又便宜又好吃。开饭馆的人很年轻，他常常拿着菜单在门口等客人，笑着请大家进去坐。以前我晚上不想做菜，常常吃面包，现在我几乎每天都在那儿吃饭了。去的人越来越多，上个星期六我等了半个小时才坐下。那个年轻人也越来越忙，可是他跟大家说话的时候还是又热情又认真。

Wǒ jiā lóuxià sān ge yuè qián kāi le yì jiā xiǎo fànguǎn, cài yòu piányi yòu hǎochī. Kāi fànguǎn de rén hěn niánqīng, tā chángcháng názhe càidān zài ménkǒu děng kèrén, xiàozhe qǐng dàjiā jìnqù zuò. Yǐqián wǒ wǎnshang bù xiǎng zuò cài, chángcháng chī miànbāo, xiànzài wǒ jīhū měi tiān dōu zài nàr chīfàn le. Qù de rén yuè lái yuè duō, shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ děng le bàn ge xiǎoshí cái zuòxià. Nàge niánqīng rén yě yuè lái yuè máng, kěshì tā gēn dàjiā shuōhuà de shíhou háishi yòu rèqíng yòu rènzhēn.

A little restaurant opened downstairs from me three months ago, and the food is both cheap and good. The man who runs it is young — he's often at the door with the menu in his hand waiting for customers, smiling as he invites everyone in. I used not to feel like cooking in the evening and would just eat bread; now I eat there almost every day. More and more people go, and last Saturday I waited half an hour before I got a seat. That young man is busier and busier too, but when he talks to people he is still both warm and attentive.

Why this works as a paragraph: the four structures are doing four different jobs, not lining up for inspection. 又便宜又好吃 and 又热情又认真 describe; 拿着菜单…等客人 and 笑着请大家进去坐 freeze what is going on at one moment; 现在…吃饭了 reports what has changed for the writer; and 越来越多／越来越忙 track the three months. The time words (三个月前 · 以前 · 现在 · 上个星期六) are what hold the sentences together.

Check your own paragraph:

  • 又…又…: a 又 in front of each adjective, and no 很 wedged in.
  • 着: held action first, 着 stuck to it, its object between 着 and the main verb — 拿着菜单等客人 ✓ · 等客人拿着菜单 ✗
  • Change 了 at the very end of its clause, with an explicit "before" (以前…) for it to push against.
  • 越来越 + bare adjective; the final 了 is optional and only underlines the change.
  • Does it read as one story — time words and 可是 joining it — rather than four grammar demos in a row?

Four errors. Each sentence below contains exactly one mistake, and all four are mistakes with this lesson's structures. Rewrite all four correctly in the box, then check.
1. 我们学校的图书馆又新干净。
2. 他看电视吃着面包。
3. 我现在喜欢了这个城市。
4. 最近我姐姐越来越很忙。

1. 我们学校的图书馆又新又干净。
2. 他吃着面包看电视。
3. 我现在喜欢这个城市了。
4. 最近我姐姐越来越忙了。

1. Wǒmen xuéxiào de túshūguǎn yòu xīn yòu gānjìng. 2. Tā chīzhe miànbāo kàn diànshì. 3. Wǒ xiànzài xǐhuan zhège chéngshì le. 4. Zuìjìn wǒ jiějie yuè lái yuè máng le.

1. Our school library is both new and clean. 2. He watches TV while eating bread. 3. I like this city now (I didn't before). 4. My older sister has been getting busier and busier lately.

  1. 又新干净 → 又新又干净. English spends one "and" on two adjectives; Chinese spends one 又 on each. Dropping the second 又 is the commonest slip in this pattern, and it leaves 新 and 干净 sitting side by side with nothing joining them.
  2. 看电视吃着面包 → 吃着面包看电视. 着 marks the backdrop, and the backdrop is stated first. As written, the sentence claims that eating bread is what he is doing against a TV-watching background — the reverse of what is meant. Move the 着-verb to the front, object and all.
  3. 喜欢了这个城市 → 喜欢这个城市了. This 了 is not the past-tense marker English speakers reach for; it is the sentence-final change particle, so it goes after the object, at the very end. Stuck to the verb it would try to report a completed action — and 喜欢 is a state, not something you finish.
  4. 越来越很忙 → 越来越忙了. 越来越 already means "increasingly", so a second degree word has no job to do. Add the sentence-final 了 instead if you want the change spelled out.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Does every adjective in your 又-sentence have its own 又 in front of it?
  • Is the 着-verb first, with its object right behind 着 and the main verb last?
  • Is the change 了 at the very end of the clause, after the object — never glued to the verb?
  • Did you delete 很 next to 越来越 rather than moving it somewhere else?
  • Did you rewrite the whole sentence rather than just naming the fix? Retyping is where the pattern sticks.
