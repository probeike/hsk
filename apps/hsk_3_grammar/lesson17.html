1. 只要 … 就 … "As long as X, Y"

When a speaker reaches for this: to say a single condition is enough — just X, and Y is guaranteed. "As long as you come, I'll be happy." "As long as it's not raining, we'll go."

The English trap

English uses "as long as" (condition) and "only if" (stricter). Chinese sharpens the contrast:

  • 只要 … 就 … → sufficient condition. X alone is enough for Y.
  • 只有 … 才 … → necessary condition. Without X, Y can't happen. (Lesson 19.)

Keep the pair intact: 只要 opens, 就 pivots. Don't mix (*只要…才… ✗).

只要 + condition , 就 + result
如果…就 — normal if/then
如果下雨，我就不去
If it rains, I won't go. Neutral condition; it does not say whether rain is the only issue.
只要…就 — enough condition
只要不下雨，我就去
As long as it doesn't rain, I'll go. Not raining is enough to decide it.

只要你努力，就一定能学好汉语。

Zhǐyào nǐ nǔlì, jiù yídìng néng xuéhǎo Hànyǔ.

only-need / you / work-hard, / then / definitely / able / study-good / Chinese

As long as you work hard, you'll definitely master Chinese.

只要不下雨，我们就去爬山。

Zhǐyào bú xià yǔ, wǒmen jiù qù pá shān.

As long as it's not raining, we'll go hiking.

只要你愿意，谁都有办法帮你。

Zhǐyào nǐ yuànyì, shéi dōu yǒu bànfǎ bāng nǐ.

As long as you're willing, anyone can find a way to help you.

sufficient — 只要…就
只要有时间，我就去
Having time is enough to make me go.
necessary — 只有…才
只有有时间，我才去
I'll only go if I have time (not otherwise).

  • I'll only be happy if you come.✗ That's the strict reading — would need 只有…才…

  • 只要天气好，我们还去爬山。✗ 还 doesn't mark the result of a condition.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about the small conditions that run your own week — the one thing that is enough to make something happen. Use 只要 … 就 … in every sentence, with three different verbs in the 就 clause, and make at least one condition negative (只要不 …). Every sentence has to be true for you.

只要天气不太冷，我早上就走路去上班。只要同事们不太忙，我们中午就一起去楼下吃面条。只要我妹妹给我打电话，我们就能聊一个小时。只要冰箱里还有鸡蛋，我晚上就自己做饭。

Zhǐyào tiānqì bú tài lěng, wǒ zǎoshang jiù zǒulù qù shàngbān. Zhǐyào tóngshìmen bú tài máng, wǒmen zhōngwǔ jiù yìqǐ qù lóuxià chī miàntiáo. Zhǐyào wǒ mèimei gěi wǒ dǎ diànhuà, wǒmen jiù néng liáo yí ge xiǎoshí. Zhǐyào bīngxiāng lǐ hái yǒu jīdàn, wǒ wǎnshang jiù zìjǐ zuò fàn.

As long as the weather isn't too cold, I walk to work in the morning. As long as my colleagues aren't too busy, at noon we go downstairs together for noodles. As long as my sister rings me, we can talk for an hour. As long as there are still eggs in the fridge, I cook for myself in the evening.

Why these are right: each 只要 clause names one thing that is enough on its own — no other conditions are implied — and 就 sits inside the result clause, after its subject and immediately in front of the verb (我早上就走路去上班). Negation belongs inside the condition (只要天气不太冷), not in the 就 clause.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is 就 after the subject of the result clause and right before the verb? 我就走路去 ✓ · 就我走路去 ✗
  • Did you resist 才? 只要 pairs with 就; 才 belongs to 只有 (Lesson 19).
  • Is the condition really sufficient — one thing that settles it — rather than a neutral "if" that would take 如果?
  • Three different verbs in the 就 clauses; the pattern only sticks if the verb keeps changing.
  • Is every sentence actually true about your week, not a textbook situation?

2. 介词 关于 "About / regarding" — a front-of-sentence topic preposition

When a speaker reaches for this: to flag the topic of a statement or question — "regarding X, …". 关于 introduces a topic phrase that usually sits at the very front of the clause.

The English trap

English "about" is flexible — it can come anywhere: a book about China, I know about it, about the meeting…. 关于 is stricter:

  • As a topic marker, 关于 + phrase goes at the front of the sentence.
  • As an attributive ("a book about X"), 关于 + phrase + 的 + noun: 关于中国的书.
  • For "I know about X / heard about X", Chinese usually uses 知道/听说 + X directly, not 关于 — don't overreach.
关于 + topic , Subj + comment

关于这个问题，我有一个好办法。

Guānyú zhège wèntí, wǒ yǒu yí ge hǎo bànfǎ.

regarding / this / problem, / I / have / one / M / good / method

Regarding this problem, I have a good solution.

这是一本关于中国文化的书。

Zhè shì yì běn guānyú Zhōngguó wénhuà de shū.

This is a book about Chinese culture.

关于他的爱好，你知道多少？

Guānyú tā de àihào, nǐ zhīdào duōshao?

How much do you know about his hobbies?

关于 vs 对于

Both translate as "about / regarding" in English. Quick split:

  • 关于 — sets a topic. Leads into a discussion of that topic.
  • 对于 — expresses an attitude toward something. 对于这件事，我很生气 (regarding this, I'm angry about it).

For now you only need 关于 productively; just recognize 对于 when you see it.

Correct the English word order

  1. English says "talk about X" with about after the verb.
  2. Do not copy that as 说关于X or 聊天关于X. Chinese prepositions do not trail after the verb.
  3. If you use 关于, front the topic: 关于这件事，我想说几句 — "About this matter, I want to say a few words."
  4. If the verb naturally takes the topic, you can also skip 关于: 我们谈谈这件事 — "Let's talk about this matter."

As for next year's travelling, I still haven't decided which country to go to. As for my new neighbour, all I know is that his surname is Wang — everything else is unclear. I'm watching a film about Chinese music at the moment; I'd never heard any of the songs in it before. As for why I started learning Chinese, friends ask me all the time, and I answer differently every time.

Why these are right: 关于 sets a frame, so the whole 关于-phrase lands ahead of the subject and is closed by a comma; the comment that follows is a complete sentence with its own subject and verb (我还没决定…). The third sentence shows the other job 关于 does — as an attributive it needs 的 to hook the topic onto the noun (关于中国音乐的电影). Note what 关于 never does: it never trails a verb the way English "about" does.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is 关于 in front of its topic, and the whole phrase in front of the subject, with a comma after it?
  • Did you avoid 说关于这件事 / 知道关于他? If the verb takes the topic directly, drop 关于 altogether.
  • Attributive sentences: 关于 + topic + 的 + noun — is the 的 actually there?
  • Could the comment clause stand alone as a sentence if you deleted the 关于 phrase? It should.
  • Four genuinely different topics, and every statement true about you?

3. Disyllabic verb reduplication: ABAB "Just have a little Verb" — softening, tentativeness, brief action

When a speaker reaches for this: to soften a verb — suggest giving something a quick go, a little try, or a brief stint. "Let's have a little chat", "give this a check", "do some exercise" (without making it a whole workout). It's the Chinese equivalent of "have a think / have a look / have a chat".

Deliberate review from Lesson 16

This section repeats ABAB on purpose because health and advice sentences use it constantly: 锻炼锻炼, 检查检查, 休息休息. If Lesson 16 already felt clear, use this section to check two things only: is the verb controllable, and does the whole two-syllable verb repeat as a unit?

The English trap

English softens with words like "a little", "for a bit", "have a …". Chinese softens by repeating the verb. For two-syllable verbs (AB), the pattern is ABAB: 锻炼 → 锻炼锻炼 ("do a bit of exercise"), 检查 → 检查检查 ("give it a check"). English speakers often translate this literally and think it's emphatic ("exercise-exercise!") — it's the opposite. It's a gentle down-scaler.

The whole two-syllable verb repeats as a unit. Unlike monosyllabic reduplication (V → VV, e.g. 看看), you don't insert 一 and you don't pull the syllables apart.

Pattern comparison: AA vs ABAB

Monosyllabic: V → VV or V-一-V
看 → 看看 / 看一看
"Have a look" — brief/tentative action.
Disyllabic: AB → ABAB
锻炼 → 锻炼锻炼
"Do some exercise (a little)" — same softening, different shape.

我们应该多锻炼锻炼身体。

Wǒmen yīnggāi duō duànliàn duànliàn shēntǐ.

we / should / more / exercise-exercise / body

We should do a bit more exercise.

医生会仔细检查检查你的身体。

Yīshēng huì zǐxì jiǎnchá jiǎnchá nǐ de shēntǐ.

doctor / will / carefully / check-check / your / body

The doctor will give you a thorough check.

你能帮助帮助我吗？

Nǐ néng bāngzhù bāngzhù wǒ ma?

you / can / help-help / me / Q

Could you give me a little help?

Which verbs can reduplicate?

Only action/volitional verbs. You can 学习学习 (study a bit), 讨论讨论 (discuss for a bit), 休息休息 (take a little rest). You cannot reduplicate stative verbs (*喜欢喜欢, *知道知道) or adjectives-as-verbs. When in doubt: if the verb describes something someone does, it can probably reduplicate.

  • A past-tense sense — "exercised".✗ Reduplication doesn't mark tense.

This weekend I want to give my room a bit of a clean at home — there's too much stuff on the desk. Why these are right: the whole two-syllable verb repeats as one block (打扫打扫 · 复习复习 · 介绍介绍 · 休息休息), and the object still follows the doubled verb, not the halves. Each one down-scales the action — "a bit of a clean", "a quick go over" — which is exactly what makes 请我的邻居给我介绍介绍 sound like a friendly ask rather than a demand.

Check your own sentences:

  • ABAB, not AABB: 复习复习 ✓ · 复复习习 ✗
  • No 一 wedged inside: 休息一休息 ✗ — the 一 trick is for monosyllabic verbs only (看一看).
  • Object after the whole doubled verb: 打扫打扫房间 ✓ · 打扫房间打扫 ✗
  • Is each verb something you actively do? Stative verbs (知道, 喜欢) can't reduplicate at all.
  • Does the sentence really mean "a little / briefly"? If you mean "a lot", 多 + verb is the honest choice — and is every sentence true of your week?

On the subject of buying a bike, Xiaoli talked it over with me for ages last night. She said that as long as it isn't too expensive, she'll buy one straight away. I said I could first tell her a bit about the two shops near my place. Afterwards my neighbour heard about it too — he said he has an old bicycle, and as long as Xiaoli likes it, he'll give it to her. Why this works as a paragraph: the three structures are doing three different jobs. 关于买自行车这件事 opens the story because it announces what the whole thing is about — sitting ahead of the subject 小丽, never after the verb 聊. 只要…就… then appears twice in two different mouths, each time naming one condition that is enough on its own, with 就 planted right in front of the verb. 介绍介绍 is where the tone softens: offering to do a little introducing is a smaller, friendlier offer than 介绍. The time words (昨天晚上 · 后来 · 星期天早上) are what hold the five sentences together.

Check your own paragraph:

  • 关于: is the topic phrase at the very front, before the subject, with a comma after it?
  • 只要…就…: 就 after the subject of the result clause and directly before the verb — 只要不太贵，就她马上买 ✗
  • Never 只要 … 才 …. 才 answers to 只有, and it would change a sufficient condition into a necessary one.
  • ABAB doubled as one block with the object behind it: 介绍介绍两家商店 ✓
  • Does it read as one story — time words and 后来 joining it — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

1. Zhǐyào nǐ yǒu shíjiān, wǒmen jiù qù kàn diànyǐng. 2. Guānyú wǒ de xīn gōngzuò, wǒ xiǎng gēn nǐ shuōshuo. 3. Wǒ hěn lèi, xiǎng xiūxi xiūxi. 4. Tā gěi wǒ kàn le jǐ zhāng guānyú Běijīng de zhàopiàn.

1. As long as you have time, we'll go and see a film. 2. About my new job — I'd like to have a word with you. 3. I'm very tired; I want to rest for a bit. 4. He showed me a few photos of Beijing.

  1. 才 → 就. 只要 sets up a sufficient condition, and its partner is 就. Pairing it with 才 borrows the partner of 只有 and flips the meaning to "only if" — the strict reading the speaker didn't intend.
  2. 说说关于我的新工作 → 关于我的新工作，我想跟你说说. English lets "about" trail the verb; Chinese prepositions never do. 关于 + topic is a frame that goes to the very front, ahead of the subject.
  3. 休息一休息 → 休息休息. The 一 trick belongs to monosyllabic verbs (看一看, 问一问). A two-syllable verb simply repeats whole: ABAB, nothing inserted, nothing pulled apart.
  4. 关于北京照片 → 关于北京的照片. Once 关于 + topic is modifying a noun rather than framing a clause, it is an attributive and needs 的 to attach.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Is every 只要 answered by 就, and is that 就 in front of the verb rather than in front of the subject?
  • Does each 关于 phrase stand at the front of its clause, never after a verb?
  • 关于 + topic + noun: did you put the 的 in?
  • Two-syllable verbs double as ABAB with nothing inside — no 一, no split halves.

Translate — four sentences. Write them in Chinese. The structure in brackets is compulsory, and every word listed after 用 must appear.
1. As long as my mum agrees, I'll bring the puppy home. [只要 … 就 …] · 用: 同意 · 小狗 · 带
2. As for winter clothes, my mother has already decided. [fronted 关于 topic] · 用: 关于 · 冬天 · 衣服 · 决定
3. He gave me a book about world history. [关于 … 的 + noun] · 用: 关于 · 世界 · 历史 · 送
4. Could you give my homework a quick check? [ABAB reduplication] · 用: 检查检查 · 作业

1. 只要我妈妈同意，我就带小狗回家。
2. 关于冬天的衣服，我妈妈已经决定好了。
3. 他送给我一本关于世界历史的书。
4. 你能帮我检查检查作业吗？

1. Zhǐyào wǒ māma tóngyì, wǒ jiù dài xiǎogǒu huí jiā. 2. Guānyú dōngtiān de yīfu, wǒ māma yǐjīng juédìng hǎo le. 3. Tā sòng gěi wǒ yì běn guānyú shìjiè lìshǐ de shū. 4. Nǐ néng bāng wǒ jiǎnchá jiǎnchá zuòyè ma?

1. As long as my mum agrees, I'll bring the puppy home. 2. As for winter clothes, my mother has already decided. 3. He gave me a book about world history. 4. Could you give my homework a quick check?

Why these are right: (1) one condition is enough, so 只要 opens and 就 pivots — and 就 stands after 我, not before it. (2) The topic is framed, so 关于冬天的衣服 goes to the front of the clause and the comment 我妈妈已经决定好了 follows as a full sentence. (3) Here 关于 is modifying 书, so it needs 的 in front of the noun, and the whole 关于世界历史的 block sits between 一本 and 书 — English word order ("a book about history") is exactly backwards. (4) 检查检查 softens the request into "just a quick look", which is why it fits 你能帮我…吗 so naturally.

  • Sentence 2: 关于 phrase first, comma, then a complete comment clause with its own subject.
  • Sentence 3: measure word, then 关于 … 的, then the head noun — the modifier can never trail 书.
  • Sentence 4: ABAB with the object behind it, and no 一 wedged in the middle.
