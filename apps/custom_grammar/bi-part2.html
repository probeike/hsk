HSK 2 Review · Sheet 01 · 比 part 2

纽约的房子比北京的贵得多！

Niǔyuē de fángzi bǐ Běijīng de guì de duō!

Housing in New York is much more expensive than in Beijing!

What this sheet drills

You already own the basic frame: A + 比 + B + Adj (他比我高). This sheet is entirely about part 2 — the piece you said you're bad at: saying how much more, comparing how something is done (比 + 得), and the "not as … as" family (没有 / 跟…一样). Companion theory: HSK 1/2 sheet 18.

1. The degree slot lives after the adjective 大三岁, not 三岁大

The English trap

English puts the amount before the adjective: "three years older", "a bit colder", "much better". Chinese is the exact mirror image — the adjective comes first and the amount trails after it: 大三岁, 冷一点儿, 好得多. Every mistake you make in 比 sentences is this one mistake.

A + 比 B + Adj + how much

Three things can fill the "how much" slot:

Slot fillerMeaningExample
一点儿 / 一些a little more今天比昨天冷一点儿。
得多 / 多了a lot more纽约的房子比北京的贵得多。
a real amountexactly how much他比我大三岁。/ 贵五十块。

Common pitfall

很 / 非常 / 太 / 真 are banned inside a 比 sentence. 他比我很高 is the classic English-speaker error ("he is very much taller"). If you want intensity, it goes in the after-slot: 他比我高得多.

他比我大三岁。

Tā bǐ wǒ dà sān suì.

he / than / me / big / three-years

He's three years older than me.

今年工作的压力比去年大得多。

Jīnnián gōngzuò de yālì bǐ qùnián dà de duō.

this-year / work / DE / pressure / than / last-year / big / a-lot

Work pressure this year is much bigger than last year.

Practice

Combine into one sentence with 比. 我三十岁。我弟弟二十四岁。 → say in one sentence exactly how much older you are. Start with 我 (or start with 我弟弟 and flip it round).

我比我弟弟大六岁。 — the amount is the last thing in the sentence: adjective 大 first, then 六岁. English loads the number in front ("six years older") and that is exactly the order Chinese refuses: 我比我弟弟六岁大 ✗. Flipping the two people also flips the adjective — 我弟弟比我小六岁 is the same fact told the other way.

"The tea here is a bit cheaper than the coffee." Which is right?

  • 这儿的茶比咖啡便宜一点儿。✓ Adjective first, amount after: 便宜 + 一点儿.
  • 这儿的茶比咖啡一点儿便宜。✗ That's the English order ("a bit cheaper"). The amount goes after the adjective, never in front of it.
  • 这儿的茶比咖啡很便宜。✗ 很 is banned inside 比 sentences — and it isn't what "a bit" means anyway.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 这个超市的水果比那个超市的很便宜。
(Intended: "The fruit at this supermarket is much cheaper than at that one.")

这个超市的水果比那个超市的便宜得多。 — 很 cannot live inside a 比 sentence at all, so it is not a matter of moving it: it goes. The "much" it was trying to carry reappears behind the adjective as 得多 (or 多了). Simply deleting 很 — …比那个超市的便宜 — is also correct Chinese; you just lose the "much".

Build: "These trousers are eighty kuai more expensive than those."

这条裤子 比 那条 贵 八十块

Fill the degree slot: 这个星期我比上个星期忙 ___ 。
(I'm a lot busier this week than last week.)

得多 (also 多了 / 很多) — all three mean "by a lot" and all three stand behind the adjective. The one word that can never go there, or anywhere else in this sentence, is 很忙 ✗.

Translate. "Today's homework is a bit harder than yesterday's."
用: 今天的作业 · 比 · 昨天的 · 难 · 一点儿

今天的作业比昨天的难一点儿。 — English "a bit harder" puts the softener first; Chinese puts 难 first and lets 一点儿 trail. Watch the near-twin: 有点儿 goes before an adjective and means "a bit too …" as a complaint (今天的作业有点儿难) — it is not a comparison word and cannot appear in a 比 sentence.

2. 比 + 得 — comparing how something is done 她跳舞跳得比我好

This is where 比 meets the report-card 得 from Custom 1. Two orders are both correct — 比 can sit before or after the repeated verb, as long as 得 stays glued to the verb:

她跳舞跳得比我好。

Tā tiàowǔ tiào de bǐ wǒ hǎo.

she / dance / dance-DE / than / me / good

She dances better than me.

她跳舞比我跳得好。

Tā tiàowǔ bǐ wǒ tiào de hǎo.

she / dance / than / me / dance-DE / good

Same meaning, 比 moved forward — also fine.

小刚跑得比我快得多。

Xiǎo Gāng pǎo de bǐ wǒ kuài de duō.

Xiao Gang / run-DE / than / me / fast / a-lot

Xiao Gang runs much faster than me — both 得-slots at once.

Practice

"My neighbour cleans the room a lot more thoroughly than I do." Which is right?

  • 我邻居打扫房间打扫得比我干净得多。✓ Object present, so the verb repeats before 得 (打扫房间 → 打扫得), then 比我, then 干净 + 得多.
  • 我邻居打扫房间得比我干净得多。✗ 得 can only hang off a bare verb. With 房间 in the way, the verb has to be said twice: 打扫房间打扫得。
  • 我邻居打扫房间打扫得比我很干净。✗ 很 is banned in a 比 sentence even when the comparison sits inside the 得-slot.

Build: "My classmate speaks more slowly than I do." (Two orders are accepted.)

我同学 说话 说得 比我 慢

Fill with one character: 这个孩子做作业做 ___ 比他姐姐认真。
(This child does his homework more carefully than his older sister.)

得 — the gap sits after a repeated verb and introduces a judgement of how the homework gets done, so it is the complement 得. 的 would need a noun behind it; 地 goes in front of the verb. Note where 比他姐姐 lands: after the 得, not before 做 — 这个孩子比他姐姐做作业做认真 ✗.

Translate: "My teacher speaks faster than I do."

我的老师说得比我快。 (or 我的老师说话说得比我快。 with the object spelled out — then the verb repeats.)

Combine into one sentence with 比. 小丽唱歌唱得很好。我唱歌唱得不太好。 → one sentence starting with 小丽, and add no degree word (no 得多, no 一点儿).

小丽唱歌唱得比我好。 — the object 歌 forces the verb to be repeated before 得; 比我 then slots in either straight after the 得 or straight before that repeated verb (小丽唱歌比我唱得好), and both are said. The 很 from the first sentence simply vanishes: once an adjective is being measured against something it no longer needs its 很 crutch, and a 比 sentence will not accept it.

3. "Not as … as" — 没有, and equals — 跟…一样 plus the 不比 trap

To negate a comparison, don't negate 比 — swap it for 没有:

我妹妹没有我高。

Wǒ mèimei méiyǒu wǒ gāo.

my / little-sister / not-have / me / tall

My little sister is not as tall as me.

她跟我一样大。

Tā gēn wǒ yíyàng dà.

she / with / me / same / big

She's the same age as me.

她的汉语说得跟中国人一样好。

Tā de Hànyǔ shuō de gēn Zhōngguórén yíyàng hǎo.

her / Chinese / speak-DE / with / Chinese-person / same / good

She speaks Chinese as well as a native — 跟…一样 inside a 得-slot.

Common pitfall — 不比 is not 没有

我没有他高 = he's taller, full stop. 我不比他高 = "I'm not taller than him" — we might be the same height. Use 不比 only to push back against something someone claimed.

Practice

Fill: 这个城市的地铁 ___ 北京的方便。
(This city's subway isn't as convenient as Beijing's.)

没有 (the short 没 is also heard) — to negate a comparison you do not negate 比, you replace it. 比 leaves the sentence completely: 这个城市的地铁不比北京的方便 would mean something else, and …比北京的不方便 is not a structure at all.

A colleague insists your phone cost more than his. In fact the two cost the same. You say:

  • 我的手机不比他的贵。✓ "Mine isn't the more expensive one" — it rejects his claim and leaves room for "they're the same".
  • 我的手机没有他的贵。✗ That says his is the expensive one — a different fact, and not the one you're correcting.
  • 我的手机比他的不贵。✗ Not a structure at all — a negation never goes after 比 B.

Build: "My bike is as new as his."

我的自行车 跟 他的 一样 新

Transform. 小刚的电脑比我的快。 → tell the same fact from the other side, using 没有. Start with 我的电脑.

我的电脑没有小刚的快。 — three things move at once: the two sides swap places, 比 becomes 没有, and the adjective stays exactly as it was (快, not 慢). One thing cannot come along — a degree word. 我的电脑没有小刚的快得多 ✗: 没有 says "doesn't reach it", and there is no way to fall short of something "by a lot" in this frame.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 我家不比他家近。
(Intended: "My place isn't as close as his" — his really is the nearer one.)

我家没有他家近。 — 不比 only denies that you are the closer one; it leaves "exactly the same distance" wide open, so it can never report that his place wins. Flat "not as … as" is 没有. Keep 不比 for the one job it does well — pushing back at a claim someone has just made, exactly as in the pitfall box above.

Personalise it. Write two true sentences comparing yourself with someone you actually know: one with 没有 ("I'm not as … as X"), one with 跟…一样 ("X and I are equally …"). Two different adjectives.

我同事没有我忙，可是他工作得跟我一样认真。

Wǒ tóngshì méiyǒu wǒ máng, kěshì tā gōngzuò de gēn wǒ yíyàng rènzhēn.

My colleague isn't as busy as I am, but he works just as carefully as I do.

Check your own two sentences:

  • Is the adjective bare — no 很 / 非常 / 太 in front of it? (没有我忙 ✓ · 没有我很忙 ✗)
  • Does 一样 sit directly against the adjective, with nothing wedged in between?
  • Did you resist 不比? It denies a claim; it does not mean "not as … as".
  • If your 一样 sentence describes how something is done, is there a 得 holding the comparison onto the verb (工作得跟我一样认真)?
  • Two different adjectives — and both statements actually true?

4. Mixed practice Shuffled — plus two things from other sheets

Nothing below arrives in section order, so you have to decide which frame a sentence needs before you can build it. Two items aren't about 比 at all: one 得 decision and one 了 / 着 / 过 choice, dropped in to keep the older machinery running.

Write the paragraph. Write four or five sentences comparing this month with last month — the weather, the room you work in, someone you walk somewhere with, one object you own. Your paragraph must use all five of: 比 · 得多 · 一点儿 · 没有 · 跟…一样.

这个月比上个月热得多，我走了十分钟就累了。我们公司的新办公室没有旧的大，可是安静一点儿。我同事走路走得比我快，早上一起去地铁站的时候，我总是在他后面。他的手表跟我的一样旧，可是他一直不想换。

Zhège yuè bǐ shàng ge yuè rè de duō, wǒ zǒu le shí fēnzhōng jiù lèi le. Wǒmen gōngsī de xīn bàngōngshì méiyǒu jiù de dà, kěshì ānjìng yìdiǎnr. Wǒ tóngshì zǒulù zǒu de bǐ wǒ kuài, zǎoshang yìqǐ qù dìtiězhàn de shíhou, wǒ zǒngshì zài tā hòumiàn. Tā de shǒubiǎo gēn wǒ de yíyàng jiù, kěshì tā yìzhí bù xiǎng huàn.

It's much hotter this month than last month — ten minutes of walking and I'm worn out. Our company's new office isn't as big as the old one, but it's a bit quieter. My colleague walks faster than I do; heading to the subway together in the morning, I'm always the one behind. His watch is exactly as old as mine, but he still doesn't want to replace it.

Self-check — one pass per line:

  • 比: is the adjective bare? 很 / 非常 / 太 are banned the moment 比 arrives — 比上个月热 ✓ · 比上个月很热 ✗.
  • 得多 and 一点儿: is the amount sitting behind the adjective (热得多, 安静一点儿), never in front of it?
  • 没有: for "not as … as" you replace 比 with 没有 — not 不比 (which only denies that yours is the bigger one) and never 没比.
  • 跟…一样: nothing wedged between 一样 and the word it measures (跟我的一样旧 ✓).
  • Where the comparison is about how an action is done, is there a 得 holding it onto the verb (走路走得比我快) — and did the 很 that normally follows 得 disappear?

Answer the question. 你的中文说得跟你同事一样好吗？ — No: he's the better one. Answer with 没有 (plain 没有 — no 那么), starting with 我.

我没有他说得好。 (also 我说得没有他好 / 我的中文没有他好) — a 跟…一样 question is turned down with 没有, never with 跟他不一样好 ✗ and not with 不比 either, which would only be denying that you are the better speaker. The 没有-comparison can sit inside the 得-slot, or wrap around the whole 得-phrase; both orders are said.

From an earlier sheet. Fill with 的, 得 or 地: 我朋友吃饭吃 ___ 很慢，我已经吃完了。

得 — it follows a repeated verb and introduces a judgement of how the eating goes, so it is the complement 得. 的 would need a noun behind it; 地 goes in front of the verb. Now notice what happens the moment a comparison arrives: 我朋友吃饭吃得比我慢 — the 很 has to disappear, because 比 has taken over the job of measuring 慢.

From an earlier sheet. Fill with 了, 着 or 过: 你吃 ___ 中国菜吗？我觉得比这个饭店的菜好吃得多。

过 — the question asks whether you have ever had the experience, which is exactly 过's job. 了 would ask whether you finished eating it just now, and 着 would claim the eating is going on as we speak.

Write it all. Compare yourself now with yourself two years ago, in three sentences: one with 比 and a degree word (得多 / 一点儿 / an exact amount), one with 没有, one with 跟…一样.

我现在比两年以前忙得多，每天要工作十个小时。可是我没有两年以前累，因为我住的地方离公司很近。我跟两年以前一样喜欢跳舞，每个星期都去两次。

Wǒ xiànzài bǐ liǎng nián yǐqián máng de duō, měi tiān yào gōngzuò shí ge xiǎoshí. Kěshì wǒ méiyǒu liǎng nián yǐqián lèi, yīnwèi wǒ zhù de dìfang lí gōngsī hěn jìn. Wǒ gēn liǎng nián yǐqián yíyàng xǐhuan tiàowǔ, měi ge xīngqī dōu qù liǎng cì.

I'm much busier now than two years ago — ten hours a day. But I'm not as tired as I was then, because the place I live is close to the office. I like dancing just as much as I did two years ago: I go twice a week.

Self-check — one pass per line:

  • Sentence 1: is the degree word behind the adjective (忙得多), never in front of it?
  • Is every 很 / 非常 / 太 gone from the comparison clauses? They may survive only in a clause that isn't comparing anything (…离公司很近 ✓).
  • Sentence 2 uses 没有, not 不比 — unless you genuinely mean "I'm not the more tired one, we're about the same".
  • Sentence 3: 跟 X 一样 + the adjective or verb, with nothing between 一样 and what it measures.
  • No degree word riding along on the 没有 sentence (没有两年以前累得多 ✗).
  • Three different frames — and three true statements?
