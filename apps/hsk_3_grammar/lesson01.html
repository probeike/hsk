What you'll learn

  1. Result complement 好 — a verb finished and to the speaker's satisfaction.
  2. 一 … 也/都 + 不/没 … — the "not even one" pattern for strong negation.
  3. Conjunction 那 — "in that case…", drawing a consequence from what was just said.

How to use this first lesson

These lessons are built for learning the pattern, not just checking whether you remember it.

  • Color coding: blue marks subjects, green marks verbs, orange marks objects, purple marks grammar markers, and teal marks complements or result pieces. The same colors repeat across lessons.
  • Formula slots: read the dark formula boxes left to right. Each slot shows what kind of word belongs there; optional slots are faded.
  • Exercise types: fill-ins train recall, multiple choice trains recognition, sentence builders train word order, and meaning questions train interpretation.
  • PTS: the Principle of Temporal Sequence means Chinese often says things in the order the speaker imagines them happening: frame first, action next, result last.

1. 结果补语 "好" The result complement 好 — "done, and done well"

When a speaker reaches for this: they want to say the action is finished and the result is satisfying — the meal was enjoyed, the homework is ready to hand in, the tickets are secured. It's not just "I ate"; it's "I ate, and I'm done to my satisfaction."

The English trap

English uses one verb for "I finished my homework" whether it turned out well or not. Chinese distinguishes: 写完了 (done, neutral) vs. 写好了 (done, and ready/good). English speakers often default to 完 for everything and miss the "ready for use" nuance that 好 carries.

The verb happens first, then its finishing-with-satisfaction marker. Chinese words line up in time order (this is the Principle of Temporal Sequence — you'll see it again and again).
Subject + Verb + 好 + 了 + (Object)

我已经买好票了。

Wǒ yǐjīng mǎi hǎo piào le.

I / already / buy-ready-PERF / tickets

I've already got the tickets sorted.

饭还没做好，请你等一会儿。

Fàn hái méi zuò hǎo, qǐng nǐ děng yíhuìr.

food / still / not / make-ready, / please wait a moment

The food isn't ready yet — please wait a moment.

Contrast — 完 vs 好

我吃完了
吃完了
I finished eating. Plate empty. Neutral.
我吃好了
吃好了
I've finished and I'm satisfied — the meal is complete for me. This is what you say at a host's dinner table.

Common pitfall

好 can't replace 完 everywhere. Reach for 好 when a result-state matters ("ready", "set", "sorted"). If you just want "finished", 完 is safer.

Practice

Fill the blank: 我还没___要不要去呢。
(I still haven't decided / made up my mind whether to go.)

想好 — "thought-ready", i.e. settled in the mind.

Which sentence tells your host you've finished dinner and enjoyed it?

  • 我吃好了，谢谢！✓ 好 = finished + satisfied. The warm, polite choice.
  • 我吃完了，谢谢！✗ Just "finished", neutral — sounds like you ate and are reporting the fact, missing the warmth.
  • 我吃了，谢谢！✗ "I ate" — no sense of being done, let alone satisfied.

Build: "Are the things for the trip ready yet?"

去旅游的 东西 准备 好 了吗

What does the speaker mean?
电影票我买好了。

  • I tried to buy movie tickets but failed.✗ 买好 means the tickets are secured, not that the attempt failed.
  • I've got the movie tickets sorted (bought and ready).✓ 好 = finished + in a satisfactory result-state. The tickets are in hand.
  • I'm going to buy movie tickets soon.✗ No future sense here — 买好了 reports a completed, settled action.

Build: "The food isn't ready yet."

饭 还 没 做 好 呢

Which sentence means "I've already decided (made up my mind)"?

  • 我已经想好了。✓ 想好 = "thought-and-settled" — the decision is resolved.
  • 我已经想完了。✗ 想完 ("finished thinking") sounds mechanical — it misses the "settled" nuance 好 carries.
  • 我已经想到了。✗ 想到 = "thought of" (came to mind), not "decided".

2. 一 … 也/都 + 不/没 … "Not even one" — strong negation

When a speaker reaches for this: they want to say "not at all" with emphasis — "I didn't eat a single apple", "she didn't buy one single shirt", "I'm not the least bit tired". It's the Chinese equivalent of English not even.

The English trap

English puts negation late: I didn't eat a single apple. Chinese fronts the "not even one" scope before the verb: literally "I one-apple-even didn't-want-to-eat." Your English brain wants to reach for the verb first — don't. Set the scope (一 + measure + noun + 也/都) first, then drop the negated verb.

Why this order? (PTS)

Chinese often introduces the scope/topic before the action. In the speaker's mind, "not one apple" is the frame — it's established first, then the verb is negated within that frame. The sentence literally traces the thought: frame → verb.

Subj + 一 + Measure + N + 也/都 + 不/没 + V

我一个苹果也不想吃。

Wǒ yī ge píngguǒ yě bù xiǎng chī.

I / one / CL-apple / even / not / want / eat

I don't want to eat even one apple.

昨天他一件衣服都没买。

Zuótiān tā yí jiàn yīfu dōu méi mǎi.

yesterday / he / one / CL-clothing / even / not-PAST / buy

He didn't buy a single piece of clothing yesterday.

With adjectives: use 一点儿也/都 + 不 + Adj

When the predicate is an adjective, swap 一+measure+noun for 一点儿 ("a bit"):

他一点儿也不累。

Tā yīdiǎnr yě bù lèi.

he / a-bit / even / not / tired

He isn't the least bit tired.

With a modal inside: 不 + 能/会/可以 + V

A modal verb (能 "can", 会 "will / know how", 可以 "may") can sit inside the frame. The 不 negates the whole modal+verb unit, not just the modal.

The English trap

Seeing 一个也不能少, English speakers read 少 as "few" and get stuck ("a single one can't be few"??). Here 少 is a stative verb meaning "be missing / be lacking". So 不能少 = "can't be missing", and the whole frame means "not even one can be missing" — i.e. every single one is indispensable.

手机、电脑、地图，一个也不能少。

Shǒujī, diànnǎo, dìtú, yí ge yě bù néng shǎo.

phone, computer, map, / one / CL / even / not / can / be-missing

Phones, computer, maps — not a single one can be left out.

这件事一个都不会知道。

Zhè jiàn shì yí ge dōu bú huì zhīdao.

this / CL / matter / one / CL / even / not / will / know

Not a single person will (end up) knowing about this.

他一句话都不想说。

Tā yí jù huà dōu bù xiǎng shuō.

he / one / CL / word / even / not / want / speak

He doesn't want to say a single word.

Common pitfall: 少 means "be few" AND "be missing"

In dictionaries 少 is often glossed as "few / little". But as a stative verb it also means be missing, be lacking. Context disambiguates: 人很少 = "there are few people", but 少了一个人 = "one person is missing". In the 一…也/都+不能少 frame it's always the "missing" sense.

Practice

Build: "I didn't drink even one cup of tea."

我 一杯 茶 也 没 喝

Which sentence means "This place isn't far at all"?

  • 那个地方不远一点儿也。✗ Back-to-front. The 一点儿也 scope must come before the negated adjective.
  • 那个地方一点儿也不远。✓ Scope-then-adjective: "a-bit-even-not-far".
  • 那个地方一点儿远也不。✗ 也 belongs right before 不, not after the adjective.

Fill the blank: 我___，所以不能买衣服。
(I didn't bring any money at all, so I can't buy clothes.)

一点儿钱都没带 — "a-bit-money-even-not-brought". 也 also works in place of 都.

Build: "He didn't buy a single piece of clothing."

他 一件 衣服 都 没 买

(Note: 也 is also possible here; this bank uses 都.)

What does the speaker mean?
南方一点儿都不冷。

  • The south is a little cold.✗ 一点儿都不 is strong negation, not softening.
  • The south is not the slightest bit cold.✓ 一点儿 + 都 + 不 + adj. = "not at all".
  • The south is colder than expected.✗ There's no comparison here — just flat, emphatic negation.

What does the speaker mean?
这些东西一个也不能少。

  • Most of these things can be left out.✗ Opposite — 不能少 says none can be left out.
  • Not a single one of these things can be left out.✓ 一…也+不能+少 = "not even one can be missing" — every item is essential.
  • There are few of these things.✗ 少 here is the stative "be missing", not the adjective "few".

Build: "Not a single one of these people can be missing."

这些人 一个 也 不 能 少

(也 and 都 are both OK — this bank uses 也.)

Fill the blank: 他今天很生气，___。
(He's angry today — he doesn't want to say a single word.)

一句话都不想说 — "not one word (does he) want to speak". 也 instead of 都 also works.

In 这家公司一个员工也不能少, what does 少 mean?

  • Few / in small quantity.✗ That's the adjective sense. Inside this frame, 少 is stative "be missing".
  • Be missing / be absent.✓ The whole sentence means "not a single employee can be missing (from this company)".
  • Reduce / make fewer.✗ 少 isn't active "reduce" here; it's stative.

3. 连词 "那" The conjunction 那 — "in that case…"

When a speaker reaches for this: right after the other person has said something, to signal "given that, here's what follows." English equivalents: then, in that case, so, well then… It always sits at the start of the new sentence.

The English trap

Don't confuse this 那 with the demonstrative 那 ("that"). Here it's a discourse link — a logical bridge between what the other person said and your response. Think "in that case…", not "that one".

那 , Subj + V + rest

A: 我不想去看电影。
B: 那我也不去了。

A: Wǒ bù xiǎng qù kàn diànyǐng. / B: Nà wǒ yě bù qù le.

A: I don't want to go see the movie. / B: In-that-case / I / also / not / go / PERF

A: I don't want to go to the movie. / B: In that case, I won't go either.

A: 我早就复习好了。
B: 那也不能一直玩儿啊。

A: Wǒ zǎo jiù fùxí hǎo le. / B: Nà yě bù néng yìzhí wánr a.

A: I / long-ago / review-ready / PERF. / B: in-that-case / still / can't / keep / play

A: I finished reviewing ages ago. / B: Even so (in that case), you still can't keep playing all day.

Practice

Respond naturally with 那 …:
A: 外边下大雨了，不能去踢球了。
B: ___

Any of these work: 那我们明天去吧 / 那明天再去吧. The key is that 那 opens the reply.

Pick the natural reply:
A: 对不起，红色的手机已经卖完了。

  • 那我买白色的吧。✓ 那 = "in that case" — a logical consequence of A's statement.
  • 我那买白色的吧。✗ 那 (as "in that case") must sit first, not mid-sentence.
  • 那个我买白色的吧。✗ 那个 is "that one" — a demonstrative, not a discourse link.

Build (reply to "我不想去看电影。"): "In that case, I won't go either."

那 我 也 不 去 了

In B's reply, what does 那 mean?
A: 我已经吃饱了。
B: 那我们走吧。

  • "That" (pointing to something).✗ Sentence-initial 那 here is a discourse link, not a demonstrative.
  • "In that case / so then" — drawing a consequence from A's statement.✓ 那 bridges A's statement to B's proposed action.
  • A mild question particle.✗ 那 isn't a question particle; you're thinking of 吗/呢.

Fill the blank: A: 明天我有事，不能跟你去。
B: ___
(Tomorrow I'm busy, I can't go with you. — In that case, I'll go by myself.)

那我一个人去吧 — "In that case, I'll go alone." 自己 also works.

Translate: "He didn't eat even one apple. In that case, let's buy him some bread."

  • 他没吃一个苹果也。那我们给他买点面包吧。✗ The "not even one" scope must come before the verb, not after.
