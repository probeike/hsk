What you'll learn

  1. 如果 … (的话)，(就) … — "if … then …" conditionals.
  2. Complex state complements — V 得 + more than a single word: full descriptions of how an action went.
  3. Monosyllabic adjective reduplication — 好好, 慢慢 = "nicely, slowly" + vividness.

1. 如果 … (的话)，(就) … "If … then …"

When a speaker reaches for this: to tee up a hypothetical — "if it rains, then we'll stay in". Chinese gives you three parts, any of which can be dropped if context carries it: the 如果 front, an optional 的话 cushion, and a 就 pivot.

The English trap

English hinges on if. Chinese scatters the work:

  • 如果 — front-of-clause "if". Can be replaced by 要是 (more casual).
  • 的话 — an optional soft landing at the end of the if-clause, like English "… , that is".
  • 就 — a then-pivot at the start of the main clause. Often omitted in casual speech, but strongly preferred in writing.

You can drop 如果 or 的话, but not both — one of them must mark the conditional.

Bridge from Lesson 11 就

In Lesson 11, sequential 就 meant "once X happens, then Y": 你写完作业就可以看电视.

如果…就… uses the same "then" pivot, but the first clause is hypothetical instead of a real next step:

  • Sequential: 写完作业，就可以看电视 = once the homework is finished, then TV is allowed.
  • Conditional: 如果写完作业，就可以看电视 = if the homework gets finished, then TV is allowed.

So don't read this 就 as "already". It is the same consequence marker you used in Lesson 11.

如果 + clause 1 ( 的话 ), ( 就 ) clause 2

如果明天下雨，我就不去了。

Rúguǒ míngtiān xià yǔ, wǒ jiù bú qù le.

if / tomorrow / rain / I / then / not-go-PERF

If it rains tomorrow, then I won't go.

如果你愿意的话，我们就一起去。

Rúguǒ nǐ yuànyì de huà, wǒmen jiù yìqǐ qù.

If you're willing, (then) we'll go together.

你愿意的话，我们一起去。

Nǐ yuànyì de huà, wǒmen yìqǐ qù.

If you're willing, let's go together. (Dropped 如果 — 的话 alone can carry the "if".)

就 signals "then", not "already"

You met 就 in Lesson 11 as "already / sooner than expected". Here it's the pivot word in a conditional — same character, different job. Context tells them apart: in an 如果…就… frame, 就 means "then".

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about the next month of your own life — plans that really do depend on the weather, on money, on time, or on somebody else. Every sentence uses 如果 (or its casual twin 要是) …，… 就 …, and at least one of them closes the if-clause with 的话. Make one consequence negative, use three different verbs, and keep every condition something that might actually happen to you.

如果这个星期六不下雨，我就骑车去公园跑步。如果公司下个月还这么忙的话，我就不去上中文课了。如果我妈妈来看我，我们就一起去那家新开的饭馆吃饭。要是你那天也有空，你就跟我们一起来。

Rúguǒ zhège xīngqīliù bú xià yǔ, wǒ jiù qí chē qù gōngyuán pǎobù. Rúguǒ gōngsī xià ge yuè hái zhème máng de huà, wǒ jiù bú qù shàng Zhōngwén kè le. Rúguǒ wǒ māma lái kàn wǒ, wǒmen jiù yìqǐ qù nà jiā xīn kāi de fànguǎn chīfàn. Yàoshi nǐ nà tiān yě yǒu kòng, nǐ jiù gēn wǒmen yìqǐ lái.

If it doesn't rain this Saturday, I'll cycle to the park and go for a run. If work is still this busy next month, I'll stop going to my Chinese class. If my mum comes to visit, we'll go to that newly opened restaurant together. If you're free that day too, come along with us.

Why these are right: 如果 opens the hypothesis, and 就 opens the consequence — but 就 is an adverb, so it stands after the subject of the second clause (我就… · 我们就… · 你就…), never at the very front. 的话 is a soft landing at the end of the if-clause, which is why sentence 2 has it after 忙 and not next to 如果. Nothing in an if-clause takes 了 — these conditions haven't happened yet; the 了 in 不去…了 belongs to the consequence, where it marks a change of plan. Sentence 4 swaps 如果 for its casual twin 要是.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is 就 after the subject of the second clause? 我就不去了 ✓ · 就我不去了 ✗
  • Is 的话 at the end of the if-clause? 如果的话你忙 ✗
  • You may drop 如果 or 的话 — but not both, or the sentence turns into two plain facts.
  • No 了 inside the condition: 如果明天下了雨 ✗ — it hasn't happened.
  • Three different verbs, one negative consequence, and every condition genuinely from your own next month?

2. Complex state complements V 得 + a whole phrase of commentary

When a speaker reaches for this: to describe how well / how much / in what manner an action went — and to do it with more than a single adverb. "She speaks Chinese so well that natives can't tell." "He eats so much he can't sleep." The 得 slot can hold an entire descriptive phrase.

The English trap

English drops the manner after the verb with adverbs ("she sings beautifully") or the conjunction "so … that". Chinese uses V + 得 + phrase for both — the 得 introduces whatever follows about the performance of the verb. The longer the phrase, the more detailed the description.

V + 得 + description phrase

她的汉语说得跟中国人一样好。

Tā de Hànyǔ shuō de gēn Zhōngguórén yíyàng hǎo.

she / POSS / Chinese / speak / COMP / like / Chinese-person / same / good

She speaks Chinese as well as a native.

他累得下了班就想睡觉。

Tā lèi de xià le bān jiù xiǎng shuì jiào.

he / tired / COMP / finish-work / then / want / sleep

He's so tired that as soon as he gets off work he wants to sleep.

她笑得连话都说不出来。

Tā xiào de lián huà dōu shuō bu chūlai.

She's laughing so hard she can't even get a word out.

If there's an object, repeat the verb

You can't split V and 得 with an object. Wrong: *他说汉语得很好 (✗). Right: 他说汉语说得很好 — repeat the verb before 得. Or topicalize the object: 汉语他说得很好.

Slow parse: action → 得 → commentary

Read the sentence in three beats:

  • Action: 她笑 = she laughs.
  • 得: opens the comment slot.
  • Commentary: 连话都说不出来 = to the point that she cannot even speak.

With object verbs, keep the object with the first verb and repeat the verb before 得: 他说汉语说得很好 = he speaks Chinese, speaks it well. Pattern: Subject + V + Object + V + 得 + commentary.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about how people you actually know do things — a colleague, a family member, a friend, yourself. Every sentence uses V + 得 + description. At least two of them must have a verb with an object, so the verb has to be repeated (V + O + V + 得 …), and at least one complement must be a whole clause ("so … that …"), not just an adjective. Use three different verbs, and keep every sentence true.

我同事写汉字写得又快又好看，我写得慢极了。上个星期我跳舞跳得太多，累得第二天早上起不来。我妈妈做菜做得跟饭馆的一样好，可是她说她只会做几个菜。我弟弟玩游戏玩得连饭都忘了吃。

Wǒ tóngshì xiě Hànzì xiě de yòu kuài yòu hǎokàn, wǒ xiě de màn jí le. Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ tiàowǔ tiào de tài duō, lèi de dì-èr tiān zǎoshang qǐ bu lái. Wǒ māma zuò cài zuò de gēn fànguǎn de yíyàng hǎo, kěshì tā shuō tā zhǐ huì zuò jǐ ge cài. Wǒ dìdi wán yóuxì wán de lián fàn dōu wàng le chī.

My colleague writes characters quickly and beautifully; I write terribly slowly. Last week I danced so much that I couldn't get up the next morning. My mum cooks as well as a restaurant, though she says she only knows a few dishes. My little brother plays games so intently he forgets to eat.

Why these are right: whenever the verb drags an object along (写汉字 · 跳舞 · 做菜 · 玩游戏), the verb is said twice — object with the first copy, 得 welded to the second. In the second half of sentence 1 there is no object, so one 写 is enough. Look at what the 得 slot can hold: a doubled adjective phrase (又快又好看), a comparison (跟饭馆的一样好), and a whole clause of consequence (连饭都忘了吃, 第二天早上起不来). That is the point of this pattern — the comment on the action can be as long as you like, as long as 得 introduces it.

Check your own sentences:

  • Object present ⇒ verb repeated: 做菜做得很好 ✓ · 做菜得很好 ✗ (or topicalise: 菜她做得很好).
  • Is 得 glued to the verb, with no 了/过 in between? 跳了得很好 ✗
  • Does the complement describe how the action went — not when or where it happened?
  • Negating? The negation goes in the complement: 写得不好看 ✓ · 不写得好看 ✗
  • At least one full "so … that …" clause after 得, three different verbs, and all of it true about real people.

3. Reduplication of monosyllabic adjectives 好好 / 慢慢 / 远远 — vividness and softness

When a speaker reaches for this: to paint a more vivid or gentler version of an adjective. 慢 = slow; 慢慢 = slowly, in a relaxed, drawn-out way. 好 = good; 好好 = thoroughly, properly.

The English trap

English has no real parallel. "Slow" reduplicated sounds childish in English; in Chinese it's a native rhetorical move that adds colour and softens commands. 慢慢吃 ("eat slowly" — a warm hostess's line) ≠ 慢吃 (wrong). Reduplicated adjectives almost always pair with 地 (adverbial) or stand alone before the verb:

  • AA (地) + V — describes the manner.
  • The second syllable often becomes first tone in speech: 慢慢 mànmàn → mànmār, 好好 hǎohǎo → hǎohāo.
Adj → AA (+ 地 ) + V

你慢慢说，别着急。

Nǐ mànmàn shuō, bié zháo jí.

Speak slowly, don't rush.

我们好好休息一下吧。

Wǒmen hǎohāo xiūxi yíxià ba.

Let's have a proper rest.

她远远地看见了我。

Tā yuǎnyuǎn de kànjiàn le wǒ.

She spotted me from far away.

plain adj
慢说
✗ Ungrammatical as a command.
reduplicated
慢慢说
✓ "Speak slowly." Softer, more natural.

Tone examples you will actually hear

Dictionaries show the base adjective tones, but reduplication often makes the second syllable lighter or first-tone-like in speech. Listen for these common shapes:

  • 慢慢: mànmàn in careful reading, often mànmān / mànman in speech.
  • 好好: hǎohǎo in careful reading, often hǎohāo or hǎohāor.
  • 早早: zǎozǎo in careful reading, often zǎozāo.
  • 远远: yuǎnyuǎn in careful reading, often yuǎnyuān before 地.

The grammar is the important part for now: AA + V or AA地 + V. The tone softening will come with listening practice.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about your own routines and the people around you, each one using a reduplicated single-syllable adjective in front of the verb (好好 · 慢慢 · 远远 …). Use at least two different reduplications, put 地 after the doubled adjective in at least one, and make one sentence something you say to somebody rather than about them. Three different verbs; every sentence true.

这个周末我要好好打扫房间，把冬天的衣服都收起来。明天早上有会，所以我今天想早早地上床睡觉。我的老师说话说得太快，我常常请她再慢慢说一次。车站上人很多，可是我远远地就听见了小刚的声音。吃饭的时候我常跟小丽说：“你慢慢吃，我们时间还多。”

Zhège zhōumò wǒ yào hǎohāo dǎsǎo fángjiān, bǎ dōngtiān de yīfu dōu shōu qǐlai. Míngtiān zǎoshang yǒu huì, suǒyǐ wǒ jīntiān xiǎng zǎozāo de shàng chuáng shuìjiào. Wǒ de lǎoshī shuōhuà shuō de tài kuài, wǒ chángcháng qǐng tā zài mànmān shuō yí cì. Chēzhàn shang rén hěn duō, kěshì wǒ yuǎnyuǎn de jiù tīngjiàn le Xiǎogāng de shēngyīn. Chīfàn de shíhou wǒ cháng gēn Xiǎolì shuō: "Nǐ mànmān chī, wǒmen shíjiān hái duō."

This weekend I want to give the room a proper clean and put all the winter clothes away. I have a meeting tomorrow morning, so I want to get to bed nice and early tonight. My teacher talks too fast, so I often ask her to say it again slowly. The stop was crowded, but I heard Xiaogang's voice from way off. At meals I often say to Xiaoli, "Take your time — we've got plenty of time."

Why these are right: the doubled adjective is an adverbial, so it stands in front of the verb — 好好打扫, 早早地上床, 再慢慢说, 远远地听见, 慢慢吃. Doubling is not the same as intensifying: 好好 doesn't mean "very good", it means "properly, giving it the time it deserves", and 慢慢 carries a warmth that plain 慢 cannot. 地 is optional after a doubled adjective; it is common before longer verb phrases, and it is 地 — never 的. Notice sentence 3 keeps both patterns of this lesson apart: 说得太快 comments on how she talks, 再慢慢说 asks for a manner — and 再 comes before the whole manner phrase, not inside it. The last sentence is what reduplication was made for: said to someone, 你慢慢吃 is an act of kindness that plain 你吃得慢一点儿 could never manage.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the AA form before the verb? 慢慢吃 ✓ · 吃慢慢 ✗
  • Did you double the adjective rather than reach for 很? 好好休息 ≠ 很好休息.
  • If you added the particle, is it 地 (adverbial) and not 的?
  • Is 好好 doing "thoroughly / properly"? If you mean "well" as a result, that is 得 territory: 做得很好.
  • Two different reduplications, one sentence addressed to someone, and every line true about your week.

Write the paragraph. Next Saturday 小丽 is coming to your place to learn to cook a dish, and 小刚 will join you if he can get away from work. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all three structures of this lesson at least once each: 如果 …，… 就 …, V + 得 + description, and a reduplicated adjective (好好 / 慢慢 / 远远) in front of a verb. Cover: what happens if 小刚 can't come, how 小丽 does at the cooking, what you tell her while she works, and how the evening ends.

Xiǎolì xué de hěn kuài, kěshì dì-yī cì zuò cài, dōngxi fàng de tài duō le. Wǒ gēn tā shuō: "Nǐ mànmān zuò, búyòng zháojí, wǒmen yǒu yí ge xiàwǔ de shíjiān." Wǎnshang dàjiā chī de gāoxìng jí le, Xiǎogāng shuō zhège cài zuò de gēn fànguǎn de yíyàng hǎo. Rúguǒ Xiǎolì hái xiǎng xué de huà, xià ge yuè wǒmen jiù zài yìqǐ zuò yí cì.

Next Saturday 小丽 is coming to my place to learn to cook a Chinese dish. If 小刚 can get off work early he'll come with her; if he can't get away, the two of us will start without him. 小丽 learns fast, but it was her first time cooking and she put in too much of everything. I said to her, "Take your time, there's no rush — we've got the whole afternoon." In the evening everyone ate happily, and 小刚 said the dish was as good as a restaurant's. If 小丽 still wants to learn, we'll cook together again next month.

Why this works as a paragraph: the three structures carry three different loads. The 如果…就 pairs handle the parts of the plan that aren't settled yet — twice at the start (two branches of the same Saturday), once at the end (next month). The 得 complements are where the judgements live: 学得很快, 放得太多了, 吃得高兴极了, 做得跟饭馆的一样好 — each one comments on how an action came off, and 做菜 keeps its verb repeated. 慢慢做 is not a judgement at all but a piece of kindness in direct speech, which is exactly what reduplication is for. Time words (下个星期六 · 晚上 · 下个月) hold the whole thing together.

Check your own paragraph:

  • 如果…就: is 就 after the subject of the result clause, and is the condition free of 了?
  • 得: object present ⇒ verb repeated (做菜做得…), and 得 glued straight to the verb.
  • Reduplicated adjective in front of the verb — and is it doing manner (慢慢做), not result (做得很慢)?
  • Did you keep 得 and 地 apart? 得 follows the verb and comments on it; 地 precedes the verb and shapes it.
  • Does it read as one Saturday — connected by time words and 可是 — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

  1. 打篮球得 → 打篮球打得. 得 may only follow the verb itself, and 打篮球 is verb + object, so the object cannot be sitting between them. Repeat the verb (or topicalise: 篮球他打得很好).
  2. 就我去 → 我就去. 就 is an adverb, and Chinese adverbs go after the subject and before the verb. At the front of the clause it is not a "then" at all — it reads as "it's precisely me who…".
  3. 准备好好 → 好好准备. A doubled adjective is an adverbial: it comes before the verb. Left where it was, 准备好 is heard as verb + result complement ("get it ready"), which is not what you meant.
  4. 如果的话你不忙 → 如果你不忙的话. 的话 is a soft ending for the if-clause, so it closes it. 如果 opens, 的话 shuts — the condition lives between them, never after them.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Any 得 with an object in front of it? Repeat the verb.
  • Is every 就 standing after its subject?
  • Is every doubled adjective in front of its verb?
  • Is 的话 at the end of the condition, with 如果 (or nothing) at the front?
  • One mistake per sentence — did you keep the rest of each sentence exactly as it was?

1. Rúguǒ míngtiān zǎoshang bù lěng, wǒ jiù qù gōngyuán pǎobù. 2. Wǒ dìdi chīfàn chī de hěn kuài, wǔ fēnzhōng jiù chī wán le yì wǎn miàntiáo. 3. Qǐng nǐ mànmān xiě, wǒ kàn bu dǒng nǐ de Hànzì. 4. Rúguǒ nǐ jīntiān wǎnshang yǒu kòng de huà, wǒmen jiù hǎohāo liáoliao zhè jiàn shì.

1. If it isn't cold tomorrow morning, I'll go running in the park. 2. My little brother eats so fast he polishes off a bowl of noodles in five minutes. 3. Please write slowly — I can't make out your characters. 4. If you're free this evening, let's have a proper talk about this.

Why these are right: (1) the condition is negated with 不 (a state, not a finished event) and takes no 了; 就 waits behind 我. (2) 吃饭 is verb + object, so 吃 comes back for 得 — and the second 就 in the sentence is the Lesson 11 "as soon as / only" 就, a useful reminder that the same character does more than one job. (3) 慢慢 shapes the verb from in front; 请你慢慢写 is a request, which is exactly the register reduplication lives in. (4) 的话 closes the if-clause, 好好 stands before the verb, and 聊聊 doubles the verb — a different move from doubling an adjective, and one that makes the chat sound relaxed rather than official.

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: no 了 in the condition, and 就 after 我 — not before 如果.
  • Sentence 2: did you repeat 吃? 吃饭得很快 ✗
  • Sentence 3: 慢慢 before 写. If you wrote 写得很慢 you described his handwriting instead of asking for slowness.
  • Sentence 4: 的话 at the end of the condition, 好好 in front of the verb, and 就 after 我们.
