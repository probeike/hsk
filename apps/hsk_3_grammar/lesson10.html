What you'll learn

  1. 把字句 1: A 把 B V 了 — the cornerstone HSK 3 structure. One of the biggest gaps between English and Chinese.
  2. 左右 — "about / approximately".

Title bridge: 比 + degree is review

数学比历史难多了 uses the Lesson 9 comparison pattern: A 比 B + Adj + 多了. Before learning 把, make sure you can see the old frame: maths 比 history difficult 多了 = much harder.

1. A 把 B + Verb 了 The disposal construction — "did something to B"

When a speaker reaches for this: when they want to say what happened to a specific thing. The focus isn't on whether the action occurred — it's on the result the action produced on a particular object. "I closed the air conditioner." "He ate up the apple." "She put the book on the table." The object is known and specific; the new information is what happened to it.

The English trap — this is the big one

English: Subject → Verb → Object.
把-sentences: Subject → 把 → Object → Verb (+ result).

Your English brain will constantly want to put the object after the verb. It takes deliberate rewiring. When you feel the urge to say 我关空调了, stop. Ask: "is this specific? is there a result?" If yes → reach for 把: 我把空调关了.

The cognitive picture — "disposal"

Think of 把 as a hand that grabs the object. In Chinese, the speaker picks up the object first (mentally), then does something to it. The sentence literally traces the action: grab → do → done. Linguists call this the "disposal" schema — 把 means "take the object and dispose of it (in some way)".

"I 把 apple ate-up 了" — grab the apple, act on it, done. Read left to right, it's the exact order of the mental film.
A (Subj) + 把 + B (Obj) + V + 了 / complement

Build a 把 sentence slowly

Do not start by translating English word-for-word. Start with a plain action, then make the object specific, then add the result, then move that specific object into the 把 frame.

Step Chinese What changed
1. Plain SVO 我关空调。 I turn off air conditioners / I turn off AC. Basic action only.
2. Specific object 空调 = the AC in this room 把 needs a known, specific object, not a vague class of things.
3. Result 关了 / 关上 / 关好 The object ends up off, shut, or properly closed.
4. 把 sentence 我把空调关了。 I took that specific AC and got it turned off.

我把空调关了。

Wǒ bǎ kōngtiáo guān le.

I / [disposal] / air-conditioner / turn-off / PERF

I turned off the air conditioner.

他把苹果吃完了。

Tā bǎ píngguǒ chī wán le.

he / [disposal] / apple / eat-finish / PERF

He finished the apple / ate it up.

请你把门关上。

Qǐng nǐ bǎ mén guān shàng.

please / you / [disposal] / door / close-shut

Please close the door.

When is 把 required?

把 is required (not just optional) when the verb carries a result-flavoured piece — a directional complement, a resultative complement (like 完, 好, 到), or a location destination. You cannot write *我关了空调 if you mean "I got the AC turned off (it's now off)". You need the 把 frame to specify what happened to it.

Plain SVO
我关了空调
Grammatical, but feels incomplete — "I turned off an air conditioner" (existence of action, not a clean focus on the result).
把-sentence
我把空调关了
"I got the AC turned off." Specific object, clear result. This is what a native speaker would say in this situation.

把 can't be used with every verb

The verb must do something to the object. Stative verbs (有, 是, 喜欢, 认识) and pure perception verbs (看见, 听见) can't take 把. You can't say *把他喜欢. Rule of thumb: if nothing happens to the object, don't use 把.

上个星期五我要去旅游，早上很早就把东西都准备好了。我先把电脑和词典放在包里，又把桌子上的照片放回房间。出门以前我把灯关了，也把冰箱的门关好了。可是到了机场我才发现，我把水放在车上了。

Shàng ge xīngqīwǔ wǒ yào qù lǚyóu, zǎoshang hěn zǎo jiù bǎ dōngxi dōu zhǔnbèi hǎo le. Wǒ xiān bǎ diànnǎo hé cídiǎn fàng zài bāo li, yòu bǎ zhuōzi shang de zhàopiàn fàng huí fángjiān. Chūmén yǐqián wǒ bǎ dēng guān le, yě bǎ bīngxiāng de mén guān hǎo le. Kěshì dào le jīchǎng wǒ cái fāxiàn, wǒ bǎ shuǐ fàng zài chē shang le.

Last Friday I was going away, so I had everything ready early in the morning. First I put the laptop and the dictionary in my bag, then put the photo from the desk back in my room. Before leaving I turned the light off and shut the fridge door properly. But when I got to the airport I realised I'd left my water in the car.

Why these are right: every object here is a particular one — my laptop, that photo, the fridge door — and every verb ends in something that reports what became of it: 准备好, 放在…里, 关了, 关好. That is the whole bargain of 把: you move the object in front of the verb, and in exchange the verb has to say how the object came out. A bare verb at the end (我把灯关) is not a sentence yet.

Check your own sentences:

  • Order: 我把照片放回房间 ✓ · 我放回照片房间 ✗ — the object sits between 把 and the verb.
  • Does every verb carry a result — 了, 完, 好, 上, or 在 + place? A 把 sentence cannot end on a bare verb.
  • 了 goes after the verb and its complement: 关好了 ✓ · 关了好 ✗
  • Is the object specific and known? 把 does not work on a vague class of things (我把书放好了 when you mean "books in general").
  • Did you avoid 是/有/喜欢/认识? Nothing happens to the object with those, so 把 is impossible.

2. 左右 "Around / about" — approximate quantity

When a speaker reaches for this: to say "roughly this much" — about 30 minutes, around 100 people.

The English trap

English: about 30 minutes — the approximator comes before. Chinese: 三十分钟左右 — 左右 comes after, literally "left-right" (i.e. "give or take in either direction").

Number + Measure + 左右

我每天走一个小时左右。

Wǒ měi tiān zǒu yí ge xiǎoshí zuǒyòu.

I walk about an hour every day.

这件衣服三百块左右。

Zhè jiàn yīfu sān bǎi kuài zuǒyòu.

This piece of clothing is around 300 kuài.

左右 vs 几/多 vs adjacent numerals

Chinese has several ways to be approximate. Choose based on how precise or vague the number feels.

Tool Pattern Example Feel
左右 number/time + 左右 三点左右 around 3 o'clock; close to that number.
几 几 + measure 几个朋友 a few; a small vague number.
多 round ten + 多 / number + measure + 多 三十多个人, 两年多 more than that number.
三四 two adjacent numerals + measure 三四个车站 three or four; not thirty-four.

You can combine adjacent numerals with 左右 when you want a very soft estimate: 七八分钟左右 = around seven or eight minutes.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences giving rough figures from your own life with 左右 — how long something takes, what time you usually do it, what something cost, how many of something there are. The approximator goes after the quantity every time. Use three different kinds of quantity (a length of time, a clock time, and a price or a count), and make one of them a soft double estimate (七八 / 三四 + measure). Every figure has to be true for you.

我每天骑自行车去上班，路上要二十分钟左右。我们公司附近有三四个商店，中午我常常去那儿买点儿吃的。上个月我买的那个词典一百二十块左右，比我的旧词典好多了。晚上我一般十一点左右睡觉，周末会晚一点儿。

Wǒ měi tiān qí zìxíngchē qù shàngbān, lù shang yào èrshí fēnzhōng zuǒyòu. Wǒmen gōngsī fùjìn yǒu sān sì ge shāngdiàn, zhōngwǔ wǒ chángcháng qù nàr mǎi diǎnr chī de. Shàng ge yuè wǒ mǎi de nàge cídiǎn yìbǎi èrshí kuài zuǒyòu, bǐ wǒ de jiù cídiǎn hǎo duō le. Wǎnshang wǒ yìbān shíyī diǎn zuǒyòu shuìjiào, zhōumò huì wǎn yìdiǎnr.

I ride my bike to work every day; it takes about twenty minutes. There are three or four shops near our office, and at lunchtime I often go there to buy something to eat. The dictionary I bought last month was around 120 kuai — much better than my old one. In the evening I usually go to bed around eleven, a bit later at the weekend.

Why these are right: 左右 is post-posed — it takes a finished quantity (二十分钟 · 一百二十块 · 十一点) and loosens it, so nothing may come between the number and 左右. It works on clock times as happily as on durations and prices. 三四个 does the same job a different way: two neighbouring numerals mean "three or four", never thirty-four, and the measure word still has to be there.

Check your own sentences:

  • Position: 二十分钟左右 ✓ · 左右二十分钟 ✗ — English "about" comes first, Chinese never does.
  • Is the quantity complete before 左右? 二十左右分钟 ✗
  • Double numerals keep their measure word: 三四个商店 ✓ · 三四商店 ✗
  • Did you use three different kinds of quantity, not three durations?
  • Would 多 or 几 have been more honest anywhere? 左右 means "either side of"; 多 means "over".

上个星期天早上我去帮小刚搬家，从我家到他的新地方要一个小时左右。我们先把最大的东西搬进去，又把椅子放在桌子旁边。小刚说他把手机放在车上了，我们找了十分钟左右才找到。中午我把他的书都放在桌子上，他把旧衣服送给了楼下的邻居。三点左右我们把最后一个包也打开了，然后一起出去吃东西。

Shàng ge xīngqītiān zǎoshang wǒ qù bāng Xiǎogāng bānjiā, cóng wǒ jiā dào tā de xīn dìfang yào yí ge xiǎoshí zuǒyòu. Wǒmen xiān bǎ zuì dà de dōngxi bān jìnqù, yòu bǎ yǐzi fàng zài zhuōzi pángbiān. Xiǎogāng shuō tā bǎ shǒujī fàng zài chē shang le, wǒmen zhǎo le shí fēnzhōng zuǒyòu cái zhǎodào. Zhōngwǔ wǒ bǎ tā de shū dōu fàng zài zhuōzi shang, tā bǎ jiù yīfu sòng gěi le lóuxià de línjū. Sān diǎn zuǒyòu wǒmen bǎ zuìhòu yí ge bāo yě dǎkāi le, ránhòu yìqǐ chūqu chī dōngxi.

Last Sunday morning I went to help Xiaogang move house; it takes about an hour from my place to his new one. We carried the biggest things in first, then put the chairs beside the table. Xiaogang said he'd left his phone in the car, and it took us about ten minutes to find it. At midday I put all his books on the table, and he gave his old clothes to the neighbour downstairs. Around three we got the last bag open, and then went out to eat.

Why this works as a paragraph: a move is a chain of things happening to objects, which is exactly what 把 is for — 搬进去 (in), 放在桌子旁边 (where it ended up), 送给邻居 (who has it now), 打开 (its new state). Each verb ends in the result, so the reader always knows how the object came out. 左右 then keeps the timings honest: an hour-ish journey, ten minutes-ish of searching, around three o'clock — a moving day is never measured to the minute.

1. Tā bǎ shǒubiǎo fàng zài chuáng shang le. 2. Xiǎolì bǎ tā de jiù shǒujī huàn le. 3. Wǒ hěn xǐhuan zhè zhǒng yīnyuè. 4. Cóng wǒ jiā dào gōngsī yào èrshí fēnzhōng zuǒyòu.

1. He put his watch on the bed. 2. Xiaoli replaced her old phone. 3. I like this kind of music a lot. 4. It takes about twenty minutes from my place to the office.

  1. 放了在床上 → 放在床上了. The verb and its complement are one unit; 了 cannot be wedged inside. When 在 + place is the complement, the whole thing finishes first and 了 closes the sentence.
  2. 旧手机换 → 旧手机换了. Once you have promoted the object with 把, the verb owes you a result. A bare verb leaves the sentence hanging — add 了, or a complement like 完 / 好 / 上.
  3. 把这种音乐很喜欢 → 很喜欢这种音乐. 喜欢 is stative: nothing happens to the music, so there is nothing to dispose of. Verbs like 是, 有, 认识, 看见 refuse 把 for the same reason — put the object back after the verb.
  4. 左右二十分钟 → 二十分钟左右. 左右 is post-posed: it takes a completed quantity and loosens it. Fronting it copies the English "about", which is precisely the trap.
