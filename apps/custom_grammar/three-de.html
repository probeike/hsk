Custom 01 · 的·得·地

我的老师高兴地说：你跳舞跳得越来越好！

Wǒ de lǎoshī gāoxìng de shuō: nǐ tiàowǔ tiào de yuè lái yuè hǎo!

My teacher said happily: you're dancing better and better!

Why this sentence

Read the title again slowly. It contains all three de's, one after another: 我的老师 (的) … 高兴地说 (地) … 跳得越来越好 (得). Three different characters, three different jobs, one identical sound — de. Chinese speakers hear no difference; readers see three completely different things. This lesson gives each one a job description so you never have to guess which character to write.

1. 的 — the noun-glue Anything that describes a noun clips on with 的

When a speaker reaches for this: whenever you want to put a description in front of a noun. It doesn't matter what kind of description — an owner (my job), an adjective (pretty teacher), another noun (Korean dance), or a whole verb phrase (the words the teacher said). All four glue on the same way: description + 的 + noun. The noun being described always comes last.

The English trap

English does two opposite things, and English speakers import both mistakes. English marks possession with an ending — my teacher's book — so you remember a marker is needed there. But English glues an adjective straight onto a noun with nothing in between — a pretty teacher — so you drop 的 exactly where Chinese wants it: 漂亮的老师, never 漂亮老师. Then, over-correcting, you glue 的 into the few places Chinese leaves it out (我妈妈, 好朋友, 汉语老师).

Picture 的 as a drop of glue pointing rightwards. Whatever sits to its left describes whatever sits to its right. This is why the noun can never come first.
Description + 的 + Noun

Job 1 — an owner (possessive)

你的老板是谁？

Nǐ de lǎobǎn shì shéi?

you / DE / boss / is / who

Who's your boss?

我的工作让我压力很大。

Wǒ de gōngzuò ràng wǒ yālì hěn dà.

I / DE / work / makes / me / pressure / very / big

My job puts me under a lot of pressure.

Job 2 — an adjective

她是一个漂亮的老师。

Tā shì yí gè piàoliang de lǎoshī.

she / is / one / CL / pretty / DE / teacher

She's a beautiful teacher.

Job 3 — another noun

我很喜欢韩国的舞蹈。

Wǒ hěn xǐhuan Hánguó de wǔdǎo.

I / very / like / Korea / DE / dance

I really like Korean dance.

我要离开现在的房子。

Wǒ yào líkāi xiànzài de fángzi.

I / want / leave / now / DE / house

I want to move out of my current place.

Job 4 — a whole verb phrase

This is the one with no English shape at all. English says the words that the teacher said — a clause tacked on after the noun. Chinese puts the entire clause before the noun and glues it with 的.

老师说的话我都记得。

Lǎoshī shuō de huà wǒ dōu jìde.

teacher / say / DE / words / I / all / remember

I remember everything the teacher said.

When 的 drops out

Two situations: a close relationship, and a set label where the first word names a category rather than describing.

Common pitfall

Never put 的 after the noun. 老板的房子的 is not a thing — the noun is the end of the chain. And when several 的 stack up, the sentence is still read left to right: 我的老板的房子 = "my boss's house".

Practice

What does this mean?
小丽给我买的礼物很漂亮。

  • I bought Xiaoli a beautiful present.✗ Reversed. Inside the description 小丽给我买, 小丽 is the buyer and 我 is the receiver.
  • The present Xiaoli bought me is beautiful.✓ The whole clause 小丽给我买 + 的 clips onto 礼物; the described noun lands last, and the main sentence is 礼物很漂亮.
  • Xiaoli likes the present I bought her.✗ Nothing here means "like", and there is only one act of buying — 小丽 does it.

You want to say "a comfortable chair". Which is right?

  • 舒服的椅子✓ An adjective describing a noun needs 的 in between, and the noun comes last.
  • 舒服椅子✗ The English habit of gluing adjective straight onto noun with nothing. A two-syllable adjective wants 的.
  • 椅子的舒服✗ Reversed — that would be "the chair's comfort", a noun, not a description of a chair.

Which sounds most natural in everyday speech?

  • 我爸爸是出租车司机。✓ Close family drops 的; 出租车司机 names a kind of driver, so those two words are one label with no glue either.
  • 我的爸爸是出租车的司机。✗ Two over-corrections at once: 的 is normally dropped with close family, and 出租车司机 is a set job label.
  • 我爸爸的是出租车司机。✗ 的 can never sit after the noun it belongs to — the noun ends the chain.

Type the whole sentence, choosing 的 / 得 / 地: 昨天我在超市买了一些新鲜 ___ 水果。
(Yesterday I bought some fresh fruit at the supermarket.)

昨天我在超市买了一些新鲜的水果。 — 新鲜 says what the fruit is like, so it is a description and takes the glue. Contrast 汉语老师, where the first word names a kind and no 的 appears. 地 and 得 are both impossible here for the same reason: what follows the gap is a noun, and neither of them may sit in front of one.

Transform. 他昨天买了一个手机。那个手机很贵。 → join them into one sentence: "the phone he bought yesterday is very expensive." Start with 他. Type the whole sentence.

他昨天买的手机很贵。 — English hangs the clause after the noun ("the phone that he bought"); Chinese puts the whole clause 他昨天买 in front and clips it on with 的. The 了 disappears: 买的 already points at a finished purchase, and 了 cannot sit inside a description.

Translate. "My neighbour's dog is very cute."
用: 邻居 · 狗 · 可爱

我邻居的狗很可爱。 — English marks the owner with an ending ('s); Chinese marks it with 的 sitting between owner and thing. A neighbour is not close family, so unlike 我妈妈 this 的 stays. (我邻居 itself may keep or drop its own 的 — both are said.)

Write your own. One true sentence about something you own and use every day — a bag, a cup, a jacket, a bike. Build it around Job 4, the hard one: the noun must carry a whole clause in front of it — [who does what] + 的 + noun, the way 老师说的话 works. Then say something about that thing.

我每天上班带的那个包已经很旧了，可是我还是很喜欢它。

Wǒ měitiān shàngbān dài de nà ge bāo yǐjīng hěn jiù le, kěshì wǒ háishi hěn xǐhuan tā.

The bag I carry to work every day is pretty old now, but I still like it.

Check your sentence:

  • Does the whole description sit in front of the noun, with 的 in between? English trails the clause behind the noun ("the bag that I carry"); Chinese never does.
  • Is there a 了 inside the description? Take it out — a description carries no 了, even when the action is finished (我买的包, not 我买了的包). A 了 after the main adjective, like 很旧了, is a different 了 and is fine.
  • Did you drop 的 in the two places Chinese drops it — close family (我妈妈) and set job/category labels (汉语老师) — and keep it everywhere else?

2. 是…的 Spotlighting the when / where / how of a past event

When a speaker reaches for this: the event is already known to both of you — it definitely happened. What's being discussed is one detail of it: when, where, how, or with whom. 是 goes in front of that detail, 的 closes the sentence, and everything between them is under the spotlight.

The English trap

English spotlights with stress or a cleft: "I came to New York last year" / "It was last year that I came to New York." Chinese can't shout a syllable to do this — it uses a grammatical frame. English speakers therefore just say 我去年来纽约了, which is fine as plain news but never highlights the year. Worse, when someone asks 你是怎么来的？ the natural answer keeps the frame: 我是坐飞机来的。

Subject + 是 + when / where / how + V (+ O) + 的

我是去年来纽约的。

Wǒ shì qùnián lái Niǔyuē de.

I / SHI / last-year / come / New York / DE

It was last year that I came to New York.

我是在韩国学跳舞的。

Wǒ shì zài Hánguó xué tiàowǔ de.

I / SHI / at / Korea / learn / dance / DE

It was in Korea that I learned to dance.

你是怎么来的？

Nǐ shì zěnme lái de?

you / SHI / how / come / DE

How did you get here? (I know you're here — tell me the how.)

Plain 了 — new information
我来纽约了。
"I've come to New York." The news is the arrival itself — you're telling me it happened.
是…的 — spotlight on a detail
我是去年来纽约的。
"It was last year that I came." We both know I'm here; the year is the point.

Quick test

Could you answer the question with a single word — last year, by plane, in Korea? If yes, and the event is over and not in doubt, use 是…的. If instead you'd answer "yes, I did", it's plain 了.

Common pitfall

Don't mix the two frames. 我是去年来纽约了的 and 我是去年来纽约 are both wrong: 了 drops out, and the 的 at the end is not optional. In fast speech 是 can be left out (我去年来纽约的) — but 的 stays.

Practice

What does this mean?
他是在飞机上认识我姐姐的。

  • He's planning to meet my sister at the airport.✗ 是…的 always looks back at something already done; it cannot describe a plan.
  • It was on a plane that he met my sister.✓ That they met is taken as known; 在飞机上 is the detail under the spotlight, sitting between 是 and 的.
  • My sister often flies with him.✗ 认识 is a one-off event (getting to know someone), and 是…的 pins it to a single past occasion, never a habit.

Your friend already knows you moved house. She asks when. Which reply fits?

  • 我是上个月搬的。✓ The move is not in question — the month is. 是…的 spotlights it.
  • 我上个月搬家了。✗ Grammatical, but this announces the move as news; she already knows.
  • 我是上个月搬家了。✗ 是…的 and 了 can't share a sentence — and the 的 is missing.

Transform. 我姐姐昨天坐火车回来了。 → your friend already knows she is back and asks how she travelled. Rewrite it to spotlight 坐火车 (drop 昨天). Type the whole sentence.

我姐姐是坐火车回来的。 — three things move at once: 是 steps in front of the spotlit detail, 的 closes the sentence, and the 了 disappears. 我姐姐是坐火车回来了的 ✗ — the two frames cannot be stacked.

Translate. "It was last winter that my grandpa got ill."
用: 爷爷 · 是 · 去年冬天 · 生病 · 的

我爷爷是去年冬天生病的。 — English needs a whole cleft ("it was … that …") to lift the time into focus; Chinese just wraps 是 … 的 around the sentence with the time inside. Note the time word sits after 是: 是 has to touch the thing it spotlights.

Answer the question. 你是在哪儿学的做饭？ — at home. Start with 我 and keep the 是…的 frame.

我是在家学的做饭。 (also 我是在家学做饭的) — when the verb carries an object, the 的 may land either just before the object or right at the end; both are normal. What you cannot do is answer with 了 (我在家学做饭了): nobody asked whether you learned, only where.

Answer the question. 你是跟谁一起去看电影的？ — with a friend. Start with 我 and keep the questioner's 跟.

我是跟朋友一起去的。 — the reply keeps the questioner's frame and drops the new information into the spotlight. Chinese is happy to leave 看电影 out once it is established, but the closing 的 is never optional.

Build: "Xiaoli got back from Beijing yesterday afternoon." (She's back — the point is when, and where from.)

小丽 是 昨天下午 从北京 回来 的

Personalise it. Write two true sentences about your own past, each using 是…的. One says where you learned something you can do; one says when you came to the city you live in now.

我是在纽约学跳舞的，我很喜欢。我不是这儿的人，我是三年前搬到这儿来的。

Wǒ shì zài Niǔyuē xué tiàowǔ de, wǒ hěn xǐhuan. Wǒ bú shì zhèr de rén, wǒ shì sān nián qián bān dào zhèr lái de.

It was in New York that I learned to dance, and I love it. I'm not from here — I moved here three years ago.

Check your own two sentences:

  • Does each sentence have both halves — a 是 and a final 的?
  • Is 是 sitting immediately in front of the spotlit detail (the place, the time), rather than at the very start out of habit?
  • Is there no 了 anywhere? 是…的 and 了 cannot share a sentence.
  • Is the event itself already known or obvious — something you're adding a detail to, not announcing?
  • If the verb has an object (跳舞, 中文), is it inside the frame, in front of the 的?

3. 得 — the verb's report card How did the action turn out?

When a speaker reaches for this: the action has happened (usually many times), and now you want to judge it — fast, slow, well, badly, loudly. 得 hooks that judgement onto the back of the verb. It is a report, written after the fact: 睡得不好 (slept badly), 说得很快 (speaks fast).

The English trap

English puts the judgement before or around the verb — "he quickly ran", "I slept badly" — and it uses an adverb form (-ly). Chinese puts it strictly after the verb, hooked on with 得, and uses the plain adjective, normally with 很: 他跑得很快. Also note that the adjective still wants 很 the way any Chinese adjective does — 跑得快 alone sounds like the start of a comparison.

得 is a backward-pointing arrow: the action already happened, and the phrase after 得 grades it. That direction is the whole difference between 得 and 地 (grammar point 4).
V + 得 + (很) Adj

昨天晚上我睡得不好。

Zuótiān wǎnshang wǒ shuì de bù hǎo.

yesterday / evening / I / sleep / DE / not / good

I slept badly last night.

我的老师说得很快，我听不懂。

Wǒ de lǎoshī shuō de hěn kuài, wǒ tīng bu dǒng.

my / teacher / speak / DE / very / fast, / I / hear-can't-understand

My teacher speaks very fast — I can't follow.

你跑得真快！

Nǐ pǎo de zhēn kuài!

you / run / DE / really / fast

You run really fast!

The rule you keep tripping on: verbs with objects must repeat the verb

得 must sit directly against its verb — nothing may squeeze in between. So what happens when the verb already carries an object, like 跳舞 (dance-a-dance) or 唱歌 (sing-a-song)? The object is in the way. The fix: say the verb twice — once with its object, once with 得.

V + O + V + 得 + (很) Adj

我跳舞跳得很好。

Wǒ tiàowǔ tiào de hěn hǎo.

I / dance-dance(O) / dance / DE / very / good

I dance very well.

他唱歌唱得很大声。

Tā chànggē chàng de hěn dàshēng.

he / sing-song(O) / sing / DE / very / loud

He sings very loudly.

There is a shortcut. Instead of repeating the verb, move the object to the front as a topic — "as for the song / as for Chinese…" — and then the verb is free to take 得 on its own. Both versions are correct and both are common.

歌他唱得很大声。

Gē tā chàng de hěn dàshēng.

song(topic) / he / sing / DE / very / loud

His singing is very loud. ("As for singing, he does it loudly.")

他汉语说得很好。

Tā Hànyǔ shuō de hěn hǎo.

he / Chinese(topic) / speak / DE / very / good

He speaks Chinese very well.

✗ Object blocking 得
我跳舞得很好
Wrong: 舞 has pushed 得 away from its verb. 得 must touch the verb.
✓ Repeat the verb
我跳舞跳得很好
Right: say 跳 again so 得 has something to hold on to. (Or topicalise: 舞我跳得很好.)

Don't confuse the two 得's

You met a different 得 in Lesson 5: the possibility 得 in 看得懂 / 听得见 ("can succeed at"). Tell them apart by what follows 得:

  • followed by (很) + adjective → report card: 说得很快 = speaks fast.
  • followed by a bare result verb (懂, 见, 完, 到) → possibility: 听得懂 = can understand.

Practice

What does this mean?
面条我做得不好，可是蛋糕做得很好。

  • I don't like noodles, but I like cake.✗ Nothing here means "like". 做得不好 is a verdict on how the making turns out.
  • I'm not good at making noodles, but I'm good at making cakes.✓ 面条 and 蛋糕 have been moved to the front as topics, which frees 做 to take 得 on its own.
  • My noodles aren't ready yet, but the cake is done.✗ "Not finished" would be 没做完. 得 + adjective grades quality; it never reports progress.

You want to say "She swims very fast." Which is right?

  • 她游泳游得很快。✓ 泳 is an object standing in the way, so the verb 游 is said again for 得 to hold onto.
  • 她游泳得很快。✗ 得 has been pushed off its verb by 泳. Either repeat the verb (游泳游得很快) or drop the object (她游得很快).
  • 她游得很快游泳。✗ The copy is in the wrong order: verb+object first, bare repeated verb second — 游泳游得很快.

Type the whole sentence, choosing 的 / 得 / 地: 这个孩子今年长 ___ 很高。
(This child has grown very tall this year.)

这个孩子今年长得很高。 — a verb (长) sits in front and 很高 is the verdict on how the growing went. 地 is impossible: it would have to come before a verb, and 高 is not a verb. And note which 得 this is — what follows is an adjective, so it's the report card. Followed instead by a bare result verb (看得懂, 爬得上去) it would be the possibility 得 from Lesson 5.

Fill the missing verb: 爷爷走路 ___ 得很慢，可是骑自行车骑得很快。
(Grandpa walks slowly, but he cycles fast.)

走 — 路 is standing between 走 and 得, so the verb 走 comes back: 走路走得很慢. The second half shows the same move with a longer object: 骑自行车骑得很快.

Transform. 他打篮球。他打得很好。 → say it in one sentence. Type the whole thing.

他打篮球打得很好。 — 得 must touch its verb and 篮球 is in the way, so 打 is said twice. The other legal repair is to move the object to the front as a topic: 篮球他打得很好. What is impossible is 他打篮球得很好.

Translate. "My little brother eats very slowly."
用: 弟弟 · 吃饭 · 慢

弟弟吃饭吃得很慢。 — English puts the adverb straight after the verb with no marker ("eats slowly"); Chinese needs 得, and 得 needs a bare verb in front of it, so 吃 returns for a second turn. Keep the 很: 吃得慢 on its own sounds like the opening of a comparison.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 他写字得很漂亮。
(Intended: "His handwriting is beautiful.")

他写字写得很漂亮。 — the object 字 has pushed 得 away from 写, and 得 must touch its verb. Repeat the verb, or topicalise the object (字他写得很漂亮). 很漂亮 was fine; only the position was broken.

Build: "Grandma tells stories really well." (Use the verb-repeat pattern — 得 must touch its verb.)

奶奶 讲故事 讲 得 很好

Write your own. One true sentence about your mornings — how early or late you get up, and whether the weekend is different. Use V + 得 + (很) Adj. Note that 起床 already drags an object (床) behind the verb, so you will have to make the repeat move.

我上班的时候起床起得很早，可是周末起得特别晚。

Wǒ shàngbān de shíhou qǐchuáng qǐ de hěn zǎo, kěshì zhōumò qǐ de tèbié wǎn.

On workdays I get up very early, but at the weekend I get up really late.

Check your sentence:

  • Does 得 sit immediately after a bare verb, with nothing squeezed in between? 起床得很早 ✗ — 床 has pushed 得 off its verb.
  • So either repeat the verb (起床起得很早) or leave the object out altogether (起得很早). Both are normal; what is not allowed is object + 得.
  • Is there a 很 / 太 / 不 / 特别 in front of the adjective? 起得早 on its own sounds like the opening half of a comparison, not a finished statement.

4. 地 — the manner marker How you go into the action

When a speaker reaches for this: to say in what manner the action is (or will be) carried out — happily, slowly, carefully, quietly. 地 sits between the description and the verb, and it points forward: first you set the mood, then the verb happens in that mood.

The English trap

This is the one English actually has a shape for — the -ly adverb — so it feels easy, and that's the danger. English speakers write the right word but the wrong character, reaching for 的 (the common one they know) or 得 (the one that also sits next to verbs). Remember the position: 地 comes before the verb, while 得 comes after it. 高兴地说 vs 说得很高兴 — same two words, opposite structures.

地 is a forward-pointing arrow. Compare it with the backward arrow of 得 in grammar point 3 — the two pictures are mirror images, and so are the two characters' jobs.
Adj / state + 地 + V

我们快乐地学习汉语。

Wǒmen kuàilè de xuéxí Hànyǔ.

we / happy / DE / study / Chinese

We study Chinese happily.

妈妈慢慢地走进房间。

Māma mànmàn de zǒu jìn fángjiān.

mum / slow-slow / DE / walk / into / room

Mum walked slowly into the room.

我每天认真地练习舞蹈。

Wǒ měitiān rènzhēn de liànxí wǔdǎo.

I / every-day / earnest / DE / practise / dance

I practise dancing carefully every day.

The key contrast: 地 vs 得

Take the same two words, 高兴 and 说, and change only which de joins them. The meaning flips.

地 — forward, before the verb
老师高兴地说：…
She was already happy, and that mood is how she delivered the words. The point of the sentence is what she said; 高兴 just colours the delivery.
得 — backward, after the verb
老师说得很高兴。
She talked, and the talking turned out joyful — she enjoyed herself while speaking. The point of the sentence is the verdict 很高兴.
地 (before)得 (after)Meaning delta
慢慢地走走得很慢"walk slowly" (a chosen way) vs "walks slowly" (a judgement on the walking).
认真地工作工作得很认真"set to work seriously" vs "her work is done seriously".
快乐地跳舞跳舞跳得很快乐"dance joyfully" vs "the dancing was joyful".

Word-order memory hook

地 and 得 are on opposite sides of the verb. Read the sentence left to right: anything before the verb is a plan (地), anything after it is a result (得). Time order, as always.

Practice

These two are not the same. What does the first one mean?
(1) 叔叔慢慢地喝完了那杯咖啡。 (2) 叔叔喝咖啡喝得很慢。

  • He took his time over that cup of coffee — unhurried was the way he drank it.✓ 慢慢地 sits in front of the verb: it sets the manner he goes into the action with, on this one occasion.
  • He is a slow coffee drinker.✗ That is sentence (2): 得 after the verb delivers a verdict on his coffee-drinking in general.
  • He didn't manage to finish the coffee.✗ 喝完了 says the cup was finished; 慢慢地 only colours how he got there.

You want to say "to study hard" (i.e. to do the studying in a hard-working way).

  • 努力地学习✓ The description sits before the verb, so it takes 地.
  • 努力的学习✗ With 的 this becomes a noun phrase, "hard work / diligent study" — a thing, not an action.
  • 努力得学习✗ 得 only ever comes after the verb it grades, never before it.

Type the whole sentence, choosing 的 / 得 / 地: 服务员热情 ___ 介绍了他们的新菜单。
(The waiter warmly introduced their new menu.)

服务员热情地介绍了他们的新菜单。 — 热情 comes before the verb 介绍 and says how he went about it. Move it behind the verb (介绍得很热情) and you would need 得 — and you would be grading the introduction rather than colouring it. Notice the sentence keeps its 了 on the verb: 地 sets up the action, it does not block the aspect marker.

Transform. 他关上了教室的门。 → say that he did it carefully. Use 小心 + 地 and keep everything else. Type the whole sentence.

他小心地关上了教室的门。 — the manner phrase slides in between the subject and the verb. English can put the adverb at the end ("closed the door carefully"); Chinese cannot — after the verb is 得 territory, and there the sentence would become a verdict on the closing.

Translate. "My older sister checked her homework carefully."
用: 姐姐 · 认真 · 地 · 检查 · 作业

姐姐认真地检查了作业。 — English builds an adverb out of the adjective (careful → carefully) and parks it at the end. Chinese leaves the adjective untouched, adds 地, and puts the whole thing in front of the verb. The 了 still rides on the verb as usual; 地 does not block it.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 他是一个很聪明地学生。
(Intended: "He's a very clever student.")

他是一个很聪明的学生。 — look at what comes next: 学生 is a noun, and only 的 can glue a description to a noun. 地 would need a verb on its right. This is the standard 地 error once the marker has been learned — it gets sprayed onto every adjective.

Write your own. One true sentence about what you do when something goes wrong — you can't find your phone, the train is late, you've forgotten a name. Use Adj + 地 + Verb, and reach for an adjective this page hasn't already worn out: try 着急, 大声 or 客气 rather than 高兴 / 认真.

找不到手机的时候，我总是着急地给自己打电话。

Zhǎo bu dào shǒujī de shíhou, wǒ zǒngshì zháojí de gěi zìjǐ dǎ diànhuà.

When I can't find my phone, I anxiously call myself.

Check your sentence:

  • Read rightwards from your 地: is there a verb immediately after it, and does the word in front say how you go into that verb?
  • Have you accidentally written the 得 sentence instead? If your adjective is a verdict on how the action turned out, it belongs after the verb with 得 (说得很着急) — that is a different sentence from 着急地说.
  • Is the character you actually typed 地 — not 的, not 得? All three sound identical, so the slot has to decide, never the ear.

5. Which de? A three-question decision tree

When a speaker reaches for this: every time you write. You never hear which one it is — you have to work it out from the slot. Luckily the slot is decided by one thing only: what sits immediately to the right of the character.

Ask these in order

  1. Is a NOUN coming next? → 的. 漂亮的老师, 韩国的舞蹈, 我的老板.
  2. Is a VERB coming next, and the word before describes how you'll do it? → 地. 快乐地学习, 认真地工作, 慢慢地走.
  3. Is a VERB sitting just before it, and a judgement coming after? → 得. 睡得不好, 跑得很快, 跳舞跳得很好.

The trap sentence

All three in a row, from this lesson's title: 我的老师高兴地说：你跳舞跳得越来越好！ Walk it: 老师 is a noun → 的. 说 is a verb and 高兴 is how she said it → 地. 跳 is a verb and 越来越好 grades it → 得.

Practice

Type the three missing characters in order:
小刚 ___ 女儿每天都努力 ___ 练习，跑步跑 ___ 越来越快。
(Three characters, e.g. 的地得 — no spaces needed. "Xiaogang's daughter practises hard every day; she runs faster and faster.")

的 · 地 · 得 — walk it left to right. 女儿 is a noun → 的. 练习 is a verb and 努力 says how she goes at it → 地. 跑 is a verb sitting in front and 越来越快 is the verdict behind → 得.

的, 得 or 地? 这是我从图书馆借 ___ 书，下个星期要还。
(This is the book I borrowed from the library — it's due back next week.)

的 — a noun (书) comes next, so it is the glue, and everything to its left (我从图书馆借) is the description. 得 is ruled out because no verdict follows the gap.

的, 得 or 地? 那个孩子难过 ___ 哭了起来。
(The child burst into tears, terribly upset.)

地 — a verb (哭) is coming next and 难过 is the state he goes into it with. Swap in 得 and the verb would have to come first: 哭得很难过, a verdict on the crying rather than the mood behind it.

Which sentence is written correctly?

  • 她安静地打扫房间，房间打扫得很干净。✓ 地 before the verb, 得 after it — both slots correct.
  • 她安静得打扫房间，房间打扫地很干净。✗ The two are swapped: 得 can't stand before a verb, and 地 can't stand after one.
  • 她安静的打扫房间，房间打扫的很干净。✗ 的 only glues a description to a noun, and neither gap here has a noun on its right.

Personalise it. Pick something you do regularly. Write two true sentences about it: one with Adj + 地 + Verb (how you go into it), one with Verb + 得 + (很) Adj (how it turns out). Use a different adjective in each.

我每个星期六都认真地练习舞蹈，可是我跳舞跳得还不太好。

Wǒ měi ge xīngqīliù dōu rènzhēn de liànxí wǔdǎo, kěshì wǒ tiàowǔ tiào de hái bú tài hǎo.

Every Saturday I practise dancing seriously, but I still don't dance very well.

Check your own two sentences:

  • In the 地 sentence, does the description sit before the verb?
  • In the 得 sentence, does 得 sit immediately after the verb, with nothing squeezed in between?
  • If that verb carries an object (跳舞, 唱歌, 看书), did you repeat the verb — or move the object to the front?
  • Is there a 很 (or 不, 太, 越来越) in front of the adjective after 得?
  • Two different adjectives, and both sentences actually true of you?

Mixed practice Shuffled — plus a few things from earlier lessons

Nothing below comes in section order, so you have to work out which de a sentence needs before you can build it — which is what writing actually asks of you. Three items aren't from this lesson at all: one 还没…呢 answer, one 比 comparison and one time-word placement, dropped in to keep the older machinery running.

What does this mean?
上个星期我是跟同事一起去旅游的，我们玩得很高兴。

  • Next week I want to go travelling with colleagues and have a good time.✗ 是…的 looks back at a finished trip, and 上个星期 is last week, not next.
  • Last week I went travelling with colleagues — and we had a great time.✓ 是…的 spotlights 跟同事一起 (who with); then 玩得很高兴 is 得 delivering a verdict on how the trip went.
  • My colleagues went travelling last week and enjoyed it.✗ 我 is the subject; 跟同事一起 says the colleagues came along with the speaker.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 他生气的走出了办公室。
(Intended: "He walked out of the office angrily.")

他生气地走出了办公室。 — check what stands to the right of the marker: 走 is a verb, so the manner marker 地 is required. 的 would need a noun there. The two characters sound identical, which is precisely why the slot, not the ear, has to decide.

Transform. 我们在机场见面了。 → your friend already knows you two met and asks where. Rewrite it with the 是…的 frame. Type the whole sentence.

我们是在机场见面的。 — the meeting itself is old news, so the 了 announcement is the wrong tool; 是 goes in front of 在机场 and 的 shuts the sentence. (见面 splits, so 我们是在机场见的面 is equally good.)

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 医生说的很清楚，我都听懂了。
(Intended: "The doctor explained very clearly — I understood it all.")

医生说得很清楚，我都听懂了。 — the marker follows the verb 说 and introduces a judgement (很清楚), so it must be 得. Writing 的 here is the single commonest slip of the three, because 的 is the character you write most often. Test it: is there a noun after the marker? No → it isn't 的.

Translate. "This is the newspaper my dad bought yesterday."
用: 这 · 爸爸 · 昨天 · 买 · 报纸

这是我爸爸昨天买的报纸。 — the entire clause 我爸爸昨天买 goes in front of 报纸 and is clipped on with 的; English does the opposite and trails it behind. No 了 inside the description, even though the buying is over.

From an earlier lesson. Answer the question. 你复习完了吗？ — no, not yet, and you still mean to. Start with 我 and use 还没…呢.

我还没复习完呢。 — a 了吗 question about a completed action is answered in the negative with 没, never 不, and the 了 never survives into the answer: 我还没复习完了 ✗. The 还 is what turns "didn't" into "not yet".

From an earlier lesson. Combine into one sentence with 比: 今天很热。昨天不太热。 Start with 今天, and add no degree word (no 更, 还, 得多).

今天比昨天热。 — 比 sentences drop 很 completely. 今天比昨天很热 ✗: the adjective is already being measured against something, so it refuses its usual 很 crutch.

From an earlier lesson. Build: "Every Saturday morning I go to the park to exercise." (Two orders work — what's fixed is that time comes before the verb.)

我 每个星期六 早上 都 去公园 锻炼

Write it all. Describe someone you know well — a teacher, a colleague, a family member — in 4–5 sentences. You must use all three: at least one 的 joining a description to a noun, one Adj + 地 + Verb, and one Verb + 得 + (很) Adj. Add one 是…的 sentence saying when or where you met them.

我的舞蹈老师是一个很热情的人。我是三年前在一个小教室认识她的。上课的时候，她总是很认真地看我们练习，然后慢慢地告诉我们哪儿做错了。她跳舞跳得非常好，我每次看她跳都特别高兴。

Wǒ de wǔdǎo lǎoshī shì yí ge hěn rèqíng de rén. Wǒ shì sān nián qián zài yí ge xiǎo jiàoshì rènshi tā de. Shàngkè de shíhou, tā zǒngshì hěn rènzhēn de kàn wǒmen liànxí, ránhòu mànmàn de gàosu wǒmen nǎr zuò cuò le. Tā tiàowǔ tiào de fēicháng hǎo, wǒ měi cì kàn tā tiào dōu tèbié gāoxìng.

My dance teacher is a very warm person. I met her three years ago in a small studio. In class she always watches us practise very attentively, then slowly tells us where we went wrong. She dances beautifully, and I'm delighted every time I watch her.

Self-check — go through your paragraph once per line:

  • Every 的 has a noun immediately to its right. Read rightwards from each one and check.
  • Every 地 has a verb immediately to its right, and the word before it says how.
  • Every 得 has a verb immediately to its left, with nothing squeezed in between.
  • Where a 得 verb carries an object (跳舞, 说话, 唱歌), did you repeat the verb — or move the object to the front?
  • Does the 是…的 sentence have both halves and no 了 anywhere?
  • Did you drop 的 where Chinese drops it — close family (我妈妈) and job labels (舞蹈老师)?
