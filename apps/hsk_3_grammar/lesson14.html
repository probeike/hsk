1. 除了 … 以外，都 / 还 / 也 … "Besides X" — three very different meanings

When a speaker reaches for this: to single out X and then say something about the rest (除了…以外…都), or about X plus more (除了…还/也). Same opening, very different landings.

The English trap

English "besides X" is ambiguous — does X count or not? Chinese disambiguates with the second half:

  • 除了 X 以外，… 都 … → X is excluded. "Everyone except X."
  • 除了 X 以外，… 还/也 … → X is included, plus more. "In addition to X."

The pivotal word is the one after the comma. 都 = rest only. 还/也 = X + others.

除了 + X + 以外 , 都 / 还 / 也 + …

除了小刚以外，我们都去过北京。

Chúle Xiǎogāng yǐwài, wǒmen dōu qù guo Běijīng.

besides / Xiaogang / outside / we / all / go-PERF / Beijing

Except for Xiaogang, we've all been to Beijing. (Xiaogang has NOT been.)

除了小刚以外，我们也去过北京。

Chúle Xiǎogāng yǐwài, wǒmen yě qù guo Běijīng.

besides / Xiaogang / outside / we / also / go-PERF / Beijing

Besides Xiaogang, we've also been to Beijing. (Xiaogang has been too — we joined him.)

除了苹果以外，他还买了香蕉。

Chúle píngguǒ yǐwài, tā hái mǎi le xiāngjiāo.

Besides apples, he also bought bananas. (He bought both.)

excludes X — 都
除了他以外，大家都来了
Everyone came except him. He didn't come.
includes X — 也/还
除了他以外，大家也来了
Besides him, everyone else came too. He came, and so did they.

Decision guide after 除了

Do not decide from 除了…以外 alone. Decide from the idea you need after the comma:

  • "X is the exception" → use 都: 除了小王以外，大家都来了 = everyone came except Xiao Wang.
  • "X is one item, and there is more" → use 还: 除了咖啡以外，他还点了茶 = he ordered coffee and also tea.
  • "X did it, and others did too" → use 也: 除了老师以外，学生也去了 = the teacher went, and the students also went.

Quick test: if X did not do the thing, choose 都. If X did do it and you are adding more, choose 还/也.

以外 is optional but common

You'll see 除了他，我们都去了 without 以外 — it's fine, slightly more casual. The logic of the 都/还/也 split is unchanged.

Who ate the cake in: 除了我以外，他们都吃了蛋糕。

  • Only I ate it.✗ Opposite — 除了我以外 + 都 = everyone except me.
  • They all ate it; I didn't.✓ 除了 X + 都 excludes X.
  • Everyone including me.✗ That would need 也/还, not 都.

"Besides Chinese, he also speaks English." Pick the right pivot:

  • 除了汉语以外，他还会说英语。✓ He speaks both — 还 adds to X.
  • 除了汉语以外，他都会说英语。✗ 都 would mean he speaks everything except Chinese — wrong meaning.

Build: "Except for him, we've all been to Shanghai."

Build: "Besides apples, he also bought bananas."

Translate: 除了洗澡以外，他还喜欢锻炼。

  • He likes to bathe but not to exercise.✗ 除了…还 includes both — he does both.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about groups you belong to (your family, your team at work, your dance class, your neighbours) or about your own habits and skills. Every sentence uses 除了 … 以外. At least two of them must land on 都 (the named person or thing is the exception), and at least one on 还 or 也 (it counts too, and there is more). Use three different verbs, and make one sentence negative. Every sentence has to be true for you.

除了星期一以外，我每天早上都去公园跑步。除了跳舞以外，我还很喜欢做菜。我们公司的人除了我以外，都住在城市的南边。除了英语以外，我还会说一点儿中文。除了周末以外，我晚上都不看电视。

Chúle xīngqīyī yǐwài, wǒ měitiān zǎoshang dōu qù gōngyuán pǎobù. Chúle tiàowǔ yǐwài, wǒ hái hěn xǐhuan zuò cài. Wǒmen gōngsī de rén chúle wǒ yǐwài, dōu zhù zài chéngshì de nánbian. Chúle Yīngyǔ yǐwài, wǒ hái huì shuō yìdiǎnr Zhōngwén. Chúle zhōumò yǐwài, wǒ wǎnshang dōu bú kàn diànshì.

Except for Monday, I go running in the park every morning. Besides dancing, I also really like cooking. Everyone at my company except me lives on the south side of the city. Besides English, I can also speak a little Chinese. Except at the weekend, I don't watch television in the evening.

Why these are right: the opening half is identical in all five — the meaning is decided entirely after the comma. 除了星期一…都 and 除了我…都 subtract: Monday is the day I don't run, I'm the one who doesn't live south. 除了跳舞…还 and 除了英语…还 add: dancing and English both stay in, and something else joins them. The negative one works exactly like the 都 sentences — 除了周末以外，我晚上都不看电视 subtracts the weekend from a run of evenings, and 都 sweeps just as happily over a 不 as over a positive verb. Note also that 除了…以外 can open the sentence or sit after the subject (我们公司的人除了我以外…) — what it can never do is follow the verb.

Check your own sentences:

  • For each sentence, ask the test question: does the named person/thing do it too? No → 都. Yes, plus others → 还/也.
  • Is there really a group for 都 to sweep up? 除了我以外，我朋友都… ✗ — one friend is not a set.
  • Is the 除了 phrase at the front of the clause or right after the subject — never after the verb?
  • Did you keep 以外 (or drop it consistently)? 除了他，我们都去了 is fine; 以外他 ✗ — 以外 closes the phrase, it never opens it.
  • Three different verbs, and is every sentence actually true about your own life?

2. Flexible interrogatives 2 谁 / 什么 / 哪儿 without asking anything

When a speaker reaches for this: to say "anyone / everyone", "anything / everything", "anywhere / everywhere" — without an actual question. Chinese reuses the question words; tone and context make the job clear.

The English trap

English has separate words: who? vs anyone/everyone. Chinese uses the same word (谁) for all three senses. You tell them apart from the companion word:

  • 谁 + 都 / 也 → "everyone / no one" (universal)
  • 谁 + 不/没 after 都/也 → "no one"
  • 谁 alone in a 吗-sentence or non-question → "anyone" (existential)

Same pattern for 什么 (anything/everything), 哪儿 (anywhere/everywhere), 怎么 (any way/every way).

谁都知道这件事。

Shéi dōu zhīdào zhè jiàn shì.

Everyone knows about this.

我什么都不想吃。

Wǒ shénme dōu bù xiǎng chī.

I don't want to eat anything.

周末我哪儿也不想去。

Zhōumò wǒ nǎr yě bù xiǎng qù.

I don't want to go anywhere this weekend.

你有什么想吃的吗？

Nǐ yǒu shénme xiǎng chī de ma?

Is there anything you'd like to eat? (什么 = "anything" here, not "what".)

question
你吃什么？
"What are you eating?" — rising, information-seeking.
universal
我什么都吃
"I eat everything." — 都 converts 什么 into "everything".

Transform a question word into "any/every"

Start with the real question, then add a companion word to change its job:

  • 你认识谁？ = Who do you know? → 我谁都认识 = I know everyone.
  • 你认识谁？ = Who do you know? → 我谁都不认识 = I don't know anyone.
  • 你想吃什么？ = What do you want to eat? → 我什么都想吃 = I want to eat everything.
  • 你有想吃的吗？ = Is there something you want to eat? → 你有什么想吃的吗？ = Is there anything you want to eat?

The word order is usually question word + 都/也 + verb. Add 不/没 after 都/也 for "not anyone / not anything / not anywhere".

Which reads as "I don't recognize anyone"?

  • 我谁都不认识。✓ 谁 + 都 + 不 = "no one".
  • 我认识谁？✗ That's a question: "Who do I recognize?"
  • 我不认识谁。✗ Ambiguous at best — missing the 都/也 that forces the universal reading.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about your own week using a question word as "every / any": 谁, 什么 or 哪儿 + 都 / 也. Use at least two different question words, make at least one sentence positive ("everyone / everything") and at least one negative (… 都 / 也 + 不 / 没 …), and use three different verbs. Every sentence has to be true for you.

上个星期我太忙了，哪儿也没去，每天晚上都在公司工作。我们家的人谁都会做几个菜，只有我做得不太好。冬天的早上我什么都不想做，就想在床上多睡一会儿。公司新来的同事，我谁也不认识。

Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ tài máng le, nǎr yě méi qù, měitiān wǎnshang dōu zài gōngsī gōngzuò. Wǒmen jiā de rén shéi dōu huì zuò jǐ ge cài, zhǐyǒu wǒ zuò de bú tài hǎo. Dōngtiān de zǎoshang wǒ shénme dōu bù xiǎng zuò, jiù xiǎng zài chuáng shang duō shuì yíhuìr. Gōngsī xīn lái de tóngshì, wǒ shéi yě bú rènshi.

Last week I was too busy — I didn't go anywhere, and I was at the office every evening. Everyone in my family can cook a few dishes; I'm the only one who isn't much good at it. On winter mornings I don't want to do anything, I just want to stay in bed a bit longer. The new colleagues at work — I don't know a single one of them.

Why these are right: in every one, the question word stands in front of 都/也 and the whole block stands in front of the verb — Chinese front-loads what English leaves at the end ("didn't go anywhere" → 哪儿也没去). Positive 谁都会做 = "every one of them can"; negative 谁也不认识 = "not a single one". The negation is chosen by time, not by the pattern: 哪儿也没去 is last week and finished, 什么都不想做 is a standing attitude. And when the question word carries a topic ahead of it (公司新来的同事，我谁也不认识), the comma does the work — the object is still not allowed to sit after the verb.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the order question word + 都/也 + (不/没) + verb? 我不想去哪儿 ✗ — English order, not Chinese.
  • Did you actually include 都 or 也? Without it, 谁/什么/哪儿 slides back to being a real question.
  • Under negation, 也 is the more natural partner (哪儿也没去); with a positive verb it is almost always 都.
  • Finished past → 没, and no 了 after it. General habit or wish → 不.
  • Two different question words, one positive sentence, one negative — and all of it true about your week?

3. Adj + 极了 "Extremely / to the max"

When a speaker reaches for this: casual, emphatic praise or complaint — "amazing!", "exhausted!", "delicious!". 极了 is a degree complement: it sits after the adjective.

The English trap

English stacks intensifiers before the adjective: super good, really tired. Chinese puts 极了 after: 好极了, 累极了. Don't say *极了好 — 极了 is a tail, not a prefix.

Adj + 极了

这个菜好吃极了。

Zhège cài hǎochī jí le.

This dish is amazingly tasty.

我今天累极了。

Wǒ jīntiān lèi jí le.

I'm exhausted today.

极了 is spoken & casual

Don't stack it with 很/非常. *很好极了 (✗) — pick one intensifier. 极了 already means "to the extreme". It's also conversational; in formal writing you'll see 非常 or 十分 instead.

Register and adjective fit

极了 sounds expressive, spoken, and a little dramatic. It fits reactions, praise, complaints, and personal feeling better than neutral reports.

  • 漂亮极了 = gorgeous; 高兴极了 = extremely happy.
  • 热闹极了 = incredibly lively; 安静极了 = extremely quiet.
  • 舒服极了 = wonderfully comfortable; 麻烦极了 = terribly troublesome.
  • 干净极了 = spotless / extremely clean; 冷极了 = freezing cold.

For formal or measured writing, prefer 非常, 十分, or 特别 before the adjective.

"The movie was super interesting" — pick the natural version:

  • 那个电影有意思极了。✓ Adj + 极了.
  • 那个电影极了有意思。✗ 极了 must follow the adjective.
  • 那个电影很极了有意思。✗ Don't stack 很 with 极了.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences reacting to real things from the past week — the weather, a meal, a room, how a day left you feeling. Each sentence uses Adj + 极了, with three different adjectives; at least one must be a complaint and one a compliment. Add a second short clause to each sentence saying how you know — the fact behind the reaction. No 很 or 非常 anywhere near 极了. Every sentence has to be true.

昨天的天气冷极了，我在车站等了十分钟，手都疼了。星期五下班以前我忙极了，一杯水都没喝。我们楼下新开的饭馆便宜极了，两个人吃饭只用了三十块钱。我上个月搬进的新房间不大，可是干净极了，我几乎不用打扫。

Zuótiān de tiānqì lěng jí le, wǒ zài chēzhàn děng le shí fēnzhōng, shǒu dōu téng le. Xīngqīwǔ xiàbān yǐqián wǒ máng jí le, yì bēi shuǐ dōu méi hē. Wǒmen lóu xià xīn kāi de fànguǎn piányi jí le, liǎng ge rén chīfàn zhǐ yòng le sānshí kuài qián. Wǒ shàng ge yuè bān jìn de xīn fángjiān bú dà, kěshì gānjìng jí le, wǒ jīhū bú yòng dǎsǎo.

Yesterday's weather was freezing — I waited ten minutes at the stop and my hands hurt. Before I finished work on Friday I was flat out; I didn't drink a single glass of water. The restaurant that just opened downstairs is dirt cheap — dinner for two only came to thirty kuai. The new room I moved into last month isn't big, but it's spotless — I hardly ever need to clean it.

Why these are right: 极了 is a tail. It rides behind the adjective (冷极了 · 忙极了 · 便宜极了 · 干净极了) and nothing may be stacked in front — 很/非常 already say "very", so 很冷极了 is two intensifiers fighting for one slot. Because 极了 is a reaction rather than a measurement, each sentence earns it with a fact: ten minutes in the cold, not one glass of water, thirty kuai, no cleaning needed. Note the last one — 不大 stays plain, so the contrast with 干净极了 has something to push against.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is 极了 after the adjective every time? 极了冷 ✗ — it is a complement, not a prefix.
  • Any 很 / 非常 / 太 left in front of the same adjective? Delete it — pick one intensifier.
  • Is the word in front of 极了 really an adjective? 我极了喜欢 ✗ — a verb needs 非常喜欢 instead.
  • Three different adjectives, one complaint and one compliment — did you avoid using 累 for all of them?
  • Does each reaction come with a fact that backs it up, and is every sentence true about your real week?

Write the paragraph. Last Saturday morning you went swimming with 小丽 and 小刚. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all three structures of this lesson at least once each: 除了 … 以外，… 都 / 还 / 也 …, a question word (谁 / 什么 / 哪儿) + 都 / 也, and Adj + 极了. Cover: which of the three of you could swim, what 小刚 turned up carrying, how the water felt, and what you did instead of going on somewhere afterwards.

上个星期六早上，我和小丽、小刚一起去游泳。除了小刚以外，我们几个都会游泳，他只能坐在旁边看包。那天的水冷极了，下水以后谁都不想说话。小刚除了手机以外什么都没带，水和吃的都是小丽准备的。游完以后我们哪儿也没去，就在旁边的小饭馆吃了一碗面条，回家的时候累极了。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù zǎoshang, wǒ hé Xiǎolì, Xiǎogāng yìqǐ qù yóuyǒng. Chúle Xiǎogāng yǐwài, wǒmen jǐ ge dōu huì yóuyǒng, tā zhǐ néng zuò zài pángbiān kān bāo. Nà tiān de shuǐ lěng jí le, xià shuǐ yǐhòu shéi dōu bù xiǎng shuōhuà. Xiǎogāng chúle shǒujī yǐwài shénme dōu méi dài, shuǐ hé chī de dōu shì Xiǎolì zhǔnbèi de. Yóu wán yǐhòu wǒmen nǎr yě méi qù, jiù zài pángbiān de xiǎo fànguǎn chī le yì wǎn miàntiáo, huí jiā de shíhou lèi jí le.

Last Saturday morning 小丽, 小刚 and I went swimming. Apart from 小刚, the rest of us can all swim — he could only sit at the side watching the bags. The water that day was freezing; once we were in, nobody felt like talking. 小刚 hadn't brought a thing except his phone — the water and the food were all 小丽's doing. Afterwards we didn't go anywhere, we just had a bowl of noodles at the little restaurant next door, and by the time I got home I was wrecked.

Why this works as a paragraph: the three structures are doing three different jobs. 除了小刚以外…都 sorts the group (he is the exception, and the sentence about him proves it); 除了手机以外什么都没带 does the opposite job with the same opening — the phone stays in, everything else is swept out by 什么都没; 谁都不想说话 and 哪儿也没去 carry the story forward without naming anyone; and the two 极了 clauses are where the narrator's reaction lives, at the two moments that deserve one. Time words (上个星期六早上 · 那天 · 游完以后 · 回家的时候) are what hold it together.

Check your own paragraph:

  • 除了…以外: does the word after the comma match your meaning — 都 to subtract, 还/也 to add?
  • Question word + 都/也 sits before the verb: 什么都没带 ✓ · 没带什么 ✗ (that means "didn't bring much").
  • Finished past under 没 — and 没 kills the 了: 哪儿也没去了 ✗
  • 极了 after the adjective, with no 很/非常 in front of it, and used on reactions rather than on every sentence.
  • Does it read as one Saturday morning — time words and 可是/以后 joining it — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

  1. 都没买什么 → 什么都没买. English leaves "anything" after the verb; Chinese cannot. The block is question word + 都/也 + 没/不 + verb, all of it in front of 买. Left where it was, 没买什么 says something much weaker — "didn't buy much".
  2. 还 → 都. The intended meaning puts Xiaoli outside the group, so the word after the comma has to be 都. 还 would keep her in and add other people to her — "Xiaoli went, and so did we."
  3. 很干净极了 → 干净极了. 极了 already is the intensifier. Two of them on one adjective is the single commonest 极了 error; delete 很 (or keep 很干净 and drop 极了 — but then the reaction is gone).
  4. 极了喜欢 → 非常喜欢. 极了 has been hung on a verb. 喜欢 is a verb, not an adjective, and 极了 only ever follows an adjective — it can never stand in front of a word. Verbs take 非常/很 ahead of them instead (这个游戏有意思极了 is the 极了 version of the same idea).

Check your own rewrites:

  • Is every 谁/什么/哪儿 sitting before 都/也, and the whole block before the verb?
  • Did you pick 都 vs 还/也 from the meaning ("is X in or out?"), not from the 除了 half?
  • Is there exactly one intensifier per adjective — 很 or 极了, never both?
  • Is the word in front of 极了 an adjective? If it's a verb, the fix is 非常, not a reordering.
  • Did you leave the rest of each sentence alone? One mistake per sentence — resist rewriting the whole thing.

Translate — four sentences. Write them in Chinese. The structure in brackets is compulsory, and every word listed after 用 must appear.

1. Except for my younger brother, everyone in my family is up before seven every day. 2. Besides the rice, she also made two other dishes. 3. I was starving when I got home last night, but there was nothing at all in the fridge. 4. Besides Xiaogang, Xiaoli came to help me clean too, and two hours later the room was spotless.

Why these are right: (1) the brother is outside the group, so the landing word is 都, and 都 stays glued to the verb it quantifies — it comes after the time phrase 七点以前, not before it. (2) 还 keeps the rice in and adds the other dishes; 都 here would flip the sentence into "she cooked everything except the rice". (3) 饿 is an adjective, so the reaction tail 极了 is legal — and 什么都没有 fronts the "nothing" the way English refuses to. Past-tense "there wasn't" takes 没, and 有 is the one verb that is always negated with 没. (4) 也 (not 都) because Xiaogang was already cleaning and Xiaoli joined him; 干净极了 reports the state at the end, so it comes after the time phrase.
