HSK 2 Review · Sheet 04 · 是…的

这个工作是他帮我介绍的。

Zhège gōngzuò shì tā bāng wǒ jièshào de.

It was through his recommendation that I got this job.

What this sheet is

You said about 是…的: "I could use more examples, I think there's just a lot." So this sheet is a gallery first, drills second. The theory already lives in Custom 1, grammar point 2 — read it there if you want the full argument. Here you get one short refresher, then twelve worked examples sorted by what's under the spotlight (when / where / how / who), then practice. The title sentence above is the HSK 2 textbook's lesson-4 headline — by the end of the gallery it should read as ordinary, not as a puzzle.

1. Sixty-second refresher 是 opens the spotlight, 的 closes it

The event already happened and both of you already know it happened — nobody is announcing anything. What's actually being discussed is one detail: when, where, how, or who did it. 是 goes in front of that detail, everything after it runs to the verb, and 的 shuts the sentence. Negation keeps the whole frame and just adds 不: 不是…的. Full theory in Custom 1 §2.

S + 是 + spotlight + V (+ O) + 的

Key contrast — this is the whole thing

了 reports news: something happened, and I am telling you about it. 是…的 zooms in on a known event: we both know it happened, and I am telling you which day / place / method. Same facts, different job.

了 — here is the news
我来纽约了。
"I've come to New York." You didn't know. The arrival is the information.
是…的 — here is the detail
我是去年来的。
"It was last year that I came." You already know I'm here. The year is the point.

你是什么时候来纽约的？
——我是去年来的。

Nǐ shì shénme shíhou lái Niǔyuē de? — Wǒ shì qùnián lái de.

you / SHI / what-time / come / New York / DE? — I / SHI / last-year / come / DE

When did you come to New York? — Last year.

Two rules that never bend

No 了 inside the frame — 我是去年来纽约了的 is wrong, because 是…的 already means the event is over. And the closing 的 is not optional. (是 itself can drop in casual affirmative speech — 我去年来的 — but never in the negative.)

2. The gallery Twelve sentences, sorted by what sits in the spotlight

Read each block out loud and ask yourself: what single English word would answer this? If the answer is "last year", "online", "by plane", "my mum" — you're looking at a 是…的 sentence.

Spotlight on WHEN — 什么时候

我是去年搬到纽约的。

Wǒ shì qùnián bān dào Niǔyuē de.

I / SHI / last-year / move / to / New York / DE

It was last year that I moved to New York.

我是2019年开始学汉语的。

Wǒ shì èr líng yī jiǔ nián kāishǐ xué Hànyǔ de.

I / SHI / 2019-year / begin / learn / Chinese / DE

I started learning Chinese in 2019. (The year is the answer.)

你是什么时候认识小丽的？
——我是在大学的时候认识她的。

Nǐ shì shénme shíhou rènshi Xiǎo Lì de? — Wǒ shì zài dàxué de shíhou rènshi tā de.

you / SHI / what-time / know / Xiao Li / DE? — I / SHI / at / university / DE / time / know / her / DE

When did you meet Xiao Li? — I met her back at university.

Spotlight on WHERE — 在哪儿

这本书是在网上买的。

Zhè běn shū shì zài wǎngshàng mǎi de.

this / CL / book / SHI / at / net-on / buy / DE

I got this book online. (No subject needed — the book is the topic.)

我是在北京学的汉语。

Wǒ shì zài Běijīng xué de Hànyǔ.

I / SHI / at / Beijing / learn / DE / Chinese

It was in Beijing that I studied Chinese. (Note where 的 landed — see the note below.)

你是在哪儿吃的饭？

Nǐ shì zài nǎr chī de fàn?

you / SHI / at / where / eat / DE / meal

Where did you eat? (I know you ate — tell me where.)

Spotlight on HOW — 怎么

我是坐飞机来的。

Wǒ shì zuò fēijī lái de.

I / SHI / sit / plane / come / DE

I came by plane.

我是走回来的。

Wǒ shì zǒu huílai de.

I / SHI / walk / come-back / DE

I walked back. (The "how" can be a single verb — 走.)

我的舞蹈是跟韩国老师学的。

Wǒ de wǔdǎo shì gēn Hánguó lǎoshī xué de.

my / DE / dance / SHI / with / Korea / teacher / learn / DE

I learned my dancing from a Korean teacher.

Spotlight on WHO DID IT — 谁

Here the thing affected is fronted as the topic, and 是 sits right in front of the doer. This is the pattern of the title sentence.

这个工作是他帮我介绍的。

Zhège gōngzuò shì tā bāng wǒ jièshào de.

this / CL / job / SHI / he / help / me / introduce / DE

It was through his recommendation that I got this job.

这个菜是我妈妈做的。

Zhège cài shì wǒ māma zuò de.

this / CL / dish / SHI / my / mum / make / DE

My mum made this dish.

这件衣服是小丽帮我买的。

Zhè jiàn yīfu shì Xiǎo Lì bāng wǒ mǎi de.

this / CL / clothing / SHI / Xiao Li / help / me / buy / DE

Xiao Li picked this out for me.

A note on the object — 学的汉语 or 学汉语的?

When the verb carries an object, the closing 的 has two possible homes and you will hear both. Nothing changes in meaning. The HSK 2 textbook prefers the first one, so train that as your default and simply recognise the second.

Textbook default — V + 的 + O
我是在北京学的汉语。
的 slips in between the verb and its object. Very common with places and names: 我是去年去的北京.
Also correct — V + O + 的
我是在北京学汉语的。
的 stays at the very end, the way it does when there's no object at all (我是坐飞机来的).

And the negative — 不是…的

不 goes in front of 是, and both halves of the frame stay put. Here 是 may not be dropped.

我不是坐飞机来的，是坐火车来的。

Wǒ bú shì zuò fēijī lái de, shì zuò huǒchē lái de.

I / not-SHI / sit / plane / come / DE, / SHI / sit / train / come / DE

I didn't come by plane, I came by train.

3. Recognition — 是…的 or 了? Read the situation, then pick

The first three hang on the same question: does the listener already know the event happened? If yes → 是…的. If it's fresh news → 了. The last three switch job: the meaning is settled, and what you're judging is whether the frame is put together legally.

小丽 already knows you went to Shanghai last week — she just wants to know how you travelled. What does she ask?

  • 你是怎么去的？✓ The trip is old news; only 怎么 is in question, so it sits in the spotlight between 是 and 的.
  • 你怎么去了？✗ 了 announces a fresh event. It can't ask about a detail of something you both already know.
  • 你是怎么去了？✗ 了 and 是…的 can never share a sentence — and the closing 的 is missing.

You're telling 小刚 something he doesn't know yet: you bought a new phone. What do you say?

  • 我买了一个新手机。✓ Fresh news → plain 了. The purchase itself is the information.
  • 我是买的一个新手机。✗ 是…的 spotlights a detail of an event you both already know about — but 小刚 doesn't know yet. There's also no when/where/how here to spotlight.
  • 我买一个新手机。✗ With no 了 this reads as a plan or a habit, not a completed purchase.

You've arrived in Beijing to visit 小刚. He can see you standing there, and asks 你是什么时候到的？ You reply:

  • 我是昨天到的。✓ The arrival is obvious — you're in front of him. 昨天 goes into the spotlight, and 的 closes.
  • 我昨天到了。✗ Grammatical, but it announces the arrival as news. He can see you arrived; he asked which day.
  • 我昨天是到的。✗ 是 must sit immediately in front of the spotlighted detail: 我是昨天到的。

What is the speaker doing with this sentence?
他们是去年冬天结婚的。

  • Breaking the news that they got married.✗ News is 了's job: 他们去年冬天结婚了。The 是…的 frame assumes the wedding is already common knowledge between you.
  • Telling you which winter — you already knew about the wedding.✓ That is the whole function of the frame: the event is settled, so 去年冬天 goes into the spotlight between 是 and 的.
  • Saying they are getting married next winter.✗ Anything still ahead needs a forward-looking frame — 他们下个冬天就要结婚了。是…的 can only look backwards.

Your friend can see 奶奶 sitting in the kitchen and asks how she got here. Which sentence is built correctly?

  • 奶奶是坐公共汽车来的。✓ 是 + method + verb + 的, and nothing else added inside the frame.
  • 奶奶是坐公共汽车来了的。✗ 了 can never live inside the frame. 是…的 already says the trip is finished, so there is no completion left for 了 to mark.
  • 奶奶是坐公共汽车来。✗ The closing 的 is not optional — without it the sentence hangs open. 是 is the half that may drop in casual speech; 的 never is.

A guest assumes you baked the cake on the table. Put him right — it was your older sister.

  • 这个蛋糕不是我做的，是我姐姐做的。✓ 不 attaches to 是, and each half is a complete frame with its own 的.
  • 这个蛋糕不是我做，是我姐姐做的。✗ The negative half lost its 的. In a correction both halves are full 是…的 frames — you can't close one and leave the other open.
  • 这个蛋糕没是我做的，是我姐姐做的。✗ The baking is in the past, so English instinct reaches for 没 — but 是 is never negated with 没. It is 不是 whatever the time reference.

4. Production Now build them yourself

These climb: single-character decisions, then frames you assemble, then whole sentences typed from scratch — with one short paragraph to write early on, as soon as the two 是／的 decisions have warmed you up. Not one of them repeats a sentence from the gallery — if a shape feels familiar but the words are new, that's the point.

Fill the gap: A: 你今天起得真早！ B: 是啊，我是五点起床 ___ 。
(B: "Yep — I got up at five.")

的 — B isn't announcing that he got up (A can see that); he's supplying which hour. 是 opens in front of 五点 and 的 has to close the sentence. 我是五点起床了 ✗ — 了 and the 是…的 frame never share a clause.

Fill the gap: 这些照片不 ___ 我给她的，是我哥哥给她的。
("I wasn't the one who gave her these photos — my older brother was.")

是 — 不 lands on 是, never on the verb (这些照片我不给她的 ✗). And this is exactly where the "是 is droppable" licence expires: casual speech allows 我五点起床的, but a negative sentence must keep both 不 and 是.

Write the paragraph. Somebody asks you when you took up running (or any sport you actually do). Write the exchange out in four or five sentences: the question, when you started, who you started with, where you did it, one first time you remember. Every sentence must be a 是…的 frame — one of them the 什么时候 question, one of them a 不是…的，是…的 correction — and not one of them may contain a 了.

你是什么时候开始跑步的？——我是三年以前开始的，不是去年。我不是一个人开始的，是跟我姐姐一起开始的。那时候我们是在她家旁边的公园跑的。我们是去年第一次参加比赛的，那天下大雨，我跑得很累。

Nǐ shì shénme shíhou kāishǐ pǎobù de? — Wǒ shì sān nián yǐqián kāishǐ de, bú shì qùnián. Wǒ bú shì yí ge rén kāishǐ de, shì gēn wǒ jiějie yìqǐ kāishǐ de. Nà shíhou wǒmen shì zài tā jiā pángbiān de gōngyuán pǎo de. Wǒmen shì qùnián dì-yī cì cānjiā bǐsài de, nà tiān xià dà yǔ, wǒ pǎo de hěn lèi.

When did you take up running? — I started three years ago, not last year. I didn't start on my own; I started with my older sister. Back then we ran in the park next to her place. We entered our first race last year — it poured that day, and I was worn out.

Check your own paragraph, line by line:

  • The question: is 是 sitting in front of 什么时候 with 的 at the very end? 你什么时候开始跑步了？ is a different question — it asks for news, not for a detail of something already known.
  • The answer keeps the whole frame: 我是三年以前开始的 — you cannot answer a 是…的 question with a bare 三年以前开始了.
  • The correction: 不 lands on 是, never on the verb (我不是一个人开始的 ✓ · 我一个人不开始的 ✗), and the second half keeps its own 的.
  • Zero 了 in the whole paragraph — 了 and 是…的 cannot share a clause, because 了 announces the event while 是…的 assumes you already knew it.
  • Where the verb has an object, did 的 go into exactly one of its two homes (跑的步 / 跑步的) — not both, not neither?

Build: "It was my neighbour who lent me this dictionary." (The dictionary is the topic; the spotlight falls on the doer.)

这本词典 是 我邻居 借给我 的

Build the question: "Which shop was this hat bought in?" (You can see it's been bought — you're asking for the shop.)

这个帽子 是 在哪个商店 买 的

Transform. 我上个星期六去了图书馆。 → your friend already knows you went; rewrite it so 上个星期六 is the thing under the spotlight. Keep 我 at the front and 图书馆 in the sentence. Type the whole sentence.

我是上个星期六去的图书馆。 — three moves happen at once: the 了 disappears (the frame already says the trip is over), 是 slots in front of the time word, and 的 closes. Because there's an object here, 的 has two legal homes — 去的图书馆 (the textbook default) or 去图书馆的; both were accepted.

Transform. 小刚发了这个电子邮件。 → everyone in the office has read the email; rewrite it so the sender is under the spotlight. Start with 这个电子邮件. Type the whole sentence.

这个电子邮件是小刚发的。 — to spotlight the doer you first move the affected thing to the front as the topic, then 是 goes in immediately before the doer. The verb keeps no object after it, so 的 simply ends the sentence. Note that 小刚 is now sitting inside the frame, not in the subject slot.

Translate. "It was my aunt who gave me this camera."
用: 这个照相机 · 是 · 阿姨 · 给 · 的

这个照相机是我阿姨给我的。 — Zhège zhàoxiàngjī shì wǒ āyí gěi wǒ de. English marks the spotlight with the heavy "it was … who"; Chinese just fronts the camera and wraps the doer in 是…的. Don't be tempted into 我阿姨给了我这个照相机 — that's the 了 version, which announces the gift instead of identifying who it came from.

Answer the question. 这块手表是在哪儿找到的？ — you found it in the classroom (教室). Answer in a full 是…的 reply; leave the watch itself out, it's already the topic.

是在教室找到的。 (or 我是在教室找到的) — a 是…的 question is answered with a 是…的 sentence: the frame the questioner opened has to come back. Subject and even 是 may be dropped in a reply, but 的 stays put. Answering 我在教室找到了 would report the finding as news — but the finding is exactly what the questioner already knows.

Correct the assumption. 这个包是小丽送你的吗？ — no, it was a colleague (同事) who gave it to you. Start with 不是 and use both halves: 不是…的，是…的.

不是小丽送的，是我同事送的。 — a correction is two complete frames back to back. The 不 sits on 是, not on the verb, and each half closes with its own 的. The 是 in the second half is doing the real work: it points at the replacement answer, 我同事.

Find the error and type the corrected sentence: 我妹妹是上个月到了北京的。
(Intended: "My little sister arrived in Beijing last month.")

我妹妹是上个月到的北京。 — the 是 and the 的 were both in the right place; the 了 is the intruder. It looks harmless because the arrival really is finished, but that is precisely what 是…的 has already said, so the 了 is doing a job nobody asked for. Delete it and the frame is clean. (我妹妹是上个月到北京的 is equally correct — the other home for 的.)

Personalise it. Answer these three about yourself in full 是…的 sentences — write them out, don't just think them:
你是什么时候开始学中文的？ 你的手机是在哪儿买的？ 你是怎么认识你最好的朋友的？

我是四年以前开始学中文的。我的手机是在机场的一个小商店买的。我最好的朋友是在公司认识的——我们以前是同事。

Wǒ shì sì nián yǐqián kāishǐ xué Zhōngwén de. Wǒ de shǒujī shì zài jīchǎng de yí ge xiǎo shāngdiàn mǎi de. Wǒ zuì hǎo de péngyou shì zài gōngsī rènshi de — wǒmen yǐqián shì tóngshì.

I started learning Chinese four years ago. I bought my phone in a little shop at the airport. I met my best friend at work — we used to be colleagues.

Check your own three sentences:

  • Does every sentence still end in 的? That's the half that can never be dropped.
  • Is 是 sitting immediately in front of the spotlighted detail — the time, the place, the method — rather than earlier in the sentence?
  • Is there a stray 了 anywhere? There must not be one.
  • Where your verb has an object, did 的 go in exactly one of its two homes (学的中文 / 学中文的) — not both, not neither?
  • Would each sentence make sense to someone who already knows the event happened? If your listener would be surprised by the event itself, you wanted 了, not this frame.
