1. Extended (metaphorical) directional complements When 起来 / 下去 / 出来 stop meaning physical motion

When a speaker reaches for this: you already know 起来 as "get up", 下去 as "go down", 出来 as "come out". Each also has a large metaphorical life. Recognizing these is what separates beginner reading from intermediate reading.

Prerequisite reminder: literal direction first

Before reading the metaphor, recall the body-motion picture: 站起来 = stand up, 走下去 = go down / go on down, 走出来 = walk out. The extended meanings keep that picture: upward motion becomes "start / appear", a path going on becomes "continue", and coming out becomes "become visible / figure out".

The English trap

English uses entirely different words for the metaphorical senses ("looks like", "keep going", "figure out"). Chinese recycles the same directionals with image-schema extensions. Learning the core image lets you read new combinations:

begin / start → seem, appear → gather, collect 下去 physical: go down → continue / keep going (arrow into the future) 出来 physical: come out → figure out / tell apart (hidden → visible)
Each directional has a "literal" sense and image-schema extensions. The metaphor isn't arbitrary — 起来 = upward emergence → something starts; 出来 = hidden to visible → recognition; 下去 = downward path through time → continuation.
Meaning you need Common Chinese frame Example
Start doing something V + 起来 笑起来 = start laughing
Seem / come across through a sense 看/听 + 起来 听起来不错 = sounds good
Continue an action V + 下去 说下去 = keep talking
Recognize / figure out from evidence 看/听/想 + 出来 看出来 = tell by looking

起来 — "start / seem"

大家都笑起来了。

Dàjiā dōu xiào qǐlai le.

Everyone started laughing. (起来 = the action begins and rises.)

这个菜看起来很好吃。

Zhège cài kàn qǐlai hěn hǎochī.

This dish looks delicious. (看起来 = "when you look at it, it comes across as" — seem.)

下去 — "continue, keep V-ing"

你说下去，我在听。

Nǐ shuō xiàqu, wǒ zài tīng.

Keep talking, I'm listening.

我们一定要学下去。

Wǒmen yídìng yào xué xiàqu.

We must keep on studying.

出来 — "figure out / tell apart"

我听出来了，你是小刚。

Wǒ tīng chūlai le, nǐ shì Xiǎogāng.

I recognized your voice — you're Xiaogang. (听出来 = "from listening, the answer comes out" → identify.)

你看出来他是哪国人了吗？

Nǐ kàn chūlai tā shì nǎ guó rén le ma?

Can you tell what country he's from? (看出来 = figure out visually.)

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences in which these directionals have left the ground completely. Use 起来, 下去 and 出来 at least once each, on three different main verbs: one sentence about how something seems to you (看起来 / 听起来), one about something you mean to keep doing (Verb + 下去), and one about something you worked out about a person (看出来 / 听出来). Every sentence has to be true for you.

上个月我开始跟一位新老师学中文。第一次上课以后大家都听出来了，她不是北京人。她讲得很清楚，我听她的课一点儿也不累。她给我的那本书看起来有点儿难，不过我打算读下去。上星期我们才看出来，同事小丽也在跟她学中文。

Shàng ge yuè wǒ kāishǐ gēn yí wèi xīn lǎoshī xué Zhōngwén. Dì-yī cì shàng kè yǐhòu dàjiā dōu tīng chūlai le, tā bú shì Běijīng rén. Tā jiǎng de hěn qīngchu, wǒ tīng tā de kè yìdiǎnr yě bú lèi. Tā gěi wǒ de nà běn shū kàn qǐlai yǒudiǎnr nán, búguò wǒ dǎsuàn dú xiàqu. Shàng xīngqī wǒmen cái kàn chūlai, tóngshì Xiǎolì yě zài gēn tā xué Zhōngwén.

Last month I started learning Chinese with a new teacher. After the first class everyone could tell she isn't from Beijing. She explains things so clearly that sitting through her class doesn't tire me at all. The book she gave me looks a bit hard, but I plan to keep reading it. Only last week did we work out that my colleague Xiaoli is studying Chinese with her too.

Why these are right: each complement keeps its original picture. 听出来 is hidden→visible: the answer comes out of what you heard. 看起来 is upward emergence: the impression rises off the sense you used, so 那本书看起来有点儿难 is "the book looks hard". 读下去 is a path running on into the future — the reading continues. Note that the complement rides straight on its verb and the rest of the sentence follows the whole verb+complement block (看出来，同事小丽也在…). One trap worth naming: whatever seems is the subject of 看起来/听起来, so it has to be the thing perceived — 她的课看起来有点儿难 ✓, never 我看起来不累 for "listening to her doesn't tire me", which says something about how I look. When the tiredness is yours, say it plainly: 我听她的课一点儿也不累.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the complement welded to its verb, with the object or clause after the pair? 看出来他是谁 ✓ · 看他出来是谁 ✗
  • "Seems" only sits on a perception verb (看起来 · 听起来), and its subject is the thing being perceived: 那本书看起来很难 ✓ · 我看起来那本书很难 ✗
  • Did you use 起来 where you meant "carry on"? Continuation is 下去, always.
  • 了 lands after the whole complement: 听出来了 ✓ · 听了出来 ✗
  • Three different main verbs, and every sentence true about your own month?

2. 使 / 叫 / 让 Three causative verbs — "make / have / let someone do X"

When a speaker reaches for this: to say A caused B to do/feel X. English uses make / let / have / get (to). Chinese has three main verbs, not always interchangeable.

The English trap

English splits by force: make (strong), have (neutral), let (permissive), get to (soft). Chinese splits differently:

  • 让 (ràng) — the workhorse. Covers "let", "have", and mild "make". Most common in speech.
  • 叫 (jiào) — similar to 让, slightly more like "tell / have". Casual.
  • 使 (shǐ) — formal/written. Often pairs with emotions ("makes me happy/sad/nervous"). Not used for physical instructions.

All three share the same frame: A + 让/叫/使 + B + V. The slot structure is identical — the choice of verb is about register and meaning.

A (causer) + 让 / 叫 / 使 + B (causee) + V / Adj

妈妈让我去买东西。

Māma ràng wǒ qù mǎi dōngxi.

mother / have / me / go / buy / things

Mum had me go shopping.

老师叫我们下课以后去办公室。

Lǎoshī jiào wǒmen xià kè yǐhòu qù bàngōngshì.

The teacher told us to come to the office after class.

这件事使我很高兴。

Zhè jiàn shì shǐ wǒ hěn gāoxìng.

This made me very happy. (使 + emotion — typical pairing.)

你的话让我想起了小时候的事。

Nǐ de huà ràng wǒ xiǎngqǐ le xiǎoshíhou de shì.

What you said reminded me of childhood.

让 — everyday
爸爸让我回家
"Dad had me come home." Neutral, spoken.
使 — formal + emotion
这本书使我感动
"This book moved me." Formal; default for abstract causes + feelings.

Don't use 使 for instructions

*妈妈使我买东西 (✗) sounds wrong. 使 doesn't work for concrete commands. Use 让/叫: 妈妈让我买东西 ✓.

Decision guide: 使 / 叫 / 让

  1. Is this everyday speech, permission, or an instruction? Default to 让: 妈妈让我早点儿回家.
  2. Is someone telling someone else to do a concrete action? 叫 is also natural: 老师叫我们去办公室.
  3. Is an abstract thing causing a feeling or result, especially in writing? Use 使: 这个消息使大家很高兴.
  4. If the sentence sounds like a physical command, do not use 使. It is not the normal word for "make someone go buy things".

  • 这件事叫我很难过。✗ 叫 here sounds off; 叫 skews toward instructions.

  • 这个故事使我很感动。✗ Fine — abstract cause + emotion is the textbook slot for 使.

  • The teacher let us ask questions in Chinese.✗ 回答 = answer, not 问 = ask.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about the people who get you to do things and the things that move you. Use 让 or 叫 for at least two everyday instructions — a real person telling you to do something concrete — and reserve one sentence for 使 with an abstract cause and a feeling. Use a different causer in every sentence, and name the person being caused in each one. Every sentence has to be true for you.

每天早上妈妈都叫我先吃饭再出门。公司的经理让我们每个星期开一次会。医生让我少喝咖啡，可是我一直做不到。上个月那个坏消息使我很难过，到现在我还常常想起它。

Měi tiān zǎoshang māma dōu jiào wǒ xiān chī fàn zài chū mén. Gōngsī de jīnglǐ ràng wǒmen měi ge xīngqī kāi yí cì huì. Yīshēng ràng wǒ shǎo hē kāfēi, kěshì wǒ yìzhí zuò bu dào. Shàng ge yuè nàge huài xiāoxi shǐ wǒ hěn nánguò, dào xiànzài wǒ hái chángcháng xiǎngqǐ tā.

Every morning my mum tells me to eat before I go out. The manager at my company has us hold a meeting every week. The doctor told me to drink less coffee, but I still can't manage it. That bad news last month left me very upset — even now I still think about it often.

Why these are right: all three verbs share one frame — causer + 让/叫/使 + causee + what the causee does or feels — and the causee is never dropped, because it is the subject of the second verb. 叫 and 让 carry the concrete instructions (吃饭, 开会, 少喝咖啡); 使 takes the abstract cause 那个坏消息 and an emotion (难过), which is exactly the slot it owns. Swap them and 妈妈使我吃饭 turns into nonsense.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the causee actually there? 经理让我们开会 ✓ · 经理让开会 ✗
  • Is 使 sitting with an abstract cause plus a feeling? A person giving an order takes 让 or 叫.
  • No 了 on the causative verb: 妈妈叫了我吃饭 ✗ — aspect belongs to the second verb or the end of the sentence.
  • Nothing wedged between 让/叫/使 and the causee — no 对, no 给.
  • A different causer in each sentence, and every sentence true about your own week?

3. 不但 … 而且 … "Not only…, but also…"

When a speaker reaches for this: to stack two positive (or negative) qualities about the same thing — "he's not only smart, but also hard-working", "this place is not only cheap, but also tasty". The first clause sets up one quality; the second adds another, usually an even stronger or more surprising one.

The English trap

English maps this almost 1:1: "not only X, but also Y". The pitfall is placement — in Chinese 不但 sits after the subject (if the two clauses share one), not before it. And 而且 sits at the start of the second clause, often paired with 也 or 还 before the second verb.

Subj + 不但 + (V / Adj) 1 , 而且 ( 也 / 还 ) (V / Adj) 2

这家饭馆不但便宜，而且还很好吃。

Zhè jiā fànguǎn búdàn piányi, érqiě hái hěn hǎochī.

this / CL / restaurant / not-only / cheap / but-also / still / very / tasty

This restaurant is not only cheap, but also very tasty.

他不但会说汉语，而且也会写汉字。

Tā búdàn huì shuō Hànyǔ, érqiě yě huì xiě Hànzì.

he / not-only / can / speak / Chinese, / but-also / also / can / write / characters

He not only speaks Chinese, but also writes characters.

贵公司不但很有名，而且工作环境好。

Guì gōngsī búdàn hěn yǒumíng, érqiě gōngzuò huánjìng hǎo.

your(honorific) / company / not-only / very / famous / but-also / work / environment / good

Your company is not only well-known, but also has a good work environment.

When the two clauses have different subjects

If clause 1 and clause 2 are about different subjects, 不但 moves to the very start (before subject 1), and subject 2 appears at the start of clause 2:

不但他喜欢这本书，而且我也喜欢。

Búdàn tā xǐhuan zhè běn shū, érqiě wǒ yě xǐhuan.

Not only does he like this book — I do too.

Same subject: 不但 after subject
他不但聪明，而且努力
He's not only smart, but also hard-working.
Different subjects: 不但 before subject 1
不但他去，而且我也去
Not only is he going, but I'm going too.

Common pitfall

Don't drop 而且. In English you can say "he's smart and also hard-working" — but 不但 alone in Chinese sounds unfinished; it demands a 而且 second clause. If you just want "and also", use 也 or 还 without 不但 on the first clause. Also: Chinese often inserts 也 or 还 before the second verb for rhythm — it's natural, not redundant.

Subject-position walkthrough

  1. Ask whether both halves describe the same subject. Same subject: say it once, then put 不但 after it: 他不但聪明，而且很努力.
  2. If the second half has a different subject, move 不但 before subject 1: 不但他去，而且我也去.
  3. Do not use the different-subject order for a same-subject sentence: 不但贵公司很有名，而且工作环境好 sounds like you are about to compare your company with another subject.
  4. Keep 而且 at the front of the second half; add 也 or 还 when it sounds natural.

Wǒmen lóuxià xīn kāi de nà jiā chāoshì búdàn dōngxi piányi, érqiě lí dìtiězhàn hěn jìn. Wǒ de xīn tóngshì búdàn gōngzuò rènzhēn, érqiě hái hěn huì zhàogù rén. Zhège xīngqī de tiānqì búdàn bù lěng, érqiě měi tiān dōu yǒu tàiyáng. Búdàn wǒ cháng qù zhège gōngyuán pǎobù, érqiě wǒ mèimei yě cháng qù.

The new supermarket downstairs is not only cheap, it's also close to the subway station. My new colleague is not only conscientious at work, she's also very good at looking after people. This week's weather isn't just not cold — there's sun every day as well. Not only do I often go running in this park, my sister does too.

Why these are right: in the first three the two halves are about one subject, said once, so 不但 slides in behind it (那家超市不但东西便宜). In the last one the halves have different subjects (我 and 我妹妹), and only then does 不但 climb in front of the first subject. 而且 always opens the second half, and 也/还 sits before the second predicate for rhythm — natural, not redundant. Note too that both halves point the same way: 不但…而且… adds, it never contrasts.

Check your own sentences:

  • One shared subject ⇒ the marker goes after it: 我的同事不但… ✓ · 不但我的同事…，而且很会照顾人 ✗
  • Two different subjects ⇒ marker first, and the second subject reappears at the head of the second half.
  • Did you finish the frame? 不但 with no 而且 sounds cut off; if you only mean "and also", drop 不但 and use 也/还 alone.
  • Do both halves push in the same direction? If the second one contradicts the first, you want 可是, not 而且.
  • Five different qualities across the set, and every sentence true about your own life?

Write the paragraph. 小刚 invites you and 小丽 to his flat and cooks for the two of you for the first time. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all three structures of this lesson at least once each: an extended directional complement (起来 / 下去 / 出来), a causative (让 / 叫 / 使), and 不但 … 而且 …. Cover: what the table looked like when you walked in, what 小刚 got each of you to do, what you worked out about where he learned to cook, and the verdict you gave him at the end.

上个星期六小刚第一次请我和小丽去他家吃饭。他的房间干干净净的，桌子上的鱼和菜看起来都很不错。小刚叫我们先坐下，又让小丽帮他拿杯子。吃了两口我就看出来了，他一定跟他妈妈学过。最后我们都说他做的鱼不但好看，而且比饭馆的还好吃，希望他能这样做下去。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù Xiǎogāng dì-yī cì qǐng wǒ hé Xiǎolì qù tā jiā chī fàn. Tā de fángjiān gāngān-jìngjìng de, zhuōzi shang de yú hé cài kàn qǐlai dōu hěn búcuò. Xiǎogāng jiào wǒmen xiān zuò xià, yòu ràng Xiǎolì bāng tā ná bēizi. Chī le liǎng kǒu wǒ jiù kàn chūlai le, tā yídìng gēn tā māma xué guo. Zuìhòu wǒmen dōu shuō tā zuò de yú búdàn hǎokàn, érqiě bǐ fànguǎn de hái hǎochī, xīwàng tā néng zhèyàng zuò xiàqu.

Last Saturday Xiaogang had Xiaoli and me over for dinner for the first time. His room was spotless, and the fish and the vegetables on the table both looked great. Xiaogang told us to sit down first, then had Xiaoli help him fetch the glasses. Two mouthfuls in and I could tell he must have learned from his mother. In the end we all said his fish was not only good-looking but tastier than the restaurant's, and we hope he keeps it up.

Why this works as a paragraph: the three structures each carry a different beat of the story. 看起来 gives the first impression from the doorway; 叫 and 让 move the two guests around, each with its own causee (我们 / 小丽); 看出来 is the discovery in the middle — something hidden coming out of the evidence in your mouth; and 不但…而且… delivers the verdict at the end, with 做下去 sending it into the future. Note the causatives never take 了 themselves, and the time markers (上个星期六 · 吃了两口 · 最后) are what make it a story rather than four demonstrations.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Complement welded to its verb, with what follows placed after the pair: 看出来他跟妈妈学过 ✓
  • Every 让/叫/使 has a causee, and no 了 on the causative verb itself.
  • Is 使 reserved for an abstract cause plus a feeling? A person issuing an instruction takes 让 or 叫.
  • 不但 after the subject when both halves are about one thing; 而且 opening the second half, never dropped.
  • Does it read as one evening — time words joining it — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

Four errors. Each sentence below contains exactly one mistake, and all four are mistakes with this lesson's structures. The meaning each sentence is trying to have is listed underneath. Rewrite all four correctly in the box, then check.
1. 我们的钱不多了，不能再花起来了。
2. 这件衣服看下去很旧。
3. 不但这个公园很大，而且很安静。
4. 姐姐让了我去洗碗。 Intended: 1. We haven't much money left — we can't go on spending. 2. This piece of clothing looks old (a judgement, not more looking). 3. This park is not only big, it's also quiet. 4. My older sister had me go and wash the dishes.

1. 我们的钱不多了，不能再花下去了。
2. 这件衣服看起来很旧。
3. 这个公园不但很大，而且很安静。
4. 姐姐让我去洗碗。

1. Wǒmen de qián bù duō le, bù néng zài huā xiàqu le. 2. Zhè jiàn yīfu kàn qǐlai hěn jiù. 3. Zhège gōngyuán búdàn hěn dà, érqiě hěn ānjìng. 4. Jiějie ràng wǒ qù xǐ wǎn.

1. We don't have much money left; we can't go on spending like this. 2. This piece of clothing looks old. 3. This park is not only big, it's also very quiet. 4. My older sister had me go and wash the dishes.

  1. 花起来 → 花下去. 再 … 了 frames an activity already under way that should stop, and continuation is the job of 下去. 起来 would start the spending rather than prolong it, which contradicts 不能再.
  2. 看下去 → 看起来. The sentence is judging the coat, not describing more looking. The "seems / comes across as" reading lives on 起来 after a perception verb; 看下去 would mean "carry on watching", which nothing here is doing.
  3. 不但这个公园 → 这个公园不但. Both halves describe the same subject, so the subject is said once and 不但 follows it. Fronting 不但 promises a second subject in the second half — and none arrives.
  4. 让了 → 让. 让/叫/使 open a pivotal sentence: the causee is simultaneously the object of 让 and the subject of the next verb, and the causative verb never carries 了 itself. Aspect belongs to the second verb or the end of the sentence.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Continuation ⇒ 下去; beginning or "seems" ⇒ 起来; working something out ⇒ 出来. Did you choose by meaning, not by feel?
  • One shared subject ⇒ 不但 after the subject; two subjects ⇒ 不但 before the first one.
  • Is every 而且 still there, opening the second half?
  • No 了 on 让/叫/使, and the causee present in every causative sentence.

Translate — four sentences. Write them in Chinese. The structure in brackets is compulsory, and every word listed after 用 must appear.
1. I could tell straight away that she was tired. [Verb + 出来] · 用: 听出来 · 累
2. The manager had us get to the office half an hour earlier tomorrow. [让 or 叫] · 用: 经理 · 办公室 · 半个小时
3. That whole business made my older sister really angry. [使 + emotion] · 用: 姐姐 · 生气
4. My little sister isn't only learning to swim, she's learning football as well. [不但 … 而且 …] · 用: 妹妹 · 游泳 · 踢足球

1. 我马上就听出来她累了。
2. 经理让我们明天早半个小时到办公室。
3. 那件事使我姐姐很生气。
4. 我妹妹不但在学游泳，而且还在学踢足球。

1. Wǒ mǎshàng jiù tīng chūlai tā lèi le. 2. Jīnglǐ ràng wǒmen míngtiān zǎo bàn ge xiǎoshí dào bàngōngshì. 3. Nà jiàn shì shǐ wǒ jiějie hěn shēngqì. 4. Wǒ mèimei búdàn zài xué yóuyǒng, érqiě hái zài xué tī zúqiú.

1. I could tell straight away that she was tired. 2. The manager had us get to the office half an hour earlier tomorrow. 3. That whole business made my older sister really angry. 4. My little sister isn't only learning to swim, she's learning football as well.

Why these are right: (1) 出来 rides on the perception verb and the whole clause 她累了 follows the pair — the conclusion comes out of what was heard. (2) The causee 我们 sits between 让 and the second verb, and the time phrase 明天早半个小时 stays in front of that verb, where Chinese always puts time. (3) An abstract cause plus a feeling is the natural home of 使; 叫 would sound like the business was giving orders. (4) Both halves are about 我妹妹, so 不但 comes after her, 而且 opens the second half, and 还 before the second 在学 is what makes it sound finished.

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: is the complement glued to 听, with the whole clause after it — not 听她出来累了?
  • Sentence 2: causee immediately after 让, time expression before the verb, and no 了 on 让.
  • Sentence 3: did you resist 让 here? Both are grammatical, but 使 is the textbook pick for abstract cause + emotion.
  • Sentence 4: 不但 after 我妹妹, because both halves are about her.
  • Did every required word after 用 actually make it into your sentence?
