What you'll learn

  1. Question-word correlatives — 你去哪儿我就去哪儿, "wherever you go, I go".
  2. 越 A 越 B — "the more A, the more B".
  3. A 跟 B 一样 (+ Adj) — "A is the same as B (in X)".
  4. Mixed practice — drills combining the patterns.

1. 你去哪儿，我就去哪儿 Question-word correlative: "wherever / whatever / whoever"

When a speaker reaches for this: to link two choices. The answer to the first clause controls the second: wherever you go, I go; whatever you eat, I eat.

The English trap

The question word is not asking a real question here. It works like English wherever / whatever / whoever. The second clause repeats the same question word, often with 就 to show "then that is what happens."

Clause 1 + question word , Clause 2 + 就 + same question word

你去哪儿，我就去哪儿。

Nǐ qù nǎr, wǒ jiù qù nǎr.

Wherever you go, I'll go.

你吃什么，我就吃什么。

Nǐ chī shénme, wǒ jiù chī shénme.

Whatever you eat, I'll eat.

谁想去，谁就去。

Shéi xiǎng qù, shéi jiù qù.

Whoever wants to go can go.

How this differs from Lesson 7

Lesson 7 used question words with 都 / 也 to mean "everyone / anything / anywhere". Here the question word is repeated across two clauses. The pair means "the choice in clause 1 is copied in clause 2."

What does this sentence mean?
你买什么，我就买什么。

  • What are you buying?✗ The repeated 什么 makes it a correlative, not a real question.
  • I'll buy whatever you buy.✓ The second clause copies the choice from the first clause.
  • I bought everything.✗ That would need 什么都, from Lesson 7.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about the choices in your life that somebody else really decides — at home, at work, with friends. Every sentence uses the correlative question word … , … 就 + same question word. Use three different question words (e.g. 什么 · 哪儿 · 谁 · 什么时候) and three different verbs. Every sentence has to be true for you.

在我家，晚上妈妈想吃什么，我们就吃什么。周末朋友想去哪儿，我们就去哪儿，我很少自己决定。在公司，谁有时间，谁就先帮新同事。中文老师什么时候有空，我就什么时候去问问题。

Zài wǒ jiā, wǎnshang māma xiǎng chī shénme, wǒmen jiù chī shénme. Zhōumò péngyou xiǎng qù nǎr, wǒmen jiù qù nǎr, wǒ hěn shǎo zìjǐ juédìng. Zài gōngsī, shéi yǒu shíjiān, shéi jiù xiān bāng xīn tóngshì. Zhōngwén lǎoshī shénme shíhou yǒu kòng, wǒ jiù shénme shíhou qù wèn wèntí.

At home, whatever Mum feels like eating in the evening is what we eat. At the weekend we go wherever my friends want to go — I rarely decide anything myself. At work, whoever has time is the one who helps the new colleague first. Whenever my Chinese teacher is free is when I go and ask my questions.

Why these are right: the same question word appears twice, once in each clause, and 就 stands in the second clause immediately before its verb. Nothing is asked — the first clause sets a value, the second clause copies it. When the question word is the subject (谁有时间，谁就先帮…), the second 谁 is still written out; English drops it ("whoever has time helps"), Chinese does not.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the same question word in both halves? 你去哪儿，我就去哪儿 ✓ · 你去哪儿，我就去那儿 ✗
  • Is 就 in the second clause, right before the verb — not at the front of the sentence?
  • Did you avoid 都/也 here? 你去哪儿，我也去哪儿 ✗ — 都/也 is Lesson 7's "anywhere at all" sweep, a different pattern.
  • Three different question words, three different verbs — 什么 alone in every sentence doesn't build the pattern.
  • Is each sentence a real fact about who decides what in your life?

2. 越 A 越 B "The more A, the more B"

When a speaker reaches for this: to say that one thing scales with another — eating more makes you fatter, practising more makes you faster. It's a correlation in degree.

The English trap

English says "the more… the more…", using the twice. Chinese uses 越 twice instead. The slots are tight: 越 + verb/adj, 越 + verb/adj. No extra connectors.

越 + A (V/Adj) , 越 + B (V/Adj)

我越说越快。

Wǒ yuè shuō yuè kuài.

The more I speak, the faster I go.

雨越下越大。

Yǔ yuè xià yuè dà.

It's raining harder and harder.

你越学越喜欢汉语。

Nǐ yuè xué yuè xǐhuan Hànyǔ.

The more you study, the more you'll love Chinese.

Related pattern: 越来越 + Adj

越来越 is the one-track version: one quality changes more and more over time. It does not need a second 越 clause.

天气越来越热了。

Tiānqì yuè lái yuè rè le.

The weather is getting hotter and hotter.

越来越 vs 越…越…

Don't confuse these. 越来越 means "more and more over time" (one adjective). 越 A 越 B links two different things — the first change drives the second.

Build: "The more he eats, the fatter he gets."

他 越 吃 越 胖

Build: "The more you study Chinese, the more interesting it gets."

汉语 越 学 越 有意思

Fill: 他跑步跑得___快。
(He runs faster and faster.)

越来越 — the fixed 越来越 + Adj frame expresses one-dimension increase over time.

"It's raining harder and harder." — which is natural?

  • 雨越下越大。✓ 越 V 越 Adj — the rain falls "more" and "bigger".
  • 雨越来下越大。✗ Don't mix 越来越 and 越…越… frames.
  • 雨很下很大。✗ 很 doesn't scale with itself; you need the 越…越… frame.

What does this sentence mean?
天气越来越热了。

  • The hotter the weather, the better.✗ That would need 越…越… with two different parts.
  • The weather is getting hotter and hotter.✓ 越来越 + Adj marks change over time in one dimension.
  • The weather used to be hot.✗ 越来越 points forward, not backward.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about things in your own life that keep scaling — a skill, a habit, a workload, a friendship. Use 越 A 越 B at least twice with two different verbs, and 越来越 + Adj at least once for something changing over time. Make one sentence about something that is getting worse. Every sentence has to be true for you.

最近我开始学做中国菜，越做越好吃，朋友也越来越喜欢来我家。晚上跟同事出去，我们常常越玩越晚。这个月工作越来越多，我锻炼的时间越来越少了。

Zuìjìn wǒ kāishǐ xué zuò Zhōngguó cài, yuè zuò yuè hǎochī, péngyou yě yuè lái yuè xǐhuan lái wǒ jiā. Wǎnshang gēn tóngshì chūqù, wǒmen chángcháng yuè wán yuè wǎn. Zhège yuè gōngzuò yuè lái yuè duō, wǒ duànliàn de shíjiān yuè lái yuè shǎo le.

Recently I started learning to cook Chinese food; the more I cook the better it tastes, and my friends are keener and keener to come over. When I go out with colleagues in the evening we tend to stay out later and later. This month there is more and more work, and less and less time to exercise.

Why these are right: 越做越好吃 and 越玩越晚 link two things — the amount of cooking drives the taste, the amount of going out drives the hour. 越来越多 / 越来越少 track a single quality along time, so no second clause is needed. Both frames attach straight to the verb or adjective: nothing (no 很, no 得) may be squeezed in after 越.

Check your own sentences:

  • 越 A 越 B: is there something on both sides, and are the two parts different? 越走越快 ✓ · 越走越走 ✗
  • Did the two frames stay apart? 越来越忙越累 ✗ — pick one.
  • No 很 after 越来越: 越来越贵 ✓ · 越来越很贵 ✗
  • With a 得-complement the 越来越 goes after 得: 他跑得越来越快 ✓ · 他越来越跑得快 ✗
  • Two different verbs, and at least one honest "it's getting worse" sentence?

3. A 跟 B 一样 (+ Adj) "A is the same as B"

When a speaker reaches for this: to say two things are equivalent on some measure, or simply the same.

The English trap

English: "A is as tall as B" — two ases. Chinese uses the sandwich A 跟 B 一样 (高) — B sits inside the comparison frame. Word order matters.

A + 跟 + B + 一样 + (Adj)

我跟他一样高。

Wǒ gēn tā yíyàng gāo.

I'm as tall as him.

这本书跟那本书一样。

Zhè běn shū gēn nà běn shū yíyàng.

This book is the same as that one.

Negative: A 跟 B 不一样 ("A is not the same as B"). Notice 不 goes before 一样, not before the adjective.

中国菜跟美国菜不一样。

Zhōngguócài gēn Měiguócài bù yíyàng.

Chinese food is not the same as American food.

Practice

Build: "His car is the same as my car."

他的车 跟 我的车 一样

"A is as big as B" — pick the right template:

  • A 跟 B 一样大✓ Adj comes last: A 跟 B 一样 + Adj.
  • A 大跟 B 一样✗ Adj must be final, not mid-frame.
  • A 一样跟 B 大✗ 跟 precedes B, then 一样.

Build: "My younger sister is as tall as me."

我妹妹 跟 我 一样 高

(Chinese food is not the same as American food.)

不一样 — 不 attaches to 一样, not to a following adjective.

What does this sentence mean?
这本书跟那本书一样有意思。

  • This book is more interesting than that one.✗ That would use 比, not 跟…一样.
  • This book is as interesting as that one.✓ A 跟 B 一样 + Adj — equal on that measure.
  • This book and that book are both boring.✗ 一样 means "the same", and 有意思 is positive.

Which sentence correctly negates the comparison?

  • 我的手机跟他的不一样。✓ 不 goes before 一样.
  • 我的手机不跟他的一样。✗ 不 does not attach to 跟.
  • 我的手机跟他的一样不。✗ 不 never sits sentence-final like this.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences comparing pairs of things in your own life — two people you know, two things you own, two places you spend time in. Use A 跟 B 一样 + Adj at least twice with two different adjectives, and use the negative A 跟 B 不一样 at least once. Compare three different kinds of thing (a person, an object, a place). Every sentence has to be true for you.

我姐姐跟我一样爱看书，可是我们爱看的书不一样。她的手机跟我的一样老，我们都不想买新的。我住的地方跟她住的地方一样安静，晚上几乎没有人说话。我做的菜跟妈妈做的不一样，我还要多练习。

Wǒ jiějie gēn wǒ yíyàng ài kàn shū, kěshì wǒmen ài kàn de shū bù yíyàng. Tā de shǒujī gēn wǒ de yíyàng lǎo, wǒmen dōu bù xiǎng mǎi xīn de. Wǒ zhù de dìfang gēn tā zhù de dìfang yíyàng ānjìng, wǎnshang jīhū méiyǒu rén shuōhuà. Wǒ zuò de cài gēn māma zuò de bù yíyàng, wǒ hái yào duō liànxí.

My older sister loves reading as much as I do, but the books we love are not the same. Her phone is as old as mine and neither of us wants to buy a new one. The place I live is as quiet as the place she lives — in the evening there is almost nobody talking. My cooking is not the same as my mum's — I still need more practice.

Why these are right: B always sits inside the frame — A 跟 B 一样 — and the adjective (or the verb phrase 爱看书) comes last, after 一样. The negative is 不 in front of 一样 — 跟…不一样 — and there the sentence can simply stop, as it does in 我做的菜跟妈妈做的不一样. Note 她的手机跟我的: once the noun is obvious you keep just 的, exactly as English keeps "mine".

Check your own sentences:

  • Order: A + 跟 + B + 一样 + Adj. 这个跟那个一样贵 ✓ · 这个跟那个贵一样 ✗
  • Negation on 一样: 跟…不一样 ✓ · 不跟…一样 ✗
  • No 很 in the frame: 跟他一样很高 ✗ — 一样 already carries the degree.
  • Are A and B really comparable things (two phones, two people), not a thing and a quality?
  • Two different adjectives, three different kinds of thing, and every comparison actually true?

"The older he gets, the healthier he is." — pick the natural Chinese:

Write the paragraph. A Saturday when you and 小丽 went looking for a quiet place to study. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all three structures of this lesson at least once each: question word … 就 … same question word, 越 A 越 B (or 越来越), and A 跟 B 一样 (+ Adj). Cover: who chose the place and how you said it was up to her, how it compared with the place you went last time, what changed as the afternoon went on, and what you agreed for next time.

上个星期六我跟小丽出去找一个安静的地方学习。我对她说：“你想去哪个地方，我们就去哪个地方。”她选了公司附近的图书馆，那儿跟我们上次去的地方一样安静，可是人越来越多。三点以后我越坐越累，中文也越看越不明白。最后我们决定回家，下次谁先到，谁就先进去等。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ gēn Xiǎolì chūqù zhǎo yí ge ānjìng de dìfang xuéxí. Wǒ duì tā shuō: "Nǐ xiǎng qù nǎge dìfang, wǒmen jiù qù nǎge dìfang." Tā xuǎn le gōngsī fùjìn de túshūguǎn, nàr gēn wǒmen shàng cì qù de dìfang yíyàng ānjìng, kěshì rén yuè lái yuè duō. Sān diǎn yǐhòu wǒ yuè zuò yuè lèi, Zhōngwén yě yuè kàn yuè bù míngbai. Zuìhòu wǒmen juédìng huí jiā, xià cì shéi xiān dào, shéi jiù xiān jìnqù děng.

Last Saturday Xiaoli and I went out looking for a quiet place to study. I said to her, "We'll go to whichever place you want to go to." She picked the library near our office; it was as quiet as the place we went last time, but there were more and more people. After three o'clock the longer I sat there the more tired I got, and the more I read the less Chinese I understood. In the end we decided to go home — next time, whoever arrives first goes in and waits.

Why this works as a paragraph: each structure has its own job. The correlative hands the decision over (你想去哪个地方，我们就去哪个地方) and closes the story with a rule for next time (谁先到，谁就先进去等). 跟…一样安静 measures the new place against the old one. And the 越 frames carry the change: 越来越多 for the crowd along the afternoon, 越坐越累 / 越看越不明白 for two things scaling together. Time words (三点以后 · 最后) are what hold it together.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Correlative: same question word twice, 就 in the second clause before the verb.
  • 越来越 for one quality along time; 越 A 越 B when one thing drives another — did you keep them apart?
  • Comparison order: A 跟 B 一样 + Adj, with B inside the frame and the adjective last.
  • Nothing extra after 越 or 一样: no 很, no 得 — 越来越很多 ✗ · 跟…一样很安静 ✗
  • Does it read as one afternoon, joined by time words and 可是, rather than three grammar demos in a row?

1. 老师说几点开始，我们也几点开始。
2. 我最近越忙越来越累。
3. 今天的天气跟昨天热一样。
4. 他现在越来越很健康。

1. 老师说几点开始，我们就几点开始。
2. 我最近越忙越累。
3. 今天的天气跟昨天一样热。
4. 他现在越来越健康了。

1. Lǎoshī shuō jǐ diǎn kāishǐ, wǒmen jiù jǐ diǎn kāishǐ. 2. Wǒ zuìjìn yuè máng yuè lèi. 3. Jīntiān de tiānqì gēn zuótiān yíyàng rè. 4. Tā xiànzài yuè lái yuè jiànkāng le.

1. We start at whatever time the teacher says. 2. Lately, the busier I am the more tired I get. 3. Today's weather is as hot as yesterday's. 4. He is getting healthier and healthier now.

  1. 也几点 → 就几点. The correlative is held together by 就 in the second clause. 也 belongs to Lesson 7's sweep (什么都/也), where one question word covers all values; here the second clause takes the value fixed by the first.
  2. 越忙越来越累 → 越忙越累. Two frames spliced together. If the sentence already has 越 + A driving 越 + B, the second half is a plain 越 — 越来越 is only for a single quality moving along time, with no driver clause.
  3. 跟昨天热一样 → 跟昨天一样热. The adjective is the last element: A 跟 B 一样 + Adj. English "as hot as yesterday" tempts you to put 热 before the second half; Chinese finishes the frame first.
  4. 越来越很健康 → 越来越健康了. 越来越 already supplies the degree, so 很 has no work to do and is ungrammatical here. The sentence-final 了 is what marks the change of state.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Correlative sentences: 就, not 也 — and the same question word in both halves.
  • One frame per sentence: 越 A 越 B or 越来越 + Adj, never both.
  • Comparisons end with the adjective, after 一样.
  • No 很 after 越来越 or after 一样; use 了 for a change of state instead.

1. 你想看什么电影，我们就看什么电影。
2. 他越等越着急。
3. 这个电梯跟那个一样老。
4. 公共汽车越来越慢了，下次我坐地铁去。

1. Nǐ xiǎng kàn shénme diànyǐng, wǒmen jiù kàn shénme diànyǐng. 2. Tā yuè děng yuè zháojí. 3. Zhège diàntī gēn nàge yíyàng lǎo. 4. Gōnggòng qìchē yuè lái yuè màn le, xià cì wǒ zuò dìtiě qù.

1. Whatever film you want to see is the film we'll see. 2. The longer he waits, the more anxious he gets. 3. This lift is just as old as that one. 4. The bus is getting slower and slower — next time I'll take the subway.

Why these are right: (1) 什么电影 is repeated whole in the second clause, with 就 in front of its verb — Chinese has no word for "the one". (2) 等 and 着急 are two different things scaling together, so the frame is 越…越…, and nothing sits between 越 and the word it measures. (3) B is 那个 alone: once 电梯 has been said, repeating it is unnatural, and 老 closes the frame. (4) One quality along time ⇒ 越来越 + Adj, and the change gets a final 了.

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: did you repeat 什么电影 rather than replacing it with 那个?
  • Sentence 2: 越等越着急 — no 很, no 得, nothing wedged after either 越.
  • Sentence 3: 一样 before 老, and 跟 before 那个 — the adjective must be last.
  • Sentence 4: 越来越慢了, not 越来越很慢.
