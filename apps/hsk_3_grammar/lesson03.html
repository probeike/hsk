What you'll learn

  1. 还是 vs 或者 — two words for "or", used in different kinds of sentences.
  2. Existence: Place + V着 + Noun — how Chinese says "there's X at Y".
  3. 会 for possibility — "is likely to happen".

1. 还是 vs 或者 Two "or"s — one for questions, one for statements

When a speaker reaches for this: to offer or list alternatives. The trick: Chinese uses two different words for or, and which one you pick depends on whether the sentence is a real question.

The English trap

English has one word — or. Chinese learners often reach for 或者 everywhere because it "feels" like or. But if the sentence is a question asking which, you need 还是. 或者 belongs in statements (including statements inside a yes/no question).

还是 — in questions
你喝茶还是咖啡？
Tea or coffee? (asks the listener to choose)
或者 — in statements
茶或者咖啡都可以。
Tea or coffee — either is fine. (no choice being asked)

明天是晴天还是阴天？

Míngtiān shì qíngtiān háishi yīntiān?

tomorrow / be / sunny / or / cloudy?

Will tomorrow be sunny or cloudy?

天冷了或者工作累了的时候，喝杯热茶很舒服。

Tiān lěng le huòzhě gōngzuò lèi le de shíhou, hē bēi rè chá hěn shūfu.

weather / cold-LE / or / work / tired-LE / TIMES, / drink / cup / hot / tea / very comfy

When it's cold or when you're tired from work, a cup of hot tea feels great.

Edge case: embedded questions

If a statement contains a mini-question, that embedded bit still needs 还是:

他的生日是10月还是11月，我不记得了。

Tā de shēngrì shì shí yuè háishi shíyī yuè, wǒ bù jìde le.

I can't remember if his birthday is October or November.

  • 还是✓ It's a question — 还是.
  • 或者✗ Questions asking which need 还是.

Fill in: 我们出去吧，买东西 ___ 看电影。 (This is a suggestion — let's do one or the other.)

  • 还是✗ Not a question asking which, just listing options within a statement.

Fill the blank: 周太太40岁___50岁，我们不知道。
(We don't know whether Mrs. Zhou is 40 or 50.)

Build: "Do you want to drink tea or coffee?"

你 想 喝 茶 还是 咖啡

What does this sentence mean?
周末我看书或者听音乐。

  • "Do I read or listen to music on the weekend?"✗ No question here — 或者 signals a statement, not a choice being asked.
  • "On the weekend I read or listen to music."✓ 或者 lists alternatives inside a plain statement.
  • "On the weekend I read and listen to music at the same time."✗ 或者 means "or" (either/either), not "and" — that would be 一边...一边 or 和.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about real choices in your own week. Use 还是 at least twice — once in a question you would genuinely ask someone, and once inside a statement that still hides a question (我不记得 … 还是 … / 我不知道 … 还是 …) — and 或者 at least twice in flat statements that offer options, closing one of them with 都可以. Use three different verbs.

这个周末我想出去走走，你想去公园还是去看电影？我不太忙的时候，早上跑步或者游泳都可以。晚上我不想做饭，我们出去吃或者叫朋友带点儿东西来。她说她今天要来，可是我不知道她坐地铁还是走路来。

Zhège zhōumò wǒ xiǎng chūqu zǒuzou, nǐ xiǎng qù gōngyuán háishi qù kàn diànyǐng? Wǒ bú tài máng de shíhou, zǎoshang pǎobù huòzhě yóuyǒng dōu kěyǐ. Wǎnshang wǒ bù xiǎng zuò fàn, wǒmen chūqu chī huòzhě jiào péngyou dài diǎnr dōngxi lái. Tā shuō tā jīntiān yào lái, kěshì wǒ bù zhīdào tā zuò dìtiě háishi zǒulù lái.

I want to get out this weekend — do you want to go to the park or go to a film? When I'm not too busy, running or swimming in the morning both work. I don't want to cook tonight; we can eat out or ask a friend to bring something over. She said she's coming today, but I don't know whether she's taking the subway or walking.

Why these are right: the test is not "does the sentence have a question mark" but is somebody being asked to pick one? 你想去公园还是去看电影 hands the choice to the listener, and 我不知道她坐地铁还是走路来 hands it to nobody but still contains the same unanswered choice — both take 还是. The two 或者 sentences only list; nothing is being chosen, which is why 都可以 can close one of them. Note also that when the two options take different verbs, the verb is repeated: 去公园还是去看电影.

Check your own sentences:

  • For every "or": is somebody being asked to pick? Yes → 还是; no → 或者.
  • An embedded question after 不记得 / 不知道 / 问 still takes 还是 even though the whole sentence is a statement.
  • Could you close the sentence with 都可以? Then it is a list, and 还是 is wrong there.
  • Different verbs in the two options → repeat the verb after 还是.
  • Are these choices you actually face this week, not invented ones?

2. Place + V着 + NP Existence: what's located somewhere

When a speaker reaches for this: to describe a scene — what's on the table, who lives upstairs, what's written on a sign. It's depictive — the listener should picture the setting.

First-time 着 reminder

着 has more than one job in Chinese. This lesson is not the "doing something continuously" use. Here 着 turns the verb into a scene-state: 放着 means "is sitting/placed there", and 写着 means "is written there". Nobody has to be actively placing or writing right now.

The English trap

English uses existential there is/are: "There are drinks on the table." Chinese flips it: the place comes first, then the verb with 着 (indicating a stative situation), then the thing. Literally: "Table-on has-stationed many drinks." The place is the topic; the item is the new info being introduced.

着 freezes the action into a scene. 放着 isn't "placing" in progress — it's "(something is) placed, and stays that way." Imagine a still photograph.
Place + V + 着 + Indefinite NP

桌子上放着很多饮料。

Zhuōzi shang fàngzhe hěn duō yǐnliào.

table-on / placed-着 / many drinks

There are lots of drinks on the table.

我家楼上住着一个老师。

Wǒ jiā lóushàng zhùzhe yí ge lǎoshī.

my-home-upstairs / lives-着 / one / CL / teacher

A teacher lives upstairs from us.

上面写着320元。

Shàngmiàn xiězhe sān bǎi èrshí yuán.

on-top / written-着 / 320-yuan

It says ¥320 on the label.

Common pitfall — NP must be indefinite

This structure introduces new information. You can say 桌子上放着一杯咖啡 (a cup of coffee), but not *桌子上放着这杯咖啡 (this cup). "This" / "Manager Zhou" / proper names — none of them work here, because they're already known, not new.

Build: "There is a cup of coffee on the table."

桌子上 放 着 一杯 咖啡

Build: "A teacher lives upstairs from us."

我家 楼上 住 着 一个 老师

Which is grammatical?

  • 公共汽车上坐着一个老人。✓ Place + V着 + indefinite NP ("one old person").
  • 公共汽车上坐着那个老人。✗ 那个老人 = "that old person" is definite. Existence sentences need new/indefinite NPs.

Fill in: 这上面 ___ 320元。
(This sign says ¥320.)

写着 — "written-着", i.e. the text is sitting there on the sign.

Fill the blank: 我的房间里___一张桌子。
(There is a table in my room.)

放着 — Place + V着 + indefinite NP. The table is pictured as sitting there.

What does this sentence mean?
公共汽车上坐着一个老人。

  • "The old person is sitting down on the bus (right now)."✗ 着 here is stative — a held scene, not an action in progress.
  • "There's an old person seated on the bus."✓ Place + V着 + indefinite NP = existence/scene description.
  • "The old person sat down on the bus (completed)."✗ That would use 了, not 着. 着 = ongoing state, not completion.

Personalise it. Describe a place you actually pass through every day and that is not your own desk — a station, a lift, a doorway, the studio or gym you use. Write 3–5 true sentences using Place + V着 + NP at least three times with three different verbs (住 · 写 · 坐 · 站 · 放), and keep every thing you introduce indefinite — 一个 / 一些 / 很多, never 这个 / 那个 or a name.

我家楼下的门口站着一个人，他每天都在那儿等公共汽车。电梯里写着“请别在这儿吃东西”。楼下的商店前面放着很多花，红的、黄的、绿的都有。地铁上坐着很多人，大家都在看手机。

Wǒ jiā lóuxià de ménkǒu zhànzhe yí ge rén, tā měi tiān dōu zài nàr děng gōnggòng qìchē. Diàntī li xiězhe "qǐng bié zài zhèr chī dōngxi". Lóuxià de shāngdiàn qiánmiàn fàngzhe hěn duō huā, hóng de, huáng de, lǜ de dōu yǒu. Dìtiě shang zuòzhe hěn duō rén, dàjiā dōu zài kàn shǒujī.

There's a man standing in the doorway downstairs from us; he waits there for the bus every day. Inside the lift it says "please don't eat in here". In front of the shop downstairs there are a lot of flowers — red, yellow and green ones. There are plenty of people sitting on the subway, all of them looking at their phones.

Why these are right: the place is the topic and comes first, 着 freezes the verb into a still photograph, and the thing arrives last as new information — which is exactly why it must be indefinite. 站着 · 写着 · 放着 · 坐着 are four different postures inside that photograph: nobody is standing up, writing, putting something down or sitting down while you watch. Note 写着 needs no writer at all — the words are simply there.

Check your own sentences:

  • Place first, then V着, then the thing. English "there is X at Y" runs the other way round.
  • Is the introduced noun indefinite? 坐着很多人 ✓ · 坐着那些人 ✗
  • No 在 and no 了 on the verb: 放着 ✓ · 在放 ✗ · 放了 reports the act of putting, not the scene.
  • Three different verbs — the pattern is about the place, not about 放.
  • Can you actually see or clearly remember the place you described?

3. 会 — possibility "Likely to happen / will (probably)"

When a speaker reaches for this: to predict or warn about an outcome. "You'll catch a cold." "He's going to be upset." It's about future likelihood, not past fact.

The English trap

You already know 会 as "know how to" (我会说中文 — I can speak Chinese). This is a different use: not skill, but prediction. Context tells you which. If 会 sits in front of a verb describing a future event (感冒, 生气, 下雨), it's the "likely to" sense.

Two 会s: learned ability vs possibility

learned ability
我会开车
I know how to drive. This is a learned skill or trained ability.
possibility / prediction
明天会下雨
It will probably rain tomorrow. This is about an outcome being likely.

你穿得那么少，会感冒的。

Nǐ chuān de nàme shǎo, huì gǎnmào de.

you / wear / so little, / will / catch-cold / DE

Dressed that lightly, you're going to catch a cold.

你不给他打电话吗？他会不高兴的。

Nǐ bù gěi tā dǎ diànhuà ma? Tā huì bù gāoxìng de.

you / not / call him? / he / will / be-unhappy / DE

Aren't you going to call him? He'll be upset.

天气这么热，会下雨的。

Tiānqì zhème rè, huì xià yǔ de.

weather / so / hot, / will / fall-rain / DE

It's so hot — it's going to rain (for sure).

The sentence-final 的 often pairs with prediction 会 to reinforce "for sure".

Practice

Finish: 别吃那么多，再吃就 ___ 。
(Don't eat so much — if you keep eating, you'll get sick.)

会生病的 — "will get sick, for sure".

Which sentence uses 会 to mean likely / predicted, not know how to?

  • 我会说中文。✗ This is "know how to" — skill/ability.
  • 医生说不运动会生病。✓ Prediction: not exercising leads to (is likely to cause) illness.
  • 他会开车。✗ "He can drive" — ability.

Build: "It's going to rain tomorrow (for sure)."

明天 会 下雨 的

Fill the blank: 快起床吧，再不起就___。
(Get up, if you don't you'll be late.)

会迟到的 — prediction 会 + 的 ("for sure"). The 的 is optional but common with warnings.

What does this sentence convey?
你不吃早饭，会饿的。

  • "You know how to be hungry without breakfast."✗ That's the skill sense. 饿 is a state, not a skill — the prediction reading is the only one available.
  • "If you don't eat breakfast, you'll get hungry (for sure)."✓ 会...的 = prediction / warning about a likely outcome.
  • "You didn't eat breakfast and got hungry."✗ Past completion would use 了, not 会...的.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true warnings or predictions you would genuinely say to somebody you know this week. Every sentence uses 会 + V (+ 的), with a reason clause in front in at least two of them and three different predicted outcomes. Make one sentence 不会 … 的 ("it won't happen") — and notice that this is not the same as 会不 … 的 ("it will be un-…").

你现在就走吧，再等下去会迟到的。这两天天气这么冷，周末不会有很多人去公园的。小丽这个星期工作太累了，晚上她不会想出去跳舞的。你别喝那么多咖啡，今天晚上会睡得很晚的。

Nǐ xiànzài jiù zǒu ba, zài děng xiàqu huì chídào de. Zhè liǎng tiān tiānqì zhème lěng, zhōumò bú huì yǒu hěn duō rén qù gōngyuán de. Xiǎolì zhège xīngqī gōngzuò tài lèi le, wǎnshang tā bú huì xiǎng chūqu tiàowǔ de. Nǐ bié hē nàme duō kāfēi, jīntiān wǎnshang huì shuì de hěn wǎn de.

Leave now — if you keep waiting you'll be late. It's this cold at the moment, so there won't be many people at the park this weekend. Xiaoli has had far too heavy a week at work; she won't want to go out dancing tonight. Don't drink that much coffee — you'll end up going to sleep really late.

Why these are right: prediction 会 sits directly in front of the verb and points forward, and the reason clause is what forces that reading — 你会迟到 could never be a learned skill, and the 再等下去 in front makes it unmistakable. Negating a prediction is 不会 … 的 (the event won't happen); predicting a negative state is 会不 … 的 (会不高兴的 = "he'll be unhappy"). The final 的 is optional, but it is what turns a guess into a confident claim.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is there a reason or condition in front? Without one, a bare 会 sentence often reads as "know how to".
  • 不会…的 = won't happen · 会不…的 = will be un-…. Which did you mean?
  • Is the 的 right at the very end, after the whole verb phrase? 会迟到的 ✓ · 会的迟到 ✗
  • No 了 on a prediction: 会迟到了 ✗ — 了 reports what already happened.
  • Would you actually say each of these to that person this week?

Which is grammatical?

  • 桌子上放着一本书。✓ Place (桌子上) + V着 (放着) + indefinite NP (一本书) — textbook existence structure.
  • 桌子上放着这本书。✗ 这本书 = "this book" is definite. The V着 existence pattern requires indefinite NPs.
  • 一本书桌子上放着。✗ Wrong word order — the place must come first in existence sentences.

你 穿得 那么 少 会 感冒 的

Write the paragraph. The first time you walked into somewhere new — a shop, a café, a classroom, a friend's building. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all three of this lesson's structures at least once each: 还是 / 或者, Place + V着 + NP, and 会 … 的. Cover: what was written or placed where when you walked in, who was already there, a question somebody asked you, what you said you did not mind either way, and what you predict about going back.

上个星期六我第一次去那家新开的书店。门上写着“九点开门”，我到得有点儿早。里面放着几张大桌子，旁边坐着两三个人在看书。服务员问我：“您在这儿喝还是带走？”我说在这儿喝，咖啡或者热茶都可以。那儿又安静又舒服，我想下次小丽也会喜欢的。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ dì-yī cì qù nà jiā xīn kāi de shūdiàn. Mén shang xiězhe "jiǔ diǎn kāimén", wǒ dào de yǒudiǎnr zǎo. Lǐmiàn fàngzhe jǐ zhāng dà zhuōzi, pángbiān zuòzhe liǎng-sān ge rén zài kàn shū. Fúwùyuán wèn wǒ: "Nín zài zhèr hē háishi dài zǒu?" Wǒ shuō zài zhèr hē, kāfēi huòzhě rè chá dōu kěyǐ. Nàr yòu ānjìng yòu shūfu, wǒ xiǎng xià cì Xiǎolì yě huì xǐhuan de.

Last Saturday I went to that newly opened bookshop for the first time. "Opens at nine" was written on the door, and I'd got there a bit early. Inside there were several big tables, with two or three people sitting beside them reading. The assistant asked me, "Drinking it here, or taking it away?" I said here — coffee or hot tea, either was fine. It's quiet and comfortable there, and I think Xiaoli will like it next time too.

Why this works as a paragraph: 写着 · 放着 · 坐着 build one still photograph of the room, each with the place first and a brand-new, indefinite thing last. The assistant's 还是 is a genuine choice put to me, so it must be 还是; my own reply only lists what I don't mind, so it takes 或者 and ends 都可以 — swapping those two is the single commonest slip. The closing 会喜欢的 predicts a visit that hasn't happened yet, and 上个星期六 · 里面 · 那儿 · 下次 are what hold the sentences together as one visit.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Every existence sentence: place first, V着 next, and an indefinite thing last.
  • 还是 only where somebody must choose; a list nobody is choosing from takes 或者, often closed by 都可以.
  • Is your 会 sentence pointing forward, with 的 at the very end and no 了?
  • Does 着 read as a photograph? If you meant "was sitting down" as an action, this isn't the structure.
  • Does it read as one visit, joined by time and place words, rather than three grammar demos in a row?

Four errors. Each sentence below contains exactly one mistake, and all four are mistakes with this lesson's structures. Rewrite all four correctly in the box, then check.
1. 你想去看电影或者在家休息？
2. 一些杯子放着椅子上。
3. 楼下坐着那个学生。
4. 我想小刚今天会不来的。

1. 你想去看电影还是在家休息？
2. 椅子上放着一些杯子。
3. 楼下坐着一个学生。
4. 我想小刚今天不会来的。

1. Nǐ xiǎng qù kàn diànyǐng háishi zài jiā xiūxi? 2. Yǐzi shang fàngzhe yìxiē bēizi. 3. Lóuxià zuòzhe yí ge xuésheng. 4. Wǒ xiǎng Xiǎogāng jīntiān bú huì lái de.

1. Do you want to go to a film or stay in and rest? 2. There are some cups on the chair. 3. There's a student sitting downstairs. 4. I don't think Xiaogang is coming today.

  1. 或者 → 还是. The listener is being handed a choice and is expected to answer, so this is a real question. 或者 can only list options inside a statement; it can never carry the "which one?" of a question.
  2. 一些杯子放着椅子上 → 椅子上放着一些杯子. The place is the topic and has to open the sentence; the thing is the new information and arrives last. An indefinite 一些杯子 cannot lead a sentence at all, so moving the place to the front is the only repair available here.
  3. 那个学生 → 一个学生. Place + V着 + NP introduces something new, so the NP must be indefinite. 那个学生 is already identified — for a known person you would say 那个学生坐在楼下 instead.
  4. 会不来的 → 不会来的. 不 goes in front of 会 when you are denying that the event will happen. 会不来的 negates the verb instead and reads as "he will deliberately not come" — a different, much stranger claim.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Is anybody being asked to choose? That, not the punctuation, decides 还是 vs 或者.
  • Does every existence sentence start with the place?
  • Is the introduced noun indefinite — 一个 / 一些 / 很多, never 这 / 那?
  • Denying a prediction: 不 in front of 会, not after it.

1. 你的衬衫是绿的还是白的？
2. 星期六或者星期天，我都可以。
3. 床上放着一件绿衬衫。
4. 他已经等了一个小时了，会生气的。

1. Nǐ de chènshān shì lǜ de háishi bái de? 2. Xīngqīliù huòzhě xīngqītiān, wǒ dōu kěyǐ. 3. Chuáng shang fàngzhe yí jiàn lǜ chènshān. 4. Tā yǐjīng děng le yí ge xiǎoshí le, huì shēngqì de.

1. Is your shirt the green one or the white one? 2. Saturday or Sunday — either works for me. 3. There's a green shirt lying on the bed. 4. He's already been waiting an hour — he's going to get annoyed.

Why these are right: (1) the listener must pick one colour, so it is 还是, and 是…的 frames both options the same way. (2) Nothing is being chosen — the sentence hands over two acceptable days — so 或者, and 都可以 confirms it. (3) The place opens the sentence and 一件绿衬衫 is new information, so it is indefinite; a definite 那件衬衫 would need 那件衬衫放在床上 instead. (4) The evidence comes first and 会…的 predicts the state that follows; no 了 on the prediction, even though the waiting itself takes one.

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: 还是, because an answer is expected. Did you keep both options in the same shape (绿的 / 白的)?
  • Sentence 2: 或者 plus 都可以. If you wrote 还是 here, you accidentally asked a question.
  • Sentence 3: place first, and the shirt indefinite — 一件, not 那件.
  • Sentence 4: is the 的 at the end and the 了 kept off the prediction?
  • Did every word listed after 用 actually make it into your sentence?
