## Grammar introduced

### 1. V 得/不 + complement — Can you succeed at that verb?

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to say whether an action *can successfully produce its result*. Not "do I know how to do it" (会) or "do I have the right to do it" (能) — but "if I try, will it work out?" English: *can I make it out?*, *can you see clearly?*, *can you hear?*

### The English trap

English uses *can* for many senses. Chinese carves them out: 会 (know how), 能 (allowed / able in circumstance), and this structure (will the attempt succeed?). The marker 得 (positive) or 不 (negative) sits **inside** the verb+complement: 看得懂 "can read/understand it" / 看不懂 "can't read/understand it".

### 2. N + 呢? — A two-character shortcut: "where's X?"

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to ask where something or someone is, without the full sentence. English equivalents: *where's Mom?*, *and the dog?*. It's casual and efficient.
 

### The English trap

You might want to say the full 妈妈在哪儿？ — which is fine — but natives often drop it to 妈妈呢？ in context. Same meaning, far more natural conversationally.

### 3. 刚 vs 刚才 — Two "just nows" — one's an adverb, one's a noun

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to say something happened recently. But "recently" is flexible — 刚才 means literally *a few minutes ago*, while 刚 can mean anything from minutes to *months*, depending on the speaker's sense of "short".
 

### The English trap

English smashes both senses into *just*. Chinese separates them:

- 刚 (adverb) — sits **only** right before a verb. "I've only been in China for 2 months" = 我**刚**来中国两个月.

- 刚才 (noun) — literally "just-now moment", can sit before the verb *or* at the start of the sentence. "A moment ago you were doing homework" = **刚才**你在做作业.

刚 — subjective "a short time"

我刚来中国两个月

"I've only been in China two months" — the speaker feels two months is short.

刚才 — literally a few minutes ago

刚才你在做作业

"A moment ago you were doing homework" — strictly recent.

我刚做完作业，真累啊！

Wǒ gāng zuò wán zuòyè, zhēn lèi a!

I've just finished the homework — I'm exhausted!

刚才你去哪儿了？

Gāngcái nǐ qù nǎr le?

Where did you go a moment ago?

### Common pitfall

Don't put 刚 at the start of a sentence — **刚你去哪儿了* (✗) is wrong. It must attach right before a verb. If you want to front-load the time, use 刚才.

Check

Show answer

越来越 — "more and more" before an adjective.

Which sentence is grammatical?

- 刚你去哪儿了？✗ 刚 cannot start a sentence; use 刚才.

- 刚才你去哪儿了？✓ 刚才 can sit at the start of a sentence.

- 你刚才去哪儿？✗ Missing 了 for past event — awkward without it in this context.

What does this mean?
春天来了，天气越来越暖和了。

- Spring's coming — the weather is cold.✗ 暖和 means warm.

- Spring has come — the weather is getting warmer and warmer.✓ 越来越 + Adj = increasing change.

- Spring will come — the weather might get warmer.✗ 了 here marks a change already happening.
