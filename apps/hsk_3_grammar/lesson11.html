What you'll learn

  1. 才 vs 就 (and the other 就) — two time-scalers with opposite attitudes, plus 就 as sequential "then".
  2. 把字句 2: A 把 B V + 在 / 到 … — disposal, with a destination.

Before you start: three pieces you already know

This lesson stacks old tools in new places. Keep these in your head:

  • 了 after a verb often marks a completed change: 关了 = turned off, done.
  • 把 puts a known object up front because the sentence cares what happens to it: 把门关了.
  • Result complements say the outcome after the verb: 写完 = write-finish, 做好 = make-ready.

Today's new move is adding timing attitude and destination to that same event story.

1. 才 vs 就 "Only then" vs "already" — the speaker's feelings about timing

When a speaker reaches for this: to express an attitude about how fast or slow something happened. 就 = "earlier/faster than expected" (already). 才 = "later/slower than expected" (only then).

The English trap

English uses already vs only, but lazily: "I only got home at 9pm" vs "I was already home by 9pm". Chinese marks this tiny attitude shift directly — and misusing them flips the whole feeling of your sentence.

就 — "sooner than expected"
他七点就来了
He already arrived at 7 (impressively early).
才 — "later than expected"
他九点才来
He didn't come until 9 (annoyingly late).

我六点就起床了。

Wǒ liù diǎn jiù qǐ chuáng le.

I was already up at 6 (early for me).

我九点才起床。

Wǒ jiǔ diǎn cái qǐ chuáng.

I didn't get up until 9 (later than I usually do).

Notice: sentences with 就 usually end with 了 (completed early); sentences with 才 typically don't use 了 (the speaker is still "complaining" about the lateness).

Choose the right word in three passes

Before you translate, ask what kind of thing sits before the marker:

  • Time expression + 就 + V + 了 → "already / as early as": 他六点就起床了.
  • Time expression + 才 + V → "only then / not until": 他九点才起床.
  • Event or condition + 就 + V → "once X, then Y": 他起床了就吃早饭.

So 就 has two jobs. If it follows a clock-time or duration, it comments on speed. If it follows a whole event, it links the next step.

Practice

What's the attitude in: 他六点才回来。

  • Speaker thinks 6 o'clock is late.✓ 才 marks "later than expected".
  • Speaker thinks 6 o'clock is early.✗ For that you'd use 就: 他六点就回来了.
  • Speaker has no feeling about the time.✗ 才/就 always carry an attitude about timing.

Fill with 才 or 就: 考试明天就开始，她昨天 ___ 复习完了。
(She finished reviewing yesterday — impressively early.)

就 — "already done (ahead of schedule)". 就 typically pairs with 了 at the end.

Fill with 才 or 就: 电影七点开始，他八点 ___ 到。
(He didn't arrive until 8 — late.)

才 — "only at 8 (later than expected)". Note no 了: the speaker is still annoyed.

What does this mean? 我五岁就开始学英语了。

  • I didn't start English until I was 5.✗ That would use 才: 我五岁才开始…
  • I started English as early as age 5.✓ 就 + 了 = impressively early.
  • I have been studying English for 5 years.✗ No duration is expressed here.

Fill with 才 or 就: 等他到了 ___ 告诉我。
(As soon as he gets here, tell me.)

就 — "…then (right away)". 就 also links two actions to mean "as soon as… then…".

The OTHER 就 — conditional / sequential "then"

When a speaker reaches for this: to say "once X happens, Y follows immediately" — "as soon as he gets here, tell me", "if it rains, let's not go", "finish this and then we'll eat". Same character, completely different meaning from the timing-attitude 就 above.

The English trap

The 就 you just learned means "earlier than expected" — an attitude about clock time. The 就 in 等他到了就告诉我 has nothing to do with expectations. It links two clauses: [condition / first event] → 就 → [immediate consequence]. English translates it as "then" or "as soon as".

[Condition / first event] , Subj + 就 + V + …

等他到了就告诉我。

Děng tā dào le jiù gàosu wǒ.

wait / he / arrive / PERF / then / tell / me

Once he arrives, tell me (right away).

外边下雨了，我们就不去了。

Wàibian xiàyǔ le, wǒmen jiù bù qù le.

outside / rain-START / we / then / not / go / SETTLED

It's raining outside — in that case, we won't go.

你写完作业就可以看电视。

Nǐ xiě wán zuòyè jiù kěyǐ kàn diànshì.

you / write-finish / homework / then / can / watch / TV

Once you finish your homework, you can watch TV.

Timing 就 (earlier than expected)
他六点就起床了。
He was already up at 6 — impressively early.
Sequential 就 (once X, then Y)
他起床了就吃早饭。
As soon as he gets up, he has breakfast.

How to tell them apart

Look at what comes before 就. A time expression (六点, 昨天, 五分钟) → timing 就. A clause describing an event or condition (等他到了, 写完作业) → sequential 就. The sequential version often pairs with 如果…, 只要…, or a bare event clause with a completion marker.

Practice

What does 就 mean here? 他一回家就睡觉。

  • He already sleeps by the time he gets home — impressively early.✗ "一…就…" is the sequential pattern, not timing.
  • As soon as he gets home, he goes to sleep.✓ 一…就… = "once X happens, Y immediately follows".
  • He only sleeps at home.✗ No "only" sense here; 就 links two events.

Build: "Once you finish your homework, you can watch TV."

你 写完 作业 就 可以 看电视

Fill the blank: 外边下雨了，我们___不去了。
(It's raining — in that case, we won't go.)

就 — "then / in that case". 就 links the rain (cause) to the not-going (consequence).

Which 就? 他五分钟就做完了。

  • Timing 就 — he finished in an impressively short 5 minutes.✓ 五分钟 is a time expression → timing 就 ("as little as 5 minutes").
  • Sequential 就 — first he did 5 minutes, then he finished.✗ There's no event clause before 就 here.
  • Neither — 就 is optional here.✗ 就 carries the "only X much time was needed" feel; removing it changes meaning.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about your own timing — things you get done earlier than people expect, and things you always end up doing later than you meant to. At least one sentence must be clock time + 就 + V + 了 (impressively early), at least one clock time + 才 + V with no 了 (later than you meant), and at least one must be the sequential 一 … 就 …. Use three different verbs, and every sentence has to be true for you.

我一般星期六早上七点就到公园了，那时候路上人很少。可是星期天我常常十点才吃早饭，因为晚上睡得太晚。我一下班就去超市买点儿东西，回家以后就开始做菜。上个星期借的那本词典，我昨天晚上才看了半个小时。

Wǒ yìbān xīngqīliù zǎoshang qī diǎn jiù dào gōngyuán le, nà shíhou lù shang rén hěn shǎo. Kěshì xīngqītiān wǒ chángcháng shí diǎn cái chī zǎofàn, yīnwèi wǎnshang shuì de tài wǎn. Wǒ yí xiàbān jiù qù chāoshì mǎi diǎnr dōngxi, huí jiā yǐhòu jiù kāishǐ zuò cài. Shàng ge xīngqī jiè de nà běn cídiǎn, wǒ zuótiān wǎnshang cái kàn le bàn ge xiǎoshí.

On Saturdays I'm usually at the park by seven — the streets are still empty then. On Sundays, though, I often don't have breakfast until ten, because I go to bed too late. The moment I finish work I go to the supermarket for a few things, and once I'm home I start cooking. That dictionary I borrowed last week — I only read it for half an hour last night.

Why these are right: both markers stand after the time expression and before the verb — 七点就到, 十点才吃 — never in front of the clock time. 就 leans on 了 because the early thing is finished and countable; 才 refuses the sentence-final 了, because the speaker is still grumbling rather than reporting a clean result. That is why sentence 4 is still legal: 才看了半个小时 has the other 了 — the one that sits on the verb to count a duration actually done — and the sentence stops on 半个小时, not on a final 了. The third pattern is a different 就 altogether: after a whole event (一下班 · 回家以后) it means "and then straight away", with no comment on early or late.

Check your own sentences:

  • Position: 七点就到了 ✓ · 就七点到了 ✗
  • 就-sentence: is there a 了? Early-and-done almost always takes one.
  • 才-sentence: did you leave the sentence-final 了 off? 十点才吃早饭了 ✗ (a verbal 了 counting a duration — 才看了半个小时 — is fine.)
  • In the 一 … 就 … sentence, is what precedes 就 a whole event, not a clock time? That is the only thing telling the two 就's apart.
  • Would a Chinese reader agree with your attitude — is the 就 time genuinely early for you, and the 才 time genuinely later than you meant?

2. 把字句 2: A 把 B V + 在 / 到 … Disposal, with a destination

When a speaker reaches for this: to say they put/sent/moved the object to a place. The "where does B end up" version of the 把 frame.

The English trap

English: "put the book on the table" — place after the verb. Chinese demands 把 here: 把书放在桌子上. You cannot write *放书在桌子上 (✗). When the verb means placing or moving something to a specific location, 把 is obligatory.

A + 把 + B + V + 在 / 到 + place

请把书放在桌子上。

Qǐng bǎ shū fàng zài zhuōzi shang.

please / [disposal] / book / put / at / table-on

Please put the book on the table.

我把行李送到机场了。

Wǒ bǎ xíngli sòng dào jīchǎng le.

I / [disposal] / luggage / deliver / to / airport / PERF

I took the luggage to the airport.

他把钱放在包里了。

Tā bǎ qián fàng zài bāo lǐ le.

He put the money in his bag.

Common pitfall

You can't split 在 / 到 from the verb. Always verb + 在/到 + place, inside the 把 frame. Writing 在 before the verb is the English-speaker instinct and always wrong here.

Convert English order into Chinese order

English says: put/send/move + object + to/at + place. Chinese asks you to build the object-result story first:

who + 把 + object + action + 在 / 到 + place
  • "put the book on the table" → 把书放在桌子上
  • "send the luggage to the airport" → 把行李送到机场

Use 在 when the place is the resulting location after putting/placing. Use 到 when the action travels all the way to a destination.

Practice

Build: "Please move the chair into the classroom."

请 把 椅子 搬 到 教室里

Pick the correct sentence:

  • 我放书在桌子上。✗ English order. Chinese needs 把 + V + 在/到 + place.
  • 我把书放在桌子上。✓ 把-frame with placement complement.
  • 我在桌子上把书放。✗ 在+place sits with the verb, not at the front.

What's the end state? 他把车开到学校了。

  • He drove away from school.✗ 到学校 = arrived at school.
  • The car ended up at school.✓ 开到学校 = drove (all the way) to school; the car is now there.
  • He is driving toward school now.✗ The 了 here signals completion — the car has arrived.

Fill the blank: 请把词典放 ___ 桌子上。
(Please put the dictionary on the table.)

在 — 放在 focuses on the resulting location (sitting on). 到 would emphasise movement to a destination.

Which sentence means "Don't forget to turn off the air conditioner"?

  • 别忘了把空调关了。✓ 把 disposes of 空调; 关了 marks completion.
  • 别忘了关空调把。✗ 把 must precede the object, not follow the verb.
  • 别把忘了关空调。✗ 把 needs a concrete object (空调), not 忘了.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about where things in your life end up — where your laptop, your coffee or your keys get put, something you carried somewhere, something you gave back. Every sentence uses A 把 B + V + 在 / 到 + place. Use three different verbs, at least one 在 and at least one 到, and make one sentence an instruction to somebody else (请 … or 别忘记 …). Every sentence has to be true for you.

每天早上我把笔记本电脑放在包里，到了公司再把它拿出来放在桌子上。上个星期天我把哥哥的旧自行车送到了他的新地方。我昨天太累了，把词典忘在图书馆了，今天早上才去拿回来。请你别把水放在电脑旁边。

Měi tiān zǎoshang wǒ bǎ bǐjìběn diànnǎo fàng zài bāo li, dào le gōngsī zài bǎ tā ná chūlai fàng zài zhuōzi shang. Shàng ge xīngqītiān wǒ bǎ gēge de jiù zìxíngchē sòng dào le tā de xīn dìfang. Wǒ zuótiān tài lèi le, bǎ cídiǎn wàng zài túshūguǎn le, jīntiān zǎoshang cái qù ná huílai. Qǐng nǐ bié bǎ shuǐ fàng zài diànnǎo pángbiān.

Every morning I put my laptop in my bag, and once I'm at the office I take it out and put it on the desk. Last Sunday I took my brother's old bike over to his new place. I was so tired yesterday that I left my dictionary at the library, and only went back for it this morning. Please don't put water next to the computer.

Why these are right: 在 and 到 are welded to the verb, not to the front of the clause — 放在包里, 送到他的新地方. 在 reports where the thing now sits; 到 traces it all the way to a destination, which is why a delivery takes 到 and a shelf takes 在. An instruction keeps its 请 or 别 at the very front, ahead of 把, and 别忘记 works the same way: the whole disposal clause hangs behind it.

Check your own sentences:

  • Never 我在桌子上把词典放 — 在 + place follows the verb, inside the 把 frame.
  • Did the object stay between 把 and the verb? 我把放在桌子上词典 ✗
  • 在 for the resulting spot, 到 for a destination reached. Could you justify each choice?
  • Places usually need their ending: 包里 · 桌子上 · 楼下 — a bare 包 is not a location.
  • The instruction sentence: is 请 / 别 in front of 把, with nothing wedged between 把 and its object?

What does this mean? 我九点才把词典还到图书馆。

  • I returned the dictionary at 9 — nice and early.✗ 才 signals "later than expected".
  • I didn't return the dictionary to the library until 9 (late).✓ 才 = only then; 还到图书馆 = returned it (all the way) to the library.
  • I will return the dictionary at 9.✗ The context is past; 才 complains about lateness that already happened.

Write the paragraph. The Saturday 小丽 and 小刚 came over to eat at your place. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using both of this lesson's structures: at least one time + 就 + V + 了 and one time + 才 + V (who was early, who was late), at least one sequential 一 … 就 …, and at least two 把 sentences with a destination (把 … 放在 / 搬到 …). Cover: what you had ready and when, when each of them turned up, what happened the moment you finished eating, and what went where at the end of the evening.

上个星期六小丽和小刚要来我家吃东西。我五点就把菜都做好了，还把新买的水果放在中间的桌子上。小丽六点半就到了，可是小刚八点才来，他说他一下班就出来了，路上的人太多。我们一吃完东西就把电视打开了，看了一个很有意思的电影。最后我把小刚的自行车搬到楼下，他就骑车回家了。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù Xiǎolì hé Xiǎogāng yào lái wǒ jiā chī dōngxi. Wǒ wǔ diǎn jiù bǎ cài dōu zuò hǎo le, hái bǎ xīn mǎi de shuǐguǒ fàng zài zhōngjiān de zhuōzi shang. Xiǎolì liù diǎn bàn jiù dào le, kěshì Xiǎogāng bā diǎn cái lái, tā shuō tā yí xiàbān jiù chūlai le, lù shang de rén tài duō. Wǒmen yì chī wán dōngxi jiù bǎ diànshì dǎkāi le, kàn le yí ge hěn yǒu yìsi de diànyǐng. Zuìhòu wǒ bǎ Xiǎogāng de zìxíngchē bān dào lóuxià, tā jiù qí chē huí jiā le.

Last Saturday Xiaoli and Xiaogang were coming over to eat. I had the food done by five and put the fruit I'd just bought on the table in the middle. Xiaoli was there by half six, but Xiaogang didn't turn up until eight — he said he'd left the moment he finished work, but the streets were packed. As soon as we'd finished eating we put the TV on and watched a really good film. At the end I carried Xiaogang's bike down to the ground floor and he rode home.

Why this works as a paragraph: 就 and 才 are what turn a timetable into a story — 五点就 says you were ahead of yourself, 八点才 says somebody kept you waiting, and the reader feels both without being told. The sequential 就 (一下班就 · 一吃完东西就) then chains the evening's steps, while the 把 sentences say where each thing ended up: the fruit on the table (在), the bike down to the ground floor (到). Note that 才来 has no 了, but every 就 clause does.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Both markers sit after the time and before the verb, and only the 就 sentences carry 了.
  • Is your 一 … 就 … built on an event (一下班 · 一吃完) rather than on a clock time?
  • 把 sentences: object between 把 and the verb, and 在/到 + place glued to the verb.
  • Did you choose 在 vs 到 by meaning — resting place vs destination reached?
  • Does it read as one evening, with the guests, the food and the film connected, rather than five separate drills?

Four errors. Each sentence below contains exactly one mistake, and all four are mistakes with this lesson's structures. Rewrite all four correctly in the box, then check.
1. 我昨天十一点才回家了。
2. 他就六点到公司了。
3. 我把词典在桌子上放了。
4. 请你送这些书到图书馆。

1. 我昨天十一点才回家。(final 了 deleted)
2. 他六点就到公司了。
3. 我把词典放在桌子上了。
4. 请你把这些书送到图书馆。

1. Wǒ zuótiān shíyī diǎn cái huí jiā. 2. Tā liù diǎn jiù dào gōngsī le. 3. Wǒ bǎ cídiǎn fàng zài zhuōzi shang le. 4. Qǐng nǐ bǎ zhèxiē shū sòng dào túshūguǎn.

1. I didn't get home until eleven last night. 2. He was at the office by six. 3. I put the dictionary on the table. 4. Please take these books to the library.

  1. 才回家了 → 才回家. 才 says the event happened later than it should have, so the speaker is still complaining, not reporting a tidy completion — the sentence-final 了 clashes with that and is dropped.
  2. 就六点 → 六点就. Both markers follow the time expression they comment on. In front of the clock time, 就 can only be read as "merely", which is not what the sentence means.
  3. 在桌子上放了 → 放在桌子上了. 在 + place is a complement, so it lives behind the verb. Fronting it is the English "on the table I put it" instinct, and inside a 把 frame it is simply ungrammatical.
  4. 送这些书到图书馆 → 把这些书送到图书馆. As soon as the verb carries a destination, the object has to move in front of it with 把. 送书到图书馆 keeps English word order and leaves the verb with two things to carry.

  • 才 sentences: no 了 at the end. 就 sentences: 了 almost always present.
  • Time expression first, then 就 / 才, then the verb.
  • 在 / 到 + place always after the verb, never before the object.
  • Verb with a destination ⇒ the object needs 把 in front of it.
  • Did you change exactly one thing per sentence, and leave the meaning intact?

1. 小丽问：你昨天几点到家的？ [time + 才 + V]
2. 妈妈问：你的电子邮件写完了吗？ [time + 就 + 把 … + 了]
3. 同事问：我的词典你放在哪儿了？ [把 … 放在 …]
4. 小刚问：我们几点出去？ [一 … 就 …]

1. 我十二点才到家，路上等了很久。
2. 写完了，我七点就把它写完了。
3. 我把你的词典放在中间那个桌子上了。
4. 你一准备好我们就出去。

1. Wǒ shí'èr diǎn cái dào jiā, lù shang děng le hěn jiǔ. 2. Xiě wán le, wǒ qī diǎn jiù bǎ tā xiě wán le. 3. Wǒ bǎ nǐ de cídiǎn fàng zài zhōngjiān nàge zhuōzi shang le. 4. Nǐ yì zhǔnbèi hǎo wǒmen jiù chūqu.

1. I didn't get home until twelve — I waited a long time on the way. 2. Yes — I'd finished it by seven. 3. I put your dictionary on the table in the middle. 4. We'll go out as soon as you're ready.

Why these are right: (1) the answer keeps 才 after the time and skips 了, then explains the lateness — which is what 才 invites the listener to ask about. (2) Chinese answers a 吗-question by repeating the verb (写完了), and the follow-up adds the early-timing 就 with the object promoted by 把. (3) A "where did you put it" question is answered with the same frame it implies: 把 + object + 放在 + place + 了. (4) The two clauses have different subjects (你 … 我们 …), so each keeps its own — 一 sits in front of the first verb, 就 in front of the second.

Check your own answers:

  • Answer 1: 才 after the time, no 了 — and does your sentence sound like a complaint?
  • Answer 2: did you repeat the verb instead of writing 是 for "yes"?
  • Answer 3: the place needs its ending (桌子上), and 了 closes the sentence.
  • Answer 4: 一 and 就 each stand directly in front of their own verb, even when the subjects differ.
  • Are all four full sentences that would make sense on their own, not one-word replies?
