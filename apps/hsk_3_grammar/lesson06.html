1. Duration complement How to say how long an action lasted

When a speaker reaches for this: to say they did something for a certain length of time. "I studied Chinese for three years." "We waited for half an hour."

The English trap

English puts the duration after the object: "I studied Chinese for three years." Chinese puts the duration between the verb and its object — 学了三年汉语 — literally "studied three years Chinese". This is the opposite of English instinct, and forgetting it produces sentences that sound immediately non-native.

S + V + 了 + Duration + Object

她工作了三年。

Tā gōngzuò le sān nián.

She worked for three years.

我们坐了一个小时公共汽车。

Wǒmen zuò le yí ge xiǎoshí gōnggòng qìchē.

we / sit-PERF / one / hour / bus

We sat on the bus for an hour.

First decide: finished stretch or up to now?

Before you build the sentence, decide what kind of timeline you mean. Chinese uses the same duration slot, but the final 了 changes the meaning from a completed block of time to a duration that reaches the present.

Completed duration
past start → duration → stopped
V + 了 + duration + O: the action lasted this long. It may be over now.
Up-to-now duration
past start → duration → now
V + 了 + duration + O + 了: it has been this long up to now, and the situation is still true.

To say the action has lasted a duration and is still going on, add 了 at the very end:

我学习了一年汉语了。

Wǒ xuéxí le yì nián Hànyǔ le.

I've been studying Chinese for a year (and still am).

One 了 — done
她看了半个小时电视
She watched TV for half an hour. (Possibly stopped.)
Two 了s — still going
她看了半个小时电视了
She has been watching TV for half an hour (and is still watching).

Fill the blank: 她工作___。
(She worked for three years.)

了三年 — V + 了 + duration for a completed stretch.

Which sentence means "We waited for half an hour"?

  • 我们等了半个小时。✓ V + 了 + duration — correct order.
  • 我们半个小时等了。✗ Duration cannot precede the verb here; it's a complement.
  • 我们等半个小时了。✗ Missing 了 after the verb; this reads as "still waiting" with a different structure.

What does this mean?

  • I studied Chinese for one year (and stopped).✗ That would use only one 了 — 我学了一年汉语.
  • I've been studying Chinese for a year (and I still am).✓ The final 了 signals the action is ongoing up to now.
  • I will study Chinese for a year.✗ 了 marks completed/ongoing, not future.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about how long you really spent on things — this week, or across your life so far. Every sentence uses S + V + 了 + Duration + (O), with four different verbs, and one of them must be the up-to-now kind, with a second 了 at the very end, about something that is still true today.

上个星期六我在图书馆看了三个小时书，眼睛都累了。回家以后我做了四十分钟菜，可是十分钟就吃完了。我在纽约住了六年了，还是觉得这个城市很有意思。我妈妈昨天给我打了半个小时电话，讲的都是我小时候的事情。

Shàng ge xīngqīliù wǒ zài túshūguǎn kàn le sān ge xiǎoshí shū, yǎnjing dōu lèi le. Huí jiā yǐhòu wǒ zuò le sìshí fēnzhōng cài, kěshì shí fēnzhōng jiù chī wán le. Wǒ zài Niǔyuē zhù le liù nián le, háishi juéde zhège chéngshì hěn yǒu yìsi. Wǒ māma zuótiān gěi wǒ dǎ le bàn ge xiǎoshí diànhuà, jiǎng de dōu shì wǒ xiǎo shíhou de shìqing.

Last Saturday I read in the library for three hours and my eyes were worn out. Once I got home I cooked for forty minutes — and it was eaten in ten. I've been living in New York for six years and I still find the city interesting. My mum rang me for half an hour yesterday, and all of it was about when I was little.

Why these are right: the duration is wedged between 了 and the object — 看了三个小时书, 做了四十分钟菜 — which is the exact reverse of the English order. 打电话 is two pieces, so the duration drops into the seam: 打了半个小时电话. And 住了六年了 carries a second 了 because the six years run right up to today; take that 了 away and it would sound like the New York years are over.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the duration between V了 and the object? 看了三个小时书 ✓ · 看书了三个小时 / 看了书三个小时 ✗
  • Separable verbs split the same way: 打了半个小时电话 ✓ · 打电话了半个小时 ✗
  • Second 了 at the end only if the stretch reaches now and is still running.
  • Is it really a duration and not a clock time? 三个小时 (how long) goes after the verb; 三点 (when) goes before it.
  • Four different verbs, and every length honest — the point is to attach the pattern to your real week.

2. 对 … 感兴趣 "Interested in"

When a speaker reaches for this: to say they find X interesting. The topic of interest slides into the slot between 对 and 感兴趣.

The English trap

English: "I'm interested in X." Chinese uses 对 (a preposition roughly meaning towards/regarding) before the topic, and 感兴趣 ("feel interest") after. It's a frame, not a prepositional phrase: 对 X 感兴趣.

S + 对 + topic + (很) 感兴趣 / 有兴趣

他们对电影感兴趣。

Tāmen duì diànyǐng gǎn xìngqù.

They're interested in movies.

同学们对汉语有兴趣。

Tóngxuémen duì Hànyǔ yǒu xìngqù.

The students are interested in Chinese.

Negative: 对 … 不感兴趣 / 没有兴趣. Degree adverbs (很, 非常) go before 感兴趣, not before 对.

Practice

Build: "I'm very interested in music."

我 对 音乐 很 感兴趣

Fill the blank: 他们___电影感兴趣。
(They're interested in movies.)

对 — 对 + topic + 感兴趣.

Which sentence is grammatical?

  • 同学们对汉语有兴趣。✓ 对 + topic + 有兴趣 is the fixed frame.
  • 同学们很对汉语感兴趣。✗ 很 goes before 感兴趣, not before 对.
  • 同学们感兴趣对汉语。✗ 对 + topic must come before 感兴趣.

What does this mean?
我对中国文化非常感兴趣。

  • Chinese culture is very interesting to me (it's the topic).✗ Close, but the subject here is 我 ("I am very interested in…").
  • I'm extremely interested in Chinese culture.✓ 非常 intensifies 感兴趣.
  • I'm not interested in Chinese culture.✗ No negation — would need 不感兴趣.

Build: "He's not interested in games."

他 对 游戏 不 感兴趣

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about what you — and the people around you — really are and aren't interested in. Every sentence uses the frame 对 … 感兴趣 (or its noun twin 对 … 有兴趣 / 没有兴趣), across three different subjects (at least one of them somebody other than you). Make one sentence negative with 对 … 不感兴趣, and place a degree word (很 · 非常 · 特别) in the correct slot at least once.

我对中国的历史特别感兴趣，去年买了五六本这样的书。我妈妈对新手机一点儿也不感兴趣，她的手机已经用了八年了。我们公司的经理对跳舞很有兴趣，上个月还请了老师来教大家。可是我对开车没有兴趣，在纽约也不太需要。

Wǒ duì Zhōngguó de lìshǐ tèbié gǎn xìngqù, qùnián mǎi le wǔ-liù běn zhèyàng de shū. Wǒ māma duì xīn shǒujī yìdiǎnr yě bù gǎn xìngqù, tā de shǒujī yǐjīng yòng le bā nián le. Wǒmen gōngsī de jīnglǐ duì tiàowǔ hěn yǒu xìngqù, shàng ge yuè hái qǐng le lǎoshī lái jiāo dàjiā. Kěshì wǒ duì kāichē méiyǒu xìngqù, zài Niǔyuē yě bú tài xūyào.

I'm especially interested in Chinese history — last year I bought five or six books about it. My mum has no interest whatsoever in new phones; hers is eight years old. The manager at our company is very keen on dancing, and last month she even brought in a teacher for everyone. I'm not interested in driving, though — you don't really need it in New York.

Why these are right: 对 … 感兴趣 is a frame, and the topic lives inside it, never after. Everything that modifies the interest — 特别, 很, 一点儿也不 — clips onto 感兴趣 at the back end, which is why 很对…感兴趣 is impossible: 对 is a preposition, and you can't say something is "very towards" a topic. Both halves of the pair have a noun form too: 有兴趣 / 没有兴趣 behave identically.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the topic sitting between 对 and 感兴趣? 对历史感兴趣 ✓ · 感兴趣对历史 ✗
  • Degree word immediately before 感兴趣: 对跳舞很感兴趣 ✓ · 很对跳舞感兴趣 ✗
  • Negation goes inside the frame too — 对 … 不感兴趣 / 没有兴趣, never 不对 … 感兴趣.
  • If you switched to the noun version, is it 有兴趣 / 很有兴趣 (not 感有兴趣)?
  • Three different subjects, at least one of them somebody else — and are those interests actually theirs?

3. 半 / 刻 / 差 Telling the clock

When a speaker reaches for this: to say the time in natural spoken Chinese.

The English trap

English has "half past", "a quarter past / to". Chinese uses:

  • 半 (bàn) = half — "half past"
  • 刻 (kè) = quarter — "a quarter past" (三刻 = three quarters past, not "three quarters to"!)
  • 差 (chà) = "lacking" — used before the hour to mean "before".

现在三点半。

Xiànzài sān diǎn bàn.

It's half past three. (3:30)

十二点三刻 = 差一刻一点 = 差十五分一点

shí'èr diǎn sān kè / chà yí kè yī diǎn / chà shíwǔ fēn yī diǎn

12:45 = "twelve and three quarters" = "a quarter to one" = "fifteen minutes to one"

Don't confuse 一刻钟 with 一刻

一刻 on its own (after a time) = 15 minutes into the hour. But 一刻钟 is a duration: "a quarter of an hour" = 15 minutes. Compare: 我等了你一刻钟 = "I waited for you 15 minutes".

Clock map before you practice

Read the hour first, then decide whether the minutes are after that hour or just before the next hour.

Time Natural Chinese How to think
3:15 三点一刻 一刻 = one quarter after the hour.
3:30 三点半 半 = half past.
3:45 三点三刻 三刻 = three quarters after the hour.
3:45 差一刻四点 差 = lacking: one quarter before four.

Practice

Which is 10:30?

  • 十点半✓ 半 after the hour = half past.
  • 差半十点✗ 差 means "lacking" — used only for before-the-hour.
  • 十半点✗ 半 goes after 点, not between the numeral and 点.

Say the time 11:45 in Chinese:

Three natural options: 差一刻十二点 (a quarter to 12) / 十一点三刻 (11 and three quarters) / 差十五分十二点.

What time is this?
差五分八点

  • 8:05.✗ 差 = "lacking", so it's before 8, not after.
  • 7:55 (five to eight).✓ 差 + amount + hour = that much before the hour.
  • 5:08.✗ Not how the structure works — the hour comes after.

现在 差 一刻 六点

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about the clock times that actually run your day — when you get up, when you leave, when a class or a meeting starts, when you eat, when you go to bed. Use 半, 刻 and 差 at least once each, saying each time the way you would really say it out loud, and add what happens at that time rather than only the number.

我每天早上六点半起床，六点三刻出门，差一刻八点到公司。中午十二点一刻我下楼买点儿吃的，坐在公园里看手机。星期三晚上七点有中文课，可是我常常差五分七点才出门。晚上十一点半我就上床睡觉了，有的时候一点儿也睡不着。

Wǒ měi tiān zǎoshang liù diǎn bàn qǐchuáng, liù diǎn sān kè chūmén, chà yí kè bā diǎn dào gōngsī. Zhōngwǔ shí'èr diǎn yí kè wǒ xià lóu mǎi diǎnr chī de, zuò zài gōngyuán li kàn shǒujī. Xīngqīsān wǎnshang qī diǎn yǒu Zhōngwén kè, kěshì wǒ chángcháng chà wǔ fēn qī diǎn cái chūmén. Wǎnshang shíyī diǎn bàn wǒ jiù shàng chuáng shuìjiào le, yǒu de shíhou yìdiǎnr yě shuì bu zháo.

Every morning I get up at half six, leave the house at a quarter to seven, and reach the office at a quarter to eight. At a quarter past twelve I go downstairs for something to eat and sit in the park looking at my phone. There's a Chinese class at seven on Wednesday evening, but I often don't leave the house until five to seven. At half eleven at night I go to bed, and sometimes I can't fall asleep at all.

Why these are right: 半 and 刻 come after 点 (六点半 · 六点三刻 · 十二点一刻), because they count what has already gone by inside that hour — 三刻 is three quarters past, never three quarters to. 差 works the opposite way round: it subtracts, so it stands in front of the whole expression and names the hour you have not reached yet — 差一刻八点 is 7:45, and 差五分七点 is 6:55.

Check your own sentences:

  • 半 and 刻 after 点: 六点半 ✓ · 半六点 / 六半点 ✗
  • 差 in front of the whole time, with the coming hour named: 差一刻八点 = 7:45.
  • Did you mean :45 past or :15 to? 三刻 and 差一刻 point at the same moment from opposite ends — pick one and stay consistent.
  • 一刻 is a point on the clock; 一刻钟 is a fifteen-minute stretch: 我等了一刻钟.
  • Are these your real times, and does each sentence say what actually happens then?

Write the paragraph. A Saturday you spent somewhere because of something you are genuinely interested in — a second-hand bookshop, a market, a museum, a park, a class. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using all three structures of this lesson at least once each: 对 … 感兴趣, S + V + 了 + Duration + (O), and a clock time built with 半, 刻 or 差. Cover: why you went, what time you got there, how long you stayed, and what has come of it since.

我对中国的历史很感兴趣，上个星期六去了一家旧书店。我坐了四十分钟地铁，差一刻十点到那儿，门口已经有很多人在等了。我在里面看了三个小时书，站得腿都疼了。下午一点半我和小丽在旁边的咖啡店见面，我们聊天儿聊了一个多小时。我买的那本书现在已经看了一半了，还是觉得特别有意思。

Wǒ duì Zhōngguó de lìshǐ hěn gǎn xìngqù, shàng ge xīngqīliù qù le yì jiā jiù shūdiàn. Wǒ zuò le sìshí fēnzhōng dìtiě, chà yí kè shí diǎn dào nàr, ménkǒu yǐjīng yǒu hěn duō rén zài děng le. Wǒ zài lǐmiàn kàn le sān ge xiǎoshí shū, zhàn de tuǐ dōu téng le. Xiàwǔ yì diǎn bàn wǒ hé Xiǎolì zài pángbiān de kāfēi diàn jiànmiàn, wǒmen liáotiānr liáo le yí ge duō xiǎoshí. Wǒ mǎi de nà běn shū xiànzài yǐjīng kàn le yíbàn le, háishi juéde tèbié yǒu yìsi.

I'm very interested in Chinese history, so last Saturday I went to a second-hand bookshop. I was on the subway for forty minutes and got there at a quarter to ten, and there were already plenty of people waiting at the door. I read inside for three hours until my legs ached from standing. At half past one I met Xiaoli at the coffee shop next door, and we talked for over an hour. I'm halfway through the book I bought, and I still find it fascinating.

Why this works as a paragraph: the three structures each carry a different part of the day. 对…感兴趣 supplies the reason — it is why there is a story at all; the durations (坐了四十分钟地铁 · 看了三个小时书 · 聊了一个多小时) supply the shape of the day, each one with the length wedged in after 了; and the clock times (差一刻十点 · 一点半) pin it to real hours. Notice 聊天儿聊了一个多小时: when a separable verb already has its object attached, you repeat the verb before the duration rather than breaking the pair.

Check your own paragraph:

  • 对 … 感兴趣: topic inside the frame, degree word next to 感兴趣, not next to 对.
  • Duration after 了 and before the object: 看了三个小时书 ✓ · 看书三个小时 ✗
  • A second 了 at the end only where the stretch is still running today.
  • Clock: 半／刻 after 点, 差 in front of the hour you have not reached.
  • Does it read as one Saturday — joined by time words — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

  2. 打电话了半个小时 → 打了半个小时电话. 打电话 is already verb + object, so it has to be prised apart — 了 and the duration both go into the seam. Treating 打电话 as one solid verb is what produces the wrong version.

English puts the subtraction after the hour is in mind ("a quarter to nine"), Chinese puts it first: 差 announces that something is missing from the hour that follows. 九点差一刻 is not the order this lesson drills, and read straight through it invites the opposite reading — 九点一刻, 9:15.

Why these are right: (1) the length goes in after 了 and the object follows — 看了四十分钟电视, never 看电视四十分钟. (2) 一点儿也 belongs with the negation inside the frame: 对运动一点儿也不感兴趣; nothing may sit in front of 对. (3) 差 leads, and the hour named is the one still to come — 差一刻七点 is 6:45; the 了 on the end reports that the moment has arrived. (4) The person waited for goes straight after the verb and the duration after them (等了你半个小时), and the final 了 is what makes the waiting still be happening — drop it and you have simply waited half an hour at some point.

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: did the duration land before 电视? English order here is the trap.
  • Sentence 2: is 一点儿也 in front of 不感兴趣, and 对 untouched at the front?
  • Sentence 3: 差一刻七点 = 6:45. If you wrote 七点一刻 you have moved 30 minutes the wrong way.
  • Sentence 4: is there a 了 both after 等 and at the very end? Only the second one keeps you waiting.
  • Did every word after 用 actually make it into your sentence?
