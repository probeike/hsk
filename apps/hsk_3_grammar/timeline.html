What you'll learn

This is a custom lesson, not a textbook chapter. It gathers everything about when — naming days and months, counting forwards and backwards from now, and, above all, where a time word is allowed to sit in a Chinese sentence. Lesson 6 already gave you clock times and duration; this lesson builds the calendar around them.

  1. 上 / 这 / 下 — calendar navigation — 上个月, 下个星期, 前天 … 后天, 前年 … 后年.
  2. Counting from now — 两个月以前, 两个星期之后, 过几天, 下课以后, 吃饭以前.
  3. 前两个月 vs 两个月前 — the trap: a span or a point? Plus duration word order.
  4. Where the time word sits — never at the end; Time → Place → Verb; big → small.
  5. Sequencing a story — …以后 + 就, and …的时候.
  6. Mixed practice

1. 上 / 这 / 下 Calendar navigation — naming a week, a month, a year without a date

When a speaker reaches for this: to point at a day, week, month or year relative to right now, without saying an actual date. "Next month I'm going to look for a dance class." "Last week I slept badly." "The year before last I was working in Beijing." These are the most common time words in the language — you will use them in almost every conversation about plans or the past.

The English trap

English says last and next — words about a line you walk along. Chinese says 上 (up) and 下 (down), which feels arbitrary until you picture the object: a wall calendar. You flip the finished pages upward over the top of the pad, so everything 上 is behind you, and the pages still hanging below are what's coming. Up = earlier. Down = later.

The wall-calendar picture. Pages you have already flipped sit above — 上个月, 上个星期. Pages still to come hang below — 下个月, 下个星期. Whenever 上/下 feels random, put your hand on the pad and flip.
Unitearlier ←now→ later
星期 / 周 上(个)星期 这(个)星期 下(个)星期
月 上个月 这个月 下个月
年 (no 上/下!) 前年 · 去年 今年 明年 · 后年
天 (no 上/下!) 前天 · 昨天 今天 明天 · 后天
Days and years use a different pair: 前 (in front / two back) and 后 (behind / two forward) — never *上天, *下年. Learn the five-dot line as one picture; the year line 前年–去年–今年–明年–后年 has exactly the same shape.

Two things these words never do

  • No 的. 下个月我要辞职 ✓ — *下个月的我要辞职 ✗. They are already time words; they don't need to be turned into modifiers.
  • No 在. 明天我去银行 ✓ — *在明天我去银行 ✗. English on Monday / in March tempts you into 在, but 在 is for places (在纽约), not for these calendar words.

下个月我想去找一个舞蹈课。

Xià ge yuè wǒ xiǎng qù zhǎo yí ge wǔdǎo kè.

next month / I / want / go / find / one / CL / dance class

Next month I want to go and find a dance class.

上个星期我睡得不好。

Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ shuì de bù hǎo.

last week / I / sleep / DE / not good

Last week I slept badly.

前年我在北京工作，去年我搬到了纽约。

Qiánnián wǒ zài Běijīng gōngzuò, qùnián wǒ bāndào le Niǔyuē.

The year before last I was working in Beijing; last year I moved to New York.

后天 — two days forward. 后 = "behind you on the road ahead".

Fill: 昨天我很忙，___ 我更忙。

Which sentence is correct for "Next month I'm going to quit"?

  • 下个月我要辞职。✓ Bare time word, sitting in front of the subject or the verb.
  • 下个月的我要辞职。✗ No 的 — these words are already time words.
  • 在下个月我要辞职。✗ No 在 — 在 introduces places, not calendar words.

What does this mean?
前年他在北京工作。

  • Last year he worked in Beijing.✗ That would be 去年.
  • The year before last he worked in Beijing.✓ 前年 = two years back.
  • The year after next he'll work in Beijing.✗ That would be 后年 — and 工作 here is past.

2. Counting from now X 以前 · X 以后 / 之后 · 过几天 · 下课以后

When a speaker reaches for this: when the calendar words of point 1 aren't enough — because the distance is "two months", not "last month", or because the landmark is an event ("after class", "before dinner") rather than a date. This is how you say two months ago, in two weeks, in a few days, after I quit.
length of time / clause + 以前 / 以后 · 之后

The English trap

English puts the landmark word first: after class, before dinner, in two weeks. Chinese puts it last, like a postposition: 下课以后, 吃饭以前, 两个星期以后. Say the event, then say which side of it you mean. Nothing goes in front.

Measure the distance from 现在. The length of time comes first, then 以前 (backwards) or 以后 (forwards). 以 is optional in speech: 两个月前, 两个星期后.

两个星期之后我要辞职。

Liǎng ge xīngqī zhīhòu wǒ yào cízhí.

two / CL / week / after / I / will / resign

In two weeks I'm going to quit my job.

下课以后我就去跳舞。

Xià kè yǐhòu wǒ jiù qù tiào wǔ.

finish class / after / I / then / go / dance

After class I go straight off to dance.

吃饭以前我不喝饮料。

Chī fàn yǐqián wǒ bù hē yǐnliào.

I don't drink anything sweet before a meal. (Whole clause + 以前 — the clause needs no 了.)

过几天我给你打电话。

Guò jǐ tiān wǒ gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.

pass / a-few / days / I / to you / make phone call

I'll call you in a few days. (Also 过两天 — "two" here just means "a couple".)

Standing alone: 以前 = "formerly", 以后 = "in the future"

With nothing in front of them, these two words stop being "before X / after X" and become whole eras: everything up to now, and everything from now on.

以前 — back then
以前我不喜欢跳舞
"I didn't use to like dancing" — the era before now.
以后 — from now on
以后你想当舞蹈老师吗？
"In the future, do you want to be a dance teacher?" — the era after now.

以后 or 之后?

After a length of time or a clause they are interchangeable — 两个星期以后 = 两个星期之后, 辞职以后 = 辞职之后. 之后 sounds slightly more written or deliberate, which is why it suits the sentence in the title. But standing alone for "in the future", only 以后 works: 以后我想当老板 ✓, *之后我想当老板 ✗.

Practice

Fill: ___ 我要辞职。
(In two weeks I'm going to quit.)

两个星期以后 / 两个星期之后 / 两个星期后 — all fine.

Which says "After class I go straight off to dance"?

  • 下课以后我就去跳舞。✓ Event first, then 以后; the main clause follows.
  • 以后下课我就去跳舞。✗ 以后 must come after the event, not before it.
  • 我就去跳舞下课以后。✗ The time frame can't be tacked on at the end — see point 4.

What does this mean?
以前我不喜欢跳舞。

  • I didn't use to like dancing.✓ Bare 以前 = the era before now.
  • I won't like dancing later.✗ That would need 以后.
  • Before dancing, I'm unhappy.✗ For "before dancing" you'd need 跳舞以前.

Fill: ___ 你想当舞蹈老师吗？
(In the future, do you want to be a dance teacher?)

以后 — standing alone it means "from now on". 之后 cannot do this job.

What does this mean?
过几天我给你打电话。

  • A few days ago I called you.✗ 过 here means "let a few days pass" — it looks forward.
  • I'll call you in a few days.✓ 过几天 = once a few days have gone by.
  • I call you every few days.✗ That would need 每.

3. 前两个月 vs 两个月前 A span, or a point? The word order decides

When a speaker reaches for this: you have "two months" and "before" in your head, and you must choose which one comes first — because the choice changes the meaning completely. One says at a moment two months back. The other says throughout the two months just gone.

The English trap

English hides the difference behind the same three words: "two months ago I moved house" and "for the past two months I haven't practised". Both start with "two months", so an English speaker reaches for 前两个月 for both — and gets one of them wrong. In Chinese the position of 前 is the meaning: 前 after the number = a point; 前 before the number = a span.

Same two words, two shapes. 两个月前 is a dot you can put an event on. 前两个月 / 最近两个月 is a band you can spread a habit — or the lack of one — across.
两个月前 — a point
两个月前我搬家了
"Two months ago I moved house." One event, at one moment.
前两个月 — a span
前两个月我很忙
"Over the two months just gone I was busy." A whole stretch of time.

The sentence you actually said

You wrote 前两个月我搬家了 meaning "Two months ago I moved house." A Chinese ear reads that as "throughout the last two months, I was moving house" — a two-month removal job. For the single event, flip the two halves: 两个月前我搬家了 ✓. When you want the span, 最近两个月 is the safest choice of all — it can never be misread as a point.

Duration: after the verb — except when it's a gap

Lesson 6 gave you the rule: how long an action lasted goes after the verb (学习了两年汉语). But when you say how long something didn't happen, the same length of time jumps to the front, before 没. A gap is not a duration of doing — it's a stretch of time you're describing, so it behaves like a time word.

Doing — duration after V
我在纽约住了三年了
"I've lived in New York for three years."
Not doing — duration before V
我两个月没跳舞了
"I haven't danced for two months."

最近两个月我没练习汉语。

Zuìjìn liǎng ge yuè wǒ méi liànxí Hànyǔ.

recent / two / CL / month / I / not / practise / Chinese

I haven't practised Chinese for the past two months.

我学习了两年汉语了。

Wǒ xuéxí le liǎng nián Hànyǔ le.

I / study-PERF / two years / Chinese / LE

I've been studying Chinese for two years (and still am).

Practice

What does this mean?
两个月前我搬家了。

  • Two months ago I moved house.✓ 前 after the number = a point in time.
  • I spent the last two months moving house.✗ That span reading needs 前两个月 / 最近两个月.
  • In two months I'm going to move house.✗ That would be 两个月以后.

What does this mean?
最近两个月我没练习汉语。

  • Two months ago I stopped practising Chinese.✗ That's a point reading; this is a span.
  • For the past two months I haven't practised Chinese.✓ 最近两个月 spreads across the whole stretch up to now.
  • I'll practise Chinese for two months.✗ Nothing here is in the future.

You mean: "Two months ago I moved house." Which is right?

  • 两个月前我搬家了。✓ Number first, then 前 — a single moment two months back.
  • 前两个月我搬家了。✗ This says you were moving house all through the last two months.
  • 我搬家了两个月前。✗ Time can never sit at the end of the sentence.

Fill: 我在纽约 ___ 三年了。
(I've lived in New York for three years.)

住了 — the duration 三年 follows the verb; the final 了 says it reaches up to now.

Which says "I studied Chinese for two years"?

  • 我学习了两年汉语。✓ Duration sits between the verb and its object (Lesson 6).
  • 我学习汉语两年。✗ English order — the duration must come before the object.
  • 我两年学习了汉语。✗ Fronting the length turns it into a time frame, not a duration.

4. Where the time word sits Before the verb — never at the end

When a speaker reaches for this: every single time you mention when. This is the rule that makes the difference between "sounds foreign" and "sounds Chinese", and it is broken more often than any other by English speakers.

The English trap

English parks time at the end: "I'll go to the bank tomorrow." "I didn't eat this morning." Chinese has no such slot. The time word must be in front of the verb — either at the very start (topic position) or right after the subject. *我去银行明天 ✗ is not a small stylistic wobble; it is ungrammatical.

Time + Subject + Time + Place + Verb + Object
A Chinese sentence sets the stage before anything happens on it: when, then where, then the verb. English does the opposite and sweeps the setting to the back.

今天早上我还没吃饭。

Jīntiān zǎoshang wǒ hái méi chī fàn.

today morning / I / still / not / eat / meal

I still haven't eaten this morning. (Time fronted as the topic — very common.)

我明天去银行。

Wǒ míngtiān qù yínháng.

I / tomorrow / go / bank

I'm going to the bank tomorrow. (Time after the subject — equally correct.)

我想每天在纽约跳舞、工作。

Wǒ xiǎng měitiān zài Niǔyuē tiào wǔ, gōngzuò.

I / want / every day / in New York / dance, work

I want to dance and work in New York every day.

Stacking time words: big → small

When you stack two or more time words, Chinese runs from the largest container down to the smallest — the opposite of English's "at 3 o'clock on Saturday next week".

English (small → big)Chinese (big → small)
in March next year明年三月
this morning今天早上
at 3 p.m. the day after tomorrow后天下午三点
on Saturday evening next week下个星期六晚上

Practice

Which says "I'm going to the bank tomorrow"?

  • 我明天去银行。✓ Time sits between the subject and the verb.
  • 我去银行明天。✗ The English habit — time can't go at the end.
  • 我去明天银行。✗ Now 明天 looks like a modifier of 银行 — "tomorrow's bank".

Which is the standard Time → Place order?

  • 我每天在家学习汉语。✓ When (每天) before where (在家) before the verb.
  • 我在家每天学习汉语。✗ Place before time — the reverse of the normal frame.
  • 我学习汉语每天在家。✗ Both the frame words have fallen off the end.

Fill: ___ 我要去首尔。
(In March next year I'm going to Seoul.)

明年三月 — year first, then month. Never 三月明年.

Fill: ___ 我们一起吃饭，好吗？
(Shall we eat together on Saturday evening next week?)

下个星期六晚上 — week → day → part of day. English says it backwards.

5. Sequencing a story …以后 + 就 · …的时候

When a speaker reaches for this: to lay two events out in order — one after the other, or one during the other. Chinese has no tense endings to do this work, so the order of the clauses and a small marker at the join carry the whole story.

Principle of Temporal Sequence

Chinese clauses come in the order the events happened. So the "after" clause is always written first, and the result follows: 下课以后 …then… 我就去跳舞. The adverb 就 sits right before the second verb and means "and then, right away" — you already met it in Lesson 2 as V₁了…就 V₂.

Event 1 + 以后 , S + 就 + Event 2

下课以后我就去跳舞。

Xià kè yǐhòu wǒ jiù qù tiào wǔ.

As soon as class finishes, off I go to dance.

辞职以后我想当老板。

Cízhí yǐhòu wǒ xiǎng dāng lǎobǎn.

resign / after / I / want / be / boss

After I quit, I want to be my own boss.

…的时候 — "when / while"

Where 以后 puts event 2 after event 1, 的时候 puts it inside event 1. Same postposition habit: the clause comes first, the marker last.

以后 — one then the other
吃饭以后我们聊天儿
We chat after the meal — first eat, then talk.
的时候 — at the same time
吃饭的时候我们聊天儿
We chat while we eat — both at once.

跳舞的时候，我的压力就没有了。

Tiào wǔ de shíhou, wǒ de yālì jiù méiyǒu le.

dance / when / my / pressure / then / not-have / LE

When I'm dancing, my stress just disappears.

我小的时候，妈妈是老师。

Wǒ xiǎo de shíhou, māma shì lǎoshī.

When I was little, my mother was a teacher.

Practice

Fill: 下课以后我 ___ 去跳舞。
(As soon as class ends I go off to dance.)

就 — right before the second verb: "and then, immediately".

Which says "While we're eating, we chat"?

  • 吃饭的时候我们聊天儿。✓ 的时候 = during; both things happen together.
  • 吃饭以后我们聊天儿。✗ That's "after the meal", one thing then the other.
  • 我们聊天儿吃饭的时候。✗ The time clause must come first, not trail at the end.

What does this mean?
辞职以后我想当老板。

  • Before I quit, I want to be a boss.✗ That would need 辞职以前.
  • After I quit, I want to be my own boss.✓ Event 1 + 以后, then the plan.
  • I quit because I'm a boss.✗ No 因为 here — 以后 is purely about order in time.

Pick the time expression for: "For the past two months I haven't practised Chinese."

  • 最近两个月我没练习汉语。✓ A span reaching up to now — and it can't be misread as a point.
  • 两个月前我没练习汉语。✗ That fixes one moment two months back, not the whole stretch.

What does this mean?
后天下午三点我在公司。

  • The day before yesterday at 3 p.m. I was at the office.✗ 后天 is forward, not back — 前天 is back.
  • At 3 p.m. the day after tomorrow I'll be at the office.✓ 后天 → 下午 → 三点: big to small.
  • Every afternoon at 3 I'm at the office.✗ That would need 每天下午.

One of these is ungrammatical. Which is the good sentence?

  • 我搬到纽约去年。✗ Time at the end — the commonest English-speaker error.
  • 去年我搬到纽约了。✓ Time fronted, verb after it.
  • 我在去年搬到纽约了。✗ Calendar words don't take 在.
