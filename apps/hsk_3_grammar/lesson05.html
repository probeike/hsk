What you'll learn

  1. V 得/不 + complement — the "can/can't + achieve result" pattern.
  2. N + 呢? — a super-short way to ask where is X?.
  3. 刚 vs 刚才 — two words for "just now", used differently.
  4. 越来越 + Adj / mental verb — "more and more…" from the lesson title.

1. V 得/不 + complement Can you succeed at that verb?

When a speaker reaches for this: to say whether an action can successfully produce its result. Not "do I know how to do it" (会) or "do I have the right to do it" (能) — but "if I try, will it work out?" English: can I make it out?, can you see clearly?, can you hear?

The English trap

English uses can for many senses. Chinese carves them out: 会 (know how), 能 (allowed / able in circumstance), and this structure (will the attempt succeed?). The marker 得 (positive) or 不 (negative) sits inside the verb+complement: 看得懂 "can read/understand it" / 看不懂 "can't read/understand it".

V + 得 / 不 + Complement (result / direction / adj)

我看得懂那个汉字。

Wǒ kàn de dǒng nàge Hànzì.

I / look / CAN / understand / that character

I can read (understand) that character.

我找不到好饭馆。

Wǒ zhǎo bu dào hǎo fànguǎn.

I / look-for / CAN'T / reach / good restaurant

I can't find a good restaurant (despite trying).

老师说的话你听得见听不见？

Lǎoshī shuō de huà nǐ tīng de jiàn tīng bu jiàn?

Can you hear what the teacher is saying? (The "A-not-A" question form: V得C V不C?)

会 — skill
我会游泳
I know how to swim (I was taught).
V得/不+C — success
我游得过去
I can make it across (I'll succeed if I try).

Decision tree: which "can"?

Ask this in order

  1. Is it a learned skill? Use 会: 我会游泳 = I know how to swim.
  2. Is it permission, time, physical condition, or circumstance? Use 能: 今天我不能去 = I can't go today.
  3. Is it whether this attempt reaches its result? Use V得/不+C: 听得懂, 找不到, 写得完.

Practice

Fill: 你说话说得太快了，我 ___ 。
(You're speaking too fast — I can't follow.)

听不懂 — "listen-can't-understand".

Fill: 这本书的汉字不难，我 ___ 。
(The characters in this book aren't hard — I can read them.)

看得懂 — "look-can-understand".

Fill: 我的手机 ___ 了，你看见了吗？
(I can't find my phone — have you seen it?)

找不到 — "look-can't-reach" = can't find.

Which expresses "I can finish the homework before 7"?

  • 7点以前写得完。✓ V得完 = can finish (success complement).
  • 7点以前会写完。✗ 会 is "know how" — not what we want here.
  • 7点以前写完不。✗ 不 goes between the verb and complement: 写不完.

What does this mean?
饭太多了，我吃不完。

  • I don't know how to eat.✗ That would be 我不会吃.
  • There's too much food — I can't finish it.✓ 吃不完 = can't finish (even if I try).
  • I'm not allowed to eat.✗ That would use 不能.

2. N + 呢? A two-character shortcut: "where's X?"

When a speaker reaches for this: to ask where something or someone is, without the full sentence. English equivalents: where's Mom?, and the dog?. It's casual and efficient.

The English trap

You might want to say the full 妈妈在哪儿？ — which is fine — but natives often drop it to 妈妈呢？ in context. Same meaning, far more natural conversationally.

Noun + 呢 ?

A: 你的书呢? 放在哪儿了？
B: 就在桌子上啊。

A: Nǐ de shū ne? Fàng zài nǎr le? / B: Jiù zài zhuōzi shang a.

A: Where's your book? Where did you put it? / B: Right on the table.

A: 我的手机呢? 你看见了吗？
B: 我没看见啊。

A: Wǒ de shǒujī ne? Nǐ kànjiàn le ma? / B: Wǒ méi kànjiàn a.

A: Where's my phone? Have you seen it? / B: I haven't.

Practice

Complete: A: ___？ B: 在我包里。
(Where's your phone? — In my bag.)

Complete: A: 妈妈在厨房做饭， ___？ B: 他还没回来。
(Mom's cooking — and Dad? — He's not back yet.)

爸爸呢 — shortcut for "where's Dad?".

Which is the most natural casual way to ask "Where's the dog?"

  • 小狗呢？✓ Noun + 呢 is the casual shortcut.
  • 小狗吗？✗ 吗 turns it into yes/no, not "where".
  • 哪儿小狗？✗ Wrong word order.

What does this mean?
你的裙子呢？

  • Is this your skirt?✗ That would use 吗.
  • Where's your skirt?✓ Noun + 呢 = "Where's X?" in context.
  • Do you like the skirt?✗ Completely different meaning.

Build: "Where's my book?"

我 的 书 呢

3. 刚 vs 刚才 Two "just nows" — one's an adverb, one's a noun

When a speaker reaches for this: to say something happened recently. But "recently" is flexible — 刚才 means literally a few minutes ago, while 刚 can mean anything from minutes to months, depending on the speaker's sense of "short".

The English trap

English smashes both senses into just. Chinese separates them:

  • 刚 (adverb) — sits only right before a verb. "I've only been in China for 2 months" = 我刚来中国两个月.
  • 刚才 (noun) — literally "just-now moment", can sit before the verb or at the start of the sentence. "A moment ago you were doing homework" = 刚才你在做作业.
刚 — subjective "a short time"
我刚来中国两个月
"I've only been in China two months" — the speaker feels two months is short.
刚才 — literally a few minutes ago
刚才你在做作业
"A moment ago you were doing homework" — strictly recent.

我刚做完作业，真累啊！

Wǒ gāng zuò wán zuòyè, zhēn lèi a!

I've just finished the homework — I'm exhausted!

刚才你去哪儿了？

Gāngcái nǐ qù nǎr le?

Where did you go a moment ago?

Common pitfall

Don't put 刚 at the start of a sentence — *刚你去哪儿了 (✗) is wrong. It must attach right before a verb. If you want to front-load the time, use 刚才.

Practice

Pick the correct word: ___ 我做完作业，真累啊！

  • 刚✗ 刚 must come directly before the verb, not at the start of the sentence.
  • 刚才✓ 刚才 can begin a sentence; 刚 cannot.

Fill with 刚 or 刚才: 他 ___ 到北京，还没休息呢。

刚 — sits right before the verb 到 (to arrive); could be minutes or hours, felt as recent.

Fill with 刚 or 刚才: ___ 你在做什么？我叫你，你没听见。
(What were you doing a moment ago? I called you and you didn't hear.)

刚才 — fronted time word; strictly "a few minutes ago".

Fill with 刚 or 刚才: 我 ___ 来中国两个月，汉语还不太好。
(I've only been in China two months — my Chinese isn't great yet.)

刚 — the speaker feels two months is short; 刚 attaches directly before the verb 来.

What does this mean?
刚才我看见小丽了。

  • I always see Xiaoli.✗ 刚才 is specifically "a moment ago".
  • I saw Xiaoli a moment ago.✓ 刚才 = a few minutes ago (fronted).
  • I just started to see Xiaoli.✗ Not the meaning.

4. 越来越 + Adj / mental verb "More and more…" — the title grammar

When a speaker reaches for this: to describe a trend that keeps increasing over time. The lesson title 我最近越来越胖了 does not mean "I am very fat"; it means the degree is moving upward lately.

The English trap

English often says "getting more and more..." with a separate verb like getting. Chinese puts the trend marker directly before the adjective or mental verb: 越来越胖, 越来越喜欢. Do not add 很 after it: *越来越很胖 is wrong.

Subject / topic + 越来越 + Adj / mental verb + 了

我最近越来越胖了。

Wǒ zuìjìn yuè lái yuè pàng le.

I / lately / more-and-more / fat / LE

I've been getting fatter and fatter lately.

我越来越喜欢夏天了。

Wǒ yuè lái yuè xǐhuan xiàtiān le.

I / more-and-more / like / summer / LE

I like summer more and more now.

Why final 了 often appears

越来越 already says the degree is changing. Sentence-final 了 fits naturally because the speaker is noticing a new trend: before it was less true, now it is more true.

Mixed practice All four structures together

Fill: 我最近 ___ 胖了，应该少吃点儿。
(I've been getting fatter lately — I should eat less.)

越来越 — "more and more" before an adjective.

Which sentence is grammatical?

  • 刚你去哪儿了？✗ 刚 cannot start a sentence; use 刚才.
  • 刚才你去哪儿了？✓ 刚才 can sit at the start of a sentence.
  • 你刚才去哪儿？✗ Missing 了 for past event — awkward without it in this context.

What does this mean?
春天来了，天气越来越暖和了。

  • Spring's coming — the weather is cold.✗ 暖和 means warm.
  • Spring has come — the weather is getting warmer and warmer.✓ 越来越 + Adj = increasing change.
  • Spring will come — the weather might get warmer.✗ 了 here marks a change already happening.

Build: "Lately I like summer more and more."

我 最近 越来越 喜欢 夏天 了
