## Grammar introduced

### 1. 才 vs 就 — "Only then" vs "already" — the speaker's feelings about timing

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to express an *attitude* about how fast or slow something happened. 就 = "earlier/faster than expected" (already). 才 = "later/slower than expected" (only then).
 

### The English trap

English uses *already* vs *only*, but lazily: "I only got home at 9pm" vs "I was already home by 9pm". Chinese marks this tiny attitude shift directly — and misusing them flips the whole feeling of your sentence.

就 — "sooner than expected"

他七点就来了

He already arrived at 7 (impressively early).

才 — "later than expected"

他九点才来

He didn't come until 9 (annoyingly late).

我六点就起床了。

Wǒ liù diǎn jiù qǐ chuáng le.

I was already up at 6 (early for me).

我九点才起床。

Wǒ jiǔ diǎn cái qǐ chuáng.

I didn't get up until 9 (later than I usually do).

Notice: sentences with 就 usually end with 了 (completed early); sentences with 才 typically *don't* use 了 (the speaker is still "complaining" about the lateness).

### Practice

- Speaker thinks 6 o'clock is early.✗ For that you'd use 就: 他六点就回来了.

- Speaker has no feeling about the time.✗ 才/就 always carry an attitude about timing.

### The OTHER 就 — conditional / sequential "then"

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to say "once X happens, Y follows immediately" — "as soon as he gets here, tell me", "if it rains, let's not go", "finish this and then we'll eat". Same character, completely different meaning from the timing-attitude 就 above.
 

### The English trap

The 就 you just learned means "earlier than expected" — an attitude about clock time. The 就 in 等他到了就告诉我 has nothing to do with expectations. It links two clauses: *[condition / first event]* → 就 → *[immediate consequence]*. English translates it as *"then"* or *"as soon as"*.

Timing 就 (earlier than expected)

He was already up at 6 — impressively early.

Sequential 就 (once X, then Y)

他起床了就吃早饭。

As soon as he gets up, he has breakfast.

### How to tell them apart

Look at what comes before 就. A *time expression* (六点, 昨天, 五分钟) → timing 就. A *clause describing an event or condition* (等他到了, 写完作业) → sequential 就. The sequential version often pairs with 如果…, 只要…, or a bare event clause with a completion marker.

### Practice

What does 就 mean here? 他一回家就睡觉。

- He already sleeps by the time he gets home — impressively early.✗ "一…就…" is the sequential pattern, not timing.

- As soon as he gets home, he goes to sleep.✓ 一…就… = "once X happens, Y immediately follows".

- He only sleeps at home.✗ No "only" sense here; 就 links two events.

### 2. 把字句 2: A 把 B V + 在 / 到 … — Disposal, with a destination

**When a speaker reaches for this:** to say they put/sent/moved the object *to a place*. The "where does B end up" version of the 把 frame.
 

### The English trap

English: "put the book **on the table**" — place after the verb. Chinese demands 把 here: 把书**放在桌子上**. You cannot write **放书在桌子上* (✗). When the verb means placing or moving something *to* a specific location, 把 is obligatory.

请把书放在桌子上。

Qǐng bǎ shū fàng zài zhuōzi shang.

Please put the book on the table.

我把行李送到机场了。

Wǒ bǎ xíngli sòng dào jīchǎng le.

I / [disposal] / luggage / deliver / to / airport / PERF

I took the luggage to the airport.

他把钱放在包里了。

Tā bǎ qián fàng zài bāo lǐ le.

He put the money in his bag.

### Common pitfall

You can't split 在 / 到 from the verb. Always *verb + 在/到 + place*, inside the 把 frame. Writing 在 *before* the verb is the English-speaker instinct and always wrong here.
