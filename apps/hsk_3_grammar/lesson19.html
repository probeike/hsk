What you'll learn

  1. 被字句 — the passive. The second big word-order rewire of HSK 3, after 把. Extra care required for English speakers.
  2. 只有 … 才 … — "only if … then …", the strict-condition counterpart to 只要…就…

1. 被字句 — The passive construction "B was V-ed (by A)"

When a speaker reaches for this: when the speaker wants to foreground what happened to B — especially something done to B that B didn't choose. Historically 被 carried an adversative flavour (something bad); modern Chinese has softened this, but the "something happened to a patient" frame remains its core.

The English trap — the First Noun Principle

Your English brain reads first noun = doer. That instinct is wrong in 被-sentences. In English passive, "was" / "by" signal the flip: "The mouse was eaten by the cat." In Chinese, the flip lands on one small word — 被. If you skip past 被, you'll read the sentence completely backwards.

Train yourself to stop at 被 and re-assign roles: the noun before 被 is the patient (done-to), the noun after 被 is the agent (doer) — or, if no agent is given, it's omitted.

鱼被猫吃完了 — "The fish was eaten up by the cat." First noun = patient; 被 flips roles.
B (patient) + 被 + A (agent, optional) + V + result / 了

Passive role checklist

  1. Stop at 被. Do not trust "first noun = doer".
  2. Name the noun before 被: this is B, the patient, the thing affected.
  3. Name the noun after 被, if there is one: this is A, the agent, the doer.
  4. Check the verb has a result, complement, or 了: 拿走了, 吃完了, 打破了.
  5. Translate last: "B was V-ed by A." If A is omitted, say "by someone / unspecified".

我的手机被小弟拿走了。

Wǒ de shǒujī bèi xiǎodì ná zǒu le.

my / phone / [passive] / little-brother / take / away / PERF

My phone was taken (away) by my little brother.

蛋糕被吃完了。

Dàngāo bèi chī wán le.

cake / [passive] / eat / finish / PERF

The cake was all eaten. (Agent omitted — unknown or unimportant.)

他被老师叫到办公室去了。

Tā bèi lǎoshī jiào dào bàngōngshì qu le.

He was called to the office by the teacher.

被 vs 把 — mirror images

They describe the same event from opposite angles:

  • 猫把鱼吃完了 — active: cat (agent first) → fish → done. Focus on the doer.
  • 鱼被猫吃完了 — passive: fish (patient first) → cat → done. Focus on what happened to the fish.

Both require a result (了, complement, place). Neither works with bare stative verbs.

Convert 把 to 被 using the same event

  1. Start with the event: 弟弟把手机拿走了 — little brother took the phone away.
  2. Identify A and B: A = 弟弟 (doer), B = 手机 (thing affected).
  3. For 把, keep A first: 弟弟把手机拿走了.
  4. For 被, move B first and put A after 被: 手机被弟弟拿走了.
  5. Keep the result the same: 拿走了 stays 拿走了. Only the foregrounded role changes.

被 needs a result, too

*他被看 (✗) alone is incomplete. Always pair 被 with some result or complement: 他被看见了 (✓ seen), 他被人打了 (✓ got hit), 衣服被洗好了 (✓ got washed). Same rule as 把.

Negation and questions

Negation and modals go before 被, not between 被 and the verb:

我的电脑没被拿走。

Wǒ de diànnǎo méi bèi ná zǒu.

My computer wasn't taken. (没 before 被, not *被没.)

Who did what to whom in: 小狗被他带回家了?

  • The puppy brought him home.✗ First-noun trap! 被 flips the roles — the puppy is the patient, not the agent.
  • He brought the puppy home.✓ B + 被 + A + V: puppy (B, patient), he (A, agent), bring-back-home.
  • The puppy and he went home together.✗ 被 is not a connector; it marks the passive flip.

In 杯子被拿走了 (no agent given), what happened?

  • The cup took something away.✗ First-noun trap again. Stop at 被.
  • The cup was taken away (by someone unspecified).✓ No agent after 被 is fine — the action happened to the cup.

Pick the correct passive for "He was called by the teacher":

  • 他被老师叫了。✓ B + 被 + A + V + 了.
  • 他叫被老师了。✗ 被 must come before the agent, not between the patient and the verb wrongly.
  • 老师被他叫了。✗ This says "the teacher was called by him" — roles reversed.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about things of yours that somebody else did something to — at home, at work, on the subway, wherever. Use B + 被 (+ A) + Verb + result / 了 at least three times with three different verbs, leave the doer out of one sentence (被 straight onto the verb), and make one sentence negative with 没被. Every sentence has to be true for you.

我的东西常常被别人拿走。上个星期我的伞被同事带到办公室去了，第二天他才还给我。昨天我放在冰箱里的牛奶被喝完了，我到现在也不知道是谁喝的。我的自行车一直放在外面，可是还没被人骑走。

Wǒ de dōngxi chángcháng bèi biéren ná zǒu. Shàng ge xīngqī wǒ de sǎn bèi tóngshì dài dào bàngōngshì qu le, dì-èr tiān tā cái huán gěi wǒ. Zuótiān wǒ fàng zài bīngxiāng li de niúnǎi bèi hē wán le, wǒ dào xiànzài yě bù zhīdào shì shéi hē de. Wǒ de zìxíngchē yìzhí fàng zài wàimiàn, kěshì hái méi bèi rén qí zǒu.

My things are forever getting taken by other people. Last week my umbrella was carried off to the office by a colleague, and he only gave it back the next day. Yesterday the milk I'd put in the fridge got drunk up — I still don't know who drank it. My bike has been standing outside all this time, but nobody has ridden off with it yet.

Why these are right: every sentence opens with the thing affected (伞 · 牛奶 · 自行车), never the doer — that is what 被 buys you. Each verb carries a result (拿走 · 带到…去了 · 喝完 · 骑走), because 被 cannot sit on a bare verb. The milk sentence drops the agent completely (被喝完了), which is normal when you don't know or don't care who did it. The last sentence is the negative half: 没 stands in front of 被, and the 了 disappears along with it.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the noun in front of 被 really the thing done to? If it is the doer, you want a plain sentence or 把 — not 被.
  • Does every verb carry a result or 了? 被喝完了 ✓ · 被喝 ✗
  • Negation goes before 被, and 没 kills the 了: 还没被人骑走 ✓ · 被没骑走了 ✗
  • Three different verbs after 被 — repeating one verb three times drills nothing.
  • Did one sentence leave the doer out, with 被 landing straight on the verb?

2. 只有 … 才 … "Only if … then (and only then) …"

When a speaker reaches for this: to mark a necessary condition — without X, Y will not happen. Contrast with 只要…就… (L17), which marks a sufficient condition.

The English trap

English blurs the two: "only if", "as long as", "only when" all sound similar. Chinese keeps them sharply separate through the pivot word:

  • 只要 … 就 … → X is enough.
  • 只有 … 才 … → X is required. Without X, no Y.

The 才 is doing the work you met in Lesson 11 ("only then / not until"). It restricts.

只有 + condition , Subj + 才 + result

只有努力，才能学好。

Zhǐyǒu nǔlì, cái néng xué hǎo.

only-have / work-hard, / only-then / able / learn-well

Only by working hard can you learn well.

只有你愿意帮忙，这件事才能做完。

Zhǐyǒu nǐ yuànyì bāngmáng, zhè jiàn shì cái néng zuò wán.

Only if you're willing to help can this job get done.

只要 … 就 … (sufficient)
只要你来，我就高兴
Your coming is enough; I'll be happy.
只有 … 才 … (necessary)
只有你来，我才高兴
I'm only happy if you come — no one else will do.

Before the drills: enough or required?

  1. If X is enough to make Y happen, choose 只要…就: 只要你来，我就高兴.
  2. If X is required and without X there is no Y, choose 只有…才: 只有你来，我才高兴.
  3. Use the pivot to check yourself: 就 opens the result after an enough-condition; 才 restricts the result to "only then".

In 只有考得好，才能上好大学, what's being claimed?

  • A good exam score is enough to get in.✗ That's 只要…就…
  • A good exam score is required to get in.✓ 只有…才… = without it, no good university.

Build: "Only by listening a lot can you speak well."

只有 你 帮忙 这件事 才能 做 完

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about conditions that are genuinely required in your own life — the thing without which the result simply does not happen. Use 只有 … 才 … in every one, with three different verbs in the result half, and keep the subject of the result clause in front of the pivot word. Then add one last sentence with 只要 … 就 … for something that is merely enough — and be honest about which of your conditions is which.

只有把手机放到别的房间，我才能认真工作。只有周末不下雨，我才会出去走走。只有大家都到了，我们才开始吃饭。只要有一杯热咖啡，我就觉得这一天不错。

Zhǐyǒu bǎ shǒujī fàng dào biéde fángjiān, wǒ cái néng rènzhēn gōngzuò. Zhǐyǒu zhōumò bú xià yǔ, wǒ cái huì chūqu zǒuzou. Zhǐyǒu dàjiā dōu dào le, wǒmen cái kāishǐ chī fàn. Zhǐyào yǒu yì bēi rè kāfēi, wǒ jiù juéde zhè yì tiān búcuò.

Only if I put my phone in another room can I work properly. Only if it doesn't rain at the weekend will I go out for a walk. Only once everyone has arrived do we start eating. As long as I have a hot coffee, I feel the day is going fine.

Why these are right: 只有 opens the condition and 才 sits inside the result clause, immediately before its verb or modal — 我才能认真工作, never 才我能认真工作. Each claim is honestly a necessary one: take the condition away and the result is gone with it. The final sentence switches to 只要…就… on purpose: one hot coffee is enough to make the day feel fine, and nobody is claiming it is the only thing that could.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is the pivot after the subject of the result clause? 我们才开始吃饭 ✓ · 才我们开始吃饭 ✗
  • Did 只有 pull in 才 and not 就? Mixing the pairs (只有…就…, 只要…才…) is the standard slip.
  • Don't hang 了 on the end of the result clause: 我才开始工作 ✓ · 我才开始工作了 ✗ — 才 restricts, it doesn't report completion.
  • Test each sentence: would it still be true as 只要…就…? If yes, your condition is only enough, not required — say so honestly.
  • Three different verbs in the result halves, and every sentence true of your own week.

Write the paragraph. An autumn weekend away with 小丽: a small 宾馆, and a morning out on a 船. Tell it in 4–6 connected sentences, using both structures of this lesson at least once each: 被 (with a result on the verb) and 只有 … 才 …. Cover what you left in the room, what had happened to it by the time you got back, what the hotel staff said, and how it ended.

去年秋天我和小丽去了一个很小的城市，住在一个很老的宾馆里。第二天早上我们坐船出去玩，把伞和照相机都放在房间里了。中午回来的时候，我的伞被人拿走了，照相机还在桌子上。服务员说，只有找到那位客人，这个问题才能解决。晚上那位客人自己把伞送回来了，还跟我们说了对不起。

Qùnián qiūtiān wǒ hé Xiǎolì qù le yí ge hěn xiǎo de chéngshì, zhù zài yí ge hěn lǎo de bīnguǎn li. Dì-èr tiān zǎoshang wǒmen zuò chuán chūqu wán, bǎ sǎn hé zhàoxiàngjī dōu fàng zài fángjiān li le. Zhōngwǔ huílai de shíhou, wǒ de sǎn bèi rén ná zǒu le, zhàoxiàngjī hái zài zhuōzi shang. Fúwùyuán shuō, zhǐyǒu zhǎodào nà wèi kèren, zhège wèntí cái néng jiějué. Wǎnshang nà wèi kèren zìjǐ bǎ sǎn sòng huílai le, hái gēn wǒmen shuō le duìbuqǐ.

Last autumn Xiaoli and I went to a very small city and stayed in an old hotel. The next morning we took a boat out, leaving the umbrella and the camera in the room. When we got back at midday my umbrella had been taken by somebody; the camera was still on the table. The receptionist said that only if they found that guest could the problem be sorted out. In the evening the guest brought the umbrella back himself, and apologised to us as well.

Why this works as a paragraph: the two structures are doing two different jobs. 我的伞被人拿走了 leads with the thing affected because that is the news — and 拿走 supplies the result 被 insists on, with the doer left unnamed since nobody knows who it was. 只有找到那位客人，这个问题才能解决 is a genuine necessary condition: no guest, no solution, and 才 sits after 这个问题, restricting the result. 把伞送回来了 in the last line shows the same event from the doer's side — 把 and 被 are one pair of glasses turned around. The time words (第二天早上 · 中午 · 晚上) are what make it a story instead of a list.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Does each 被-sentence open with the thing done to, and does its verb carry a result? 被拿走了 ✓ · 被拿 ✗
  • Is 才 inside the result clause, after its subject? 这个问题才能解决 ✓ · 才这个问题能解决 ✗
  • If you negated a passive, is 没 in front of 被 — and did the 了 go?
  • Is your 只有…才… claim really a necessary condition, or would 只要…就… have been the honest choice?
  • Does it read as one weekend — complication, reaction, ending — rather than two grammar demos side by side?

1. 我的自行车被邻居用了。
2. 我的词典没被人借走。
3. 只有妈妈同意，我才可以去。
4. 只有天气好，我们才去公园。

1. Wǒ de zìxíngchē bèi línjū yòng le. 2. Wǒ de cídiǎn méi bèi rén jiè zǒu. 3. Zhǐyǒu māma tóngyì, wǒ cái kěyǐ qù. 4. Zhǐyǒu tiānqì hǎo, wǒmen cái qù gōngyuán.

1. My bike was used by a neighbour. 2. My dictionary hasn't been borrowed by anybody. 3. Only if my mother agrees can I go. 4. Only if the weather is good do we go to the park.

  1. 被邻居用 → 被邻居用了. 被 will not sit on a bare verb: the passive reports what the thing ended up like, so the verb needs at least 了, or else a complement. 了 is the smallest repair and the one to reach for; 被邻居骑走了 is equally correct and says more — he rode off on it. Same requirement you already know from 把.
  2. 被没人借走 → 没被人借走. Negation and modals stand in front of 被, never between 被 and its verb. And once 没 is there the sentence takes no 了.
  3. 只有…就 → 只有…才. 只有 marks a condition you cannot do without, and only 才 answers it. 就 would quietly downgrade the claim to "mum's agreement is enough" — that sentence would need 只要.
  4. 才我们去 → 我们才去. 才 is an adverb living inside the result clause: subject first, then 才, then the verb. Fronting it copies the English "only then we go" word order.

Check your own rewrites:

  • Does every 被 verb carry a result or 了?
  • Is 没 (or 不) in front of 被 — and did the 了 disappear with it?
  • 只有 answered by 才, 只要 answered by 就. Did you check the pair, not the feel?
  • Is 才 after the subject of the result clause, immediately before the verb or modal?

  • Answer 3: 才 after the subject, and is the claim honestly "only then" rather than merely "usually then"?
  • Are all three answers actually true about your own week?
