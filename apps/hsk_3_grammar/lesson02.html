What you'll learn

  1. Simple directional complements (V + 来 / 去) — direction encoded from the speaker's point of view.
  2. V₁了 … 就 V₂ … — one action immediately triggers the next.
  3. Rhetorical 能 … 吗? / 不 … 吗? — questions that aren't questions.

1. V + 来 / 去 Simple directional complements — motion, relative to the speaker

When a speaker reaches for this: to say something moves, and to make clear which way relative to them — towards themselves (来) or away (去). Common stems: 上, 下, 进, 出, 回, 过, 起 — plus learned verbs 买, 带, 搬.

The English trap

English says "come up" or "go up" with different verbs, but uses up the same way either way. Chinese folds speaker-orientation into the complement: 上来 vs 上去. Ask yourself where is the speaker standing? — 来 if motion is towards them, 去 if away. You cannot pick 来/去 by guessing; the physical setup decides.

Why this order? (PTS)

The main verb names the motion, then 来/去 tells you which way the motion ended up. Action, then destination — literally in the order it unfolds.

V + 来 / 去

我们在楼上等你呢，你上来吧。

Wǒmen zài lóushàng děng nǐ ne, nǐ shànglai ba.

we / upstairs / waiting for you, / you / come-up

We're waiting for you upstairs — come on up. (Speaker is upstairs, so motion is towards them → 来.)

谁在外边？你出去看看吧。

Shéi zài wàibian? Nǐ chūqu kànkan ba.

who / outside? / you / go-out / take-a-look

Who's outside? Go out and have a look. (Speaker is inside, so motion is away → 去.)

Place objects go before 来/去

If the object is a place (school, upstairs, the classroom), it slots between the verb and 来/去:

老师进教室来了。

Lǎoshī jìn jiàoshì lai le.

teacher / enter / classroom / come / PERF

The teacher came into the classroom. (Speaker is in the classroom.)

If the object is a thing, it can go on either side:

明天要带作业来。 / 明天要带来作业。

Míngtiān yào dài zuòyè lai.

(You) need to bring your homework tomorrow.

Guided walkthrough: choosing 来 or 去

Think in space

Before you answer, place the speaker in the scene. Then name the motion. If the motion ends nearer to the speaker, use 来. If it moves away from the speaker's current place, use 去. If a place is named, put that place before 来/去.

speaker upstairs
你上来吧
You are coming up to where I am.
speaker downstairs
你上去吧
You are going up away from where I am.
speaker in classroom
老师进教室来了
The teacher entered toward the speaker's location.
speaker outside classroom
老师进教室去了
The teacher entered away from the speaker's location.

Practice

You're upstairs, on the phone with a friend downstairs. You want them to come up. Pick:

  • 你上来吧。✓ Motion towards speaker (up to where I am).
  • 你上去吧。✗ That's "go up" — used if the speaker is on the ground floor sending someone else up.
  • 你来上吧。✗ Word order is reversed — the main verb (上) comes first.

Build: "Tomorrow I'll bring (my) homework." (Speaker is the teacher in class.)

A place-object ("upstairs", "classroom") must slot where?

  • Between the verb and 来/去 (e.g. 上楼来).✓ Place objects always sit before 来/去.
  • After 来/去 (e.g. 上来楼).✗ That's ungrammatical — place objects must precede the directional.
  • Either side, freely.✗ Only thing-objects have that flexibility; places are fixed.

Fill the blank: 门没关，你 ___ 吧。
(The door's open — come in. Speaker is inside.)

Use V + 来 / 去 at least three times with three different main verbs (走 · 带 · 搬 · 拿 · 回 …), and in at least one sentence put a place word between the verb-plus-direction and the final 来/去. Before each sentence, decide where you are standing in that scene — that decision, not the English wording, picks 来 or 去.

我住在十四楼，电梯很慢，朋友来的时候我常常说：“你走上来吧，别等电梯了。”每天早上我带电脑出去，晚上又带回来。上个星期新邻居搬进楼里来了，我帮她拿了很多东西上楼去。周末妹妹要从上海回家来看我们。

Wǒ zhù zài shísì lóu, diàntī hěn màn, péngyou lái de shíhou wǒ chángcháng shuō: "Nǐ zǒu shànglai ba, bié děng diàntī le." Měi tiān zǎoshang wǒ dài diànnǎo chūqu, wǎnshang yòu dài huílai. Shàng ge xīngqī xīn línjū bān jìn lóu li lái le, wǒ bāng tā ná le hěn duō dōngxi shàng lóu qù. Zhōumò mèimei yào cóng Shànghǎi huí jiā lái kàn wǒmen.

I live on the fourteenth floor and the lift is slow, so when friends come over I usually say, "Just walk up — don't wait for the lift." Every morning I take my laptop out, and in the evening I bring it back. Last week a new neighbour moved into the building, and I helped her carry a lot of things up. At the weekend my sister is coming home from Shanghai to see us.

Why these are right: the narrator's position moves, and 来/去 moves with it. 走上来 and 搬进楼里来 are said from inside the flat, so the motion arrives at the speaker; 带电脑出去 and 拿…上楼去 are said from where the speaker is at that moment (at home, then downstairs helping), so the motion leaves. 搬进楼里来 and 拿…上楼去 show the place word sitting inside the pair, in front of the final 来/去, while 电脑 is a thing, so it may either stand in front of the whole pair or sit inside it (带出电脑去).

Check your own sentences:

  • Did you place the speaker before choosing? 来 = the motion ends nearer you · 去 = it moves away from you.
  • Place word inside the pair: 搬进楼里来 ✓ · 搬进来楼里 ✗
  • A thing-object may stand in front of the pair or inside it (带电脑出去 / 带出电脑去) — a place word only ever goes inside.
  • Three different main verbs (走 · 带 · 搬 · 拿 …), not the same one three times.
  • Is every sentence true of your own building and week, not a textbook one?

2. V₁了 … 就 V₂ … Action A finishes, then immediately action B

When a speaker reaches for this: two actions happen back-to-back, with the second triggered by the first. English often uses "as soon as…" or "once I've finished…, I'll…". Chinese does it compactly: V₁了 … 就 V₂.

Why this order? (PTS)

Action one, then action two — same order they actually happen. The 了 closes off the first action; 就 then launches the second immediately.

Subj + V₁ + 了 …+ 就 + V₂

我下了课就吃饭。

Wǒ xià le kè jiù chī fàn.

I / finish-PERF / class / then-immediately / eat / meal

As soon as I finish class, I'll eat.

妈妈起了床就做早饭。

Māma qǐ le chuáng jiù zuò zǎofàn.

mom / get-PERF / bed / then-immediately / make / breakfast

Mom makes breakfast as soon as she gets up.

Two different subjects? Second subject hugs 就

If the two actions have different subjects, the second subject sits right before 就:

你下了课，我们就去书店。

Nǐ xià le kè, wǒmen jiù qù shūdiàn.

you / finish-PERF / class, / we / then / go / bookstore

As soon as you finish class, we'll go to the bookstore.

Finish the thought: 我太累了，___ 。
(I'm exhausted — as soon as I get home I'll sleep.)

e.g. 回家了就睡觉 (get home → sleep) or 吃了饭就睡觉.

Build: "As soon as he arrives at the office, I'll tell him."

Build: "As soon as I finish class, I'll go to the bookstore."

Two different subjects. Which is correct for "As soon as you finish class, we'll go eat."?

  • 你下了课我们就去吃饭。✓ Second subject 我们 hugs 就 directly.
  • 你下了课就我们去吃饭。✗ 就 must come right before V₂, after the second subject.
  • 下了课你我们就去吃饭。✗ Subject of V₁ (你) belongs at the start of its clause.

Fill: 妈妈 ___ 做早饭。
(Mom makes breakfast as soon as she gets up.)

起了床就 — action 1 (get up) finishes, 就 kicks off action 2 (make breakfast).

What does this imply?
他到了家就给我打电话。

  • He got home a while ago and then called me.✗ The pattern stresses immediacy, not a delay.
  • The moment he gets home, he (will) call me.✓ V₁了…就V₂ = action 2 follows action 1 immediately.
  • He called before getting home.✗ 了 closes action 1 first; action 2 follows.

Personalise it. Write 3–5 true sentences about the chains in your own day — what you do the moment something else finishes. Every sentence must use V₁了 … 就 V₂ …, with three different V₁ verbs across the set, and one sentence must have two different subjects (the second subject sits right in front of 就). Real routine, not an ideal one.

我每天起了床就先喝一杯水，然后看一会儿手机。到了公司我就开始写电子邮件，中午吃了饭就出去走一走。晚上小丽回来了，我们就一起去跳舞。周末我洗了衣服就去看我妈妈。

Wǒ měi tiān qǐ le chuáng jiù xiān hē yì bēi shuǐ, ránhòu kàn yíhuìr shǒujī. Dào le gōngsī wǒ jiù kāishǐ xiě diànzǐ yóujiàn, zhōngwǔ chī le fàn jiù chūqu zǒu yi zǒu. Wǎnshang Xiǎolì huílai le, wǒmen jiù yìqǐ qù tiàowǔ. Zhōumò wǒ xǐ le yīfu jiù qù kàn wǒ māma.

Every day I get up and have a glass of water first, then look at my phone for a bit. Once I reach the office I start writing emails; at midday, the moment I've eaten, I go out for a short walk. In the evening, as soon as Xiaoli gets back, we go dancing together. At the weekend, once I've done the laundry, I go and see my mum.

Why these are right: the 了 rides on V₁ and comes before V₁'s object — 起了床, 吃了饭, 洗了衣服. That 了 is not a past tense: every sentence above is habitual or still to come. 就 then stands immediately in front of V₂ with nothing wedged between them. In 晚上小丽回来了，我们就一起去跳舞 the subject changes, so the new subject 我们 comes first and 就 still hugs its own verb.

Check your own sentences:

  • Is 了 attached to V₁ and in front of its object? 吃了饭就… ✓ · 吃饭了就… ✗
  • Is 就 the last thing before V₂? Adverbs and subjects go in front of 就, never between 就 and the verb.
  • New subject in clause 2: 我们就一起去 ✓ · 就我们一起去 ✗
  • V₂ stays bare — no second 了 unless you really are reporting one finished occasion.
  • Three different V₁ verbs, and does each chain actually happen in your day?

3. Rhetorical questions: 能 … 吗? / 不 … 吗? Questions that mean the opposite

When a speaker reaches for this: they're making a point, not asking. English does the same thing ("Didn't I tell you?", "Can you really expect…?"). The form is a question, but it flips to deliver the answer. Positive question → negative meaning. Negative question → positive meaning.

The English trap

You're tempted to read these as real yes/no questions and answer them. They're not — they're assertions dressed as questions. Context and tone tell you it's rhetorical; your job is to hear the implied claim.

你不做作业，也不练习，能学好吗？

Nǐ bù zuò zuòyè, yě bù liànxí, néng xué hǎo ma?

you / not do homework / nor / practise / — can-you-learn-well?

You don't do homework or practise — how could you possibly learn it well? (= you can't.)

你每天晚上吃了饭就睡觉，能不胖吗？

Nǐ měi tiān wǎnshang chī le fàn jiù shuìjiào, néng bù pàng ma?

you / every night / eat-PERF / then sleep, / can-not-be-fat?

You eat dinner and go straight to bed every night — how could you not put on weight? (= of course you will.)

Decoding rule (keep this handy)

positive question
能…吗?
= negative assertion ("no, you can't")
negative question
能不…吗?
= positive assertion ("yes, of course")

Mini conversion table

Literal question Intended meaning
不练习，能学好吗？
Can you learn it well?		 You can't learn it well.
每天吃了饭就睡觉，能不胖吗？
Can you not get fat?		 Of course you'll put on weight.
每天都练习，能打不好吗？
Can you play badly?		 Of course you can play well.

Practice

What is the speaker actually claiming?
天气这么冷，你穿得这么少，能不生病吗？

  • You probably won't get sick.✗ Negative question 能不…吗? flips to positive.
  • You're definitely going to get sick.✓ 能不生病吗? = "how could you not get sick?" = you will.
  • The speaker is asking for information.✗ It's rhetorical — a claim, not a real question.

What is the speaker claiming?
你今天爬山爬得那么快，能不累吗？

  • You're obviously tired (no surprise).✓ 能不累吗 → "how could you not be tired" → you are.
  • Maybe you won't be tired.✗ Negative form + 吗 gives positive assertion.

A friend says 我不练习，也不复习，能学好吗？ What are they really claiming?

  • They definitely won't learn it well.✓ Positive 能…吗? → negative meaning.
  • They definitely will learn it well.✗ That would require 能不…吗?
  • They're genuinely asking.✗ It's rhetorical — an assertion disguised as a question.

Build: "You exercise every day — how could you not get thin?"

(You dress so lightly in this cold — how could you not get sick?)

Decode: 你每天都练习，能打不好吗？

  • You probably play badly.✗ Negative rhetorical flips to positive.
  • Of course you play well.✓ 能…不好吗? = "how could you play badly?" = you play well.
  • The speaker is unsure.✗ Rhetorical form → confident assertion.

Personalise it. Pick things in your own life you have a firm opinion about — your commute, your sleep, the weather where you live, how you study Chinese. Write 3–5 true sentences, each one built as reason clause first, then the fake question. Use 能 … 吗？ ("of course not") at least once and 能不 … 吗？ ("of course yes") at least once. Nothing you write should be a question you would actually want answered.

纽约的地铁早上总是这么慢，我上班能不迟到吗？我每天只睡五个小时，白天能不累吗？汉语我每天都写、都听，能学不好吗？可是我不常跟别人说汉语，能说得好吗？

Niǔyuē de dìtiě zǎoshang zǒngshì zhème màn, wǒ shàngbān néng bù chídào ma? Wǒ měi tiān zhǐ shuì wǔ ge xiǎoshí, báitiān néng bú lèi ma? Hànyǔ wǒ měi tiān dōu xiě, dōu tīng, néng xué bù hǎo ma? Kěshì wǒ bù cháng gēn biéren shuō Hànyǔ, néng shuō de hǎo ma?

The subway here is always this slow in the morning — how could I not be late for work? I only sleep five hours a night; how could I not be tired during the day? I write and listen to Chinese every single day — how could I not learn it well? But I hardly ever speak it with anyone, so can I really expect to speak it well?

Why these are right: each one hands over the evidence before it asks, which is what stops the listener treating it as a real question. 能不 + X + 吗 asserts X (of course I'm late, of course I'm tired); plain 能 + X + 吗 denies it (能说得好吗 = no, I can't); and 能学不好吗 is the same positive claim built with the negation inside the resultative instead — "how could I learn it badly?"

Check your own sentences:

  • Is there a reason clause in front? Without it the sentence collapses back into a genuine question.
  • Read each one back with the flipped meaning: 能不…吗 = "of course yes", 能…吗 = "of course no". Is that what you believe?
  • The 不 goes inside: 能不累吗 ✓ · 不能累吗 ✗ (that means "mustn't be tired").
  • With a result complement you can negate the result instead — 能学不好吗 — and it is still a positive claim.
  • Would you be irritated if someone replied 是 or 不是? If not, it isn't rhetorical yet.

Tell the evening in 4–6 connected sentences, using all three of this lesson's structures at least once each: V + 来 / 去, V₁了 … 就 V₂ …, and one rhetorical 能 … 吗？ or 能不 … 吗？. Xiǎolì jīntiān wǎnshang bā diǎn dào jīchǎng, wǒ shuō hǎo le qù jiē tā. Wǒ xià le bān jiù zuò dìtiě guòqu, lùshang huā le yí ge bàn xiǎoshí. Tā dài zhe hěn duō dōngxi zǒu chūlai, wǒ bāng tā ná le yíbàn. Wǒmen chū le jīchǎng jiù zuò shàng chē, kěshì lùshang chē tèbié duō, wǒ shuō: "Zhège shíjiān huí jiā, néng kuài ma?" Dào le jiā Xiǎolì jiù shuō è le, wǒmen jiù xià lóu qù chī diǎnr dōngxi.

Xiaoli was landing at eight tonight and I'd agreed to go and get her. As soon as I finished work I took the subway over; the trip took an hour and a half. She came out carrying a lot of stuff and I took half of it off her. The moment we were out of the airport we got in a car, but the traffic was awful — I said, "Heading home at this hour, how could it be quick?" The second we got home Xiaoli said she was hungry, so we went down to get something to eat.

Why this works as a paragraph: the three structures are doing three different jobs. The directionals track where the narrator is standing and the reference point moves — 过去 is said from the office, 走出来 from inside arrivals, 下楼去 from the flat upstairs; 下楼去 also shows the place word inside the pair. 下了班就 / 出了机场就 / 到了家…就 chain the evening in the order it happened, and the last of them switches subject (小丽就说饿了). The rhetorical 能快吗 is the complaint, not a request for a traffic report.

Check your own paragraph:

  • Does each 来/去 match where the narrator is standing at that moment? The direction is allowed to flip inside one paragraph — that is the whole point.
  • Place word between verb and directional: 下楼去 ✓ · 下去楼 ✗
  • 了 on V₁, in front of its object: 出了机场就… ✓ · 出机场了就… ✗
  • Where the subject changes, does it stand in front of 就? 到了家小丽就说 ✓ · 到了家就小丽说 ✗
  • Does it read as one evening — joined by time words and 可是 — rather than three grammar demos in a row?

1. Míngtiān shì wǒ mèimei de shēngrì, wǒ qù tā jiā wán. 2. Wǒmen kàn le diànyǐng jiù huí jiā. 3. Nǐ dào le jīchǎng wǒ jiù gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà. 4. Nǐ zǎoshang shénme dōu méi chī, néng bú è ma?

1. Tomorrow is my sister's birthday and I'm going over to her place. 2. As soon as we've seen the film we'll go home. 3. The moment you get to the airport I'll call you. 4. You didn't eat anything this morning — how could you not be hungry?

  1. 来 → 去. English "come over to her place" hides the direction; Chinese doesn't. You are speaking from your own flat, so her place is away from you: 去. 来 would only be right if you were already standing in her flat when you said it.
  2. 看电影了就 → 看了电影就. The 了 has to sit on V₁, in front of V₁'s object. Pushed to the end, 看电影了 closes the whole sentence ("we've seen the film") and leaves 就 with nothing to launch.
  3. 就我给 → 我就给. The clauses have different subjects, so the second subject opens its own clause and 就 stays glued to its verb. 就 can never stand in front of a subject.
  4. 能饿吗 → 能不饿吗. A plain 能…吗 rhetorical denies what it names, so 能饿吗 claims "you can't possibly be hungry" — the opposite of the point. Put the 不 inside.

  • For every 来/去: where is the speaker standing? Answer that before you pick the character.
  • Is 了 on V₁ and in front of V₁'s object?
  • Is 就 immediately before V₂, with any new subject in front of it?
  • Say the intended meaning out loud, then decide whether 不 belongs inside 能…吗.

3. A: "小丽 isn't here yet." B: "The moment she arrives, we'll start." [V₁了 … 就 V₂, two subjects] · 用: 到 · 我们 · 就 · 开始
4. You bought this many things — how could it not be expensive? [能不 … 吗?] · 用: 买 · 很多 · 东西 · 贵

1. 姐姐昨天回上海去了。
2. 我做了作业就跟朋友去游泳。
3. 她到了我们就开始。
4. 你买了很多东西，能不贵吗？

1. Jiějie zuótiān huí Shànghǎi qù le. 2. Wǒ zuò le zuòyè jiù gēn péngyou qù yóuyǒng. 3. Tā dào le wǒmen jiù kāishǐ. 4. Nǐ mǎi le hěn duō dōngxi, néng bú guì ma?

1. My older sister went back to Shanghai yesterday. 2. As soon as I've done my homework I'll go swimming with a friend. 3. The moment she arrives, we'll start. 4. You bought this many things — how could it not be expensive?

Why these are right: (1) 上海 is a place, so it must sit inside 回 … 去, with 了 at the very end of the sentence. (2) 了 rides on 做 and 作业 follows it; 就 then launches 去游泳, and V₂ stays bare because the swim hasn't happened yet. (3) The two clauses have different subjects, so 她 opens the first, 我们 opens the second, and 就 stays glued to 开始. (4) The evidence comes first, then 能不…吗 turns "it was expensive" into a claim nobody is invited to argue with.

Check your own four:

  • Sentence 1: is 上海 between 回 and 去, with 了 at the end? 回去上海了 ✗
  • Sentence 2: 做了作业 ✓ · 做作业了 ✗ — and is V₂ still bare?
  • Sentence 3: 我们 in front of 就, not behind it.
  • Sentence 4: did the 不 go inside 能…吗? Without it you are claiming the opposite.
  • Did every word listed after 用 actually make it into your sentence?
